A kicker is a two-candle pattern in which the second candle opens with a gap in the opposite direction to the first and keeps going. The market changes direction abruptly and does not trade back into the first candle. Traders see it as a sharp change of sentiment, often after news. It is rare.

## What is a kicker pattern?

A kicker is a reversal where the second candle does not trade back into the first candle's range at all. Price gaps and then keeps moving away. Because the move is so one-sided, it is often tied to an event: earnings, a news release or a surprise decision.

It is similar to [engulfing](/bullish-engulfing/) candles in that it reverses direction. It differs because the second candle opens beyond the first candle's open, and the gap is not filled. It is also related to the [marubozu](/marubozu/) and [belt hold](/belt-hold/), since the second candle is often long with little or no shadow on the opening side.

## How it is identified

**Bullish kicker** (after a downtrend or a decline):

1. The first candle is bearish.
2. The second candle is bullish and opens at or above the open of the first candle, leaving a gap.
3. The second candle does not fall back into the body of the first candle. In its strict form, its low stays above the first candle's high.

**Bearish kicker** is the reverse: the first candle is bullish, and the second opens at or below the open of the first and moves down, with no overlap.

Many books describe the first candle as being in the direction of the trend, which can be of any size. The second candle is usually long.

**Worked example.** A candle opens at 52 and closes at 50 (bearish, low at 49.5, high 52.5). The next candle opens at 55, a gap above 52.5, and closes at 58 with a low of 55. It never trades back into the first candle, so this is a bullish kicker.

## How to read it

The gap shows that the market's view changed overnight or over a news event. Sellers who were comfortable the day before find themselves trapped and may have to cover into the move. Because price does not return into the first candle, anyone who sold in that range is now on the wrong side.

The pattern is most meaningful when the earlier trend had been orderly. Some traders use the open of the second candle, or the gap itself, as a reference for where the idea fails.

Gaps often fill. A kicker that gets filled within a few candles has failed.

## Best market states

**Reversal (best).** After a clear trend, a kicker marks a decisive change.

**Breakout (good).** A kicker through a range edge, often on news, can start a new move.

**Range (weak).** Gaps inside a range are often filled by the next swing.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Gap | Clean, with no overlap with the first candle |
| Second candle | Long body, holding away from the gap |
| Cause | A known catalyst such as news or earnings (where relevant) |
| Volume | Notably high, where volume data is reliable |
| Follow-through | The gap is not filled in the next few candles |

## Common mistakes

- Calling a normal gap a kicker without the colour change.
- Allowing overlap and still using the name.
- Expecting kickers on 24-hour markets, where clean gaps are rare.
- Ignoring the news behind the gap.
- Trading it in a range, where gaps tend to fill.

## FAQ

**Is a kicker the same as an engulfing pattern?**
No. An engulfing candle takes in the previous candle's body. A kicker leaves a gap and never trades back into the first candle. The kicker is the sharper pattern, and also much rarer.

**Why is it considered strong?**
The gap against the old direction, with no overlap, shows an abrupt change of sentiment. That is the reasoning behind its reputation. Treat that as an interpretation, not a proven edge.

**Does it need news?**
No, but it often follows news or a surprise. Check what happened. A kicker with no cause is less convincing.

**Can it work in forex?**
Clean gaps are uncommon, except around weekend closes and some events. Many traders loosen the rules there, which changes what the pattern means.

**What happens if the gap fills?**
Then the pattern has failed. Price has traded back into the first candle's range, which breaks the main rule.
