An inside bar is a bar whose whole range sits inside the range of the bar before it. The earlier bar is called the mother bar. The inside bar shows a pause: price did not extend either way. Traders place orders above and below the mother bar and take the side that breaks first.

## What is an inside bar?

An inside bar has a lower high and a higher low than the previous bar. In plain terms, today's range fits inside yesterday's. Volatility has dropped and the market is waiting.

On a daily chart this is often called an inside day. The same idea works on any time frame. Japanese candlestick readers know a close relative, the harami, where the body (not the whole range) sits inside the previous body.

There is no known inventor. The pattern is described in many price action books and courses. Al Brooks (2012) treats inside bars as pauses and as small trading ranges, whose breakout direction is uncertain, so he stresses reading the context rather than treating them as a signal on their own.

## How it is identified

1. Find a mother bar, usually a larger-than-average bar.
2. The next bar has a high lower than the mother's high and a low higher than the mother's low.
3. Some traders also accept equal highs or lows. Choose a rule and keep it.

Several inside bars in a row are possible, and traders usually keep the mother bar's range as the trigger.

**Worked example.** The mother bar has a high of 110 and a low of 100. The next bar has a high of 107 and a low of 103, so it is inside. A trader places a buy order at 110.1 and a sell order at 99.9. If price breaks above 110.1, the buy triggers with a stop at 99.9 or at the midpoint of the mother bar, 105. The risk is 10.2 points with the wide stop or 5.1 with the midpoint stop. If the buy triggers, the sell order is cancelled.

**Variants.** Some traders trade only in the direction of the trend. Others use the inside bar's own range for a tighter stop, which gives more false triggers.

## How to read it

**Compression.** An inside bar shows reduced range. It can lead to expansion in either direction, so the bar says little about direction. Context does the work.

**With the trend.** In an uptrend, an inside bar after a strong bar is often a pause before a continuation break. See [Market structure](/market-structure/).

**At a level.** An inside bar that forms at support or resistance can mark a decision point. A break from there can be strong, or a failure that reverses. See [False breakout](/false-breakout/).

**Mother bar size.** A very small mother bar gives a tiny range and many false triggers. A very large one gives a wide stop. Use [ATR](/atr/) to judge.

**Failed break.** If price breaks one side and closes back inside the mother bar, the break has failed. This is the basis of the fakey pattern. See [Fakey (false inside-bar break)](/fakey-pattern/).

## Best market states

**Pauses in trends (good).** The inside bar acts as a rest before the next leg.

**Compression before breakouts (good).** After a period of narrowing ranges, an inside bar can mark the final squeeze. See [Bollinger squeeze](/bollinger-squeeze/).

**Ranges (weak).** In a sideways market, inside bars are common and the breaks tend to reverse. They are noise rather than signals.

**Volatility.** The bar is a volatility signal: it says range has contracted. It does not say which way the expansion will go.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Inside | High lower and low higher than the mother bar |
| Mother size | Mother bar is not tiny, and not so large that risk is excessive |
| Direction | Break in the direction of the trend, or at a clear level |
| Close | A close beyond the mother bar, not only a wick |
| Level | Not breaking directly into a nearby opposing level |
| Count | Fewer inside bars in a row suggests less noise; many in a row suggests a range |

## Common mistakes

- Trading every inside bar. Most appear in the middle of nothing and lead nowhere.
- Assuming the direction. An inside bar is neutral until it breaks.
- Using the inside bar's own range as the stop when the market is noisy.
- Ignoring that the mother bar may be a news spike.
- Not planning for a failed break. A close back inside the mother bar means the idea has failed.
- Entering against the larger trend without a level to justify it.

## FAQ

**What does an inside bar mean?**
It means price traded within the previous bar's range and failed to extend either way. This signals a pause and lower volatility. Which way price goes next is not shown by the bar itself.

**How do you trade an inside bar?**
A common approach places a buy stop order above the mother bar's high and a sell stop below its low, cancelling the other when one triggers. Many traders take only the side in line with the trend. A stop goes at the opposite end or the midpoint.

**Is an inside bar bullish or bearish?**
It is neutral. The bar shows compression. The direction of the break matters, and the trend and nearby levels give a clue as to which break is more likely. Neither is certain.

**What is the difference between an inside bar and a harami?**
A harami compares candle bodies: the second body sits inside the first body. An inside bar compares whole ranges, including wicks. All inside bars that sit within a mother body are close to a harami, but not every harami is an inside bar.

**Can an inside bar be a reversal signal?**
Sometimes. At a major level after a strong move, a break against the move may mark a reversal. Without that context, an inside bar is mostly a pause. A failed break back into the mother bar is sometimes read as a reversal.
