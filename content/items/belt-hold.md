A belt hold is a single long candle that opens at its low (bullish) or high (bearish) and then runs in one direction with little or no shadow on the opening side. It often appears after a gap against the trend. It is a warning that the old trend may be turning, and it is stronger with confirmation.

## What is a belt hold?

A belt hold is a candle that "holds the belt": the open sits right at one end of the candle and price drives away from it. A bullish belt hold opens at the low of the period and rises. A bearish belt hold opens at the high and falls.

It is close to a [marubozu](/marubozu/), a candle with no shadows at all. The belt hold only requires no shadow on the opening side. It may have a shadow at the other end.

## How it is identified

**Bullish belt hold** (after a downtrend):

1. The trend before it is down.
2. The candle is bullish with a long body, longer than recent candles.
3. The open is at, or very close to, the low. There is no lower shadow, or a tiny one.
4. It often opens with a gap down from the prior close, and the buying then wipes out that opening move.

**Bearish belt hold** (after an uptrend) is the mirror image: a long bearish candle that opens at or near its high, with little or no upper shadow, often after a gap up.

There is no fixed number for "long" or "little". A common working rule is a body at least as long as the average of the last 10 candles, and an opening-side shadow below about 5% of the range.

**Worked example.** A downtrend closes at 50. The next candle opens at 48 (a gap down), never trades below 48, and rises to 55 before closing at 54. The body is 6 long, there is no lower shadow, and the previous candles were about 2 long. This is a bullish belt hold.

## How to read it

The story is a day that starts badly for the old trend and never recovers. A bullish belt hold opens where sellers expect to push lower, and buyers take control from the first trade. The longer the body, the stronger the message.

Some traders treat the low of a bullish belt hold (or the high of a bearish one) as a rough stop reference, because a return to the opening price would undo the message. Others look for a higher close on the next candle before acting.

## Best market states

**Reversal (best).** After an extended move, especially into a key level, a belt hold shows a sharp change of control.

**Range (weak).** Long candles in a range are often just a swing between the edges.

**Trend (weak).** A belt hold in the direction of the trend is only a normal strong candle.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear move in the opposite direction |
| Candle size | Body longer than recent candles |
| Location | Near support (bullish) or resistance (bearish) |
| Next candle | Follow-through in the new direction |
| Volume | Higher than recent bars, where reliable |

## Common mistakes

- Calling any long candle a belt hold when it has a large opening-side shadow.
- Reading a belt hold with the trend as a reversal.
- Skipping the context: a belt hold in the middle of a range means little.
- Ignoring that markets trading around the clock rarely gap, so the opening drama may be missing.
- Acting before the next candle holds the gain.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between a belt hold and a marubozu?**
A marubozu has no shadow at either end. A belt hold needs no shadow only on the opening side and may have one on the other. In practice the two overlap, and software definitions vary.

**Is a belt hold the same as the Japanese "yorikiri"?**
Nison notes that the Japanese name is often given as yorikiri line. Different books use slightly different wording, so treat the names as one idea.

**Does a belt hold need a gap?**
No. A gap makes the picture more dramatic, but the core rule is the open at the extreme and a long body.

**Is a belt hold a buy or sell signal?**
It is not a signal by itself. It is a warning that control may have changed. Most traders want a clear prior trend, a sensible level and a confirming candle.

**Which time frames work best?**
Daily and higher time frames usually carry more meaning. On very short time frames, long candles with no shadow are common and often mean little.
