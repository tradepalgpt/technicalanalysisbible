A tasuki gap is a three-candle continuation pattern built around a gap. In an uptrend, a bullish candle gaps up, then a bearish candle opens inside it and closes inside the gap without filling it. The unfilled gap suggests the uptrend will continue. The downside version is the mirror image.

## What is a tasuki gap?

A gap is a space on the chart where no trading occurred between two candles. In Japanese charting, a gap in the direction of a trend is a sign of strength. The tasuki gap looks at what happens when price tries to refill that gap.

In the upside version, two bullish candles are separated by a gap. A bearish candle then opens inside the second bullish body and closes inside the gap. It falls back toward the gap but does not close it. The gap holds, which is read as support for the trend.

The word "tasuki" is Japanese for a sash worn across the shoulder. Nison uses the term in his book. The pattern is related to [gap three methods](/gap-three-methods/), where the gap is filled completely and the reading is different.

## How it is identified

For the **upside** tasuki gap in an uptrend:

1. **Candle 1:** a bullish candle.
2. **Candle 2:** a bullish candle that gaps up above candle 1's high.
3. **Candle 3:** a bearish candle that opens inside candle 2's body and closes inside the gap, between candle 1's close and candle 2's open.
4. The gap is not fully closed.

The **downside** version is a mirror image: two bearish candles with a gap down, followed by a bullish candle that closes inside the gap.

In markets with few gaps, such as forex during the week, a true gap is rare, so the pattern appears seldom.

**Worked example.** Candle 1 opens at 100 and closes at 104. Candle 2 gaps up, opening at 106 and closing at 109. Candle 3 opens at 108 and closes at 105. The gap runs from 104 to 106, and candle 3 closes at 105, inside it, without reaching 104. This is an upside tasuki gap.

## How to read it

The first move in the opposite direction fails to close the gap. That means the trend's push was real, and dip buyers (in an uptrend) were present.

If price later closes the gap fully, the pattern's reading is cancelled. A close through the far side of the gap is an early sign that the trend may be failing.

## Best market states

**Established trends (best).** The pattern describes a continuation and needs a trend to continue.

**Trends with fresh gaps (good).** The gap shows strength in the move.

**Ranges (weak).** Gaps in a range often fill and mean little.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear trend before candle 1 |
| Gap | A visible gap between candles 1 and 2 |
| Candle 3 | Closes inside the gap, not through it |
| Next candle | Resumes in the trend direction |
| Volume | Lighter volume on the pullback candle, where available |

## Common mistakes

- Calling it when the gap has been fully closed.
- Expecting it in 24-hour markets with few gaps.
- Treating it as a reversal because the third candle is against the trend.
- Ignoring that a gap fill later cancels the reading.
- Trading it in a sideways market.

## FAQ

**What does tasuki mean?**
It is a Japanese word for a sash worn over the shoulder. Candlestick authors use it as the name of this pattern, which links the gap and the candle that tries to refill it.

**Is a tasuki gap bullish or bearish?**
The upside version is bullish and the downside version is bearish. Both are continuation patterns that say the existing trend is likely to carry on.

**What if the gap fully closes?**
Then the pattern is no longer a tasuki gap. A fully closed gap in a trend is usually read as a sign of weakness, and it is closer to the gap three methods pattern.

**Does it work in forex?**
True gaps are less common in forex during the week, so the pattern is rare there. It is more useful on stocks and indices that gap at the open.

**Is it reliable?**
It is a minor pattern. Use it as supporting evidence within a trend and not as a stand-alone trigger.
