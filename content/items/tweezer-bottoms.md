A tweezer bottom is a two-candle pattern at the end of a downtrend. Both candles reach the same low, and the second candle turns up. The matching lows show that price was rejected twice at the same level. It is a warning of a possible turn higher, and it works best when it forms at support.

## What are tweezer bottoms?

The name comes from the shape, like the two prongs of a pair of tweezers pointing down. Price falls to a low on the first candle. On the second candle it tries again, reaches the same low, and bounces. The second failure to go lower suggests that buyers are defending the level.

Tweezer patterns have no strict official definition. Traders disagree about candle colours and sizes, and about how closely the lows must match. For that reason, figures from different books and studies are hard to compare.

## How it is identified

A tweezer bottom has:

1. **A prior downtrend.** Without a fall, there is nothing to reverse.
2. **A first candle that continues the trend.** It is usually bearish, often with a solid body.
3. **A second candle with the same low.** The lows are equal or nearly equal. How close is a personal rule, for example within a small fraction of the average candle range.
4. **A second candle that turns up.** It is usually bullish. A bullish second candle that closes near or above the first candle's open is a stronger signal.

**Worked example.** After a fall, candle one opens at 80, drops to a low of 74 and closes at 75. Candle two opens at 75, dips to 74 again and closes at 79. Both lows are 74, and the second candle closed well above its open. This is a tweezer bottom with support at 74.

Most traders count only candles that sit directly next to each other.

## How to read it

**Rejection of a level.** Two attempts failing at the same price shows that buyers are active there. The low becomes a reference: the pattern is wrong if price later closes below it.

**Location matters.** A tweezer bottom at known [support](/support-and-resistance/), a prior low or a round number is more meaningful than one in open space.

**The second candle.** A long bullish second candle, or one that closes back inside the first candle's body, shows stronger buying than a small one.

**Next candle.** Many traders wait for a third candle that closes higher before acting. This also gives a clear stop level at the shared low.

**Similar patterns.** A tweezer bottom can overlap with a [bullish engulfing](/bullish-engulfing/), a [piercing line](/piercing-line/) or a [hammer](/hammer/). Where it overlaps, the stronger pattern is usually named instead.

## Best market states

**End of a downtrend (best).** A clear fall followed by a rejection gives the pattern a story.

**At support (good).** Matching lows at an existing floor carry more weight than isolated ones.

**Strong trends (caution).** A pause at a low can be followed by a new low. One pair of matching lows is not enough on its own.

**Ranges (weak).** In sideways markets matching lows are common and mean little unless they sit at the bottom of the range.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear fall into the pattern |
| Matching lows | Lows equal or within a tight, consistent tolerance |
| Second candle | Bullish, ideally closing above the first candle's midpoint |
| Level | A known support, prior low or round number |
| Follow-through | A third candle closing above the pattern's high |
| Volume | Rising volume on the turn adds weight where volume is reliable |
| Stop | Below the shared low |

## Common mistakes

- Seeing a tweezer bottom in a sideways market where there is no trend to reverse.
- Insisting on exactly equal lows, or accepting lows so far apart the pattern means nothing.
- Acting on the pattern alone, without a level or a confirming candle.
- Ignoring the second candle: a small or bearish second candle is a weak signal.
- Using it on very short time frames where matching lows appear constantly.
- Confusing it with a [double bottom](/double-bottom/), which forms over a longer time with a peak between two lows.

## Pairs well with

- **[Support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/)**: the pattern is far more useful at a level that has held before.
- **[RSI](/rsi/)**: an oversold reading or bullish divergence at the second low strengthens the signal.
- **[Volume](/volume/)**: heavy volume on the rejection suggests real buying.
- **[Divergence](/divergence/)**: weaker downside momentum at the second low supports the reversal idea.

## FAQ

**What is a tweezer bottom?**
It is a two-candle pattern where both candles share the same low after a downtrend, and the second turns up. The matching lows show a price level that sellers failed to break twice, which can warn of a reversal.

**Is a tweezer bottom bullish or bearish?**
It is a bullish reversal pattern. It forms at the end of a downtrend and suggests that buyers are taking control. Its mirror image, the tweezer top, is the bearish version at the end of an uptrend.

**Do the two candles have to be different colours?**
Not by strict rule, but the usual textbook form has a bearish first candle and a bullish second one. A bullish second candle shows that the rejection was decisive, so it is the more convincing version.

**How exact do the lows need to match?**
There is no standard. Many traders allow a small tolerance, such as a fraction of the average candle range. Pick a rule and keep to it so that results can be compared fairly.

**How reliable is a tweezer bottom?**
There is no solid evidence base that makes it work on its own. It is a simple warning about price rejection, and it is best used with a level, a trend and a confirming candle.
