A bullish harami is a two-candle pattern at the end of a downtrend. A long bearish candle is followed by a small candle whose body sits inside the body of the first. It shows selling pressure has eased. It is a warning that a fall is tiring, not a buy signal, and it needs confirmation.

## What is a bullish harami?

"Harami" is an old Japanese word for a pregnant woman. The long first candle is the mother and the small second candle is the baby inside. After a decline, the first candle shows sellers firmly in control. The second candle opens inside that range and barely moves, so the sellers have stopped pressing.

That is the whole message: the fall has paused. It does not prove buyers have taken over. This is why the bullish harami is usually seen as weaker than a [bullish engulfing](/bullish-engulfing/) pattern, where the second candle covers the whole first body.

## How it is identified

A bullish harami has:

1. **A prior downtrend.** Without one there is nothing to reverse.
2. **A long bearish (down) candle** with a large body.
3. **A small second candle** whose body sits inside the first body. Nison's description allows either colour; a bullish second candle is often read as slightly stronger.
4. **Wicks.** Some definitions want the whole second candle, wicks included, inside the first body. Others only require the body. Charting platforms and scanners differ.

**Worked example.** Day one opens at 110 and closes at 100, a bearish body of 10. Day two opens at 103 and closes at 106. The second body (103 to 106) is inside the first (100 to 110), so this is a bullish harami. If day two had closed at 99, it would not count.

## How to read it

**After a strong fall.** Several strong down candles, then a harami, suggests selling is fading. Price may bounce, stall or drift sideways.

**At support.** A harami at known support or a previous low has a reason behind it, so it carries more weight.

**The next candle matters.** A close above the high of the first candle is a stronger sign than the harami alone. If the next candle closes below the first candle's low, the pattern has failed.

**Size.** The smaller the second body compared with the first, the clearer the loss of momentum. If the second candle is a [doji](/doji/), the pattern is called a harami cross.

## Best market states

**Reversals after an extended fall (best).** The pattern is about momentum fading at the end of a move.

**Strong trends (weak).** In a strong downtrend, small pauses are normal and the fall often resumes.

**Ranges (weak).** Small candles inside larger ones are common in sideways markets and mean little.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear, extended fall into the pattern |
| First candle | Long body, larger than recent candles |
| Second candle | Body fully inside the first body |
| Next candle | A close above the first candle's high, or a clear bullish candle |
| Level | Support, a prior low or a lower band nearby |

## Common mistakes

- Buying on the harami alone without waiting for confirmation.
- Labelling any small candle after a large one as a harami without checking the prior trend.
- Reading the second candle as proof of a reversal, when it only shows a pause.
- Using it on very short time frames, where inside candles appear constantly.
- Switching between "body inside" and "whole candle inside" rules from one chart to the next.

## FAQ

**What does a bullish harami mean?**
It means selling pressure has paused after a fall. A small candle forms inside the body of a large bearish one. It warns that the decline may be losing force, and it needs confirmation from the next candle or a key level.

**Is a bullish harami a buy signal?**
Not by itself. The second candle only shows a pause. Most traders wait for a bullish follow-through candle and look for support or other evidence before acting.

**What is the difference between a bullish harami and a bullish engulfing?**
In a harami the second body sits inside the first. In an engulfing pattern the second body covers the first. Engulfing shows buyers taking control and is usually read as stronger. See [Bullish engulfing](/bullish-engulfing/).

**Does the second candle have to be bullish?**
Not under Nison's description, which allows either colour. A bullish second candle is often seen as slightly stronger. Some scanners insist on it, so check your software.

**What is a bearish harami?**
It is the mirror image: a long bullish candle followed by a small candle inside its body, after a rise. It warns that buying is tiring. See [Bearish harami](/bearish-harami/).
