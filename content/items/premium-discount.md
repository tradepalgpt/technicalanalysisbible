Premium and discount zones split a price range at its midpoint. The upper half is the premium, where price is considered expensive, and the lower half is the discount, where it is considered cheap. Traders use the split as a filter: look to buy in discount and sell in premium. It is a trader-education concept with no academic evidence base.

## What are premium and discount zones?

Take a swing from a low to a high, or the boundaries of a range. The midpoint is the equilibrium. Price above it is in premium. Price below it is in discount.

The idea is simple value logic. A buyer would prefer to buy cheaply and a seller would prefer to sell dearly, so long entries are favoured below the midpoint and short entries above it. In an uptrend, traders wait for a pullback into discount before buying. In a downtrend they wait for a rally into premium before selling.

The maths is the same as a 50% [Fibonacci retracement](/fibonacci-retracement/). The 50% level is not a Fibonacci ratio, but many charting tools include it, and the idea of a halfway pullback predates smart money teaching. The premium and discount labels come from retail ICT and smart money concept (SMC) teaching, where the concept is used to filter other setups such as [order blocks](/order-block/). No peer-reviewed study supports an edge from the 50% split.

## How it is identified

1. **Choose the range.** Use the last major swing low and high in a trend, or the top and bottom of a trading range.
2. **Find the midpoint.** Midpoint = (high + low) / 2.
3. **Label the halves.** Above the midpoint is premium, below is discount.
4. **Filter.** Only look for longs in discount and shorts in premium, or at least favour them.

Some traders add finer levels, such as 25% and 75%, or use the 62% to 79% retracement band as an "optimal trade entry". These are conventions that vary between teachers.

**Worked example.** A swing runs from a low of 100 to a high of 120. The midpoint is (120 + 100) / 2 = 110. Price is in discount below 110 and in premium above it. After the high, price falls to 106. That is in discount, so a trader who wants a long can look for a setup there, such as a bullish order block. The same trader would not look to buy at 116, which is premium.

## How to read it

**Direction filter.** The rule is a filter, not a signal. It narrows where to look, and another tool gives the trigger.

**In a trend.** An uptrend pulling back into discount is a normal pattern. A pullback that fails to reach the midpoint shows strength, and one that falls well below it shows weakness.

**In a range.** The midpoint divides the range. Range traders buy near the lower part and sell near the upper part, which is plain [range trading](/range-trading/).

**Updating.** The zones change when a new swing high or low is set. A range that is not redrawn gives the wrong halves.

**Caution.** A strong move can stay in premium or discount for a long time. Price being cheap does not mean it will rise.

## Best market states

**Trends with pullbacks (best).** The filter keeps entries to the better half of a pullback.

**Ranges (best).** Boundaries are clear, and the midpoint is a natural divider.

**Breakouts (weak).** Price leaves the range and stays in what was premium or discount, so the filter blocks the best moves.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the midpoint as a signal. It only filters.
- Using stale swings, so the zones do not match current price.
- Buying every discount. Falling markets stay in discount for long runs.
- Treating the 50% level as a Fibonacci ratio. It is a simple midpoint.
- Using it in a breakout, where price stays in one half.
- Treating the concept as proven. It has no academic evidence base.

## FAQ

**What are premium and discount zones?**
They are the two halves of a price range, split at the midpoint. Above it is premium, where price is thought expensive. Below it is discount, where price is thought cheap. Traders use the split to filter long and short setups.

**Is premium and discount the same as the 50% Fibonacci level?**
The calculation is the same: the halfway point of a swing. The 50% level is not one of the Fibonacci ratios, but most platforms include it. The labels premium and discount come from smart money concept teaching.

**Should I only buy in discount?**
It is a common rule among those who use the idea, since it favours buying lower in the range. It does not guarantee a rise, because price can stay in discount while a downtrend continues.

**How do you draw premium and discount zones?**
Mark the swing low and swing high, or the range boundaries. Find the midpoint by adding them and dividing by two. Everything above it is premium and everything below it is discount. Redraw when a new swing forms.

**Does it work in a breakout?**
Poorly. In a breakout price moves out of the range and stays in what was premium or discount. A filter that only allows trades in the other half would miss the move.
