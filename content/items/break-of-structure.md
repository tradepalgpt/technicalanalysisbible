A break of structure (BOS) is when price moves beyond the last swing high in an uptrend, or the last swing low in a downtrend. Traders read it as the trend continuing. The term is common in smart money concepts (SMC) and ICT-style trader education, and it is not from academic research.

## What is a break of structure?

In an uptrend, price makes a series of higher highs and higher lows. Each time price takes out the previous swing high, the trend has made a new high and shown it is still intact. Traders who use the term BOS call that moment a break of structure. In a downtrend the same idea applies to a break below the previous swing low.

The ideas underneath are old. Dow theory, covered by Murphy (1999) and by Edwards and Magee, says a trend continues while it keeps making new peaks and troughs. A close beyond the last swing is the same idea as a trendline or a channel break, using swings instead of lines. Al Brooks (2012) describes the same behaviour as a breakout of a swing point.

The label "BOS" belongs to trader education, especially smart money concepts and ICT material. It has no standard definition in the published research, so definitions vary. This guide uses one clear rule and notes where others differ.

## How it is identified

1. Define a swing with a fixed rule. See [Swing highs and swing lows](/swing-highs-lows/).
2. Identify the trend from the sequence of swings. See [Market structure](/market-structure/).
3. Mark the last confirmed swing in the direction of the trend: the last swing high in an uptrend.
4. A BOS occurs when a bar closes beyond that swing.

Some traders count a wick through the swing. A stricter rule needs a body close beyond it. Write down which one you use.

**Worked example.** An uptrend has a swing low at 100, a swing high at 110 and a higher swing low at 104. Price rallies and a bar closes at 111. That close is above the 110 swing high, so a bullish BOS has occurred. The structure now reads higher high (111), with the last higher low at 104 as the point that would break the trend.

If price had only spiked to 110.5 and closed at 109, many traders would call that a sweep or a failed break, not a BOS.

## How to read it

**Continuation.** A BOS in the direction of the trend shows the trend is still making progress. Traders often wait for a pullback after a BOS rather than chase the break. See [Pullback (retracement entry)](/pullback-trading/).

**Strength of the break.** A bar with a large body that closes well beyond the swing is stronger than a small bar that closes just past it. A bar that breaks, then falls back inside the old range, is a failed break.

**Direction against the trend.** A break of the last swing against the prevailing trend is called a change of character or a market structure shift in the same language. See [Change of character](/change-of-character/) and [Market structure shift](/market-structure-shift/). The terms are not used the same way by every teacher.

**Retest.** After a break, price often returns to the broken swing level. See [Breakout and retest](/breakout-retest/).

## Best market states

**Trends (best).** The idea is built for trends with clear steps.

**Breakouts (good).** A BOS out of a range or consolidation can mark the start of a move, though many fail.

**Ranges (weak).** In a range, price breaks small swings constantly and then returns. The breaks show noise, not trend continuation.

**Fast, news-driven markets.** Spikes can break a swing for a few seconds and reverse. A close-based rule helps.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Close | A bar closes beyond the swing, not only a wick |
| Trend | The break is in the direction of the higher time frame trend |
| Body | The breaking bar has a large body and closes near its extreme |
| Follow-through | The next bars hold beyond the level |
| Retest | Price returns to the broken level and holds it |
| Context | Not straight into a major opposing level |

## Common mistakes

- Treating every wick through a swing as a BOS. A close-based rule filters many false ones.
- Using different swing definitions on each chart.
- Buying the break far from the last higher low, which makes the stop too wide.
- Calling a BOS in a range. The pattern needs a trend to continue.
- Assuming a BOS predicts how far price will go. It shows only that the trend continued.
- Mixing up BOS with change of character. One is continuation and the other is the first warning of a reversal.

## FAQ

**What does BOS mean in trading?**
BOS stands for break of structure. It describes price moving beyond the last swing high in an uptrend or the last swing low in a downtrend, which traders read as continuation. The term is used mostly in smart money concepts material.

**What is the difference between BOS and CHoCH?**
A BOS is a break in the direction of the trend, showing continuation. A change of character (CHoCH) is a break against the trend, a first sign of reversal. Not every teacher uses these exact definitions, so check the source.

**Does a wick count as a break of structure?**
Definitions differ. Some traders count any trade beyond the swing. Stricter ones need a candle close beyond it. A wick through a level that closes back inside is often called a liquidity sweep instead. Choose one rule and keep it.

**Is break of structure the same as a breakout?**
They overlap. A breakout usually means price leaving a range or pattern. A BOS means exceeding the last swing in a trend. A BOS can also be a breakout, but the term focuses on the swing sequence.

**Is break of structure supported by research?**
The underlying idea of trends made of rising or falling swings comes from Dow theory. The BOS label is from trader education and has no standard definition or published test of its own. Treat it as a way to describe the chart.
