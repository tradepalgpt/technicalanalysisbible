A broadening formation, also called an expanding triangle or megaphone, is a chart pattern where price swings get wider over time. Highs rise and lows fall between two diverging trendlines. It signals rising volatility and uncertainty, and is more often used as a warning filter than as an entry signal.

## What is a broadening formation?

A broadening formation is the opposite of a [symmetrical triangle](/symmetrical-triangle/). In a triangle, swings get smaller and the lines converge. In a broadening formation, the swings grow, so the lines spread apart like a megaphone.

The pattern shows that traders are becoming less sure. Each rally goes higher than the last and each fall goes lower, but neither side wins. Buyers push to new highs and are rejected, then sellers push to new lows and are also rejected. The market is getting more active without agreeing on a direction.

Classic chart-pattern books describe the pattern, with Edwards and Magee noting that it tends to appear near market tops, on high and erratic volume. Bulkowski's encyclopedia also covers broadening patterns. Because of the loose shape, it is harder to trade than most patterns, and many traders use it as a warning rather than a signal.

## How it is identified

A broadening formation needs:

1. **At least two rising highs and two falling lows.** Together these give five reversal points, which is the usual minimum.
2. **An upper trendline** rising through the highs.
3. **A lower trendline** falling through the lows.
4. **Diverging lines.** The gap between them grows with time.

Variants include a broadening top (after an uptrend), a broadening bottom (after a downtrend) and right-angled versions, where one line is flat.

**Worked example.** Price swings between 100 and 110 at first. The next rally reaches 115 and the next fall reaches 95. A third rally reaches 120 and a third fall reaches 90. The width grows from 10 to 20 to 30 points. A measured-move style target would use the widest height. For example, a close below the lower line at 90 with a widest height of 30 gives 90 - 30 = **60** as a rough, unreliable target.

Targets from broadening patterns are particularly loose, so many traders do not use them.

## How to read it

**Warning filter.** The pattern's main use is to flag unstable conditions. Wide, erratic swings make stops harder to place and trend signals less reliable.

**Breakout.** A close outside either line, ideally after several touches, completes the pattern. Direction is not fixed. A broadening top often breaks down, but not always.

**False breaks.** Because the lines diverge, price often pokes through one and then reverses into the pattern. A close beyond the line is a stricter test than a wick.

**Volume.** High and uneven volume is typical during the pattern. Volume on the break gives some support.

## Best market states

**Volatility expansion (best).** The pattern is a picture of rising volatility.

**Reversals near tops (best, with caution).** Classic descriptions associate it with late-stage markets, but a pattern alone is not a reversal signal.

**Ranges (weak).** In a stable range, swings stay about the same width. If they begin to grow, the pattern is forming.

**Breakouts (weak).** The lines are loose, so breakouts are unreliable and often reverse.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Touches | At least five reversal points on the two lines |
| Lines | Clearly diverging, not parallel |
| Break | A close beyond a line, not a wick |
| Volume | High and erratic during the pattern, rising on the break |
| Context | Prior trend and whether price is near a major level |
| Stop | Back inside the pattern, with room for the wide swings |

## Common mistakes

- Trading the pattern as if it had a reliable direction. It does not.
- Using tight stops inside a widening range.
- Drawing lines through noise to force the shape.
- Acting on the first wick through a line.
- Using a precise measured target when the shape is loose.
- Forgetting that the swings are growing, so risk per trade also grows.

## FAQ

**What is a broadening formation?**
It is a chart pattern where swing highs rise and swing lows fall, so price moves between two diverging trendlines. It shows growing volatility and uncertainty rather than a clear trend.

**Is a broadening formation bullish or bearish?**
It has no fixed direction. A broadening top is often linked with late-stage markets and a possible fall, but the break can go either way, so traders wait for a close beyond a line.

**What is the difference between a broadening formation and a triangle?**
A triangle has converging lines, with swings getting smaller. A broadening formation has diverging lines, with swings getting bigger. They are visual opposites.

**How many touches does it need?**
Five reversal points is the usual minimum: three on one line and two on the other. Fewer touches leave too much room to see a shape that is not there.

**Can you trade it?**
Some traders do, but the loose shape makes entries and stops hard. Many use it as a filter that conditions are unstable, and reduce size or wait for clearer setups.
