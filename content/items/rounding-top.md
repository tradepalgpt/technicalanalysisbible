A rounding top is a slow reversal pattern at the end of an uptrend. Price rises more gradually, stalls, then falls in a mirror arc, forming a dome. A close below the support level at the base of the dome is the usual signal that the uptrend has given way to a downtrend.

## What is a rounding top?

A rounding top, sometimes called a dome, shows demand slowly drying up. In the first half, price keeps rising but each push gains less. At the peak, buyers and sellers balance. In the second half, price starts falling, first slowly and then faster, mirroring the climb.

The pattern is the top-side twin of the [rounding bottom](/rounding-bottom/), also called a saucer. Both are slow patterns, which makes them easy to see in hindsight. Spotting them live is harder, because there is no sharp turning point to mark. Edwards and Magee discuss rounding tops, and Bulkowski's encyclopedia covers them as well.

Because the move is gradual, the base level is not obvious. Traders usually mark it as the lowest point of the left side of the dome, where the rally began.

## How it is identified

The parts, in order:

1. **A prior uptrend.** The dome grows out of it.
2. **Left side.** Gains shrink. Each rally covers less ground than the last.
3. **Peak.** A broad, flat top, not a sharp spike.
4. **Right side.** Price curves down, with falls growing larger. Volume often rises as the slide gathers pace.
5. **Base level.** A horizontal line at the start of the dome, from the left side's lowest point.
6. **Break.** A close below that line.

**Measured target.** Measure the height of the dome from the peak to the base level. Subtract that distance from the breakout point.

**Worked example.** The dome peaks at 130 and the base level is at 110: a height of 20. Price closes below the base at 109. The measured target is 109 - 20 = **89**.

The target is a guide, not a promise. Many patterns fall short, and some go further.

## How to read it

**Curve.** The shape matters more than any single bar. A smooth, evenly curving arc is a better fit than a jagged one.

**Volume.** In classic descriptions, volume fades toward the peak, then picks up on the way down. A rise in volume on the break adds weight.

**Momentum.** A fading [momentum](/momentum-indicator/) reading or [divergence](/divergence/) on the left side and peak fits the idea of drying demand.

**Break.** A close below the base level completes the pattern. A retest of the broken level from below is common.

**Failure.** A move back above the peak means the pattern has failed.

## Best market states

**Reversals after a gradual uptrend (best).** The pattern suits slow, orderly tops.

**Fast markets (weak).** Sharp spikes and sudden drops do not form domes. See the [V reversal](/v-reversal/) for those.

**Ranges (weak).** A gentle rise and fall inside a range looks like a dome but may be nothing.

**Volatility.** Choppy price action breaks up the curve and makes the shape hard to confirm.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | Clear uptrend leading into the dome |
| Curve | Smooth, with gains shrinking then falls growing |
| Time | Takes many bars. A few bars is not a dome |
| Break | Close below the base level |
| Volume | Rising on the right side and the break |
| Stop | Above the dome's peak, or above the most recent swing high |

## Common mistakes

- Calling a short pause at a high a rounding top.
- Entering before the base level breaks.
- Putting the base level in a different place each time, so the signal keeps shifting.
- Expecting a precise entry on a pattern with no sharp turning point.
- Treating the measured target as certain.
- Ignoring the prior trend. Without an uptrend, there is nothing to reverse.

## FAQ

**What is a rounding top?**
It is a reversal pattern where price rises gradually, flattens, then falls in a mirror arc, forming a dome. A close below the base level at the start of the dome completes the pattern.

**How long does a rounding top take to form?**
It is a slow pattern, usually many bars on any timeframe. A few bars of rise and fall do not make a dome. The slow build is part of why it is hard to trade in real time.

**Is a rounding top bullish or bearish?**
It is bearish. It forms after an uptrend and points to a possible downtrend. Its mirror image, the rounding bottom, is bullish.

**Where do you enter on a rounding top?**
Many traders wait for a close below the base level. Entering earlier on the right side of the curve means acting before the pattern is confirmed, which carries more risk.

**How is it different from a head and shoulders?**
A head and shoulders has three distinct peaks and a neckline. A rounding top has a single smooth arc and no sharp turning points, so it is slower and its trigger is less precise.
