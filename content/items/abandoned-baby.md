An abandoned baby is a rare three-candle reversal pattern. A doji sits alone after a gap, with a second gap on the other side, so price has no overlap with the candles around it. It is a stricter form of the morning or evening star. It warns that a move has stalled, and it needs confirmation.

## What is an abandoned baby?

The name comes from the picture: the doji looks like a baby left alone on an island, cut off by gaps on both sides. Because a gap is a price range where no trading happened, the doji's whole range, shadows included, sits apart from the candles before and after it.

It works like a [morning star](/morning-star/) or [evening star](/evening-star/) with a [doji](/doji/) in the middle, but with the extra condition that both gaps are complete. That condition makes it rare, especially in markets that trade around the clock, where gaps are uncommon outside the weekend or news events.

## How it is identified

A bullish abandoned baby (after a downtrend) has three candles:

1. **A long bearish candle** continuing the fall.
2. **A doji** whose high is below the low of the first candle. It gaps down.
3. **A bullish candle** whose low is above the high of the doji, gapping up. Many traders also want it to close well into the first candle's body.

The bearish version is the mirror image after an uptrend: a long bullish candle, a doji gapping up, then a bearish candle gapping down.

**Worked example.** After a fall, candle 1 opens at 105 and closes at 100, low 99. Candle 2 gaps down, with a high of 98.5, a low of 97 and open and close both near 98. Candle 3 gaps up with a low of 99 and closes at 103. The doji's shadows (97 to 98.5) overlap neither neighbour, so this is a bullish abandoned baby.

## How to read it

The gap down into the doji looks like more selling. The doji shows the push ran out of force. The gap back up shows buyers took over at once. Traders read this as a sharp change of mind in the market.

In stock and index charts, the gaps often appear at session opens. Some data feeds fill in the gap by setting each open to the previous close, which hides the pattern. Check how your platform draws gaps.

Many traders treat the pattern as a warning and wait for the next candle to follow through before acting.

## Best market states

**Reversal (best).** The pattern needs a clear trend before it, so it can mark the turn.

**Range (weak).** Inside a sideways market a gapped doji is usually just noise.

**Trend (weak).** In a strong trend a pause often resolves in the trend's direction, so a lone pattern against it is less reliable.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear move before the pattern |
| Strict gaps | Doji shadows overlap neither neighbouring candle |
| Third candle | A strong candle, closing well into the first body |
| Context | A support or resistance level nearby |
| Volume | Higher volume on the third candle adds weight, where reliable |

## Common mistakes

- Calling a pattern an abandoned baby when the shadows overlap. That is an ordinary star pattern.
- Using it on 24-hour markets without checking whether a true gap exists.
- Treating its rarity as a sign of strength.
- Trading it in the middle of a range.
- Acting on the doji alone, before the third candle.

## FAQ

**How is an abandoned baby different from a morning star?**
A morning star has a small middle candle with a body, and its gaps need only be body to body. An abandoned baby needs a doji whose entire range, shadows included, is gapped away from both neighbours. See [Morning Star vs Abandoned Baby](/compare/morning-star-vs-abandoned-baby/).

**Why is it so rare?**
It needs two full gaps in a row. Gaps are common in stocks at the open, but much rarer in forex and other near-continuous markets. Strict rules also exclude many near misses.

**Is it a strong signal?**
Traders often describe it as a significant pattern because the gaps are dramatic. Treat that as a reading of the picture, not a proven edge. Test it on your own market and time frame.

**Do I need to wait for the third candle?**
Yes. Without the third candle you only have a gapped doji, which is a warning of indecision.

**Does it work on any time frame?**
It can appear on any, but longer time frames such as daily usually carry more meaning than very short ones, where gaps and doji arise from thin trading.
