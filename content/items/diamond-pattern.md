A diamond is a rare reversal chart pattern. Price swings widen, then narrow, so the outline looks like a diamond. A diamond top forms after an uptrend and a diamond bottom after a downtrend. A close through the edge in the direction of the break completes the pattern.

## What is a diamond pattern?

A diamond has two halves. In the first, price swings get wider: highs rise and lows fall, like a [broadening formation](/broadening-formation/). In the second, the swings get narrower and price squeezes into a point, like a [symmetrical triangle](/symmetrical-triangle/). Together the two halves form a diamond shape.

The idea is that trading becomes more active and uncertain as the trend runs out, then calms as buyers and sellers settle on a new direction. When price leaves the shape, the move that follows is often the start of a reversal. Classic chart-pattern books such as Edwards and Magee's discuss the diamond, and Bulkowski's encyclopedia covers it too. Both stress that it is uncommon.

Because the pattern is rare and its edges are loose, the most common error is finding a diamond in noisy price action after the fact.

## How it is drawn

A valid diamond needs these parts:

1. **A prior trend.** A diamond top needs an uptrend. A diamond bottom needs a downtrend.
2. **Widening half.** At least two rising highs and two falling lows, so the swings expand.
3. **Narrowing half.** Highs now fall and lows rise, so the swings contract. This half is usually the clearer one.
4. **Trendlines.** Four lines make the diamond: two for the widening half and two for the narrowing half. Draw them through the swing highs and lows.
5. **Break.** A close through one of the narrowing-half lines, in the direction of the reversal.

**Measured target.** Measure the height of the diamond at its widest point. Project that distance from the breakout point, in the direction of the break.

**Worked example.** A diamond top forms after an uptrend. Its widest point runs from a high of 120 to a low of 100, a height of 20. Price closes below the lower right edge at 105. The measured target is 105 - 20 = **85**.

The target is a guide, not a promise. Many patterns fall short, and some go further.

## How to read it

**Breakout direction.** In a diamond top, the expected break is down. In a diamond bottom, it is up. Because the diamond is meant to be a reversal, a break in the direction of the old trend is a sign the pattern has failed.

**Volume.** Volume is often high in the widening half, then fades as price squeezes in. A rise in volume on the break adds weight to it.

**Retest.** Price may come back to the broken edge. If it holds as resistance (in a top) or support (in a bottom), some traders treat that as a second entry point.

**Failed pattern.** If price breaks out and then returns inside the diamond, or breaks the other way, the pattern has failed and the idea is wrong.

## Best market states

**Reversal after a long, extended trend (best).** The pattern needs a trend to reverse and enough room for the swings to build.

**Trends (caution).** A diamond-like pause inside a young trend is often just consolidation before the trend resumes.

**Ranges (weak).** Sideways markets generate lots of rising and falling swings that can be forced into the shape.

**Volatility.** The widening half is a sign of rising volatility, so wide stops are needed and false edges are common.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | Clear and extended before the pattern |
| Shape | Widening then narrowing swings, with trendlines that fit |
| Break | A close beyond the edge, not just a wick |
| Volume | Higher on the break than in the narrowing half |
| Stop | Beyond the opposite edge of the narrowing half, or beyond the last swing |

## Common mistakes

- Calling any messy top a diamond. True diamonds are uncommon.
- Drawing the lines afterwards to fit price instead of marking swings as they form.
- Acting before the close beyond the edge.
- Ignoring the prior trend. Without one, there is nothing to reverse.
- Treating the measured target as a certainty.
- Forgetting that a break in the old trend's direction means the reversal idea failed.

## FAQ

**What is a diamond top?**
It is a reversal pattern that forms after an uptrend. Swings widen, then narrow, producing a diamond outline. A close below the lower edge on the right side completes the pattern and signals a possible fall.

**What is a diamond bottom?**
It is the mirror image, forming after a downtrend. A close above the upper edge on the right side completes the pattern and signals a possible rise.

**How rare is the diamond pattern?**
It is generally described as uncommon. Its loose shape also makes it easy to see in noisy charts, so many apparent diamonds are not valid ones.

**How do you find the target for a diamond?**
Measure the height at the widest part, then project that distance from the breakout point. It is a guide only. Many patterns stop short of it and some go further.

**Is a diamond the same as a broadening formation?**
No. A broadening formation only widens. A diamond widens and then narrows, so it combines a broadening shape with a triangle.
