The Initial Balance (IB) is the price range traded in the first hour of a session, from the high to the low. It comes from Market Profile, and it is treated as the range where early buyers and sellers first "balance". Traders watch whether price stays inside it or pushes beyond it to judge what kind of day it may be.

## What is the Initial Balance?

Market Profile, developed by J. Peter Steidlmayer in the 1980s at the Chicago Board of Trade, organises trading by time and price. In that framework, the first hour of the session is when participants set up the first view of fair value. The high and low of that hour are the Initial Balance.

The IB gives two levels, the IB high and the IB low, and traders add the midpoint. The rest of the day is read relative to them. If price keeps moving within the IB, the day is in balance. If price breaks out and keeps going, that is called a range extension, and it signals that one side has taken control.

The IB is closely related to the [opening range breakout](/opening-range-breakout/), which uses the first few minutes (often 5, 15 or 30) rather than the first hour. The main difference is in the length and in the Market Profile context.

The idea of an hour for the Initial Balance comes from the original exchange hours, where the first hour was the busiest. For markets with other hours, traders may adapt the length, so it is a convention and not a law.

## How it is identified

1. Define the session start (for example, the stock exchange open).
2. Record the highest and lowest prices traded in the first 60 minutes.
3. Draw two horizontal lines at the IB high and the IB low. Many traders add a midpoint line.
4. The range is fixed after the hour ends. It does not move for the rest of the session.

**Worked example.** In the first hour, price trades between 4,500 and 4,520.
- IB high = 4,520
- IB low = 4,500
- IB range = 20 points; midpoint = 4,510

If price later trades up to 4,535, it has extended the range by 15 points above the IB high. If it trades to 4,490, it has extended 10 points below the IB low.

Some traders measure the IB size relative to recent days. A narrow IB suggests a quiet start, and a wide IB suggests a busy one.

## How to read it

**Narrow IB.** A tight first hour can precede a move out of the range later, as the market has not yet shown its hand. Many traders watch for a breakout of either side.

**Wide IB.** A wide first hour often means much of the day's range was used early. Moves beyond it may be less frequent, though there is no fixed rule.

**Inside the IB.** If price stays within the range, traders look for range trades: fade the edges, aim for the midpoint or the other side.

**Breakout of the IB.** A move outside the high or low, ideally with strong participation, suggests a range extension and a possible trending day. Some use a hold above the IB high as a bullish sign, and below the IB low as bearish.

**Failed breakout.** If price pokes outside the IB then returns inside, the move may have failed, and traders look for a return toward the other side.

**Extension multiples.** Some traders measure targets as multiples of the IB size, such as 1.5 or 2 times, from the breakout point. These multiples are common habits and not tested rules.

## Best market states

**Ranges (good).** When the market lacks direction, price often rotates within the IB for much of the day, and the IB edges act as reference levels.

**Breakouts (good).** The IB gives a clear, objective line to define a breakout.

**Trends.** On a strong trend day, price leaves the IB early and does not return. The IB then marks where the trend began.

**Reversals.** A return into the IB after an extension is considered a sign that the move failed.

**Volatility.** Volatility shapes the IB's size. After a news-driven open, the range may be abnormally wide and less useful.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Session definition | Fix the start time and length in advance |
| Close beyond the edge | A closed bar outside the IB is stronger than a wick |
| Volume | Rising participation on the break gives more weight |
| Context | Look at where the IB sits relative to the previous day's range and value area |
| Midpoint | Failure to hold the midpoint after a break is a warning |
| Stop | Back inside the IB, or beyond the opposite side for wider stops |

## Common mistakes

- Using the IB on markets with no clear session start, without defining one.
- Entering on the first touch of the IB edge, without waiting for any confirmation.
- Ignoring the size of the IB. A very wide IB leaves little room for a move beyond it.
- Treating the IB as predictive. It describes the first hour and says little by itself about the rest.
- Confusing the IB with the opening range breakout, which normally uses a much shorter period.
- Setting stops that are inside the noise of the range.

## FAQ

**What is the Initial Balance in trading?**
It is the high-to-low range of the first hour of a trading session. It comes from Market Profile. Traders watch whether price stays inside it or moves beyond it, to help judge the type of day.

**How long is the Initial Balance?**
The usual length is the first 60 minutes of the regular session. Some traders use 30 minutes or adapt the length for markets with different hours. The shorter versions overlap with the opening range breakout.

**What is the difference between Initial Balance and the opening range?**
Both use the early part of the session. The opening range normally covers the first 5, 15 or 30 minutes, and the Initial Balance covers the first hour. The IB is also tied to Market Profile ideas about balance and range extension.

**What is a range extension?**
It is a move of price beyond the IB high or low after the first hour. It suggests that one side is taking control. Extensions in both directions on one day are possible, and that usually points to a more rotational day.

**Does the Initial Balance work in forex and crypto?**
These markets trade nearly all day, so there is no natural first hour. Traders choose a session start, such as a major exchange open. The method can be applied, but results depend on that choice and are less tied to Market Profile's original setting.
