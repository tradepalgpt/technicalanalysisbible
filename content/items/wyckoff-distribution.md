Wyckoff distribution is a schematic for a trading range that forms after an advance, where large sellers are said to be passing their holdings to the public before a fall. It moves through phases A to E, with set events such as the buying climax, the upthrust and the sign of weakness. It is a reading framework, and real charts rarely match it exactly.

## What is Wyckoff distribution?

In the Wyckoff view, a market that has risen for a long time may stop rising because large holders begin to sell to the crowd, who are buying with enthusiasm. They sell slowly so that price does not fall at once. The result is a sideways range near the high. When the selling is done and demand is used up, price leaves the range downward, which is the start of the markdown phase.

Richard D. Wyckoff linked these moves to the actions of an imagined single operator, the Composite Man. It is a thinking aid. See [Wyckoff Method](/wyckoff-method/) for the three laws. Distribution is the mirror of [Wyckoff accumulation](/wyckoff-accumulation/). It is a template that readers compare to a chart, and the match is often loose.

## How it is identified

The range follows an advance. Phases A to E describe what happens inside and just after it.

**Phase A: stopping the advance.**
- **PSY (preliminary supply):** the first sign of heavy selling after the rise, often on rising volume. Price usually pushes higher afterwards.
- **BC (buying climax):** a sharp rise on very heavy volume with wide bars, then a drop. It often marks the high of the range.
- **AR (automatic reaction):** the drop after the climax. Its low sets the bottom of the range.
- **ST (secondary test):** price returns toward the climax high on lower volume. Lower volume suggests less buying pressure.

**Phase B: building the cause.** Price swings between the range edges, with several tests. Volume often swells on moves up and on moves down as large players sell into rallies.

**Phase C: the test.**
- **UTAD (upthrust after distribution):** a brief move above the range high that reverses quickly, designed to trap late buyers and test whether demand remains. See [Spring and upthrust (Wyckoff)](/wyckoff-spring-upthrust/).
Not every range has a UTAD. Some show a simple upthrust or none at all.

**Phase D: weakness appears.**
- **SOW (sign of weakness):** a fall from the high area to or below the bottom of the range, on wide bars and rising volume.
- **LPSY (last point of supply):** a weak rally after the SOW that fails below the range high, on lower volume. There may be more than one.

**Phase E: leaving the range.** Price breaks below the bottom of the range, often retests it from below as resistance, and then the markdown runs.

**Worked example.** A stock rises from 40 to 70. A buying climax at 70 on heavy volume is followed by an automatic reaction to 62. The range is 62 to 70. Price retests 69 on lower volume (ST), then swings inside the range for weeks (phase B). A UTAD pushes to 72 and closes back at 68 the same day (phase C). Price then falls to 60 on wide bars and rising volume (SOW), rallies weakly to 65 on low volume (LPSY) and fails. The range height is 8, so a rough count suggests a possible 8-point fall below the break, to around 54. That count is a guide only.

## How to read it

**The range edges.** The climax high and the automatic reaction low set resistance and support.

**Volume at the highs.** Heavy volume with little upward progress is the effort-versus-result warning: buyers are working hard and price is not moving. That suggests sellers are absorbing the demand.

**The upthrust.** A fast fall back into the range after a break above the high is the key phase C signal. A break above the high that holds and keeps rising is not an upthrust. It is a breakout, and the idea is wrong.

**Sign of weakness.** A fall to the bottom of the range with wide bars and rising volume suggests sellers are in charge. A low-volume fall is less convincing.

**Last point of supply.** A lower high on lower volume after the SOW shows that buyers are not returning. Some traders enter here, with a stop above it.

**Failure of the range.** If price leaves the top and keeps rising on strong volume, the range was not distribution. It was a pause in the advance.

## Best market states

**Ranges after an advance (best).** The schematic only applies where a rise has stopped and price is moving sideways.

**Reversals.** The aim is to catch the turn from an uptrend into a downtrend.

**Trends (weak).** A range that is only a brief pause in a strong advance can look like distribution but is continuation, and price breaks higher.

**Low volume markets.** The volume reading is not reliable where volume data is poor or fragmented.

**Time frames.** The schematic is used on daily, weekly and intraday charts. Very small time frames often show noise.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior advance | A clear rise before the range |
| Climax | BC on heavy volume with a clear AR drop |
| Tests | Secondary tests on lower volume, or heavy volume with little progress |
| Phase C | An upthrust or UTAD that reverses quickly |
| Weakness | SOW on wide bars and rising volume |
| Resistance | LPSY fails below the earlier highs |
| Exit | A close below the range bottom, with a retest that fails |
| Stop | Above the upthrust high or the last point of supply |

## Common mistakes

- Calling every range after a rise "distribution". Many ranges are only a rest before more advance.
- Selling the first volume spike. A buying climax is clear only after the drop.
- Expecting an upthrust in every range. It is a common event, not a required one.
- Treating a break above the range that keeps going as an upthrust.
- Ignoring volume and using price shape alone.
- Entering without a stop at the point where the idea is proven wrong.

## FAQ

**What is Wyckoff distribution?**
It is a schematic for a range that forms after an advance, where large holders are thought to sell to the public before price falls. It moves through phases A to E and ends with price leaving the range downward.

**What are the main Wyckoff distribution events?**
They are PSY (preliminary supply), BC (buying climax), AR (automatic reaction), ST (secondary test), UTAD (upthrust after distribution), SOW (sign of weakness) and LPSY (last point of supply).

**What is a UTAD?**
It is a short move above the range high that quickly reverses back into the range. It traps late buyers and tests whether demand remains. If price holds above the high and keeps rising, it was a real breakout, not a UTAD.

**Does distribution always lead to a fall?**
No. A range near a high can resolve upward. A range that looks like distribution can fail, which is why traders look for confirmation, such as a sign of weakness and a failed retest, and use a stop.

**How is distribution different from accumulation?**
Distribution forms after an advance and aims to end with a fall. [Accumulation](/wyckoff-accumulation/) forms after a decline and aims to end with a rise. They are mirror images with matching events on the opposite side.
