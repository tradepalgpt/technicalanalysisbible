A harami cross is a two-candle pattern. A long candle is followed by a doji that sits inside the long candle's body. It is a harami where the second candle is a doji, so it shows indecision rather than a small move. It warns that the trend is tiring, and it needs confirmation.

## What is a harami cross?

"Harami" is an old Japanese word often translated as "pregnant". The first long candle is the mother and the small second candle sits inside it. In a [bullish harami](/bullish-harami/) or [bearish harami](/bearish-harami/), the second candle has a small body. In a harami cross, the second candle is a [doji](/doji/): the open and close are equal or nearly so.

Because a doji is a stronger sign of indecision than a small candle, Nison regards the harami cross as a more important sign of a turn than an ordinary harami. It is also sometimes described as a possible early version of a three-candle star pattern.

## How it is identified

1. **A trend** is in place, ending in a long candle. In a downtrend it is bearish, in an uptrend bullish.
2. **The second candle is a doji**, with its open and close inside the body of the first candle.
3. **Shadows.** Strict rules require the whole doji, shadows included, to sit inside the first body. Looser rules allow the shadows to poke outside, so long as the doji's body is inside.

**Worked example.** After a fall, candle 1 opens at 110 and closes at 100, a long bearish candle. Candle 2 opens and closes at 104 (a doji), with a high of 106 and a low of 102. The doji sits inside 100 to 110, so this is a bullish harami cross.

## How to read it

The long first candle shows the trend at full strength. The doji that follows is smaller in range and shows no direction. The market has gone from strong conviction to hesitation in a single period.

It does not say who will win. Traders treat it as a sign that momentum is fading and watch the next candle. A strong candle in the opposite direction supports a reversal. A candle in the trend's direction means the pause has ended.

## Best market states

**Reversal (best).** After a long move, particularly near a support or resistance level, the pattern is a useful flag.

**Range (weak).** Small candles inside larger ones are common in sideways markets.

**Trend (weak).** In a healthy trend, hesitation is often followed by more of the same.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear move, ending in a long candle |
| Doji inside | Doji sits in the first body (strict: shadows too) |
| Next candle | A strong candle in the opposite direction |
| Location | A key level near the pattern |
| Momentum | RSI divergence or an extreme reading |

## Common mistakes

- Treating a small-bodied second candle as a doji.
- Accepting a first candle that is not clearly long.
- Trading it in a range.
- Treating it as a signal without the next candle.
- Mixing up strict and loose rules between charts, so results cannot be compared.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between a harami and a harami cross?**
In a harami the second candle has a small body inside the first. In a harami cross the second candle is a doji. Nison treats the cross as the more significant version.

**Is a harami cross bullish or bearish?**
It depends on the trend before it. After a fall it is bullish (a possible bottom). After a rise it is bearish (a possible top). Neither is a firm prediction.

**Does the doji need to sit fully inside the first candle?**
Strict definitions say yes, shadows included. Many traders accept a doji whose body is inside the first body, even if its shadows go beyond. Pick one rule.

**Is it stronger than a harami?**
Traditionally yes, because a doji shows more indecision than a small candle. That is a reading of the chart picture, not a proven edge.

**Is a harami cross the same as an inside bar?**
They are related. An inside bar is a bar whose range sits inside the previous bar's range. A harami cross is about the doji's body relative to the previous body. See [Inside Bar](/inside-bar/).
