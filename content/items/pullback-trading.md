A pullback entry means joining an established trend after a temporary move against it, rather than buying a breakout at the high. In an uptrend the trader waits for a dip to a level such as support, a rising moving average or a Fibonacci retracement, then enters when price turns up again. The stop sits beyond the pullback.

## What is a pullback entry?

Trends move in steps. Price pushes, pauses or retraces, then pushes again. A pullback is the retrace. Buying the pullback in an uptrend gives a better price than buying at the high, and a nearby stop beyond the pullback low. The cost is that a deep pullback can turn into a reversal, and a shallow trend may never pull back at all.

The idea is as old as trend following. Edwards and Magee (1948) discuss reactions within a trend, and Murphy (1999) covers retracements of one third to two thirds of the previous move. Al Brooks (2012) builds much of his trend trading around buying pullbacks, with entries after a bar that shows buyers returning.

The depth rule of thumb comes from Dow theory and Fibonacci retracements. It is a guide, not a law, and each market behaves differently.

## How it is identified

1. **Confirm the trend.** A sequence of higher highs and higher lows. See [Market structure](/market-structure/).
2. **Measure the last swing.** From the last swing low to the swing high. See [Swing highs and swing lows](/swing-highs-lows/).
3. **Wait for the pullback.** Price falls back toward a level of interest: a prior swing, a rising moving average or a retracement zone.
4. **Look for a turn.** A rejection bar, a break of a short-term down move or a higher low forming.
5. **Enter and set the stop.** Beyond the pullback low, or below the level that was defended.

**Worked example.** An uptrend runs from a swing low of 100 to a swing high of 120, a range of 20. A 38% retracement is 120 - 7.6 = 112.4 and a 62% retracement is 120 - 12.4 = 107.6. Price falls to 110 and prints a bullish bar. A trader buys at 111 with a stop at 106.5, below the 62% zone, risking 4.5. The previous high at 120 is 9 points away, so reward is twice the risk. A fall below 100 would break the uptrend structure.

**Depth.** Shallow pullbacks (under about 38%) show strong trends. Deep ones (past 62%) warn that the trend may be weakening, and a close below the last swing low breaks structure.

## How to read it

**Shallow versus deep.** A shallow pullback means buyers are eager. A deep one means more selling than usual, and a break of the last swing low is a change of character.

**Confluence.** The best-looking pullbacks arrive at several levels together, such as a retracement zone, a prior swing and a rising moving average. See [Fibonacci retracement](/fibonacci-retracement/) and [Exponential moving average](/exponential-moving-average/).

**Pullback shape.** Orderly, overlapping bars with falling volume are often read as a pause. Large bars against the trend show real selling.

**Entry trigger.** Many traders wait for a bar that breaks the previous bar's high before buying, rather than buying into the fall.

**After a break of structure.** A common sequence: price breaks the last swing high, pulls back, then continues. See [Break of structure](/break-of-structure/).

## Best market states

**Established trends (best).** Pullbacks have a clear direction to return to.

**Ranges (weak).** There is no trend to rejoin. Dips at the range top are not pullbacks, they are rotations.

**Reversals (weak).** A pullback in a trend that is ending is the first leg of the new trend. Use structure to tell the two apart.

**Very strong trends.** Price may never pull back to the usual zones. Waiting means missing the move.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Trend | Higher highs and higher lows (reverse for downtrends) |
| Structure | Price holds above the last higher low |
| Level | Pullback ends at support, a retracement zone or a moving average |
| Trigger | A bar that shows buyers back, or a break above the last bar's high |
| Stop | Beyond the pullback low, sized so reward is larger than risk |
| Higher time frame | Trend agrees on the larger chart |

## Common mistakes

- Buying every dip, including in a range or after structure has broken.
- Treating Fibonacci levels as exact. They are zones, not lines.
- Entering during the fall without a sign it has stopped.
- Placing the stop inside normal noise.
- Waiting for a perfect level and missing strong trends that never pull back.
- Ignoring that a pullback is also how a reversal starts. Keep the stop.

## FAQ

**What is a pullback in trading?**
A pullback is a temporary move against the main trend, such as a dip in an uptrend. Traders use it to enter at a better price than chasing the high, then place a stop beyond the pullback.

**How deep should a pullback be?**
Common rules of thumb say 38% to 62% of the last swing, with 50% as a midpoint. Strong trends may retrace less, and weak ones more. These numbers are not laws, so test them on the market you trade.

**What is the difference between a pullback and a reversal?**
A pullback keeps the trend structure: price holds above the last higher low in an uptrend. A reversal breaks that structure. You usually only know the difference afterwards, which is why a stop is needed.

**Should I buy the dip in every uptrend?**
No. Dips can continue into larger falls. Buying works better when structure is intact, price is at a level and a bar shows buyers returning. Without these, a dip is only a falling price.

**Can you use pullbacks in downtrends?**
Yes. A rally in a downtrend is a pullback. Traders sell the rally at resistance, a falling moving average or a retracement zone, with a stop above the rally high.
