## What is the Crab pattern?

The Crab is one of the harmonic patterns, shapes in which each leg is a set Fibonacci ratio of another. It is attributed to Scott Carney, who has described harmonic trading in his books. The Crab is the most extended of the common harmonic shapes: D lies well beyond the starting point X, at 1.618 times the first leg.

Compared with the [Butterfly](/butterfly-pattern/), the Crab has a shallower B (0.382 to 0.618, not 0.786) and a much longer final leg. The final move from C to D is often a sharp, fast extension, 2.24 to 3.618 times the BC leg. That makes the swing into D steep, and traders describe it as a possible capitulation point.

The idea is that this extreme extension exhausts the move, so a reversal at D is possible. This idea has no academic evidence base. Swing points are chosen by the analyst and ratios are applied with tolerance, so identification is subjective. See [harmonic patterns](/harmonic-patterns/).

## How it is drawn

A bullish Crab runs as follows. A bearish one is the mirror image.

1. **X to A:** the first leg, a move down.
2. **A to B:** a retracement of 0.382 to 0.618 of XA.
3. **B to C:** a move of 0.382 to 0.886 of AB.
4. **C to D:** an extension of 2.24 to 3.618 of BC.
5. **D:** at 1.618 of XA, below X.

The defining rule is that D completes at 1.618 of XA, and that this lines up with the BC extension.

**Worked example.** X is at 120 and A at 100, so XA is 20 points. B retraces 0.50: B = 100 + 10 = 110. D at 1.618 of XA means D = 120 - (1.618 x 20) = 120 - 32.36 = **87.64**. That is 12.36 below A (100). If C is at 104 (a fall of 6 from B, which is 0.6 of AB), the fall from C to D is 16.36, about 2.7 times BC, inside the 2.24 to 3.618 range.

If price keeps falling well past 1.618 of XA, the pattern is treated as failed.

## How to read it

**The completion zone.** Traders look at the area around D for signs of a reaction. The pattern does not say price will turn. It says a turn would fit the shape.

**Trigger.** Many traders wait for price action at D, such as a rejection candle, before acting. Entering without confirmation is risky since extended moves can continue.

**Stop.** A typical stop sits just beyond D. Because D is the extreme, the stop can be close to the entry, but a stop that is too tight is hit by normal noise.

**Targets.** Common targets are retracements of the AD leg, such as 0.382 and 0.618, or a return toward point C. These are conventions, not tested outcomes.

**Tolerance.** Real charts rarely match ratios exactly. Different practitioners accept different tolerances, which is a source of subjectivity.

**Momentum.** A sharp final leg often comes with a spike in volume or a momentum extreme, which some traders use as a hint that the move is tiring.

## Best market states

**Reversal (best, with care).** The pattern is built to catch the end of an extreme extension.

**Strong trends (weak).** An extended move can simply be a breakout of a new trend and carry on well beyond 1.618.

**Range (weak).** Small swings in sideways markets can be forced into the ratios.

**Breakout.** A move this far past X is often a breakout, and the pattern asks you to oppose it.

**Volatility.** Volatile markets can overshoot D by a wide margin.

| Level | D near a known support or resistance area |
| Stop | Just beyond D |

## Common mistakes

- Forcing swings to match the ratios and trusting the result.
- Entering at D without any confirmation from price.
- Confusing the Crab with the Butterfly. The Crab's D is at 1.618 of XA, not 1.27.
- Fighting a genuine breakout because the ratios seem to match.
- Treating harmonic patterns as proven. Evidence for them is limited.
- Placing the stop so tight that normal noise triggers it.

## FAQ

**What is the Crab pattern in trading?**
It is a harmonic reversal shape with five points, X, A, B, C and D. Point D extends to 1.618 of the first leg, beyond the start at X. Traders look for a possible reversal near D.

**What are the Crab pattern ratios?**
B is 0.382 to 0.618 of XA. C is 0.382 to 0.886 of AB. The CD leg is 2.24 to 3.618 of BC. D is at 1.618 of XA. Ratios are usually applied with some tolerance.

**What is the difference between the Crab and the Butterfly?**
Both have D beyond X. The Butterfly has D at 1.27 to 1.618 of XA and B at 0.786. The Crab has B at 0.382 to 0.618 and D at 1.618 of XA, a longer extension.

**Where do you place the stop on a Crab pattern?**
Many traders put it just beyond point D. If price keeps moving well past 1.618 of XA, the pattern has failed. The stop may be close to the entry, so make sure normal noise will not trigger it.

**Is the Crab pattern reliable?**
There is no strong academic evidence that harmonic patterns predict reversals, and identifying one is subjective. Test it on your own market and use it as one input among several.
