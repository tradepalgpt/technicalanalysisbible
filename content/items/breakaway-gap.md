A breakaway gap is a price gap that opens beyond the edge of a trading range or chart pattern and starts a new move. Price jumps clear of the old area without trading in between. It is read as a sign of strong, urgent buying or selling, but some breakaway gaps fail.

## What is a breakaway gap?

A gap is an empty space on the chart where no trading took place between one bar and the next. On a daily chart it usually appears between one day's close and the next day's open, after news or a shift in demand.

A breakaway gap happens at the start of a move. Price has been moving sideways, perhaps inside a [rectangle](/rectangle-pattern/), a triangle or a long base. Then it opens above the top of that area (or below the bottom) and leaves a hole behind it. Buyers or sellers were so keen that they were willing to pay a much different price than yesterday's.

The term comes from the classic chart-pattern literature. Edwards and Magee's *Technical Analysis of Stock Trends* (first published 1948) separates gaps into types, including breakaway, runaway (measuring) and exhaustion gaps. No single person is credited with the term.

Gap types are only known in hindsight. At the moment a gap appears, you cannot be sure which type it is, so the label is a working guess that later price action confirms or contradicts.

## How it is identified

A breakaway gap usually has these features:

1. **A prior consolidation.** The gap follows a period of sideways trading or a recognisable pattern.
2. **A gap through the boundary.** The opening price is beyond resistance (for an upward gap) or support (for a downward gap), with empty space between the old range and the new bar.
3. **Heavy volume.** Volume is usually well above average, showing wide participation.
4. **Follow-through.** Price stays beyond the old boundary and does not trade back into the range.

**Worked example.** A stock trades between 48 and 50 for several weeks. On a Monday, news pushes demand, and it opens at 52 after closing the Friday at 49.5. Volume is about three times the recent average. The area between 50 and 52 contains no trades.

The gap is a candidate breakaway gap. If price stays above 50 over the next few sessions and keeps climbing, the label looks right. If price falls back into the 48 to 50 range, the move was a failed breakout.

**Measuring the move.** Some traders project the height of the old range from the breakout point as a rough target. The height here is 2 (50 minus 48), so the target would be around 54. This is a guide only.

## How to read it

**Strength signal.** A gap on strong volume shows that buyers (or sellers) took control quickly. The more clearly the gap clears the old boundary, the more significant it is considered.

**Gaps tend to hold.** In classic teaching, breakaway gaps are less likely to be filled soon than common gaps. That is a tendency, not a rule, and some are filled within days.

**The gap as support.** After an upward gap, the top of the old range and the gap area can act as support on a pullback. A close back inside the old range weakens the signal. See [role reversal](/role-reversal/).

**News context.** Many gaps follow earnings or announcements. A gap driven by a one-off event may fade, whereas one that matches a real change in the trend is more durable.

**Time frame.** Gaps are clearest on daily and weekly charts of markets that close overnight. Continuous 24-hour markets such as most forex pairs seldom show true gaps except over weekends.

## Best market states

**Breakouts (best).** The pattern is built for the moment when price leaves a base. It is one of the clearer breakout signals because the gap itself shows urgency.

**Trends (follow-up).** A breakaway gap often starts a trend. The later [runaway gap](/runaway-gap/) and [exhaustion gap](/exhaustion-gap/) can appear as that trend develops.

**Ranges (weak).** In a range, an opening gap beyond the edge is often a false break that gets filled quickly. See [Gap fill](/gap-fill/) and [False breakout](/false-breakout/).

**Volatility.** Volatile, news-driven periods produce many gaps. Wide gaps mean the stop has to sit further away, which reduces position size.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior base | A clear range or pattern before the gap |
| Boundary | The gap clears the edge of the range, not just a small level inside it |
| Volume | Well above recent average |
| Close | Bar closes near its high (for an upward gap), not back in the gap |
| Hold | Following sessions do not close back inside the old range |
| Stop | Inside the gap or at the old boundary; a close back in the range is the warning |

## Common mistakes

- Assuming every gap is a breakaway gap. Many are common gaps in ranges and fill quickly.
- Ignoring volume. A gap on thin volume is less convincing.
- Chasing the opening price without a plan for a gap that fails.
- Placing the stop too tight, inside normal post-gap volatility.
- Confusing an early gap with an exhaustion gap that follows a long run.
- Forgetting that the gap name is decided later, by what price does next.

## FAQ

**What is a breakaway gap?**
It is a price gap that opens beyond the edge of a range or pattern and starts a new move. It shows that buyers or sellers acted with unusual urgency, which is why many traders treat it as a strong breakout signal.

**Is a breakaway gap bullish or bearish?**
Either. An upward gap above resistance is bullish and a downward gap below support is bearish. The direction depends on which boundary price jumps through.

**Do breakaway gaps always stay open?**
No. They are less likely to be filled quickly than common gaps, but some are filled within days. A gap that closes and returns into the old range suggests the breakout has failed.

**How is a breakaway gap different from a runaway gap?**
A breakaway gap comes at the start of a move, out of a base. A [runaway gap](/runaway-gap/) appears in the middle of a trend that is already running. Both show strength, but at different stages.

**Can forex have breakaway gaps?**
Rarely during the week, because forex trades nearly around the clock. Weekend gaps do occur, and gaps appear more often in stocks, indices and futures that close overnight.
