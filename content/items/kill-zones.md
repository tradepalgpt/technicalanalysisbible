Kill zones are fixed time windows in the trading day, taught in ICT and smart money material, in which traders expect more volatility and clearer moves. They are used as a time filter: take setups inside the windows and ignore the quiet hours. The windows are a trader-education convention with no academic evidence base.

## What are kill zones?

Markets are not equally active all day. In foreign exchange, activity generally rises when the London and New York sessions open, because more participants are trading. Traders have long watched session times for this reason.

Kill zones apply that idea in a stricter way. They name short windows when, according to the teaching, large participants are most likely to move price. Traders then only look for setups inside the windows.

The term comes from ICT (Inner Circle Trader) teaching and is part of [smart money concepts](/smart-money-concepts/). The idea that volatility and volume vary by time of day is widely accepted in market structure studies, but the specific windows, their names and the claims about what happens inside them have no academic evidence base. Treat the windows as a convention.

## How it is identified

Kill zones are fixed clock times, so they are marked on the chart rather than found in price. The usual versions, given in New York time (US Eastern), are:

| Window | New York time (typical) |
|---|---|
| Asian | 8 pm to midnight |
| London open | 2 am to 5 am |
| New York open | 7 am to 10 am |
| London close | 10 am to noon |

Teachers give slightly different times, so use the version you follow and keep it fixed.

**Worked example.** The New York kill zone starts at 7 am New York time.

- In winter, New York is on standard time (UTC minus 5), so 7 am is 12:00 UTC.
- In summer, New York is on daylight time (UTC minus 4), so 7 am is 11:00 UTC.
- The UK and the US change clocks on different dates, so for a few weeks a year the gap between London and New York time is not the usual five hours.

Setting your chart to New York time avoids the conversion, as the windows then stay at the same clock time all year. If your platform uses a fixed-offset time zone, the windows will shift by an hour when daylight saving changes.

## How to read it

**As a filter.** Look for setups only inside the window. Outside it, stay out, on the idea that price is quieter and moves are less meaningful.

**Session highs and lows.** The Asian range is marked and traders watch whether London takes out its high or low, a [liquidity sweep](/liquidity-sweep/), before reversing. This is a common ICT pattern, but it is a convention.

**Entry timing.** Within a window, the first part is seen as more volatile. Traders may wait for a sweep followed by a [displacement](/displacement/) move before acting.

**Skipping the dead hours.** For many people, the practical benefit is simply a smaller window to watch, which limits time spent at the screen.

## Best market states

**Volatility (best).** The windows are meant to match the periods when volatility is highest.

**Breakouts.** Opening-hour moves such as an [opening range breakout](/opening-range-breakout/) often occur in the same hours.

**Ranges (weak).** Outside the windows markets can range, and a filter that excludes those hours also hides some moves.

**Instruments.** Windows were built around foreign exchange and index futures. For an instrument with its own hours, such as a stock that opens at the New York cash open, the windows will not fit in the same way.

## Settings

| Time zone | New York time | Keeps windows fixed through daylight saving changes |
| Windows | Asian, London, New York, London close | Choose those that fit your market and hours |
| Display | Shaded bands or vertical lines on the chart | Makes it easy to see when a setup is inside a window |

There are no tested best times. Check the hours when your own market is most active and compare them with the windows before relying on them.

## Common mistakes

- Using the wrong time zone, so the windows are an hour or more out.
- Forgetting that daylight saving changes on different dates in the UK and the US.
- Taking any trade in a window just because it is in a window. The window is a filter, not a signal.
- Using forex windows on a market with different hours.
- Assuming moves outside the windows are meaningless.

## FAQ

**What are kill zones in trading?**
They are fixed time windows, taught in ICT material, when traders expect the most volatility, such as the London and New York opens. They work as a time filter so you only look for setups during active hours.

**What time is the London kill zone?**
It is commonly given as 2 am to 5 am New York time. That is 7 am to 10 am in London when both places are on the same type of time. Teachers differ slightly, so use the version you follow.

**Do kill zones change with daylight saving?**
The clock times in New York time stay the same all year. In UTC they shift by an hour when New York changes clocks. The UK and the US change on different dates, so check your charts at those times.

**Are kill zones proven to work?**
The idea that markets are more active at some hours is widely observed. The specific windows and the claims about what happens in them have no academic evidence base. Test them on your own market.

**Can I use kill zones for stocks?**
They were built for foreign exchange and index futures. Stocks mostly trade in the cash session, so the windows do not fit in the same way. Many stock traders watch the open and close instead.
