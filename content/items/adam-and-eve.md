Adam and Eve is a variation of the double bottom. The first low is narrow and sharp (Adam), and the second is wider and rounder (Eve). Price then breaks above the high between the two lows. It forms at the end of a downtrend, and a close above that high is the usual signal that selling pressure may be fading.

## What is the Adam and Eve pattern?

A standard [double bottom](/double-bottom/) has two lows at about the same level with a rally between them. Adam and Eve adds a rule about the shape of the two lows.

- **Adam** is the first low. It is sharp and narrow, often a quick V-shape formed by a sudden drop and quick recovery. It can include a spike and is sometimes driven by panic selling.
- **Eve** is the second low. It is rounder and wider, a slow U-shape. Price drifts down, hangs near the same level for some time and then rises.

The idea is that the first low shows an emotional, fast sell-off, and the second shows a calmer, quieter test of the same area where sellers no longer have momentum. The pattern is completed when price breaks above the peak between the two lows.

The name is used by chart-pattern authors and traders. It is not credited to a single inventor. Thomas Bulkowski's pattern catalogue includes this variant among the double bottoms. Like most chart patterns, it has no strong academic evidence base, and its reliability depends on how it is defined and tested.

## How it is identified

A valid pattern needs these parts, in order:

1. **A prior downtrend.** Without a trend to reverse, the pattern means little.
2. **Adam:** a sharp, narrow low.
3. **Rally:** a bounce to a peak between the two lows. This peak is the neckline.
4. **Eve:** a second low at about the same level as Adam, but rounder and wider.
5. **Break:** a close above the neckline.

**Measured target.** Measure the height from the lows to the neckline and add it to the breakout point.

**Worked example.** Adam and Eve both bottom near 80, and the peak between them is at 90. The height is 10. If price breaks the neckline at 91, the measured target is 91 + 10 = **101**.

The target is a guide only. Many patterns fall short, and some go further.

## How to read it

**Shape difference.** The contrast between the two lows is the point. If both lows are sharp (Adam and Adam) or both are round (Eve and Eve), the pattern is a different variation of the double bottom.

**Neckline break.** A close above the peak between the lows completes the pattern. Many traders wait for a close and not an intrabar touch.

**Retest.** After the break, price often returns to the neckline, which may now act as support. Some traders enter on this retest.

**Volume.** Volume is often described as heavy on Adam, lighter on Eve and rising on the breakout. This is a common observation and not a rule.

**Depth and time.** A very shallow rally between the lows, or lows that are weeks apart with no relationship, make the pattern less meaningful.

**Failure.** If price breaks out and then falls back below the second low, the pattern has failed.

## Best market states

**Reversal at the end of a downtrend (best).** The pattern needs a meaningful decline to reverse.

**Strong downtrends (caution).** In a strong downtrend, what looks like a second low may be a pause before more falls.

**Range (weak).** Sideways markets produce many small bottoms that can be fitted to the shape.

**Breakout.** The completed pattern is itself a breakout above the neckline, so it is judged by the strength of that break.

**Volatility.** Very volatile markets produce messy lows and false breaks.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear downtrend before Adam |
| Adam | A narrow, sharp low |
| Eve | A wider, rounder low near the same level |
| Level | Eve at roughly the same price as Adam, not far below |
| Break | Close above the neckline, not just a wick |
| Volume | Rising on the break |
| Stop | Below the second low, or below both lows for a wider stop |

## Common mistakes

- Buying before the neckline breaks. Until then it is only a possible pattern.
- Forcing the label onto any double bottom, even when both lows have the same shape.
- Ignoring the prior downtrend.
- Treating the measured target as certain.
- Placing the stop based on a number of ticks, not on the structure.
- Assuming the Adam and Eve version is more reliable than a plain double bottom without testing it.

## FAQ

**What is an Adam and Eve double bottom?**
It is a double bottom in which the first low is sharp and narrow (Adam) and the second is wider and rounder (Eve). Price then closes above the high between the two lows. It is read as a possible reversal after a downtrend.

**Is Adam and Eve better than a normal double bottom?**
It is a subset of the double bottom, and some pattern studies compare the variants. Do not assume the shape improves results without testing it yourself on your market. Evidence for chart patterns in general is mixed.

**Which low comes first, Adam or Eve?**
In the version described here, Adam comes first and is the sharp low, and Eve comes second and is the rounder one. Authors do not always agree on the order and on which combinations count, so check how your source defines it.

**How do you trade the Adam and Eve pattern?**
Many traders wait for a close above the neckline, which is the peak between the lows. Some enter on a retest. The stop is usually below the second low. The measured target is the pattern height added to the breakout.

**What time frames does it work on?**
It can appear on any time frame. Longer time frames such as daily and weekly are generally seen as more meaningful because they involve more participants, but any pattern can fail on any time frame.
