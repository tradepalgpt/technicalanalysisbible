Displacement is a term from ICT and smart money teaching for a fast, one-sided move made of large-bodied candles that breaks a swing point and often leaves a gap behind. Traders use it as confirmation that a break of structure is genuine and not a weak poke. It is a trader-education concept with no academic evidence base.

## What is displacement?

Displacement describes how price moves, not where. A weak break creeps past a swing high with small, overlapping candles and often falls back. A displacement is the opposite: a few large candles in a row, closing near their highs (or lows), that cover a lot of distance in little time.

The term comes from the ICT (Inner Circle Trader) teaching community, which uses it as part of [smart money concepts](/smart-money-concepts/). The idea is that a forceful move shows urgency from large participants, while a slow break does not. That is an interpretation. There is no academic evidence base for it, and a candle can be large for ordinary reasons such as a news release.

Displacement usually leaves a [fair value gap](/fair-value-gap/) behind: a space where the first candle's high and the third candle's low do not overlap. Traders often look for that gap as the proof the move was fast.

## How it is identified

There is no formal rule. The common features are:

1. **Large bodies.** Candle bodies are clearly bigger than recent ones, often with small wicks.
2. **One direction.** Several candles in a row close the same way, or one very large candle does.
3. **A break.** Price closes beyond a recent swing high or low, which is a [break of structure](/break-of-structure/) or a [market structure shift](/market-structure-shift/).
4. **A gap.** A fair value gap appears between candles.

**Worked example.** Recent candles have a range of about 5 points. The last swing high is at 100. Three candles then close at 106, 112 and 118, each with a body of about 6 and small wicks. The first candle's high is 107 and the third candle's low is 111.

- Price has closed beyond 100, so structure is broken.
- Candle bodies are more than the recent average, so the move is strong.
- The space from 107 to 111 is a bullish fair value gap.

That would be called a bullish displacement. A break to 101 with small candles and no gap would not.

## How to read it

**Confirmation of a break.** A break with displacement is read as stronger than one without. Traders may wait for displacement before treating a [market structure shift](/market-structure-shift/) as real.

**Leaves areas to return to.** The gap and the last opposite candle before the move (an [order block](/order-block/)) are seen as places price may return to. Entries are planned there on a pullback.

**After a liquidity grab.** In ICT teaching, displacement after a [liquidity sweep](/liquidity-sweep/) is considered the sign that the sweep was the turning point.

**No displacement.** If a break has no large candles, many traders treat it with caution and wait for more.

These readings are conventions. A strong move can also be a sign of exhaustion, or can fully reverse in the next bars.

## Best market states

**Breakouts (best).** Displacement is a measure of how convincing a breakout is.

**Trends (good).** Impulsive legs in a trend often show displacement, and pullbacks in between are seen as entry areas.

**Reversals.** A displacement against the previous trend is used as an early sign of reversal, after a sweep of a high or low.

**Ranges (weak).** In a sideways market, fast spikes often fail and return into the range.

**Volatility.** In very active markets candles are large all the time, so what counts as "displacement" is harder to judge.

## Settings

There is no standard setting. Some traders use a rough rule based on the average true range ([ATR](/atr/)), such as a candle body well above the recent average, but the multiple is a personal choice and not a published standard.

| Approach | Description |
|---|---|
| Visual | Judge by eye whether candles are clearly larger than recent ones |
| Body versus ATR | Compare the body with an average of recent ranges; the multiple is a personal choice |
| Gap present | Require a fair value gap as proof of speed |

Whatever you choose, apply the same rule each time and test it on past charts so that the definition does not change to suit the trade.

## Common mistakes

- Calling every large candle a displacement. It needs a break of structure, not just size.
- Ignoring news. A single release can create a large candle that means little for later price.
- Changing the definition after the fact to fit a chart.
- Entering at the end of the move rather than waiting for a pullback to an area.
- Treating displacement as a signal by itself. It is confirmation of something else.

## FAQ

**What is displacement in trading?**
It is a fast, one-sided move of large-bodied candles that breaks a recent swing point and often leaves a fair value gap. The term is used in ICT and smart money teaching as a sign that a break is strong.

**How do you identify displacement?**
Look for several large candles closing in one direction, a close beyond a recent swing high or low, and usually a gap between candles. There is no standard rule, so use the same definition each time.

**Is displacement the same as a breakout?**
Not quite. A breakout is a move beyond a level. Displacement describes how forceful the move is. A breakout can occur without displacement, with small slow candles, and many traders trust that kind less.

**Does displacement need a fair value gap?**
Many traders look for one as proof of speed, but the definition is not fixed. Some accept large candles alone. Pick a rule and stay with it so the test does not change from trade to trade.

**Is displacement proven to work?**
No. It is a trader-education concept with no academic evidence base, and a large move can reverse. Use it as one piece of context, and test it on your own market before relying on it.
