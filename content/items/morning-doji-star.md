A morning doji star is a three-candle pattern at the bottom of a downtrend. A long bearish candle is followed by a doji that gaps down, then a bullish candle that closes well into the first body. It is a variant of the morning star with a doji in the middle, and it needs confirmation.

## What is a morning doji star?

The name comes from the idea that the morning star is the planet seen just before sunrise. The pattern is meant to look like light after a dark period.

It is the same idea as the [morning star](/morning-star/), but the middle candle is a [doji](/doji/) instead of a small body. A doji shows complete indecision. Nison treats the doji version as a stronger sign than the plain star, because it shows the selling pressure has stalled completely.

Strength is a matter of reading, not a measured fact. Bulkowski's testing of candle patterns covers the morning doji star, and results vary by market and period. Treat it as a warning that needs confirmation.

## How it is identified

1. **Candle 1:** a long bearish candle in a downtrend.
2. **Candle 2:** a doji whose body gaps below candle 1's body.
3. **Candle 3:** a bullish candle that gaps up (or opens above the doji) and closes well into candle 1's body, often beyond its midpoint.

Many descriptions require the gap on candle 2 to be real. In markets that trade nearly around the clock, such as forex, true gaps are rare, so traders relax the rule to "the doji sits below the first body".

**Worked example.** Candle 1 opens at 50 and closes at 44. Candle 2 opens at 43.2, trades between 42.5 and 43.8 and closes at 43.2, a doji below candle 1's body. Candle 3 opens at 43.5 and closes at 48, past the midpoint of candle 1's body (47). That is a morning doji star.

## How to read it

Candle 1 shows sellers in control. Candle 2 shows them stalling. Candle 3 shows buyers taking over. The more candle 3 recovers of candle 1, the stronger the reading.

It is a sign that a downtrend may be ending, not proof. A downtrend can resume after the pattern, especially if it forms in the middle of the move and not at a key level.

## Best market states

**Reversal points after a decline (best).** The pattern needs a prior downtrend and works best at support.

**Edges of a range (good).** At the lower edge of a range, it can mark a bounce.

**Ranges and strong trends (weak).** In the middle of a range it is noise. In a very strong downtrend it often fails.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear decline before candle 1 |
| Candle 3 | Closes beyond the midpoint of candle 1's body |
| Level | Forms at or near support |
| Volume | Higher volume on candle 3 adds weight, where available |
| Next candle | Follow-through after candle 3 |

## Common mistakes

- Calling it without a real prior downtrend.
- Accepting a small candle 3 that barely enters candle 1.
- Ignoring the lack of gaps on markets that do not gap, without choosing a clear rule.
- Treating it as certain.
- Trading it in the middle of a range, away from any level.

## FAQ

**How is a morning doji star different from a morning star?**
The middle candle. A morning star has a small body in the middle. A morning doji star has a doji, where the open and close are equal or nearly so. The doji shows more indecision.

**Does the doji have to gap?**
In the classic description, yes. In markets with little or no gapping, many traders only ask that the doji sits below the first candle's body. Pick a rule and apply it the same way every time.

**Is it related to the abandoned baby?**
Yes. An abandoned baby is a stricter version in which the doji also gaps away with its wicks, leaving clear space on both sides. See [Abandoned baby](/abandoned-baby/).

**Is it reliable?**
No pattern is. It is a warning that selling pressure has faded. Results vary by market and time frame, so test any rule before relying on it.

**What time frame should I use?**
Daily and weekly charts are the usual choice, because patterns on very short charts appear often and mean less.
