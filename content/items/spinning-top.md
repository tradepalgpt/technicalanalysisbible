A spinning top is a candlestick with a small real body and wicks above and below that are longer than the body. It shows that price moved in both directions during the period but finished close to where it began. It means indecision. On its own it is a weak signal, and it needs context to say anything useful.

## What is a spinning top?

Each candle records four prices for a period: open, high, low and close. The real body is the box between open and close. The wicks (also called shadows) are the thin lines showing the highest and lowest prices.

A spinning top has a small body and wicks on both sides. During the period buyers pushed price up and sellers pushed it down, but neither side held control by the close. The name comes from the shape, which looks like a child's toy top.

It is a close relative of the [doji](/doji/). A doji has almost no body at all. A spinning top has a small but visible body. Both show indecision, and a spinning top is the softer signal of the two. See [Doji vs Spinning Top](/compare/doji-vs-spinning-top/).

## How it is identified

A spinning top has:

1. **A small real body.** There is no fixed rule. A common guide is a body smaller than about a third of the candle's full range (high to low).
2. **An upper wick longer than the body.**
3. **A lower wick longer than the body.**
4. **Wicks of roughly similar length.** Very uneven wicks point to a different candle, such as a [hammer](/hammer/) or [shooting star](/shooting-star/).

**Worked example.** A candle opens at 100, rises to 104, falls to 96 and closes at 101. Range = 8. Body = 1, or 12.5% of the range. The upper wick is 3 (104 minus 101) and the lower wick is 4 (100 minus 96). Both wicks are longer than the body and similar in size, so this is a spinning top. If the body had been 0.1, it would be closer to a [long-legged doji](/long-legged-doji/).

## How to read it

**After a strong move.** A spinning top after several large candles in one direction can show that the move is slowing. It is a warning to watch, not a reversal on its own.

**At support or resistance.** At a known level it shows that the level is holding for now. See [support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/).

**Inside a range.** In the middle of a sideways market spinning tops are common and mean very little.

**Wait for the next candle.** A strong candle in the opposite direction afterwards gives the spinning top meaning. A spinning top between a long bullish candle and a long bearish candle is the middle of an [evening star](/evening-star/); between a bearish and a bullish one it forms a [morning star](/morning-star/). Those patterns normally ask for a small middle candle, and a spinning top fits.

**Continuation.** In a strong trend a spinning top is often only a pause, and price carries on in the same direction.

## Best market states

**Edges of a range (best).** At the top or bottom of a range, indecision against a boundary shows the boundary is being defended.

**After an extended move (fair).** It can mark fading momentum, but only with other evidence.

**Strong trends (weak).** Trends pause often. One spinning top is not enough to bet against them.

**Middle of a range (weak).** Small candles are normal in quiet markets and the signal is mostly noise.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Context | A clear prior move, or a key level nearby |
| Next candle | A decisive candle in the opposite direction to the prior move |
| Volume | Heavy volume on a small body suggests a real fight (where volume is reliable) |
| Momentum | An [RSI](/rsi/) extreme or divergence adds weight |
| Time frame | Daily and higher candles carry more meaning than very short ones |

## Common mistakes

- Treating every spinning top as a reversal signal.
- Reading it in the middle of a range, where it is common and carries little meaning.
- Acting before the next candle confirms.
- Confusing it with a hammer or shooting star, which have one long wick, not two.
- Using a body rule so loose that most small candles count.
- Trading it on very short time frames, where these candles appear constantly.

## Pairs well with

- **[Support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/)**: gives the candle a location that matters.
- **[Volume](/volume/)**: shows whether the indecision came with real participation.
- **[RSI](/rsi/)**: an extreme reading or divergence next to the candle strengthens the warning.
- **[Doji](/doji/)**: the same idea with an even smaller body; reading the two together shows how strong the indecision is.

## FAQ

**Is a spinning top bullish or bearish?**
Neither by itself. It shows indecision. After a rally it can hint that buyers are tiring, and after a fall it can hint that sellers are tiring. The candle that follows usually decides which, so wait for it.

**What is the difference between a spinning top and a doji?**
A doji has almost no body, because open and close are equal or nearly so. A spinning top has a small but visible body. Both show indecision, and the spinning top is the weaker signal. See [Doji vs Spinning Top](/compare/doji-vs-spinning-top/).

**Does the colour of a spinning top matter?**
Very little. A small green or red body both show that the period ended near the open. Some traders read a slight lean, but the wicks and the context matter far more than the body colour.

**Can I trade a spinning top on its own?**
It is a poor basis for a trade alone. Most traders use it as a warning and wait for a confirming candle, a key level or momentum evidence before acting.

**How reliable is the spinning top pattern?**
It is a weak signal, because small-bodied candles are common. Published candlestick studies vary and depend on how the pattern is defined. Treat it as a prompt to look closer, not as a forecast.
