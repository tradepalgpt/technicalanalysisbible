The on-neck and in-neck are two-candle patterns in a downtrend. A long bearish candle is followed by a bullish candle that gaps down, then rallies only as far as the first candle's close (on-neck) or just inside it (in-neck). The weak rally suggests the downtrend will likely continue.

## What is the on-neck / in-neck?

Both patterns describe a bounce that fails. In a downtrend, price falls with a long bearish candle. The next candle opens lower, rallies, and then stops at about the previous close. Buyers pushed back but could not reclaim any real ground.

The name comes from the idea that the close of the first candle is like a neckline, and the rally stops at or near it. The reading is that the rally was weak, and sellers are still in charge.

Compare this with the [piercing line](/piercing-line/), where the rally closes deep into the first candle's body and warns of a possible reversal. The on-neck and in-neck are weaker versions that mean the opposite: continuation.

Nison classes them as continuation patterns. Treat these as minor signals.

## How it is identified

1. **Candle 1:** a long bearish candle in a downtrend.
2. **Candle 2:** a bullish candle that opens below candle 1's low (a gap down).
3. **Close of candle 2:**
   - **On-neck:** closes at or very near candle 1's close.
   - **In-neck:** closes slightly into candle 1's body, just above its close.

Sources differ on exactly what counts as "slightly". A close that reaches well into the body is no longer in-neck and begins to resemble a piercing line.

**Worked example.** Candle 1 opens at 60 and closes at 50. Candle 2 opens at 48, rallies, and closes at 50: that is an on-neck. If it closes at 50.5, it is an in-neck. If it closes at 54, it has moved too deep for either.

## How to read it

The pattern says the first bounce after a sharp fall was feeble. That supports the downtrend. The signal strengthens if the next candle falls below candle 2's low.

Without a downward follow-through, the pattern has little value. Many traders treat it as a reason to wait, not to act.

## Best market states

**Established downtrends (best).** The pattern needs a clear fall before it.

**After a sharp decline (good).** A long first candle sets up the pattern clearly.

**Ranges (weak).** The pattern has no meaning without a trend to continue.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear downtrend before candle 1 |
| Gap | Candle 2 opens below candle 1's low |
| Close | At or just inside candle 1's close |
| Next candle | A close below candle 2's low |
| Context | Not sitting on strong support |

## Common mistakes

- Confusing it with a piercing line, which has a deeper close and a different meaning.
- Using it in a market with no gaps, without a clear replacement rule.
- Treating a weak rally as a signal to act without follow-through.
- Ignoring nearby support that could end the fall.
- Expecting the pattern to appear often.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between on-neck and in-neck?**
Both end with a weak rally. The on-neck closes at or very near the first candle's close. The in-neck closes a little inside the first body. The in-neck is a slightly stronger bounce.

**Is it bullish or bearish?**
Bearish. It is a continuation pattern in a downtrend. A feeble bounce is read as a sign that sellers remain in control.

**How is it different from a piercing line?**
A piercing line closes deep into the first candle's body, often past the midpoint, and signals a possible reversal up. The on-neck and in-neck close at or just inside the first close, and signal continuation.

**How reliable is it?**
Not very. It is a minor pattern and results depend on how strictly it is defined. Use it as a small piece of evidence.

**Does it need a gap?**
In the classic description, yes: candle 2 opens below candle 1's low. In markets with little gapping, traders must choose a relaxed rule and apply it consistently.
