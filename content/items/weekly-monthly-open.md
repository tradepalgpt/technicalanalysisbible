The weekly open and monthly open are the first prices traded in the current week and month. Traders draw them as horizontal lines and watch whether price is above or below them. Price above the open is read as a mild bullish lean for that period, below as a mild bearish lean, and the lines often act as support or resistance.

## What are the weekly and monthly open?

Each period has an opening price. The weekly open is the first price of the week, and the monthly open is the first price of the month. These do not change for the rest of that period, so they act as fixed reference lines.

The idea is simple. The open is where the period started. If price trades above it, buyers have held the market higher through the period so far. If below, sellers have. Some traders also see the open as a "fair" price for the period, which price returns to after moves away.

No single person created this practice. Opening prices are standard data on every chart, and traders have used them in many styles. They are especially common in the ICT and smart money concepts community, for example in the [power of three](/power-of-three/) idea. This guide treats that use neutrally. The lines have no academic evidence base as a predictor, and their use is a convention.

## How it is identified

The level is read from the chart rather than calculated.

1. Switch to a weekly (or monthly) chart, or use a tool that plots higher-time-frame opens on your chart.
2. Read the open price of the current candle.
3. Draw a horizontal line at that price across the period.

**Worked example.** The weekly open is 100 and the monthly open is 96. Price is trading at 103.

- Price is 3 above the weekly open and 7 above the monthly open.
- Both opens are below price, so the lean is mild bullish.
- If price pulls back, 100 and 96 are the first reference levels to watch.

**Where the week starts.** This differs by market. Many foreign exchange charts treat the Sunday evening open as the start of the week, while stocks start on Monday. Charting platforms and data vendors also differ on the time zone they use for the week and month boundary. Choose one source and keep to it, because the open will differ if the boundary differs.

## How to read it

**Directional filter.** A simple use is to take only long ideas when price is above the weekly open and only short ideas when below. This is a filter, not a signal. It stops you from fighting the main lean.

**Support and resistance.** Price often pauses or reverses at an open. The reaction is common enough that traders watch it, but not reliable enough to trade by itself.

**Pullback to the open.** In a trend, price may fall back to the open and then continue. Traders look for a rejection candle there.

**Failed break.** If price closes beyond the open and then returns, that may show a failed break and a move in the opposite direction.

**Two opens together.** When the weekly and monthly opens sit close together, the area is often treated as more significant.

## Best market states

**Ranges (best).** In a sideways market, price often moves around the open, which sits near the middle of the range.

**Trends (good).** In a trend, the open gives a pullback level and a simple filter on direction.

**Breakouts.** A move well away from the open early in the period can show strength, but the open gives no target.

**Volatility (weak).** In sharp, news-driven moves, price jumps through the open with no reaction, and the filter flips often.

## Settings

| Setting | Typical value | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Period | Weekly and monthly | Weekly gives short-term levels; monthly gives broader ones |
| Week start | Sunday or Monday | Changes the open price, depending on market and platform |
| Time zone | Platform default | Moves the boundary between periods |
| Past opens | Show the last few | Older opens can still act as levels |

There is no tested best setting. Use the same week definition on every chart so the levels stay consistent.

## Common mistakes

- Using different week start times on different charts, so the open does not match.
- Treating the open as a reversal signal by itself.
- Ignoring the bigger trend and taking trades only because price touched an open.
- Forgetting that price above the open is only a mild lean and not a trend.
- Over-marking the chart. Too many opens, highs and lows crowd the chart and make everything look like a level.

## FAQ

**What is the weekly open?**
It is the first price traded in the current week. Many forex charts count the Sunday evening open, and stock charts count Monday. It is drawn as a horizontal line and watched as a possible support or resistance level.

**What does it mean when price is above the monthly open?**
It means price has held higher than where the month started. Some traders read this as a mild bullish lean for the month. It is a filter and not a signal, and it does not predict what happens next.

**Do the weekly and monthly open work as support and resistance?**
Price often reacts near them, so many traders watch them. The reaction is not reliable on its own, and no study is cited here to support it. Use a rejection candle or another signal before acting.

**Why does my weekly open differ from someone else's?**
Platforms and data vendors set the week boundary and time zone differently, and markets open at different times. Check your platform's week start and keep it the same on all charts.

**Is the weekly open part of ICT?**
It is used in ICT and smart money teaching, for example in the power of three idea. Opening prices themselves are standard chart data used by many traders, so the idea is not unique to that community.
