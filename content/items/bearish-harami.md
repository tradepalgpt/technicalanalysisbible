A bearish harami is a two-candle pattern at the end of an uptrend. A long bullish candle is followed by a small candle whose body sits inside the body of the first. It shows the rally has lost momentum. It is a warning, not a sell signal, and most traders wait for the next candle to confirm it.

## What is a bearish harami?

"Harami" is an old Japanese word for a pregnant woman. The long first candle is the mother and the small second candle is the baby inside her. On a chart, the first candle shows buyers firmly in control. The second candle opens inside that range and moves very little, so the buyers have stopped pushing.

That is the whole message: the momentum has paused. It does not say sellers have taken over. For that reason the bearish harami is a weaker signal than the [bearish engulfing](/bearish-engulfing/) pattern, where the second candle swallows the first.

## How it is identified

A bearish harami has:

1. **A prior uptrend.** The pattern means little without something to reverse.
2. **A long bullish (up) candle** with a large body.
3. **A small second candle** whose body sits inside the first candle's body. The second candle can be bullish or bearish. Nison's original description counts either colour, though a bearish second candle is often read as slightly stronger.
4. **Wicks.** Many definitions also want the second candle's whole range, wicks included, inside the first body. Others only require the body. Platforms differ, so check which rule your scanner uses.

**Worked example.** Day one opens at 100 and closes at 110, a bullish body of 10. Day two opens at 107 and closes at 104. The second body (104 to 107) lies inside the first (100 to 110), so this is a bearish harami. If day two had closed at 111, it would not count.

## How to read it

**After a strong rise.** Several strong up candles, then a harami, suggests buying is fading. Price may stall, pull back or simply move sideways.

**At resistance.** A harami at a known resistance level or a previous high is more meaningful, because the level explains why buyers stopped.

**The next candle matters.** A close below the low of the first candle is a stronger sign than the harami alone. If the next candle closes above the first candle's high, the pattern has failed.

**Size.** The smaller the second body compared with the first, the clearer the loss of momentum. If the second candle is a [doji](/doji/), the pattern is called a harami cross.

## Best market states

**Reversals after an extended rise (best).** The pattern is about momentum fading at the end of a move.

**Strong trends (weak).** In a strong trend, small pauses are normal and the move often carries on.

**Ranges (weak).** In a sideways market, small candles inside bigger ones appear all the time and carry little meaning.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear, extended rise into the pattern |
| First candle | Long body, larger than recent candles |
| Second candle | Body fully inside the first body |
| Next candle | A close below the first candle's low, or a clear bearish candle |
| Level | Resistance, a prior high or an upper band nearby |

## Common mistakes

- Selling on the harami alone without waiting for confirmation.
- Calling any small candle after a big one a harami without checking the prior trend.
- Ignoring that the second candle only shows a pause, not a shift in control.
- Using it on very short time frames, where inside candles appear constantly.
- Mixing the rules: some traders need the wicks inside the first body, others only the body. Pick one and apply it consistently.

## FAQ

**What does a bearish harami mean?**
It means buying momentum has paused after a rise. A small candle forms inside the body of a large bullish one. It is a warning that the move may be tiring, and it needs confirmation from the next candle or from a key level.

**Is a bearish harami reliable?**
It is one of the weaker reversal patterns because the second candle only shows a pause. No candlestick pattern works every time. Context, such as a nearby resistance level, and a confirming next candle matter more than the shape.

**What is the difference between a bearish harami and a bearish engulfing?**
In a harami the second body sits inside the first. In an engulfing pattern the second body covers the first. Engulfing patterns show sellers taking control and are usually read as stronger. See [Bearish engulfing](/bearish-engulfing/).

**Does the second candle have to be bearish?**
Not under Nison's description, which allows either colour. A bearish second candle is often read as a bit stronger. Some scanners require it, so check the rule your software uses.

**What is a bullish harami?**
It is the mirror image. A long bearish candle is followed by a small candle inside its body, after a fall. It warns that selling pressure is easing. See [Bullish harami](/bullish-harami/).
