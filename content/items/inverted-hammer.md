An inverted hammer is a single candlestick with a small body near the low and a long upper wick, appearing after a decline. Buyers pushed price up but could not hold it. It hints that selling may be easing, but it is a weak signal that needs bullish confirmation.

## What is an inverted hammer?

The candle looks like an upside-down [hammer](/hammer/). During the period, buyers pushed price well above the open. Sellers then pushed it back, so the close finished near the low. On its own that looks bearish.

The reason traders still watch it after a fall is that buyers showed up at all. Having a bid strong enough to lift price that far, in a market that has been falling, suggests selling pressure is easing. But the close near the low means sellers won the period, so the signal is weak until a later candle backs it up.

It has exactly the same shape as a shooting star. Position tells them apart: an inverted hammer comes after a decline, a shooting star after a rise.

## How it is identified

An inverted hammer has:

1. **A prior downtrend.**
2. **A small real body** near the low of the candle. The colour of the body is not critical, though a bullish body is often read as slightly better.
3. **A long upper wick**, usually at least twice the body.
4. **Little or no lower wick.**

**Worked example.** A candle opens at 40.00, rises to 42.00, falls back and closes at 40.50, with a low of 39.95. Body = 0.50 (40.00 to 40.50). Upper wick = 1.50, which is three times the body. Lower wick = 0.05. In a downtrend, this is an inverted hammer.

## How to read it

**After a fall.** Several down candles, then an inverted hammer, suggests sellers may be tiring.

**At support.** The signal is stronger at a known support level or prior low.

**The next candle matters.** Nison advised waiting for confirmation, such as a gap up or a strong bullish candle closing above the inverted hammer's body or high. Without it, the candle may simply be a failed bounce.

**Failure.** A close below the inverted hammer's low suggests the idea has failed.

## Best market states

**Reversals after a fall, near support (best).** That is the only setting where the candle has a story.

**Strong downtrends (weak).** A failed rally in a downtrend is a normal part of the trend.

**Ranges (weak).** Candles with long upper wicks appear often in sideways markets.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear decline into the candle |
| Shape | Small body near the low, upper wick at least twice the body |
| Next candle | A bullish close above the inverted hammer's body or high |
| Level | Support, a prior low or a lower band nearby |
| Stop idea | Below the inverted hammer's low |

## Common mistakes

- Buying it without confirmation. It is weaker than a hammer, which closes near the high.
- Confusing it with a shooting star. The shape is the same; the location is different.
- Ignoring context and trading every candle with a long upper wick.
- Using a loose wick-to-body rule and counting ordinary candles.
- Reading it on very short time frames where such candles are common.

## FAQ

**Is an inverted hammer bullish or bearish?**
After a decline it is read as a possible bullish reversal, but a weak one, because the close is near the low. It needs a bullish follow-up candle. Without that, it is just a candle with a long upper wick.

**What is the difference between an inverted hammer and a shooting star?**
They look the same. An inverted hammer appears after a fall and hints at a turn up. A shooting star appears after a rise and hints at a turn down. See [Shooting star](/shooting-star/).

**Why is the inverted hammer weaker than the hammer?**
A hammer closes near its high, showing buyers won the period. An inverted hammer closes near its low, so sellers won it. It only gains meaning if the next candle confirms. See [Hammer](/hammer/).

**Does the body colour matter?**
Not much. A bullish (up) body is often seen as slightly better, but the shape and location matter more. Confirmation matters more than colour.

**Should I trade an inverted hammer on its own?**
It is unwise. Most traders wait for a confirming candle and look for support or other evidence before acting.
