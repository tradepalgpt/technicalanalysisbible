A price gap is an empty space on a chart where no trading took place, because one bar opened clearly above the previous bar's high or below its low. Traders treat the edges of a gap as support or resistance, and watch whether price returns to "fill" it. The meaning depends on where the gap appears.

## What is a price gap?

A gap forms when the next bar's low is above the previous bar's high (a gap up), or the next bar's high is below the previous bar's low (a gap down). In shares it often appears at the open after news, such as earnings, because the stock traded at a different price while the market was closed. In a market that trades nearly around the clock, such as forex, gaps are mostly seen over weekends.

Japanese candlestick traders call a gap a "window". The edges of a gap are used as levels: the top and bottom of the empty space. Price that returns into a gap is often seen as testing it, and price that closes the whole gap has "filled" it. See [gap fill](/gap-fill/).

Gaps are commonly sorted into types: the [breakaway gap](/breakaway-gap/) that starts a new move, the [runaway gap](/runaway-gap/) that appears in the middle of a trend, and the [exhaustion gap](/exhaustion-gap/) near the end. This sorting is done only afterwards, which limits its use in real time.

## How it is identified

1. **Compare consecutive bars.** A gap up needs the new low to be above the old high. A gap down needs the new high to be below the old low.
2. **Mark the edges.** The previous bar's high and the new bar's low (for a gap up) are the two edges.
3. **Check the context.** Note where in the trend it appears, how big it is compared with normal ranges and how much volume came with it.
4. **Watch for a return.** Does price come back into the gap, hold at its edge or fill it fully?

**Worked example.** A share closes at 50.00 with a high of 50.40. Next morning it opens at 52.00 and never trades below 51.80. The gap runs from 50.40 to 51.80. If price later falls back to 51.80 and bounces, the top of the gap has acted as support. A close below 50.40 means the gap has filled.

## How to read it

**Gap and go.** A gap beyond a key level on heavy volume, which stays open for several bars, suggests strong conviction. This is the breakaway case.

**Gap and hold.** Price returns to the edge of the gap and bounces. The gap edge is working as support or resistance, much like [role reversal](/role-reversal/).

**Gap fill.** Price returns through the whole gap. Traders often say gaps "always fill", but that is not true: many gaps stay open for a long time, particularly breakaway gaps.

**Exhaustion.** A gap near the end of a long move, followed by a quick reversal, may mark a final burst. An island with gaps on both sides is the [island reversal](/island-reversal/).

**Size.** A gap that is small compared with the usual daily range, for example below a typical [ATR](/atr/), is often just noise.

## Best market states

**Breakouts (best).** A gap through a well-watched level, with volume, is one of the clearer breakout signals.

**Reversals (good, with care).** Gaps against the trend that fail, or exhaustion gaps at the end of long runs, can mark turns. These are judged mostly after the fact.

**Trends (mixed).** A gap in the trend direction may signal continuation. In a long trend it is also a warning of possible exhaustion, so context matters.

**Ranges (weak).** Gaps inside a range are often filled quickly and carry little information.

**Volatility.** Gaps are more common and larger when volatility is high, such as around earnings.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Size | Large compared with the average range of recent bars |
| Volume | Clearly above average on the gap bar |
| Context | Through a known level, or at the end of a long trend |
| Hold | Price stays beyond the gap edge for several bars |
| Cause | A known news event is usually more meaningful than a random gap |
| Stop | Beyond the far edge of the gap, or inside the gap for aggressive entries |

## Common mistakes

- Believing every gap must fill. Many stay open for months.
- Fading a large gap on news, which can be the start of a long move.
- Labelling the gap type (breakaway, runaway, exhaustion) in real time. That is usually only clear afterwards.
- Ignoring size: tiny gaps are often noise.
- Treating a data glitch or a thin pre-market print as a real gap.
- Confusing a price gap with a [fair value gap](/fair-value-gap/), which is a different, three-candle idea.

## FAQ

**What is a gap in trading?**
A gap is an empty space on the chart where price jumped from one level to another without trading between. It happens when a bar opens beyond the previous bar's high or low, often after news or over a weekend.

**Do gaps always fill?**
No. Some gaps fill within days, others stay open for a long time or never fill. The idea that all gaps fill is a common myth. Gaps that appear with heavy volume and a major breakout are less likely to be filled quickly.

**What are the different types of gaps?**
Common labels are the breakaway gap at the start of a move, the runaway or measuring gap in the middle, and the exhaustion gap near the end. They are usually identified after the fact, so use them with care.

**How do I trade a gap?**
Common approaches are to trade in the gap direction when it breaks a level on volume, or to wait for a retest of the gap edge. Others fade small gaps back toward the fill. Each approach needs a stop and a test.

**Is a price gap the same as a fair value gap?**
No. A price gap is a real empty space between two bars. A fair value gap is a three-candle pattern from smart money concepts, where the outer candles do not overlap. See [fair value gap vs price gap](/compare/fair-value-gap-vs-price-gap/).
