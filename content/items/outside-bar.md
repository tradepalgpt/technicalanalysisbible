An outside bar is a bar whose high is above the previous bar's high and whose low is below the previous bar's low. It is the opposite of an inside bar. It shows that the range expanded, and traders often read the close as a clue to direction, especially at an important level.

## What is an outside bar?

An outside bar has a larger range than the bar before it, and covers it on both sides. The high is higher and the low is lower. The previous bar sits entirely inside it, which is why it is "outside".

On daily charts it is called an outside day. On candlestick charts, when the body also covers the previous body, it is the same shape as a [bullish engulfing](/bullish-engulfing/) or [bearish engulfing](/bearish-engulfing/) candle. The difference is that an outside bar only needs the range to cover, not the body.

The bar shows that buyers and sellers both pushed price beyond the prior range. Where it closes tells the story. A close near the high suggests buyers won the bar, a close near the low suggests sellers did, and a close in the middle shows indecision. It is a plain description of what happened, not a forecast.

## How it is identified

The test is two comparisons on the current bar:

1. High is greater than the previous bar's high.
2. Low is less than the previous bar's low.

Some traders add rules: a minimum size (such as a range larger than the average true range, [ATR](/atr/)), or a close in the top or bottom third of the bar.

**Worked example.** The previous bar has a high of 105 and a low of 100. The next bar trades from 98 to 108, closing at 107. The high (108) is above 105 and the low (98) is below 100, so it is an outside bar. It closes in the top fifth of its range (107 within 98 to 108), so it leans bullish. A trader might buy above 108 with a stop below 98, risking 10 points, or below the 100 low for a tighter stop. Stop choice changes the trade a lot.

## How to read it

**Close position.** Closing near the high is read as bullish, near the low as bearish.

**Location matters.** An outside bar after a long fall and at a support level is read as a possible reversal. The same bar in the middle of nowhere means little.

**Not always a reversal.** An outside bar in the direction of the trend can simply be a strong continuation bar.

**Expansion.** It marks a jump in range, so volatility is rising. After a quiet run of [inside bars](/inside-bar/), an outside bar may signal a move starting.

**False break risk.** Taking out both sides is also what a failed breakout looks like. A fast reversal of the bar can trap early entries.

## Best market states

**Reversals at levels (best).** A large bar at support or resistance with a close at one end carries more information.

**Volatility expansion (moderate).** It can mark the end of a quiet spell, though it does not give direction alone.

**Ranges (weak).** Bars overlap constantly, so outside bars appear all the time and are mostly noise.

| Close | In the top or bottom third of the bar |
| Level | At support, resistance or a key level |
| Trigger | A break of the bar's high or low |
| Stop | Beyond the opposite end of the bar |

## Common mistakes

- Trading every outside bar. Most carry no information in the middle of a range.
- Ignoring the close. An outside bar that closes in the middle shows indecision.
- Using a stop at the far end of a very large bar, which makes the risk too wide.
- Treating it as a reversal signal inside a strong trend.
- Mixing it up with an engulfing candle. It only needs the range to cover, not the body.
- Expecting a fixed success rate. Results depend on market, timeframe and context.

## FAQ

**What is an outside bar?**
It is a bar with a higher high and a lower low than the bar before it. The previous bar sits fully inside its range. It shows that the range expanded, and the close hints at which side won that bar.

**Is an outside bar the same as an engulfing candle?**
Not quite. An engulfing candle needs the body to cover the previous body. An outside bar only needs the high and low to cover the previous range. Many outside bars are also engulfing, but not all.

**Is an outside bar bullish or bearish?**
It depends on the close and the location. A close near the high, at support, leans bullish. A close near the low, at resistance, leans bearish. A close in the middle shows indecision and is not much use.

**Where does the stop go on an outside bar?**
Many traders use the opposite end of the bar, which gives a clear invalidation point but can be wide. Others use a fraction of the bar or an ATR multiple. A wide bar means a smaller position for the same risk.

**What is the difference between an outside bar and an inside bar?**
An inside bar sits within the previous bar's range and shows a pause. An outside bar covers the previous bar on both sides and shows expansion. Inside bars are about compression, outside bars about expansion.
