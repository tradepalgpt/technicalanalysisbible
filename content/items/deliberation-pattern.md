The deliberation pattern, also called the stalled pattern, is three white candles in an uptrend. The first two are long and strong, and the third is small and opens near the second candle's high. The climb is slowing. It warns that buyers may be pausing, and it needs confirmation.

## What is the deliberation pattern?

"Deliberation" means thinking things over. After two decisive up candles, the market seems to hesitate: the third candle barely moves. The pattern looks like [three white soldiers](/three-white-soldiers/) at first glance, but the third soldier is tiny.

Nison treats it as a variation on the [advance block](/advance-block/). Both show an advance losing strength. In the advance block the weakness spreads over all three candles, while here it appears all at once in the third.

## How it is identified

1. **An uptrend** is in progress.
2. **Two long white (bullish) candles** in a row, each closing higher than the last, the second opening inside the first body.
3. **A third white candle with a small body**, opening at or near the close of the second candle (often with a small gap up), and closing a little higher.

The third body is often described as a spinning top. There is no fixed measure for "small". A working rule is a body less than about half the average of the previous bodies.

**Worked example.** Candle 1 opens at 100 and closes at 106. Candle 2 opens at 104 and closes at 111. Candle 3 opens at 111.5 and closes at 112.5, a body of 1 against bodies of 6 and 7. Closes rise (106, 111, 112.5), but the third candle barely moves. This is a deliberation pattern.

## How to read it

The first two candles show strong buying. The third opens at the high of the second and goes nowhere, which suggests buyers are running out of steam, or that sellers are starting to meet them.

It is not a reversal on its own. A bearish candle that closes below the small body, or below the second candle's body, would add weight. If the next candle is another strong up candle, the pattern has failed and the trend carries on.

## Best market states

**Reversal (best).** A mature rise, ideally near resistance, gives the pattern context.

**Range (weak).** Three rising candles with a stall are just a swing inside the range.

**Breakout (weak).** A breakout can briefly pause with a small candle before carrying on.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear rise before the pattern |
| Candle shape | Two long bodies, then a small third |
| Location | Near resistance or a prior high |
| Next candle | A bearish candle closing below the third body |
| Momentum | Falling volume or RSI divergence on the rise |

## Common mistakes

- Calling any small third candle a deliberation pattern, even after only a short rise.
- Selling at once without waiting for a bearish candle.
- Confusing it with the advance block, which fades gradually across the three.
- Ignoring that a small candle can be a normal pause in a healthy trend.
- Taking it as a strong signal, when the pattern is generally viewed as mild.

## Pairs well with

- **[RSI](/rsi/)**: a high or diverging reading adds to the warning.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between the deliberation pattern and an advance block?**
Both are three white candles showing fading strength. In an advance block the bodies shrink or the upper shadows lengthen across the three candles. In the deliberation pattern the first two are strong and the third is suddenly small.

**Why is it called "stalled"?**
Because the advance stalls on the third candle. Western books often use "stalled pattern" and Japanese-derived texts "deliberation", but they mean the same thing.

**Is it bearish?**
It is a warning that an uptrend may pause or turn, not a bearish signal by itself. Confirmation from the next candle or from other tools is needed.

**Is there a bearish-trend version?**
The standard pattern is described for uptrends only. Some traders look for a similar stall in downtrends, but it is not a standard named pattern.

**How reliable is it?**
Be careful with claims. It is a rare and mild pattern, and this guide knows of no dependable evidence for a fixed edge. Test it on your own data before relying on it.
