Hidden divergence is a disagreement between price and an oscillator that points to the trend continuing. In an uptrend, price makes a higher low while the oscillator makes a lower low. In a downtrend, price makes a lower high while the oscillator makes a higher high. It is the mirror of regular divergence.

## What is hidden divergence?

In a trend, price pulls back and then continues. During the pullback, momentum often drops more than price does, because oscillators swing hard on small moves. Hidden divergence spots that: price holds higher (in an uptrend) while the oscillator drops lower.

It is called "hidden" because it is less obvious than [regular divergence](/divergence/), which looks for price making a new extreme. Here price makes a shallower pullback while the oscillator resets more deeply. The sellers did not push price below the last low, so the trend remains intact.

- **Bullish hidden divergence (uptrend).** Price makes a higher low. The oscillator makes a lower low.
- **Bearish hidden divergence (downtrend).** Price makes a lower high. The oscillator makes a higher high.

No single person is credited with it. It is described in various forms in oscillator textbooks, and some authors disagree on whether it should be traded at all.

## How it is identified

1. **Confirm a trend first.** For an uptrend, price should be making higher highs and higher lows.
2. **Pick an oscillator** such as [RSI](/rsi/) and use it throughout.
3. **Find two swing lows** on price during pullbacks (for an uptrend).
4. **Compare.** Price low 2 is above price low 1, and the oscillator low 2 is below oscillator low 1.
5. **Draw a line** on each to see the disagreement.

**Worked example (uptrend).** Price falls to a swing low of 100 and RSI hits 32. After a rally, price pulls back to 103, a higher low, but RSI drops to 27, a lower low. Price is higher by 3 while RSI is lower by 5. That is bullish hidden divergence: the pullback was weak in price terms even though momentum dipped further.

## How to read it

**A pullback is ending, not a new trend starting.** It says the dip may be over and the trend may resume. It does not say how far the trend will run.

**Needs a trend.** Without a clear trend, a "higher low" is just a bump in a range and the pattern has nothing to continue.

**Wait for resumption.** Many traders wait for a price signal, such as a break above the pullback's high, a bounce from a rising trendline or moving average, or a bullish candle at support.

**Compare with regular divergence.** Regular divergence says a trend is tiring. Hidden divergence says the pullback is a pause. Seeing both on one chart in different places helps separate the two.

**Failure.** If price closes below the last swing low, the hidden divergence has failed and the trend may be turning.

## Best market states

**Trends (best).** In an orderly trend with regular pullbacks, hidden divergence marks where a pullback may end. It works best in trends that are already established.

**Ranges (weak).** There is no trend to continue, so signals are random bumps.

**Reversals (weak).** At a real reversal, hidden divergence can look like a continuation signal just before the trend fails.

**Volatility.** Very volatile markets produce deep, messy pullbacks, so swing lows are hard to compare.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Trend | Clear sequence of higher highs and lows (or lower for a downtrend) |
| Matching swings | Compare pullback low to pullback low (or high to high) |
| Clear disagreement | Price and oscillator move in opposite directions |
| Support | Pullback holds at a known support level, trendline or moving average |
| Price trigger | Break of the pullback high, or a reversal candle |
| Stop | Beyond the pullback low (uptrend) or high (downtrend) |

## Common mistakes

- Looking for it in a range, where there is no trend to continue.
- Treating a deep, broken pullback as hidden divergence when price has made a lower low.
- Mixing up the direction. Hidden bullish needs a higher low in price and a lower low in the oscillator.
- Entering without a trigger or a stop.
- Using it against the higher time frame trend.
- Seeing it everywhere. With enough oscillator wiggles, almost any pullback can be made to fit.

## Pairs well with

- **[Divergence](/divergence/)**: the reversal counterpart; knowing both stops you mixing them up.
- **[Support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/)**: a hidden divergence at a held support level is a stronger setup.
- **[Exponential moving average](/exponential-moving-average/)**: a rising EMA helps confirm the trend, and pullbacks to it often form hidden divergences.
- **[RSI](/rsi/)**: the oscillator most often used to find them.

## FAQ

**What is hidden bullish divergence?**
It is when price makes a higher low but the oscillator makes a lower low, in an uptrend. It is read as a sign that the pullback is ending and the trend may continue upward.

**What is hidden bearish divergence?**
It is when price makes a lower high but the oscillator makes a higher high, in a downtrend. It is read as a sign the bounce is ending and the downtrend may continue.

**Is hidden divergence reliable?**
It is not reliable on its own. It is a loose continuation clue and needs a clear trend, a level and a price trigger. Any success rate depends on how it is defined and tested, so be sceptical of quoted figures.

**What is the difference between hidden and regular divergence?**
Regular divergence suggests a reversal: price makes a new extreme the oscillator does not confirm. Hidden divergence suggests continuation: price makes a shallower pullback while the oscillator makes a deeper one. See [Divergence](/divergence/).

**Which indicators work for hidden divergence?**
Any momentum oscillator can be used. RSI, MACD, Stochastic and CCI are common. Pick one and keep it, so you are not hunting for an oscillator that agrees with your view.
