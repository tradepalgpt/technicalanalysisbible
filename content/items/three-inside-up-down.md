Three inside up and three inside down are three-candle reversal patterns. A harami (a small candle inside the previous large body) is followed by a third candle that confirms the turn. Three inside up appears after a decline. Three inside down appears after a rise. The third candle is what makes it more useful than a harami alone.

## What is three inside up / down?

The pattern starts with a [bullish harami](/bullish-harami/) or [bearish harami](/bearish-harami/). A harami is a two-candle pattern where a small candle sits inside the body of a large one before it. It hints that the trend is slowing, but it is only a hint.

The third candle adds the confirmation. In three inside up, it is a bullish candle that closes above the first candle's open. In three inside down, it is a bearish candle that closes below the first candle's open. Nison describes this as the confirmation of the harami.

It is a close relative of the [three outside up and down](/three-outside-up-down/), which is built on an engulfing pattern instead of a harami.

## How it is identified

For **three inside up** (bullish, after a decline):

1. **Candle 1:** a long bearish candle.
2. **Candle 2:** a smaller bullish candle whose body sits inside candle 1's body.
3. **Candle 3:** a bullish candle that closes above candle 1's open (many descriptions say simply above candle 1's body).

**Three inside down** (bearish, after a rise) is the mirror image: a long bullish candle, a small bearish candle inside it, then a bearish candle that closes below candle 1's open.

**Worked example.** After a fall, candle 1 opens at 50 and closes at 44. Candle 2 opens at 45 and closes at 47, inside candle 1's body (44 to 50). Candle 3 opens at 47 and closes at 51, above 50. That is a three inside up.

Books differ slightly on how much of the first body candle 3 must clear, so check the rule your software uses.

## How to read it

The first candle shows the trend. The second shows it stalling. The third shows the other side taking over. The more clearly candle 3 clears candle 1, the stronger the reading.

It is still a warning of a possible turn. A trend can resume after the pattern, especially in the middle of a move and away from key levels.

## Best market states

**Reversal points after a clear move (best).** The pattern needs a trend to reverse.

**At support or resistance (good).** A key level adds meaning.

**Ranges and strong trends (weak).** In ranges it appears by chance. In strong trends it often fails.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear move before candle 1 |
| Harami | Candle 2's body sits inside candle 1's body |
| Candle 3 | Closes beyond candle 1's open |
| Level | Forms at support or resistance |
| Volume | Higher volume on candle 3, where available |

## Common mistakes

- Acting on the harami alone, before the third candle.
- Using it without a real prior trend.
- Ignoring that candle 3 must close beyond the first candle's open.
- Treating it as certain.
- Confusing it with the three outside pattern, which starts with an engulfing candle.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between three inside up and a bullish harami?**
A bullish harami is two candles. Three inside up adds a third bullish candle that closes above the first candle's open. The third candle is the confirmation that a harami lacks.

**What is the difference between three inside and three outside?**
Three inside starts with a harami, where the second candle sits inside the first. Three outside starts with an engulfing pattern, where the second candle covers the first.

**Is three inside up bullish?**
Yes. It follows a decline and points to a possible rise. Three inside down is the bearish version after a rise.

**Is it reliable?**
No pattern always works. It is a signal that needs context, such as a key level, and results vary by market and time frame.

**Should I wait for the third candle to close?**
Yes. Until the third candle closes, the pattern is only a harami.
