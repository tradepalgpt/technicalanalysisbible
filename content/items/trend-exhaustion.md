Trend exhaustion is the idea that a trend has run out of fuel and may be about to stall or reverse. It is not one indicator. Traders look for signs such as stretched price, falling momentum, a climactic bar or a gap that does not hold. Most use it to take profit, not to pick a top.

## What is trend exhaustion?

Trends do not last forever. Near the end, buying (in an uptrend) can dry up while price still pushes higher. Traders call that exhaustion. The word describes a state and a set of clues, and it cannot be measured with one number.

The clues are drawn from several older ideas:

- **Momentum divergence.** Price makes a new high while an oscillator makes a lower high. See [divergence](/divergence/).
- **Volume climax.** A burst of volume with a wide bar, followed by a stall. See [volume climax](/volume-climax/).
- **Exhaustion gap.** A late gap that fills quickly. See [exhaustion gap](/exhaustion-gap/).
- **Stretched price.** A move far beyond its average, such as many multiples of ATR above a moving average.
- **Three pushes.** Three similar rallies, each with less strength. See [three pushes](/three-pushes/).

No single clue is reliable. The difficulty is that strong trends often show every one of them, then continue. That is why many traders use exhaustion to take profits, rather than to open a trade in the other direction. The signs describe what has happened. They do not show when it ends.

## How it is identified

There is no standard formula. A simple checklist looks like this:

1. **Extended move.** Price is far from a moving average, for example more than 3 times the [ATR](/atr/) away.
2. **Weaker momentum.** An oscillator such as RSI makes a lower high against a higher price high.
3. **Climactic bar.** A wide-range bar, ideally on high volume, followed by little progress.
4. **Stall.** Price fails to make a new high, or closes back inside the bar.

**Worked example.** A stock rises to 120. Its 20-day average is 100 and ATR is 4, so price is 5 ATRs above the average. On the final push to 122 the RSI reads 68, lower than its previous peak of 74, and volume is the heaviest of the month. The next bar opens at 123 and closes at 119. That is four clues together. A trader may take profits or move the stop to 118. It does not prove the trend is over.

## How to read it

**Exit, not prediction.** The usual action is to reduce risk, such as taking part of a position off, or trailing the stop closer.

**More clues, more weight.** One sign alone, such as an oscillator reading, often appears many times before a trend ends.

**Confirmation by structure.** A break of a recent swing low, such as a [change of character](/change-of-character/), gives the stall a concrete trigger.

**Context.** The same stretched move can be a sign of strength at the start of a breakout. Look at where in the trend you are.

**Failure.** If price makes a new high and the clues reset, the exhaustion call was early. This happens often.

## Best market states

**Late-stage extended trends (best).** The idea only makes sense after a large move.

**Parabolic moves (moderate).** Very steep moves often end sharply, but can run a long way first.

**Ranges (weak).** There is no trend to exhaust, and edges are better read as support and resistance.

**Early trend (weak).** Strong momentum at the start of a trend is a sign of strength, not exhaustion.

## Common mistakes

- Calling a top on one sign. Divergence alone can persist for many bars.
- Shorting a strong trend because it "looks stretched".
- Ignoring the stage of the trend. Early strength is not exhaustion.
- Treating it as a single indicator with fixed settings.
- Forgetting a stop. Exhausted trends can still extend.
- Expecting a fixed outcome. Results depend on the market and timeframe.

## FAQ

**What is trend exhaustion?**
It is the idea that a trend has used up its momentum and may stall or reverse. Traders look for clues such as divergence, a volume climax or a gap that fails. There is no single formula or indicator.

**How do you spot trend exhaustion?**
Use a checklist: price stretched far from its average, weaker momentum on a new high or low, a climactic bar and a stall. The more clues agree, the more attention they deserve, but none is reliable alone.

**Is trend exhaustion a good reason to go against the trend?**
Usually not on its own. Strong trends often keep going despite apparent exhaustion. Many traders use the signs to take profit or tighten a stop, and wait for a structure break before considering a reversal trade.

**What is the difference between trend exhaustion and overbought?**
Overbought is an oscillator reading, such as RSI above 70. Trend exhaustion is a wider picture that may include that reading, along with volume, gaps and structure. Overbought can persist for a long time in a strong trend.

**Which indicators show trend exhaustion?**
No indicator measures it directly. Traders use RSI or MACD for divergence, ATR for stretch, volume for climax bars, and price action for structure breaks. Each has false signals, so combining them helps.
