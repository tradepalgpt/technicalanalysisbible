A long-legged doji is a candlestick with a very small body and long wicks on both sides. Price moved a long way up and down, then closed near where it opened. It shows a wide, unresolved fight between buyers and sellers. After a strong move or at a key level it can warn that the move is tiring.

## What is a long-legged doji?

A candlestick shows the open, high, low and close for one period. In a long-legged doji the open and close are almost equal, so the body is a thin line. The wicks (also called shadows) are long on both sides.

The long wicks are what set it apart from a plain [doji](/doji/). Price travelled far in both directions during the period, so volatility was high. Neither side won. The close ended near the middle of the range.

Nison describes this kind of candle as a sign of strong indecision. The wide range makes it a louder signal than a small, quiet doji, but a loud signal is not the same as a clear one. The candle only says that the market was torn.

## How it is identified

A long-legged doji has:

1. **A very small body.** A common rule of thumb is a body of about 5 to 10% of the full range (high to low) or less. Books give no single exact cut-off.
2. **Long wicks on both sides.** Each wick is clearly longer than the body, and usually the two are of broadly similar length.
3. **A close near the middle of the range**, not stuck at either end. A close near the high or low points to a dragonfly or gravestone instead.
4. **A large range compared with recent candles.** Many traders compare the range with the recent average, for example using [ATR](/atr/).

**Worked example.** A candle opens at 100, rises to 106, falls to 94 and closes at 100.5. Range = 12. Body = 0.5, about 4% of the range. The upper wick is 5.5 and the lower wick is 6. Both are long and similar, so it counts as a long-legged doji.

## How to read it

**After a strong run.** The candle shows that buyers (or sellers) pushed hard but could not hold the gain. It is a warning that the trend may be losing force.

**At support or resistance.** A long-legged doji right at a known level shows the level was tested from both sides. Read it together with what happens next.

**Before news.** Wide, undecided candles often appear when the market is waiting for an event, or just after one. They can mark a pause rather than a turn.

**The next candle matters.** As with the plain doji, most traders wait for a confirming candle. A strong candle against the prior move gives the doji more meaning. A candle in the same direction as the prior move suggests the trend simply paused.

## Best market states

**Volatile turning points (best).** After an extended move, a wide-range indecision candle is the classic first warning.

**Reversal zones (good).** At a key level with prior evidence of exhaustion.

**Quiet ranges (weak).** In sideways markets, doji of all sorts are common and mean little.

**Strong trends (weak).** Trends often pause with a wide candle and then continue.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Context | A clear prior move, or a key level |
| Next candle | A decisive candle in the opposite direction |
| Range | A range clearly larger than recent candles |
| Volume | Higher volume adds weight, where volume data is reliable |
| Time frame | Daily and higher carry more weight than very short charts |

## Common mistakes

- Treating the candle as a reversal signal on its own.
- Confusing it with a [spinning top](/spinning-top/), which has a small but visible body.
- Ignoring the context: in a quiet range it is noise.
- Acting before the next candle confirms.
- Using it on very short time frames where wide wicks are common.

## Pairs well with

- **[Support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/)**: gives the candle a place to matter.
- **[ATR](/atr/)**: shows whether the range was truly unusual.
- **[Volume](/volume/)**: shows whether the indecision came with heavy trading.
- **[RSI](/rsi/)**: an extreme reading or divergence strengthens the warning.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between a long-legged doji and a doji?**
A plain doji can have short wicks. A long-legged doji has long wicks on both sides, so price moved much further before closing near the open. The long range shows more volatility and a bigger fight between buyers and sellers.

**Is a long-legged doji bullish or bearish?**
Neither on its own. It shows indecision. After a rally it can warn of a fall, and after a decline it can warn of a rise. The candle that follows usually decides which reading fits.

**How is it different from a spinning top?**
A spinning top has a small but visible body. A long-legged doji has almost no body. Both show indecision, and the long-legged doji usually has longer wicks.

**Does it work on all time frames?**
It can appear on any chart, but it appears constantly on short time frames, where it carries little meaning. Most traders give daily and higher time frames more weight.

**Should I trade it by itself?**
Most traders treat it as a warning, not a trigger. They look for a confirming candle, a key level or a second tool before acting.
