Dark cloud cover is a two-candle bearish reversal pattern at the end of an uptrend. A long bullish candle is followed by a bearish candle that opens above the previous high and closes below the middle of the first body. It shows buyers who pushed higher were rejected, and it needs confirmation.

## What is dark cloud cover?

The pattern shows a sudden change of mood. On day one buyers are in charge and the candle closes near its high. On day two the market gaps or pushes up to a new high, which looks bullish. Then sellers take over and drive price back down through the middle of the previous day's body. A dark cloud hangs over the rally.

It sits between a [bearish harami](/bearish-harami/) (a pause) and a [bearish engulfing](/bearish-engulfing/) (a full reversal of the first candle). The deeper the second candle cuts into the first body, the more convincing the pattern.

## How it is identified

Nison's description has these parts:

1. **A prior uptrend.**
2. **A long bullish candle.**
3. **A bearish candle that opens above the high of the first candle** (above the first candle's upper wick, not only above its close).
4. **A close below the midpoint of the first body**, but still above the first candle's open. If it closes below the open, the pair is a bearish engulfing instead.

Many platforms and authors relax rule 3 and only require an open above the first candle's close, because true gaps are rare in markets that trade nearly around the clock, such as forex. Check which version your scanner uses.

**Worked example.** Day one opens at 100 and closes at 110 with a high of 111. The midpoint of the body is 105. Day two opens at 112 (above the high), then closes at 104. That is below 105 and above 100, so it is dark cloud cover.

## How to read it

**After a strong rise.** The pattern matters most after a clear advance, where buyers have been winning for some time.

**At resistance.** A dark cloud at a known resistance level or prior high shows the level doing its job.

**Depth of penetration.** The closer the close is to the first candle's open, the stronger the signal. A close only just below the midpoint is weaker.

**Confirmation.** Nison advised watching for a bearish follow-through. A close above the second candle's high suggests the pattern has failed.

## Best market states

**Reversals after an extended rise (best).** It describes buyers losing control at the top of a move.

**Strong trends (weak).** A strong trend can shrug off a single bearish candle.

**Ranges (weak).** In sideways markets, red candles after green candles are routine.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear rise into the pattern |
| First candle | Long bullish body |
| Second candle | Opens above the first high; closes below the first body's midpoint |
| Penetration | Deeper is stronger; below the first open makes it engulfing |
| Follow-through | A further lower close or a break of the second candle's low |

## Common mistakes

- Treating it as a sell signal without a prior uptrend.
- Using a close only just under the midpoint and calling it a strong signal.
- Ignoring the opening rule and mixing the strict (above the high) and relaxed (above the close) versions.
- Forgetting that a close above the pattern's high cancels it.
- Looking for it on very short time frames where noise creates the shape constantly.

## FAQ

**What does dark cloud cover mean?**
It means an advance met strong selling. After opening higher, price fell to close below the middle of the prior bullish candle. It warns that buyers may be losing control, but a turn is not certain.

**What is the difference between dark cloud cover and bearish engulfing?**
Dark cloud cover closes below the midpoint of the first body but above its open. A bearish engulfing closes below the first candle's open as well, covering the whole body. Engulfing is generally read as the stronger signal.

**Does dark cloud cover need a gap?**
Under Nison's description the second candle opens above the first candle's high. In markets with little or no gapping, such as forex, many traders accept an open above the first close instead.

**Is dark cloud cover reliable?**
No candlestick pattern works every time. Dark cloud cover is better judged with context: a clear uptrend, a resistance level and a confirming candle matter more than the shape alone.

**What is the bullish version?**
The piercing line. A long bearish candle is followed by a bullish candle that opens below the low and closes above the midpoint of the first body. See [Piercing line](/piercing-line/).
