The three drives pattern is a reversal shape made of three similar pushes in one direction, separated by two pullbacks. Traders measure each push and pullback with Fibonacci ratios and look for a turn at the end of the third drive. It is a trader-education concept with no academic evidence base.

## What is the three drives pattern?

The three drives pattern describes a market that pushes in one direction three times, each time with a pullback between, until the move runs out. A bullish version (a bottom) has three lower lows. A bearish version (a top) has three higher highs.

It is related to the [AB=CD pattern](/abcd-pattern/), which has two legs and one pullback. Three drives adds a third leg and so looks like two AB=CD structures chained together. It also resembles the idea of five waves in [Elliott wave](/elliott-wave/) theory, with the third drive standing in for an exhausted final push.

Like other [harmonic patterns](/harmonic-patterns/), the pattern lacks an academic evidence base. The ratios are conventions, and different sources quote slightly different ones. Patterns drawn on past charts tend to look neat because swing points can be chosen after the event.

## How it is drawn

A bearish three drives pattern (a top) uses these parts:

1. **Drive 1:** a rise to a high.
2. **Correction 1:** a pullback of about 0.618 or 0.786 of drive 1.
3. **Drive 2:** a rise to a higher high, about 1.272 or 1.618 of correction 1.
4. **Correction 2:** a second pullback, again about 0.618 or 0.786 of drive 2.
5. **Drive 3:** a rise to a third higher high, about 1.272 or 1.618 of correction 2.

The end of drive 3 is the potential reversal zone. A bullish pattern is the same picture flipped.

Some traders also expect the drives to look alike in size and time, so the shape looks balanced. Others use only the ratios.

**Worked example.** Drive 1 rises from 100 to 120: 20 points. Correction 1 falls 0.618 of that, 12.4 points, to about 107.6. Drive 2 rises 1.272 of correction 1: 1.272 x 12.4 = 15.8 points, to about 123.4. Correction 2 falls 0.618 of drive 2's rise from 107.6 to 123.4, which is 15.8 x 0.618 = 9.8 points, to about 113.6. Drive 3 rises 1.272 of correction 2: 1.272 x 9.8 = 12.5 points, to about **126.1**. That is the area where a turn is expected.

## How to read it

**Reversal zone.** The end of drive 3 is an area of interest, not a signal. Most traders wait for evidence of a turn, such as a reversal candle.

**Momentum.** Each drive often has weaker momentum than the last. A fading [momentum](/divergence/) reading across the drives is a commonly cited supporting clue.

**Stop.** A stop beyond the drive 3 extreme is usual, as a move past it breaks the shape.

**Targets.** Typical targets are retracements of the whole pattern. These are conventions, not tested rules.

**Balance.** If the third drive is far larger or smaller than the others, the pattern looks forced.

## Best market states

**Reversal after an extended move (best).** The pattern describes a trend running out of energy after three pushes.

**Trends (caution).** In a strong trend, a "third drive" is often just a step on the way to a fourth and fifth.

**Ranges (weak).** Sideways markets produce many pushes and pullbacks that can be forced into the ratios.

**Breakouts (weak).** A breakout can carry straight through the supposed reversal zone.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Count | Three clear drives with two pullbacks between |
| Ratios | Corrections and drives near the quoted ratios |
| Momentum | Weaker on each drive, or divergence on the third |
| Price action | A reversal candle or turn at the end of drive 3 |
| Level | The zone lines up with [support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/) |
| Stop | Beyond the extreme of drive 3 |

## Common mistakes

- Counting a fourth or fifth drive as a third to make the pattern fit.
- Choosing swing points after the fact to fit the ratios.
- Entering at the end of drive 3 with no sign of a turn.
- Treating the ratios as exact when sources vary.
- Ignoring that a strong trend can keep going past three drives.
- Taking neat past examples as proof of reliability.

## FAQ

**What is the three drives pattern?**
It is a reversal shape with three pushes in one direction and two pullbacks between. Traders measure the legs with Fibonacci ratios and watch the end of the third drive as a possible turn.

**How is it different from AB=CD?**
AB=CD has two legs of similar size with one pullback. Three drives has three pushes and two pullbacks, so it is roughly two AB=CD shapes chained together.

**What ratios does it use?**
Corrections are commonly 0.618 or 0.786 of the prior drive. Drives are commonly 1.272 or 1.618 of the prior correction. Sources differ slightly, so treat the figures as typical.

**Does the three drives pattern work?**
It has no academic evidence base. Patterns look neat on past charts because swing points can be chosen afterwards. Real-time results depend on confirmation and risk control.

**Where does the stop go?**
Most traders put it just beyond the extreme of the third drive. A move past it means the pattern has failed. The stop can be wide, so position size must allow for it.
