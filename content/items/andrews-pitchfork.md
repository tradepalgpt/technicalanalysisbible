Andrews' Pitchfork is a drawing tool that uses three swing points to build a median line and two parallel lines, shaped like a pitchfork. The lines form a trend channel. Traders treat them as possible areas of support and resistance, and expect price to return toward the median line. It is a manual method with no strong academic evidence.

## What is Andrews' Pitchfork?

The tool was developed by Alan Andrews, a trader and teacher, and became widely known through his teaching and later books by other authors. It has since been built into almost every charting platform.

The idea is that trends tend to move inside a sloping channel, and that price often returns to the middle line of that channel. The tool gives three parallel lines. The middle one is the median line. The two outer ones are drawn at equal distance on each side.

Andrews' own rules, which some traders follow, say price returns to the median line most of the time. That claim is a rule of thumb from his teaching and has not been confirmed by independent academic studies, so treat it with caution.

## How it is drawn

1. Choose three alternating [swing points](/swing-highs-lows/). For an uptrend, point 1 is a swing low, point 2 the next swing high and point 3 the next swing low (higher than point 1).
2. Find the midpoint between points 2 and 3.
3. Draw the median line from point 1 through that midpoint and extend it forward.
4. Draw two lines parallel to the median line, one through point 2 and one through point 3.

For a downtrend, flip the steps: swing high, swing low, then a lower swing high.

**Worked example.** Point 1 is at price 100 on bar 0. Point 2 is a high of 130 on bar 10. Point 3 is a low of 115 on bar 16. The midpoint of points 2 and 3 is at bar 13 and price (130 + 115) / 2 = **122.5**. The median line runs from (bar 0, 100) through (bar 13, 122.5). Its slope is 22.5 / 13, about 1.73 per bar, so at bar 20 the line is near 100 + 1.73 x 20 = **134.6**. The upper fork is parallel through point 2 and the lower fork is parallel through point 3.

Most platforms do this automatically from three clicks.

## How to read it

**Median line as target.** In the classic reading, price that enters the pitchfork often moves toward the median line. Traders use it as a target for pullback entries.

**Outer lines.** The upper and lower parallels act as possible resistance and support. A push beyond the upper line in an uptrend can signal acceleration.

**Break of the lower line.** In an uptrend, a close below the lower parallel suggests the trend has weakened or changed.

**Median line as a pivot.** If price fails to reach the median line and turns back, some traders read this as a sign of weakness. If price crosses it, the line may act as support or resistance afterwards.

**Variants.** The [Schiff pitchfork](/schiff-pitchfork/) shifts the starting point to change the slope. Platforms may also offer "modified Schiff" versions, so check which one is drawn.

## Best market states

**Trends (best).** The tool assumes a sloping channel. It suits trends that move in clear swings.

**Ranges (weak).** In a range, the three points give a nearly flat pitchfork and little useful information.

**Reversals.** A break of the outer line can warn of a change, but it is not a reversal signal by itself.

**Volatility.** Very volatile markets make swing points ambiguous, so the pitchfork changes shape with each choice.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Swing points | Three clear, alternating swings |
| Retest | Price respected the outer lines or median line at least once |
| Alignment | The lines agree with [horizontal levels](/support-and-resistance/) |
| Reaction | A reversal candle or momentum signal at a line |
| Stop | Beyond the nearest fork or the latest swing |
| Redraw | Do not keep moving the points to fit new price action |

## Common mistakes

- Choosing the three points after the fact so the lines fit.
- Redrawing the tool each time price breaks a line.
- Expecting price to return to the median line every time.
- Using it in a sideways market.
- Ignoring the trend direction and trying to fade a move at the outer line.
- Treating the lines as exact prices rather than zones.

## FAQ

**Who invented Andrews' Pitchfork?**
Alan Andrews, a trader and teacher, developed it and taught it from about the 1960s. Exact dates vary between sources, so this page gives a decade. The tool is now built into most charting platforms.

**How many points does a pitchfork need?**
Three. They should be alternating swing points: for example low, high, low in an uptrend. The median line starts at the first point and passes through the midpoint of the second and third.

**Does price always return to the median line?**
No. Andrews taught that it does most of the time, but no independent evidence confirms this. Use the median line as a possible target, not a certainty, and plan for it failing.

**What is the difference between Andrews' and Schiff pitchforks?**
The Schiff version moves the starting point of the median line to the midpoint between points 1 and 2, which flattens the slope. It can suit steeper trends. See [Schiff pitchfork](/schiff-pitchfork/).

**Can I use a pitchfork on any time frame?**
Yes, it works on any chart with swings. Shorter time frames give noisier swing points, so the lines change more often and are less dependable.
