Fibonacci confluence is the idea of drawing Fibonacci levels from more than one swing and looking for places where several levels land close together. Where two or more levels overlap, or overlap with another level such as support, traders treat that price as a stronger zone for a pullback to end. It narrows many levels down to a few.

## What is Fibonacci confluence?

A single [Fibonacci retracement](/fibonacci-retracement/) gives several levels. Draw a second one from a different swing and you get several more. Most of them will sit in different places, but now and then two will land within a few points of each other. That overlap is the confluence.

The same logic applies to any mix of tools. A retracement level that matches a previous swing high, a round number or a moving average is also confluence. The general idea is covered in [confluence](/confluence/).

The reasoning is simple: if several traders use different swings and arrive at the same price, more orders may cluster there. This is a plausible story rather than a proven effect. Studies of Fibonacci levels in markets have mixed results, and with so many levels on a chart, some overlap happens by chance.

## How it is drawn

1. Mark the main swing in the trend and draw a retracement across it.
2. Mark a smaller or earlier swing and draw a second retracement. Keep both in the same direction.
3. Optionally add an extension or projection from another swing, such as a [Fibonacci projection](/fibonacci-projection/).
4. Look for levels from different swings that fall within a small distance of each other.
5. Shade that area as a zone, not a single line.

**Worked example.** A market rises from 100 to 200. The 61.8% retracement is 200 - 0.618 x 100 = **138.2**. An earlier smaller swing runs from 120 to 160, so its 50% retracement is 160 - 0.5 x 40 = **140**. The two levels are 1.8 apart, so a zone from about 138 to 140 has Fibonacci confluence. If a previous swing high also sat near 139, that adds a third element.

## How to read it

**A zone, not a line.** Confluence zones are best read as areas a few ticks or points wide. Price rarely turns on an exact number.

**More inputs, more weight.** Two levels from different swings are a weak signal. Three, or two plus a non-Fibonacci level such as support, are generally treated as more meaningful.

**Wait for a reaction.** A zone only marks where to look. Many traders wait for a reversal candle, a break of a small trendline or a momentum signal before acting.

**Stops.** The stop usually goes just beyond the zone. If price closes through the whole zone, the idea has failed.

**Targets.** Prior swing highs or lows, or an extension of the move, are typical places to take profit.

## Best market states

**Trends with pullbacks (best).** Confluence is mostly used to time the end of a pullback inside a trend.

**Reversals (good).** A confluence zone that also matches a major level can mark where a counter-trend move stalls.

**Ranges (weak).** Without a clear swing, the levels drawn are arbitrary, and almost any price can be justified.

**Volatility.** In very fast markets price slices through zones. Use wider zones and wait for a reaction.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Swing choice | Swings that are obvious on the chart, not picked to fit |
| Overlap | Levels from at least two different swings within a small distance |
| Extra evidence | A non-Fibonacci level in the same area |
| Reaction | A reversal candle or momentum shift inside the zone |
| Stop | Just beyond the far edge of the zone |
| Limit | Do not add so many tools that every price becomes a zone |

## Common mistakes

- Drawing so many retracements that every price has a level nearby.
- Choosing swings after the fact so that levels line up.
- Treating the zone as an exact price and placing a stop one tick away.
- Entering at the zone without waiting for any reaction.
- Ignoring the trend and fading a strong move because a zone appeared.
- Believing that more overlapping levels means price will turn.

## FAQ

**What is Fibonacci confluence?**
It is a place where Fibonacci levels from two or more swings land close together. Traders treat the overlap as a stronger area for price to pause or turn than a single level on its own.

**Is Fibonacci confluence more accurate than a single level?**
Not proven. The idea is that more overlapping levels attract more orders, but with many levels on a chart some overlap happens by chance. Treat it as a way to narrow where to look, and test it yourself.

**How close must the levels be?**
There is no standard. Many traders use a small percentage of price or a fraction of the average true range. The key is to decide a rule in advance and apply it the same way each time.

**Can I combine Fibonacci with other tools?**
Yes. Most traders do. A Fibonacci level that matches support, resistance, a round number or a moving average is usually considered stronger than a Fibonacci level alone.

**Does it work on all markets and time frames?**
The method only needs swings, so it can be drawn anywhere. Whether it adds value depends on the market and the swings you choose. Lower time frames give more noise and more chance overlaps.
