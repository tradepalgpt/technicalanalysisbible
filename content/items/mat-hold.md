A mat hold is a five-candle trend continuation pattern. In the bullish form a long bullish candle is followed by a gap up, then three small candles that drift down but stay above the first candle's low, then a strong bullish candle that closes to a new high. It suggests a brief pause before the trend resumes.

## What is a mat hold?

The mat hold is a close relative of the [rising three methods](/rising-three-methods/) and [falling three methods](/falling-three-methods/). Those patterns show a trend taking a short rest inside the first candle's range. The mat hold differs in one way: the pause begins with a gap away from the first candle.

The idea is that a trend leg, a brief pullback that fails to do damage, and a fresh push all fit together. The small candles in the middle show that the other side tried to take control and could not.

It is a rare pattern. The name and description come from Japanese charting and appear in Nison's book. Some later authors, including Bulkowski, report that it is uncommon and that results depend on how strictly it is defined.

## How it is identified

For the **bullish** mat hold:

1. **Day 1:** a long bullish candle in an uptrend.
2. **Day 2:** a small bearish candle that gaps up above day 1's close.
3. **Days 3 and 4:** two more small candles that drift lower.
4. The small candles hold above the low of day 1 (many descriptions say they should stay within the upper half of the first body).
5. **Day 5:** a long bullish candle that opens above day 4's close and closes above the highs of the small candles.

The bearish form is the mirror image in a downtrend.

**Worked example.** Day 1 opens at 100 and closes at 108. Day 2 gaps up, opening at 109 and closing at 107. Day 3 closes at 106, day 4 closes at 105. All stay above 100 (and above 104, the middle of the first body). Day 5 opens at 106 and closes at 111, above every earlier high. This is a bullish mat hold.

Platforms and books differ on how deep the small candles may go and whether the gap is required. Check the rule your software uses.

## How to read it

The pattern says a trend paused and resumed without the opposing side gaining ground. The pullback was shallow. The final candle is the sign that buyers (or sellers) are back in charge.

The final candle is the confirmation. Before it forms, the pattern is only a pullback that may or may not succeed.

## Best market states

**Established trends (best).** The pattern needs a clear trend before it, since it describes a continuation.

**Strong trend legs (good).** A first candle that is long compared with recent candles matters.

**Ranges (weak).** In a sideways market a gap, pullback and breakout are common and mean less.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear trend before day 1 |
| Pullback depth | Small candles stay inside the rules above |
| Final candle | Closes beyond the pattern's earlier extreme |
| Volume | Heavier volume on days 1 and 5 adds weight, where available |
| Context | Not running into a nearby major level |

## Common mistakes

- Calling any pullback inside a trend a mat hold.
- Ignoring the gap on day 2 when your rule requires one.
- Allowing the small candles to fall below the first candle's low.
- Acting before the fifth candle closes.
- Treating a rare pattern as a reliable edge without testing it.

## FAQ

**How is a mat hold different from rising three methods?**
Both have a long candle, a short pullback and a new long candle. In the mat hold the pullback begins with a gap away from the first candle. The rising three methods pullback stays inside the first candle's range from the start.

**Is a mat hold bullish or bearish?**
It can be either. The bullish form continues an uptrend. The bearish form continues a downtrend. In both cases it signals that the existing trend is likely to resume.

**How common is it?**
It is rare. Many charts show only a few in years of data, and strict definitions find even fewer. That makes it hard to rely on or test.

**Does the gap have to be there?**
In the classic description, yes. Some software and authors relax this. Know which rule you are using.

**Can I trade it alone?**
Most traders use it as confirmation within a trend, not as a stand-alone trigger. They also look at the trend, key levels and volume.
