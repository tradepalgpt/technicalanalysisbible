Falling three methods is a five-candle bearish continuation pattern. A long bearish candle is followed by three small candles that stay within its range, then another long bearish candle closing at a new low. It shows a downtrend pausing, not ending, before it continues.

## What is falling three methods?

The pattern shows a downtrend taking a breath. A strong bearish candle shows sellers in charge. Then three small candles drift higher in a mild bounce, but none leaves the first candle's range. Buyers tried and failed to do real damage. A final long bearish candle shows sellers are back and the decline resumes.

Nison described the small candles as a rest within the trend, too weak to threaten it. It is a continuation pattern, so it works with the trend, unlike most candlestick patterns.

## How it is identified

The classic version has five candles:

1. **A long bearish candle**, in a downtrend.
2. **Three small candles** (usually bullish, but colour is flexible), each with a body inside the range of the first candle. They tend to drift up in a mild bounce.
3. **A fifth long bearish candle** that opens below the previous close and closes below the close of the first candle.

Some sources count wicks in the "inside the range" test; others only count bodies. Platforms and scanners differ.

**Worked example.** Candle 1 opens at 110 and closes at 100 (high 111, low 99). Candles 2 to 4 trade between 101 and 108, with closes at 103, 105 and 104. Candle 5 opens at 103 and closes at 96. The small candles stayed inside 99 to 111, and the last close (96) is below 100, so this is falling three methods.

## How to read it

**Trend pause.** The small candles show a bounce with little force. The smaller and calmer they are, the clearer the message.

**Volume.** Ideally volume is higher on the long candles and lower on the bounce, which shows the bounce lacked conviction. This is a common guideline, not a rule.

**Confirmation.** The fifth candle closing below the first close completes the pattern. If price closes above the first candle's high during the pause, it fails.

**Use.** Traders read it as evidence that a bounce is routine and the downtrend is intact, for example to stay with a short position or time an entry on the break below.

## Best market states

**Established downtrends (best).** It needs a trend to continue.

**Ranges (weak).** Sideways markets contain many small candles within bigger ones, so the shape appears by chance.

**Reversals (weak).** If the bounce breaks above the first candle's high, the pattern has failed.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear downtrend before candle 1 |
| First candle | Long bearish body |
| Middle candles | Three small candles inside the first range |
| Last candle | Long bearish; closes below candle 1's close |
| Volume | Lower on the bounce, higher on the long candles |
| Failure | A close above the high of the first candle |

## Common mistakes

- Counting any bounce as falling three methods without checking the first candle's range.
- Forgetting the final candle must close below the first one's close.
- Expecting exactly three candles every time. The classic form has three, though some traders accept a few more or fewer.
- Using it against the larger trend.
- Selling into a bounce and ignoring that it can still fail.

## FAQ

**What does falling three methods mean?**
It means a downtrend paused and then resumed. A strong bearish candle was followed by three small candles within its range, then another strong bearish candle. The small candles show the bounce was weak.

**Is falling three methods bearish?**
Yes. It is a bearish continuation pattern, so it works with an existing downtrend. It does not mark a new trend on its own.

**Do the three small candles have to be bullish?**
Classically they are small and mostly bullish, making the bounce look real. Many sources allow other colours as long as the bodies stay inside the first candle's range.

**How reliable is falling three methods?**
No pattern works every time. It is rare, and its value depends on a clear trend, a quiet bounce and a decisive final candle. Treat it as supporting evidence, not a stand-alone signal.

**What is the bullish version?**
Rising three methods. See [Rising three methods](/rising-three-methods/).
