Market structure is the sequence of swing highs and swing lows on a chart. Higher highs and higher lows describe an uptrend, lower highs and lower lows describe a downtrend, and a mix describes a range. Traders use it as a plain, indicator-free filter for direction and for spotting when a trend may be changing.

## What is market structure?

Price does not move in a straight line. It moves in waves: a push, a pullback, another push. The turning points of those waves are swing highs and swing lows (see [Swing highs and swing lows](/swing-highs-lows/)). Market structure is simply how those turning points line up.

The idea comes from Dow theory, which Murphy (1999) and Edwards and Magee describe: an uptrend is a series of rising peaks and rising troughs, and a downtrend is a series of falling peaks and falling troughs. Modern price action writers such as Al Brooks (2012) build on the same idea.

The term "market structure" itself is common trader language today, especially in online trading education. It is a way of labelling a chart, not a formula from academic research.

## How it is identified

Mark the swing highs and swing lows first, using one fixed rule. Then compare each new swing with the one before it.

| Pattern | Label | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| Higher high and higher low | HH, HL | Uptrend |
| Lower high and lower low | LH, LL | Downtrend |
| Highs and lows about level | Equal swings | Range |
| Higher high then lower low (or the reverse) | Mixed | Possible change, or a wide range |

**Worked example.** A market makes these swings in order: low 100, high 110, low 104, high 115, low 108. The lows are 100, 104, 108, each higher, and the highs are 110, 115, each higher. That is an uptrend structure. If price then falls to 106, it takes out the last higher low at 108. The sequence of higher lows is broken. This is the first warning, not yet proof of a downtrend.

A new high above 115 after that would show the uptrend has survived the test.

## How to read it

**Direction filter.** Many traders only look for buys while structure is making higher highs and higher lows, and only look for sells in the opposite case.

**The last protected swing.** In an uptrend, the most recent higher low is the point where the idea is tested. A close below it suggests the structure may be changing. Traders often place stops beyond it.

**Break of structure.** A move beyond the last swing in the trend direction shows continuation. See [Break of structure](/break-of-structure/).

**Change of character.** A break of the last swing against the trend is the first sign of a possible reversal. See [Change of character](/change-of-character/) and [Market structure shift](/market-structure-shift/).

**Multiple time frames.** The daily chart may show an uptrend while the 15-minute chart shows a downtrend pullback inside it. Always say which time frame the structure applies to.

## Best market states

**Trends (best).** Structure is clearest when swings are orderly and pullbacks are shallow.

**Reversals (useful).** The first lower low after a run of higher lows is among the most watched signals, though many are false.

**Ranges (weak).** In sideways markets the highs and lows overlap, so labels flip between "up" and "down". A range is better described by its top and bottom.

**Volatile markets.** Large spikes create swings that are really news reactions. They can distort the sequence.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Swing rule | The same definition of a swing throughout (for example 3 bars each side) |
| Sequence | At least two higher highs and two higher lows for an uptrend |
| Close | Breaks count on a close beyond the swing, not just a wick |
| Time frame | Structure agrees on the higher time frame |
| Test | Pullbacks stop above the last higher low (or below the last lower high) |
| Reversal | A break of the last swing, followed by a lower high (or higher low) |

## Common mistakes

- Changing the swing definition to make a pattern fit.
- Calling a trend after one higher high. Two swings are the minimum.
- Treating a wick below a swing as a full break.
- Using structure inside a range, where every swing flips the label.
- Mixing time frames, such as a 5-minute low against a daily trend.
- Assuming a broken structure always means a reversal. Many breaks are only deeper pullbacks.

## FAQ

**What does higher high and higher low mean?**
A higher high is a swing high above the previous swing high, and a higher low is a swing low above the previous swing low. Together they show buyers pushing price up and defending each pullback at a higher level.

**How many swings make a trend?**
Most traders want at least two higher highs and two higher lows (or the reverse). One swing is only a possible start. The more swings that follow the pattern, the more established the trend, though also the older it is.

**What breaks market structure?**
In an uptrend, a close below the most recent higher low breaks the structure. In a downtrend, a close above the most recent lower high does. Whether that is a reversal or only a deeper pullback is only clear later.

**Is market structure the same as Dow theory?**
It rests on the same core idea, that trends are made of rising or falling peaks and troughs. Dow theory has further rules, such as index confirmation. Market structure as used today is a looser chart-reading practice.

**Does market structure work in sideways markets?**
Poorly. In a range, swings overlap and the labels change often. Use the range edges as support and resistance instead, and wait for a clear break before using structure again.
