A fakey is a price action pattern in which price breaks out of an inside bar setup and then closes back inside, showing the break was false. Traders then look to trade the other way, on the idea that those who bought or sold the break are now trapped. It is a trader-education pattern with no academic evidence base.

## What is a fakey?

A fakey builds on the [inside bar](/inside-bar/). An inside bar is a bar whose high and low sit within the range of the previous bar, called the mother bar. It shows a pause. The usual next step is a break out of the mother bar's range.

In a fakey, the break goes the wrong way: price breaks above or below the mother bar, then closes back inside its range. The break is judged to be a failure, and the move expected is in the opposite direction. This is a close relative of a [false breakout](/false-breakout/).

The pattern name is common in price action teaching, but no single person is credited with it, and this guide does not give an inventor or a year. It has no academic evidence base. Because it uses clear bar rules, it is easy to define and test, but there is no published test cited here that says how well it works.

## How it is identified

A fakey needs these steps:

1. **Mother bar.** A bar with a clear range.
2. **Inside bar.** The next bar stays within the mother bar's high and low.
3. **False break.** A following bar moves beyond the mother bar's high or low.
4. **Close back inside.** That bar, or one of the next one or two bars, closes back within the mother bar's range.
5. **Entry.** Enter in the direction opposite to the false break, commonly when price moves past the inside bar's opposite extreme.

**Worked example (bearish fakey).** The mother bar runs from 100 to 110. The inside bar runs from 103 to 107. The next bar rises to 112, above the mother bar's high, but closes at 108, back inside.

- The break above 110 failed, so the setup is bearish.
- An entry is considered on a move below the inside bar's low at 103.
- The stop sits above the failed extreme at 112.
- Risk is 112 - 103 = 9 points on that entry.

A bullish fakey is the mirror: a break below the mother bar's low, a close back inside, and a long entry above the inside bar's high.

Rules differ between teachers. Some require a close back inside on the same bar, some allow up to three bars, and some enter at the close rather than at the inside bar's extreme.

## How to read it

**Trapped traders.** The idea is that traders who bought the break above the mother bar are caught when price falls back, and their exits add to the downward move. This explanation is a story, not a measured result.

**Location matters.** A fakey at a clear [support or resistance](/support-and-resistance/) level or a [key level](/key-levels/) is read as stronger than one in the middle of nowhere.

**Wick size.** A long wick beyond the mother bar, as on a [pin bar](/pin-bar/), shows a sharp rejection and some traders prefer it.

**Failure of the failure.** If price closes beyond the failed extreme afterwards, the fakey has itself failed and the stop is hit.

## Best market states

**Reversals at levels (best).** A fakey at an area where price has turned before has the most logic behind it.

**Ranges (good).** False breaks of small ranges are common, so a fakey can fit.

**Strong trends (weak).** In a trend, a break of an inside bar in the trend's direction often continues, and a counter trade is risky. A fakey against the trend is a minor pullback signal at best.

**Volatility.** Wide bars make the stop large, and mother bars that are very large make fakeys frequent but not meaningful.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Inside bar | A clear inside bar, not a tiny range |
| Break | A bar that moves clearly beyond the mother bar |
| Close | A close back inside the mother bar, not just a wick |
| Location | A key level, swing high or low, or round number nearby |
| Stop | Beyond the false-break extreme |
| Context | The bigger trend does not strongly oppose the trade |

## Common mistakes

- Trading every inside bar break as a fakey. A close back inside is required.
- Ignoring location. A fakey with no level nearby is a weak setup.
- Using a stop that is too tight, inside the false-break wick.
- Taking fakeys against a strong trend without a plan.
- Changing the number of bars allowed for the close-back rule to suit a chart.

## FAQ

**What is a fakey pattern?**
A fakey is an inside bar setup where price breaks out of the mother bar's range but closes back inside it. The break is read as false, and traders look to trade in the opposite direction.

**How do you trade a fakey?**
A common method is to enter when price moves past the inside bar's opposite extreme, with the stop beyond the false-break extreme. Rules differ between teachers, so define yours in writing and test it before using real money.

**Is a fakey the same as a false breakout?**
It is a specific kind. A false breakout is any break of a level that fails. A fakey is a false break of an inside bar setup, with a defined mother bar and inside bar, which makes the rules clearer.

**Which time frame is best for fakeys?**
No time frame is proven best. Many traders use daily and higher charts because the bars carry more weight, and lower charts have more noise. Test your own market and time frame.

**Does the fakey have a known success rate?**
No success rate is cited here. The pattern has no academic evidence base, and results depend on the market, the level and the rules used. Test it on past charts, including losing trades, before relying on it.
