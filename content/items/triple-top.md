A triple top is a reversal chart pattern that forms at the end of an uptrend. Price tests the same resistance level three times and fails each time, leaving three peaks at about the same height. A close below the lowest trough between the peaks is the usual signal that the uptrend has ended and a fall may follow.

## What is a triple top?

A triple top shows buyers failing three times at the same ceiling. Price rises to a high, pulls back, rises to about the same high, pulls back again, then makes a third attempt that also fails. Each failure at resistance shows that sellers are willing to step in at that price.

The pattern is a close cousin of the [double top](/double-top/) and the [head and shoulders](/head-and-shoulders/). Triple tops are less common than either. It also looks a lot like a [rectangle](/rectangle-pattern/), and some analysts treat a rectangle as a neutral pattern that can break either way. What gives a triple top its bearish meaning is the uptrend before it and the break of support afterwards.

## How it is drawn

A valid pattern needs these parts, in order:

1. **A prior uptrend.** Without one there is nothing to reverse.
2. **Three peaks** at about the same level. Exact matches are rare; a small band is normal.
3. **Two troughs** between the peaks.
4. **Support line:** a horizontal line through the lowest of the two troughs.
5. **Break:** a close below that line.

**Measured target.** Measure the vertical distance from the peaks to the support line. Subtract that distance from the breakout point.

**Worked example.** The peaks are at 60 and the support line through the troughs is at 50: a height of 10. If price closes below support at 49, the measured target is 49 - 10 = **39**.

The target is a guide, not a promise. Many patterns fall short, and some go further.

## How to read it

**Support break.** The pattern is only complete once price closes below the support line. Until then it may simply be a range, and price can still break upward.

**Volume.** Volume often tends to fade through the three rallies, which suggests weakening demand, and rise on the break. This is a common guideline and not a rule.

**Retest.** After the break, price often rallies back to the broken support, which may now act as resistance. This is [role reversal](/role-reversal/). Some traders enter on the retest instead of the break.

**Third peak.** Weak momentum on the third peak, for example a lower [RSI](/rsi/) high, is an early warning that is often watched.

**Failed pattern.** If price breaks support and then closes back above it, the pattern has failed. A close above the peaks removes the pattern entirely.

## Best market states

**Reversals after a long uptrend (best).** The pattern only makes sense when there is a meaningful rise to reverse.

**Ranges (weak).** Three touches of a level inside a sideways market are an ordinary range, not a reversal. The question is whether there was a trend into it.

**Strong trends (caution).** Repeated tests of a high can be a pause before a break higher.

**Volatility.** Choppy markets produce uneven peaks and frequent false breaks, so stops usually have to be wider.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear uptrend before the first peak |
| Peaks | Three highs within a tight band, with clear pullbacks between |
| Time | Peaks well spaced; very tight patterns are less meaningful |
| Break | Close below the support line, not just a wick |
| Volume | Fading on the rallies, rising on the break |
| Stop | Above the highest peak (a tighter stop sits above the third peak's retest) |

## Common mistakes

- Selling before support breaks. Until then it is only a possible pattern.
- Calling any three bumps a triple top without a prior uptrend.
- Forcing peaks that are not near each other into the pattern.
- Treating the measured target as certain.
- Ignoring the failure case: a close back above support means the idea is wrong.
- Using a very tight tolerance on peaks and missing real patterns, or a loose one that accepts anything.

## FAQ

**What is a triple top?**
A triple top is a chart pattern with three peaks at about the same price, formed after an uptrend. It shows that buyers failed three times at the same ceiling. A close below the support through the troughs completes it.

**Is a triple top bullish or bearish?**
It is a bearish reversal pattern. It signals that an uptrend may be ending. Its mirror image, the triple bottom, is the bullish version that forms at the end of a downtrend.

**How is a triple top different from a double top?**
A double top has two peaks and one trough, and a triple top has three peaks and two troughs. Both are confirmed by a break of support. The triple top takes longer to form and is less common.

**How do you find the target for a triple top?**
Measure from the peaks down to the support line, then subtract that height from the breakout point. Treat the result as a guide only. Many patterns stop short, and others run further.

**Where does the stop loss go on a triple top?**
Many traders place it above the highest peak, where the pattern would be proven wrong. A tighter stop above the retest of the broken support risks less but is hit more easily.
