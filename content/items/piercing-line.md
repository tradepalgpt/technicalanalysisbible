The piercing line is a two-candle bullish reversal pattern at the end of a downtrend. A long bearish candle is followed by a bullish candle that opens below the previous low and closes above the middle of the first body. It shows sellers were rejected, and it needs confirmation.

## What is a piercing line?

The pattern shows a sharp change of mood. On day one sellers dominate and the candle closes near its low. On day two price opens even lower, which looks bearish. Then buyers step in and drive price back up, through the middle of the previous day's body. The bullish candle "pierces" the bearish one.

It sits between a [bullish harami](/bullish-harami/) (a pause) and a [bullish engulfing](/bullish-engulfing/) (the second candle covers the whole first body). The deeper the second candle penetrates the first body, the more convincing the signal.

## How it is identified

Nison's description has these parts:

1. **A prior downtrend.**
2. **A long bearish candle.**
3. **A bullish candle that opens below the low of the first candle.**
4. **A close above the midpoint of the first body**, but still below the first candle's open. If it closes above the open, the pair is a bullish engulfing instead.

Many platforms relax rule 3 and only require an open below the first close, because true gaps are rare in markets that trade almost around the clock, such as forex. Check which version your scanner uses.

**Worked example.** Day one opens at 110 and closes at 100 with a low of 99. The midpoint of the body is 105. Day two opens at 98 (below the low) and closes at 106. That is above 105 and below 110, so it is a piercing line.

## How to read it

**After a strong fall.** The pattern matters most after a clear decline where sellers have been winning for some time.

**At support.** A piercing line at a known support level or previous low shows the level holding.

**Depth of penetration.** The closer the close is to the first candle's open, the stronger the signal. A close only just above the midpoint is weaker.

**Confirmation.** Look for bullish follow-through. A close below the second candle's low suggests the pattern has failed.

## Best market states

**Reversals after an extended fall (best).** It describes sellers losing control at the end of a move.

**Strong trends (weak).** A strong downtrend can ignore one bullish candle.

**Ranges (weak).** Bullish candles after bearish ones are routine in sideways markets.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear fall into the pattern |
| First candle | Long bearish body |
| Second candle | Opens below the first low; closes above the first body's midpoint |
| Penetration | Deeper is stronger; above the first open makes it engulfing |
| Follow-through | A further higher close or a break above the second candle's high |

## Common mistakes

- Treating it as a buy signal without a prior downtrend.
- Accepting a close just over the midpoint as a strong signal.
- Mixing the strict opening rule (below the low) with the relaxed one (below the close).
- Ignoring that a close below the pattern's low cancels it.
- Searching for it on very short time frames, where the shape appears by chance.

## FAQ

**What does a piercing line mean?**
It means a decline met strong buying. After opening lower, price rose to close above the middle of the prior bearish candle. It warns that sellers may be losing control, but a turn is not certain.

**What is the difference between a piercing line and a bullish engulfing?**
A piercing line closes above the midpoint of the first body but below its open. A bullish engulfing closes above the first candle's open too, covering the whole body. Engulfing is usually read as stronger. See [Bullish engulfing vs Piercing line](/compare/bullish-engulfing-vs-piercing-line/).

**Does a piercing line need a gap?**
Under Nison's description, the second candle opens below the first candle's low. In markets with little gapping, such as forex, many traders accept an open below the first close.

**Is a piercing line reliable?**
No candlestick pattern works every time. Context, such as a clear downtrend, support nearby and a confirming candle, matters more than the shape.

**What is the bearish version?**
Dark cloud cover. See [Dark cloud cover](/dark-cloud-cover/).
