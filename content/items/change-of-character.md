A change of character, or CHoCH, is the first break of market structure against the current trend. In an uptrend it is a close below the last higher low. In a downtrend it is a close above the last lower high. Traders treat it as an early warning that the trend may be turning, not as proof.

## What is a change of character?

A trend is a run of higher highs and higher lows (up) or lower highs and lower lows (down). That idea comes from Dow theory and is much older than the term CHoCH. A change of character marks the first time that run breaks.

In an uptrend, price keeps making higher lows. If price closes below the most recent higher low, the pattern has been broken for the first time. That break is the CHoCH. It does not say the trend has reversed. It says the trend has stopped behaving as before.

The label is popular in smart money concept (SMC) and ICT teaching. Some teachers use CHoCH for the first break against the trend and [break of structure](/break-of-structure/) for a break with the trend. Others use "market structure shift" for a similar idea, and the terms are not used consistently. No academic study tests the term.

## How it is identified

1. **Mark swings.** Find the swing highs and lows (see [swing highs and lows](/swing-highs-lows/)).
2. **Establish a trend.** At least two higher highs and higher lows, or the reverse.
3. **Mark the key swing.** In an uptrend this is the last higher low, the one that produced the most recent high. In a downtrend it is the last lower high.
4. **Wait for the break.** A close beyond that swing, not just a wick, is the CHoCH.

**Worked example.** An uptrend makes lows at 90, 95 and 100, and highs at 105, 112 and 118. The last higher low is 100. Price falls and closes at 98. That close below 100 is a bearish CHoCH. The structure is now: highest high 118, last higher low broken. A new lower high below 118 would add weight.

## How to read it

**Warning, not signal.** A CHoCH shows the trend may be weakening. Many CHoCHs are followed by a pullback and a new high.

**Wick versus close.** Some traders count a wick through the swing, and some require a close. A close gives fewer false signals but is later.

**Follow-through.** A strong, quick break (sometimes called displacement) is treated as more meaningful than a slow drift through the level.

**Liquidity idea.** Some traders want the CHoCH to follow a sweep of a prior high or low, on the view that stops were taken before the turn. That is an opinion, not a tested finding.

**What comes next.** Traders then look for an entry on a pullback, for example into an [order block](/order-block/), rather than selling the break itself.

## Best market states

**Reversals after a clear trend (best).** The signal needs a defined trend with clear swings.

**Early trend change (moderate).** It offers an earlier warning than a moving average crossover, at the cost of more false alarms.

**Ranges (weak).** In a range price breaks recent swings in both directions all the time, so CHoCHs are meaningless noise.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | At least two clear higher highs and higher lows (or the reverse) |
| Swing | The correct swing is marked, the one that made the latest extreme |
| Break | A close beyond it, not only a wick |
| Strength | A decisive move rather than a slow drift |
| Next step | A lower high (or higher low) forms afterwards |
| Higher timeframe | Higher-timeframe trend is not strongly opposed |

## Common mistakes

- Marking the wrong swing. The signal depends on which low or high is chosen.
- Treating a single CHoCH as a trend reversal.
- Using it in ranges, where it appears constantly.
- Mixing up CHoCH and break of structure. One is against the trend, the other with it.
- Reading a wick through the level as a close.
- Treating the term as proven. It has no academic evidence base.

## FAQ

**What does CHoCH mean in trading?**
It stands for change of character. It is the first break of market structure against the current trend, such as a close below the last higher low in an uptrend. Traders read it as an early sign that the trend may be turning.

**What is the difference between CHoCH and BOS?**
A break of structure (BOS) is a break in the direction of the trend, and confirms continuation. A CHoCH is the first break against the trend, and warns of a possible reversal. Usage varies between teachers.

**Is CHoCH the same as market structure shift?**
Many traders use the two terms for the same event. Others reserve market structure shift for a CHoCH that comes with a strong, fast move. Check how your source defines each.

**Does a change of character always mean the trend has reversed?**
No. Price often breaks a swing, pulls back, and continues in the original direction. A CHoCH is a warning, and needs context and risk control.

**Does CHoCH work in ranges?**
Not well. In a sideways market price breaks both highs and lows repeatedly, so the signal fires often and means little. It is best used after a clear trend.
