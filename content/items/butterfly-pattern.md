The Butterfly pattern is a four-leg reversal shape drawn with five points, X, A, B, C and D. Its point D extends beyond the starting point X, at about 127% of the first leg. Traders look for a turn there. It is a trader-education concept with no academic evidence base.

## What is the Butterfly pattern?

The Butterfly belongs to the harmonic family, shapes in which each leg is a set Fibonacci ratio of another. It is commonly credited to Bryce Gilmore. Its shape differs from the [Gartley](/gartley-pattern/) and [Bat](/bat-pattern/) in one important way: in those patterns, D stays inside the first leg, but in the Butterfly, D goes beyond X.

A bullish Butterfly starts with a drop from X to A, rises to B, falls to C and then extends below X to D. The idea is that the extension past X exhausts the move, so a reversal is possible at D.

Because D goes beyond X, a bullish Butterfly forms a new low below the starting point. That makes it a pattern about overextension. It also means the usual stop, beyond D, is sometimes close to the entry, since D is the extreme.

Harmonic patterns lack an academic evidence base. Swing points are chosen by the analyst, and the ratios are applied with tolerance, so identification is subjective. See [harmonic patterns](/harmonic-patterns/).

## How it is drawn

A bullish Butterfly runs as follows. A bearish one is the mirror image.

1. **X to A:** the first leg, a move down.
2. **A to B:** a retracement of 0.786 of XA. B stays below X's level.
3. **B to C:** a move of 0.382 to 0.886 of AB. C stays below B.
4. **C to D:** a drop of 1.618 to 2.618 of BC.
5. **D:** at 1.27 to 1.618 of XA, below X.

**Worked example.** X is at 120 and A at 100, so XA is 20 points down. B retraces 0.786: B = 100 + (0.786 x 20) = 100 + 15.72 = 115.72. D at 1.27 of XA is 1.27 x 20 = 25.4 points from X, so D = 120 - 25.4 = **94.6**. That is below A (100), so D lies beyond the first leg.

The completion zone is where D lies. If price keeps falling past 1.618 of XA, the pattern is generally treated as failed or turned into a different shape.

## How to read it

**The completion zone.** Traders look at the area near D for signs of a reversal. The pattern does not say price will turn. It says a turn would fit the shape.

**Trigger.** Many traders wait for price action at D, such as a rejection candle or a close back inside the prior range, before acting.

**Stop.** A typical stop sits just beyond D. If price continues past 1.618 of XA, the idea is wrong.

**Targets.** Common targets are retracements of the AD leg, such as 0.382 and 0.618. These are conventions, not tested outcomes.

**Tolerance.** Real charts rarely match ratios exactly. Different practitioners accept different tolerances, which is a source of subjectivity.

**Overextension.** Because D lies beyond X, the pattern depends on a sudden new extreme. In a strong trend, that new extreme can simply be a continuation.

## Best market states

**Reversal (best, with care).** The pattern is built to catch the end of an overextended swing.

**Strong trends (weak).** A move beyond X may be a normal trend continuation, and the pattern would fail.

**Range (weak).** Small swings in sideways markets can be forced into the ratios.

**Breakout.** A move past X can look like a breakout. The pattern treats it as an exhaustion and so is opposed to a breakout approach.

**Volatility.** Volatile markets can overshoot D by a wide margin.

## Common mistakes

- Forcing swings to match the ratios and trusting the result.
- Entering at D without any confirmation from price.
- Confusing the Butterfly with the Bat or Gartley, whose D point lies inside XA.
- Ignoring that a strong trend can keep extending.
- Treating harmonic patterns as proven. Evidence for them is limited.
- Moving the stop further away when price goes beyond D.

## FAQ

**What is the Butterfly pattern in trading?**
It is a harmonic reversal shape with five points, X, A, B, C and D. Point D goes beyond the start of the pattern at X, usually at 1.27 to 1.618 of the first leg. Traders watch the area around D for a possible turn.

**What are the Butterfly pattern ratios?**
B is 0.786 of XA. C is 0.382 to 0.886 of AB. The CD leg is 1.618 to 2.618 of BC. D is at 1.27 to 1.618 of XA. These are usually applied with some tolerance.

**What is the difference between the Butterfly and the Bat?**
In the Bat, D completes inside the first leg at 0.886 of XA. In the Butterfly, D goes beyond X, at 1.27 or more of XA. That makes the Butterfly a pattern of overextension.

**Where do you put the stop on a Butterfly?**
Many traders put it just beyond point D, since a further move past 1.618 of XA would show the pattern is not working. Because D is the extreme, the stop may be relatively close to the entry.

**Is the Butterfly pattern reliable?**
There is no strong academic evidence that harmonic patterns predict reversals, and identifying one is subjective. Test it on your own market and use it as one input among several.
