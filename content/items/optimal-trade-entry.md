Optimal Trade Entry (OTE) is a retracement zone, usually 62% to 79% of the last price leg, where some traders look to enter in the direction of the trend. The term comes from the ICT (Inner Circle Trader) teaching community. It is a trader-education concept with no academic evidence base.

## What is Optimal Trade Entry?

OTE is a rule for where to buy a pullback in an uptrend, or sell a rally in a downtrend. After a strong move, price often retraces part of it. [Fibonacci retracement](/fibonacci-retracement/) shows the usual levels: 38.2%, 50%, 61.8% and 78.6%. OTE picks the deep end of that range, from roughly 62% to 79%, and calls it the "optimal" zone.

The reasoning given in ICT material is that a deep pullback lets you enter at a better price with a tighter stop behind the swing, while the trend is still intact. The 70.5% level, sometimes marked as the sweet spot, is the midpoint of the zone and is used by some traders as a single reference price.

OTE sits in the family of ideas known as [Smart Money Concepts](/smart-money-concepts/), which also include order blocks and fair value gaps. These ideas are taught widely, but they come from trader education, not from published academic studies, and there is no formal evidence that the zone performs better than other retracement levels. The term is not credited to one person here, and the exact date it entered use is not clear, so it is placed in the 2010s.

## How it is identified

1. Find a clear impulse leg: a strong move that breaks market structure. In an uptrend, that is a swing low to a swing high.
2. Draw a Fibonacci retracement from the low to the high.
3. Mark the 62% and 79% levels. Some traders also mark 70.5%.
4. Wait for price to pull back into that zone.
5. Look for a reaction, such as a rejection candle or a small shift in structure on a lower time frame, before entering.

**Worked example.** An uptrend runs from 100 up to 150, a leg of 50.

- 62% level: 150 - 0.62 x 50 = 119
- 70.5% level: 150 - 0.705 x 50 = 114.75
- 79% level: 150 - 0.79 x 50 = 110.5

The OTE zone is 110.5 to 119. A trader might look for a long entry inside it, with a stop below the swing low at 100, or below the 79% level if they accept a tighter stop. A close below 100 would invalidate the idea.

Some traders use the 61.8% and 78.6% ratios instead of 62% and 79%. The difference is small.

## How to read it

**Entry zone.** Price entering the zone is a prompt to look for a trade, not a signal on its own.

**Depth.** Price that holds the zone shows a deep but healthy pullback. Price that falls through the 79% level and heads towards the start of the leg suggests the trend is weak or over.

**Structure shift.** Some users wait for a lower-time-frame break of structure inside the zone before entering, to confirm that buyers have returned.

**Premium and discount.** In ICT teaching, the zone sits in the "discount" half of the leg in an uptrend (below 50%). See [Premium and discount](/premium-discount/).

**Targets.** Typical targets are the previous swing high or an extension beyond it.

## Best market states

**Trends (best).** OTE needs a trend with clear legs and orderly pullbacks.

**Ranges (weak).** There is no impulse leg to measure, so the zone has no meaning.

**Breakouts.** After a breakout, price may not pull back at all, or may pull back only shallowly. The deep zone may never be reached.

**Reversals.** A pullback that reaches 79% and keeps going may be the start of a reversal. The zone is an entry idea, not a reversal signal.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Impulse leg | Strong, clean move that breaks structure |
| Trend | Higher time frame agrees with the direction |
| Zone | Pullback reaches 62% to 79% of the leg |
| Reaction | Rejection candle or shift in structure at the zone |
| Confluence | Zone overlaps a support level or other structure |
| Stop | Beyond the swing that started the leg |

## Common mistakes

- Entering as soon as price touches 62%, without waiting for a reaction.
- Measuring the leg from random swings instead of a clean impulse.
- Using it in a range or against the higher-time-frame trend.
- Treating 70.5% as an exact price. It is the middle of a zone.
- Believing the concept has been proven. It has not been tested in published academic work.
- Placing a very tight stop, then being stopped out by normal noise.

## FAQ

**What is optimal trade entry?**
It is a retracement zone, usually 62% to 79% of the last leg, where some traders look for entries in the direction of the trend. The idea comes from ICT teaching and has no academic evidence behind it.

**What is the 70.5% level in OTE?**
It is the midpoint of the 62% to 79% zone. Some traders use it as a single reference price. It is not a magic number, just the centre of the zone.

**Is OTE the same as the Fibonacci golden pocket?**
They overlap. The golden pocket is usually the zone between 61.8% and 65%. OTE is a wider zone that runs deeper, up to 79%. Both are used as pullback areas.

**Does optimal trade entry work?**
No formal study supports it. Some traders find it a useful way to structure entries, but results depend on the trend, the time frame and risk control. Test it on your own market.

**Can OTE be used for short trades?**
Yes. Measure the leg from the swing high down to the swing low, then mark the zone as a rally back up into 62% to 79% of that leg.
