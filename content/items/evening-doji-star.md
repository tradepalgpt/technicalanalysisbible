An evening doji star is a three-candle pattern at the top of an uptrend. A long bullish candle is followed by a doji that gaps up, then a bearish candle that closes well into the first body. It is a variant of the evening star with a doji in the middle, and it needs confirmation.

## What is an evening doji star?

It is an [evening star](/evening-star/) where the middle candle is a [doji](/doji/) rather than a small-bodied candle. Nison treats the doji version as a more important sign of a top, because a doji shows pure indecision, with the open and close the same.

The pattern tells a short story. Buyers push hard (candle 1). Price gaps up but ends where it started, so there is no net gain (the doji). Sellers then take over (candle 3).

## How it is identified

1. **An uptrend**, ending in a long bullish candle.
2. **A doji** whose body gaps above the body of the first candle. Shadows may overlap the first candle.
3. **A bearish candle** that gaps down from the doji, or opens near it, and closes well into the first candle's body. Many traders want it to close below the midpoint.

If the doji's whole range, shadows included, sits clear of both neighbours, the pattern is an [abandoned baby](/abandoned-baby/). The evening doji star only needs the body gap.

**Worked example.** Candle 1 opens at 100 and closes at 108. Candle 2 opens at 109, trades between 108.5 and 111 and closes at 109, a doji with its body above 108. Candle 3 opens at 108.5 and closes at 102, below the midpoint of candle 1 (104). This is an evening doji star.

## How to read it

The doji is the turning point. After a strong rise, it shows buyers could not push price any higher by the end of the period. The bearish third candle shows sellers took charge.

The pattern is a warning that the advance has stalled and a pullback or reversal may follow. Traders often use the high of the doji as a reference for where the idea fails.

## Best market states

**Reversal (best).** The pattern needs a clear rise before it. Near resistance it carries more weight.

**Range (weak).** A doji and a down candle in a range is just ordinary price movement.

**Trend (weak).** Strong trends often shrug off a one-off pattern and continue.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear, extended rise |
| Doji | Open and close nearly equal, body gapped above candle 1 |
| Third candle | Bearish, closing below the midpoint of candle 1 |
| Location | Near resistance or a prior high |
| Momentum | RSI divergence or falling volume |

## Common mistakes

- Treating any small candle as a doji.
- Using it after only a short rise, or in a range.
- Accepting a weak third candle that barely enters the first body.
- Forgetting gaps are rare in 24-hour markets, and forcing the pattern.
- Acting on the doji alone, before the third candle.

## FAQ

**How is it different from an evening star?**
In an evening star the middle candle has a small body. In an evening doji star it is a doji, with the open and close equal or nearly so. Nison regards the doji version as a slightly more important signal.

**How is it different from an abandoned baby?**
An abandoned baby needs the doji's full range to gap clear of both neighbours. The evening doji star only needs the doji's body to gap above the first candle's body.

**Does the third candle have to gap down?**
Not strictly. Many definitions only ask it to close well into the first body. A gap makes the picture clearer but is not always required.

**Is it a sell signal?**
It is a warning that a rise has stalled. Most traders want a level, a momentum signal or a follow-through candle before acting.

**What is the bullish mirror?**
The [morning doji star](/morning-doji-star/), which appears after a downtrend.
