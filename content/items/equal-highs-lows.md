Equal highs and equal lows are two or more swing highs, or swing lows, at almost the same price. Smart money concept traders call the area above equal highs, or below equal lows, a liquidity pool, because many stop orders are thought to sit there. Price is often said to be drawn toward it.

## What are equal highs and equal lows?

When price turns twice at about the same level, it marks a visible high or low. Classical analysis sees that as resistance or support, or as a double top or bottom.

Smart money concept (SMC) teaching adds a different reading. Traders who buy at the lows and short at the highs usually place stop orders just beyond them. Stops above equal highs are stop-to-buy orders from short sellers, and stops below equal lows are stop-to-sell orders from long holders. The idea is that large participants may push price to that cluster to fill big orders. The cluster is called a liquidity pool.

That explanation is a theory. There is no academic evidence base for it, and we cannot see other traders' stops. What is certain is that obvious highs and lows are widely watched, and that markets often move through them. The term is also not used consistently: some say "equal" needs identical prices, others allow a few ticks or a small fraction of the average range.

## How it is identified

1. **Find swing highs or lows.** Use [swing highs and lows](/swing-highs-lows/).
2. **Compare levels.** Two or more swings should sit within a small tolerance, for example a fraction of the average true range ([ATR](/atr/)).
3. **Mark the pool.** Draw a line just above the equal highs, or just below the equal lows.
4. **Wait for the reaction.** The pool is a place to watch, not a signal on its own.

**Worked example.** Price makes swing highs at 100.2, 100.0 and 100.1, with an ATR of 2. The three highs sit within 0.2 of each other, which is 10% of ATR, so they count as equal highs. The pool is marked at 100.3. If price spikes to 101 and closes back at 99.5, that is a sweep. If price closes at 101.5 and holds, it is a breakout.

## How to read it

**Magnet or barrier.** Some traders treat the pool as a likely target: they expect price to reach it and often take profit there. This is the exit use of the idea.

**Sweep and reverse.** A wick above the highs that closes back below is read as stops being taken, then a reversal. It is a common pattern but not a reliable one.

**Break and hold.** A close beyond the level, then a retest from the other side, is read as a real breakout.

**More touches.** Three or four touches create a larger visible pool, but they also mean the level has been tested and may be weakening.

**Context.** The same level can lead to a reversal or a breakout, so traders look at [market structure](/market-structure/) and the higher timeframe.

## Best market states

**Ranges (best).** Repeated turns at a boundary create equal highs and lows naturally, and the edges are obvious targets.

**Reversals (moderate).** A sweep of the pool at the end of a trend is a common reversal story.

**Strong trends (weak).** In a clean trend, levels are cut through without pause.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Tolerance | Highs or lows within a stated small distance |
| Count | At least two, ideally three, clear touches |
| Spacing | Swings are separate, not a cluster of candles |
| Reaction | Rejection or close back inside after a sweep |
| Break | A close beyond and a hold, if trading a breakout |
| Context | Higher-timeframe trend and nearby levels |

## Common mistakes

- Demanding exact prices. Real markets rarely give them.
- Treating every pair of similar highs as a signal.
- Assuming the stop-order story is proven. It is a theory.
- Taking a short at the level, without waiting for any reaction.
- Forgetting that a break and hold is as common as a sweep.
- Using the idea in a strong trend, where it fails often.

## FAQ

**What are equal highs and equal lows?**
They are two or more swing highs, or swing lows, at about the same price. Classical analysis sees support or resistance. Smart money concept traders also see a cluster of stop orders just beyond the level, called a liquidity pool.

**What is a liquidity pool?**
It is an area where many stop orders are thought to sit, usually just beyond obvious highs or lows. The idea is that price may be pulled there. It is a trader-education theory, and stop orders cannot be seen directly.

**How equal do the highs need to be?**
There is no standard. Some traders require the same price, others accept a few ticks or a fraction of ATR. Pick a rule, write it down and apply it the same way each time.

**Do equal highs always get swept?**
No. Sometimes price turns before reaching them, and sometimes it breaks through and keeps going. The idea gives an area to watch, not a forecast.

**How do you trade equal highs and lows?**
Traders use them as exit targets, as areas where a sweep may occur, or as breakout levels. Each needs confirmation and a stop. No academic evidence shows an advantage from the liquidity explanation.
