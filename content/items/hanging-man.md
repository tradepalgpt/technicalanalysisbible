A hanging man is a single candle with a small body near the top and a long lower wick, appearing after a rise. It has the same shape as a hammer, but in an uptrend it is read as a warning. It shows sellers pushed price sharply lower during the period, even though buyers recovered most of the drop.

## What is a hanging man?

The hanging man looks exactly like a [Hammer](/hammer/), with a small body at the top and a long lower wick. The only difference is where it appears. A hammer follows a fall. A hanging man follows a rise.

In *Japanese Candlestick Charting Techniques*, Nison explains the logic. In a rally, buyers are in control. If sellers suddenly push price far below the open during one period, that shows sellers are present and willing to act. Buyers pulled the close back up, so nothing has broken yet. But the market has shown it can be hit hard, and that makes the rally look less secure.

It is one of the weaker reversal signals. The close is still near the high, so the candle itself looks fairly bullish. That is why confirmation matters more here than for most patterns.

## How it is identified

A hanging man has:

1. **A prior advance.** The shape needs a rise to be a hanging man.
2. **A small real body** near the top of the candle's range.
3. **A long lower wick**, commonly at least twice the body.
4. **Little or no upper wick.**

Colour matters little, though Nison notes that a filled (falling) body is seen as slightly more bearish than a hollow one.

**Worked example.** After a rally, a daily candle opens at 80.0, falls to 76.0 and recovers to close at 79.7. High is 80.2.
- Body = 80.0 - 79.7 = 0.3
- Lower wick = 79.7 - 76.0 = 3.7
- Upper wick = 80.2 - 80.0 = 0.2

The wick is more than twelve times the body, and it follows a rise, so it is a hanging man.

## How to read it

**A warning, not a verdict.** The candle shows that sellers had enough strength to drive price down sharply, even if only briefly. It raises the question of whether buyers can keep control.

**Confirmation is the signal.** Nison stresses that the next candle should confirm the warning. A bearish candle that opens below the hanging man's body, or closes below it, gives the idea real weight. If price instead pushes to new highs, the hanging man has failed.

**Location.** A hanging man at a known resistance level, or at the top of a long advance, is far more meaningful than one in the middle of a run.

**Reading the failure.** If the next candle closes above the hanging man's high, bulls remain in control. Treat the pattern as cancelled.

## Best market states

**Reversals (best).** After a long advance, especially one that has reached resistance, a hanging man can be an early sign of fatigue.

**Ranges (weak).** Near the top of a range it can be useful, but in the middle it means very little.

**Trends (weak).** In a healthy uptrend, shapes like this appear and are absorbed. Without confirmation, it is easy to be wrong.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior move | A clear advance into the candle |
| Shape | Long lower wick, small body near the top, tiny upper wick |
| Next candle | A bearish candle, ideally closing below the hanging man's body |
| Level | Resistance, a prior high or a trendline nearby |
| Volume | Heavy volume on the hanging man or the next candle adds weight |

## Common mistakes

- Calling a hanging man a hammer, or the reverse. The preceding trend decides the name.
- Acting on the candle alone. Its close is near the high, so on its own it looks strong.
- Ignoring location. A hanging man far from any resistance is easy to dismiss.
- Using very short time frames, where the shape appears constantly.
- Treating it as a prediction of a large fall. It is a warning that the advance may be weakening.
- Forgetting that some traders skip it entirely because of its weak record in ordinary conditions. Test it on your own market.

## FAQ

**Is a hanging man bearish?**
It is a bearish warning when it follows a rise, but a weak one on its own. The candle closes near its high, so it needs a confirming bearish candle afterwards. Without that, it is just a cautionary shape.

**How do I tell a hanging man from a hammer?**
By the trend before it. The shapes are identical. After a rise, it is a hanging man. After a fall, it is a hammer. See [Hammer vs Hanging Man](/compare/hammer-vs-hanging-man/).

**Why is it called a hanging man?**
The shape looks like a figure hanging with dangling legs: a small head and body above a long lower wick. The name is just a picture, and it carries no extra meaning.

**Does the body colour matter?**
A little. Nison suggests a filled body, where the close is below the open, is slightly more bearish than a hollow one. Both versions still need confirmation from the next candle.

**Should I sell as soon as I see one?**
That would ignore the most important rule. Most traders wait for the next candle and look at the surrounding level and trend first. A hanging man alone is too weak a signal to rely on.
