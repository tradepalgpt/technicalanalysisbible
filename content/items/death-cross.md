A death cross happens when a shorter moving average, usually the 50-period, crosses below a longer one, usually the 200-period. It is read as a sign that a long-term downtrend may be starting. It comes late, because both averages lag price, and it often appears after much of a fall has already happened.

## What is a death cross?

A death cross is the bearish mirror of the [Golden cross](/golden-cross/), and a specific case of a [Moving average crossover](/moving-average-crossover/). The fast line is the 50-period average and the slow line is the 200-period average, usually [simple moving averages](/simple-moving-average/) on a daily chart.

When the 50-day average falls through the 200-day average, the average price of the last 50 days has dropped below the average of the last 200. Recent prices are now lower than the long-term norm.

The name is market slang. It has no formal definition and makes no promise that prices will keep falling. Many people watch it, so it draws headlines when it appears on a major index. Its main weakness is timing: both averages are slow, so the cross usually shows up well after the peak, and sometimes close to a low.

## How it is identified

1. Plot a 50-period and a 200-period moving average on a daily chart.
2. Wait for the 50 to move from above the 200 to below it.
3. The cross counts when it holds on a closing basis.

**Worked example.** Suppose the 50-day average is 102 and the 200-day average is 100, so the 50 is above the 200. Over the next weeks price falls. The 50-day average slips to 99.8 while the 200-day average has eased to 100.1. The 50 is now below the 200: a death cross.

By the time this happens, price may already be well below the 200-day average. In some cases the fall has run its course and price recovers soon after the signal.

## How to read it

**Long-term trend signal.** A death cross suggests the long-term trend has turned down. It is a background reading, not a precise exit or short-entry point.

**Late by design.** Slow averages mean fewer false signals than fast pairs, but the signal comes after a large part of the move. Some traders use it only to stay out of weak markets rather than to time the top.

**Slope of the 200.** A death cross is more convincing when the 200-period average is flat or falling. If the 200 is still rising strongly, the cross may be only a deep pullback.

**Retest.** After the cross, price often rallies back toward the averages. If they act as resistance, the signal gains weight. If price climbs back above both, the cross has failed.

**Failed crosses.** A death cross followed by a golden cross a few weeks later is a whipsaw. They are most common after sharp drops that reverse quickly.

## Best market states

**Trends (best).** In a long, steady downtrend the cross keeps you on the right side. The cost is that it appears after a good part of the fall.

**Reversals (mixed).** It can confirm a trend change from up to down, but late. After a fast sell-off, price may already be bouncing.

**Ranges (weak).** In a sideways market the averages bunch together and cross in both directions. Signals are unreliable.

**Volatility.** Violent drops pull the 50 below the 200 quickly, and equally violent rebounds can pull it back, producing a rapid false signal.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a death cross as proof that prices will keep falling. It only describes past averages.
- Selling at the cross after a long fall, near what turns out to be a low.
- Using the cross on short time frames and expecting the same meaning as on the daily chart.
- Trading it in a sideways market, where it often reverses.
- Ignoring that it lags. Much of the fall may have happened already.
- Reacting to headlines about a death cross without checking price structure.

## FAQ

**What is a death cross in simple terms?**
It is when the 50-day average price falls below the 200-day average price. Traders read it as a sign that a long-term downtrend may be starting. It is a slow, late signal.

**Does a death cross mean prices will keep falling?**
Not necessarily. It shows that recent prices are lower than the long-term average. Sometimes the fall continues; sometimes the decline is already over and price recovers. It is a lagging signal, not a forecast.

**How reliable is the death cross?**
It is widely watched but lags and can fail, especially in sideways markets or after sharp, short drops. Reliability depends on the market and the period tested, so check it yourself rather than rely on headlines.

**Is a death cross a sell signal?**
It can be used as an exit or risk-reduction signal by traders who follow long-term trends. It is not a sell instruction on its own. Many want price structure and volume to agree first.

**What is the difference between a death cross and a golden cross?**
A death cross is the 50-period average crossing below the 200-period average. A golden cross is the reverse. One marks a possible long-term turn down, the other a possible turn up.
