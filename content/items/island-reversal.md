An island reversal is a chart pattern where price gaps in the direction of a trend, trades for a short time in a small cluster of bars, then gaps back the other way. The cluster sits alone like an island, with empty space on both sides. It shows that those who bought (or sold) in the final push are now trapped.

## What is an island reversal?

In an up-trend, price may gap up on a burst of enthusiasm and trade at the new higher level for a day or a few days. Then, something changes. Price gaps down, leaving the recent bars isolated above empty space on both sides. Anyone who bought inside the island is now sitting on a loss, and the new gap below shows that sellers took charge quickly.

A downward island, called an island bottom, is the mirror image: price gaps down, trades briefly, then gaps back up.

The first gap often looks like an exhaustion or runaway gap, while the second gap resembles a breakaway gap in the opposite direction. The pattern is the combination of both: an [exhaustion gap](/exhaustion-gap/) followed by a [breakaway gap](/breakaway-gap/). That is why it is seen as a strong reversal sign.

The pattern is described in the classic chart-pattern books, including Edwards and Magee's *Technical Analysis of Stock Trends*. No single person is credited with the term.

Because it needs two gaps, the pattern mostly appears in markets that close overnight, such as stocks, indices and futures. It is rare in 24-hour forex markets during the week.

## How it is identified

1. **A prior trend.** There is a clear move up or down leading into the first gap.
2. **A first gap in the trend's direction.** It leaves empty space below the island (for a top).
3. **The island.** One to several bars trade in a tight area. A single bar is sometimes called a one-day island.
4. **A second gap the other way.** Price gaps back, with its high (for a top) below the lowest low of the island. The gaps may be at similar price levels, but this is not required.

**Worked example.** A stock climbs to 100, then gaps up to open at 103. Over three days it trades between 103 and 106. On day four it gaps down, opening at 100.5 with a high of 102.5 and a low of 99.

- The first gap spans 100 to 103.
- The three-day island sits between 103 and 106.
- The second gap spans 100.5 to 103, since the day's high of 102.5 stays below the island's lowest low of 103.

The island's lowest low is 103. The bar after it has a high of 102.5, which is below 103, so the gap is valid. A trader looking for a reversal might sell short at the open of the second gap day, with a stop above the top of the island at 106.5. That risk is large, so position size would be reduced.

## How to read it

**Trapped traders.** Everyone who bought on the island is now at a loss, and some will sell to cut it. That is the standard explanation for why the move after the second gap can be sharp.

**Volume.** Heavy volume on the first gap and on the second gap strengthens the case. Volume tends to be lower on the island itself.

**Gap size.** The larger the second gap, the more decisive the reversal seems. A tiny second gap is a weaker sign.

**The gap area as resistance.** In a top, the first gap area, now below the island, may act as resistance in a retest. If price closes back above it, the pattern has failed.

**Not a forecast of distance.** There is no standard target. Some traders aim at the start of the up-move or at a nearby support, but this is a guess.

**Sparse evidence.** The pattern is rare, so claims about how often it works rest on small samples. Treat any quoted success rates cautiously.

## Best market states

**Reversals at the end of an extended move (best).** The pattern needs a trend to reverse, and it works best after a clear run that has drawn in late buyers.

**Breakouts (caution).** A gap breakout followed by a gap back can be a failed breakout. See [False breakout](/false-breakout/).

**Ranges (weak).** Gaps in ranges are common and often fill. An "island" in a sideways market is usually noise.

**Volatility.** In news-driven markets with many gaps, islands may appear often and mean less.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear, extended move before the first gap |
| First gap | Opens beyond the prior range in the trend's direction |
| Island | A short cluster of bars with no overlap with the bars on either side |
| Second gap | Opens the other way, clear of the island's extreme |
| Volume | Heavy on the gaps, lighter on the island |
| Stop | Beyond the far side of the island; a close back inside warns of failure |

## Common mistakes

- Calling any two gaps an island without checking that the bars do not overlap.
- Entering before the second gap has appeared.
- Setting a stop beyond the island and not reducing the position for the larger risk.
- Using the pattern in a sideways market with no trend to reverse.
- Quoting success rates from memory or from sources that do not give their method.
- Forgetting that markets that trade around the clock rarely have true gaps.

## FAQ

**What is an island reversal?**
It is a reversal pattern in which price gaps in the direction of a trend, trades in a small cluster, then gaps back the opposite way. The cluster is cut off from the rest of the chart by gaps on both sides, like an island.

**Is an island reversal bullish or bearish?**
Either. An island top forms after an up-trend and is bearish. An island bottom forms after a downtrend and is bullish. The direction is set by the second gap.

**How long does the island last?**
It can be a single bar or several, and occasionally longer. What matters is that the bars are isolated by gaps on both sides. There is no fixed length.

**Can I use island reversals in forex?**
Rarely during the week, as forex trades nearly around the clock and seldom leaves gaps. Weekend gaps can create one. Stocks, indices and futures that close overnight show them more often.

**How reliable is the pattern?**
No pattern works every time, and islands are rare, so reliable statistics are hard to get. Many traders use it as a strong warning, then wait for confirmation and use a defined stop.
