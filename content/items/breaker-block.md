A breaker block is an order block that has failed. Price runs through it, breaks market structure the other way, and later returns to the same zone, which is now expected to work from the opposite side. It comes from ICT and smart money concept teaching, a trader-education approach with no academic evidence base.

## What is a breaker block?

An [order block](/order-block/) is the last opposite-coloured candle before a strong move. Traders who use the idea hope price will respect that zone when it returns. A breaker block is what is left when it does not.

Suppose a bullish order block forms in an uptrend. Price later closes through it and falls, breaking a swing low. The old support zone has failed, and that failure is the signal. When price rallies back into the same zone, the idea is that it now acts as resistance. In plain terms, it is the same support-becomes-resistance principle behind [role reversal](/role-reversal/), applied to a specific candle.

The term comes from retail teaching associated with Michael Huddleston (known as ICT). It has no formal definition, and different teachers mark the zone slightly differently. No peer-reviewed study supports it.

## How it is identified

For a bearish breaker (bullish ones are the mirror image):

1. **Find a bullish order block** in an uptrend, typically the last down candle before a rally.
2. **Wait for failure.** Price closes below the block and breaks a recent swing low.
3. **Mark the block.** The zone runs from the high to the low of that candle, or from the open to the low, depending on the teacher.
4. **Wait for the retest.** Price rallies back up into the zone from below.
5. **Look for a reaction**, such as rejection candles, before acting.

**Worked example.** A down candle spans 100 to 96 before a rally to 112. Later price falls and closes at 93, breaking a swing low at 95. The block (96 to 100) is now a breaker. Price rallies back to 98, and a bearish rejection candle appears. A short with a stop at 101, above the block, risks about 3 points. A target might be the next swing low.

## How to read it

**The break is the signal.** A zone that was respected is just an order block. The value claimed for the breaker is that a failed level often flips.

**Liquidity sweep.** Many traders look for price to take a prior high or low (a liquidity sweep) before the structure break. They say this adds weight, though that is an opinion rather than a tested finding.

**The retest.** A full return into the zone is the entry area. Price may stop short, ignore it, or pass straight through.

**Invalidation.** A close back through the zone, on the old side, cancels the idea.

**Timeframe.** Zones from higher timeframes are considered stronger than zones from a one-minute chart, though there is no evidence for this.

## Best market states

**Reversals after a structure break (best).** The pattern is built on a change of direction.

**Pullbacks in a new trend (moderate).** After a [change of character](/change-of-character/), a breaker can mark where the pullback may end.

**Ranges (weak).** Zones break and flip many times, so any candle can be called a breaker.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Original block | A clear last-opposite candle before a strong move |
| Failure | A close through the block, not just a wick |
| Structure | A swing high or low is broken in the new direction |
| Retest | Price returns into the zone |
| Reaction | Rejection candle or lower-timeframe shift |
| Stop | Beyond the far side of the zone |

## Common mistakes

- Calling any broken zone a breaker. It needs a structure break as well.
- Marking the zone after the move, choosing candles that fit.
- Entering as price first touches the zone, with no sign of reaction.
- Expecting a retest every time. Many reversals never return.
- Placing the stop inside the zone, where noise reaches it.
- Treating the concept as proven. It has no academic evidence base.

## FAQ

**What is a breaker block?**
It is a failed order block. Price breaks through the zone and then breaks market structure the other way. When price comes back to the zone, traders expect it to act from the opposite side, as new resistance or support.

**What is the difference between an order block and a breaker block?**
An order block is a zone expected to hold. A breaker block is the same zone after it failed and structure broke. The failure turns it from a possible support into a possible resistance, or the reverse.

**Is the breaker block the same as support and resistance flipping?**
The principle is the same: a broken level can change role. The breaker adds a specific rule for drawing the zone, from a single candle, and a requirement that structure breaks. Traditional role reversal usually uses a horizontal level.

**Who invented the breaker block?**
It comes from retail smart money concept teaching associated with ICT. No single inventor is documented, and the definition varies between teachers.

**Does the breaker block work?**
No academic evidence supports it. It is a trader-education concept, and results depend on how zones are picked and on risk control. Many retests fail, and many reversals do not retest at all.
