Fibonacci extension projects price targets beyond the end of a prior swing, using ratios such as 127.2%, 161.8% and 261.8%. After a pullback inside a trend, traders measure the earlier move and extend it from the pullback low or high. The levels are used to plan where to take profit, not to predict where price will go.

## What is Fibonacci extension?

A trend moves in legs: a push, a pullback, then another push. [Fibonacci retracement](/fibonacci-retracement/) estimates how deep the pullback might go. Fibonacci extension estimates how far the next push might travel, as a multiple of the first.

If the first push was 100 points and the extension is 161.8%, the target sits 161.8 points beyond the start of the second push. The levels beyond 100% come from the Fibonacci sequence. Dividing a number in the sequence by the number two places earlier approaches 2.618, and dividing by the one before approaches 1.618. The 127.2% level is the square root of 1.618.

Some platforms offer both an "extension" tool using two points (a swing and the retracement), and a "projection" tool using three points. Names are used inconsistently between platforms, so check which one your charting package draws. This page covers the three-point version, where the extension is projected from the end of a pullback. See [Fibonacci projection](/fibonacci-projection/) for the related method.

As with all Fibonacci tools, there is no proven reason markets should stop at these levels. They are areas where many traders look, so reactions often cluster there.

## How it is drawn

1. Pick a clear swing in the trend direction: point A (start) to point B (end).
2. Identify the pullback that follows: point C (the end of the retracement).
3. Select the three-point Fibonacci extension tool and click A, then B, then C.
4. The tool projects levels from C, based on the length of the A to B move.

**Worked example.** An uptrend runs from A = 100 to B = 150: a swing of 50. Price pulls back to C = 125.
- 100% extension: 125 + 50 = 175
- 127.2% extension: 125 + 63.6 = 188.6
- 161.8% extension: 125 + 80.9 = 205.9
- 261.8% extension: 125 + 130.9 = 255.9

In a downtrend, the levels are projected downward from C instead.

Some traders draw extensions with only two points (the swing, extended past its end). The resulting levels differ, so be clear which method is in use.

## How to read it

**100% level.** The second leg equals the first. This is the same idea as a [measured move](/measured-move/) and is the most common first target.

**127.2%.** A modest extension. Often used as a first or conservative target in trends that are slowing.

**161.8%.** The best-known extension and a frequent target. Many traders take part or all of a position off here.

**200% and 261.8%.** Larger targets in strong trends. Reaching them is less common, and traders often trail a stop rather than rely on them.

**Zones and confluence.** An extension level that coincides with prior resistance, a round number or another Fibonacci tool is more significant. See [Fibonacci confluence](/fibonacci-confluence/).

**Trend change.** If price stalls at a level and then breaks back through the start of the second leg, the projection has failed.

## Best market states

**Trends (best).** The tool needs a clear impulsive move and a pullback. It fits steady trends with orderly legs.

**Breakouts (useful).** After a breakout from a range, extensions of the range height give a first idea of where price may run.

**Ranges (weak).** With no trend there is no leg to extend, and the targets are just lines on a chart.

**Volatility.** In fast markets, price can overshoot levels, so exits are often planned as zones.

## Settings

| Setting | Levels | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 100%, 127.2% | Targets that are reached more often; smaller reward |
| Standard | 161.8% | The most watched extension; a common main target |
| Aggressive | 200%, 261.8% | Larger targets in strong trends; reached less often |

The levels are fixed ratios, so the main choices are which swing to use and which level to treat as the target. Most platforms let you add or remove levels. Avoid adding so many that every price has a level nearby.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a level as a certain turning point.
- Choosing the swing after the fact so that the levels line up.
- Using extensions in a sideways market.
- Setting a target without a stop. The projection says nothing about risk.
- Confusing the two-point and three-point versions and mixing up the levels.
- Holding for the 261.8% target in a trend that is clearly slowing.

## FAQ

**What are the Fibonacci extension levels?**
The common levels are 100%, 127.2%, 161.8%, 200% and 261.8%. Platforms may also show others such as 138.2% or 423.6%. The 161.8% level is the most widely watched.

**How do you draw a Fibonacci extension?**
Pick a swing from A to B, then the pullback end at C. Select the three-point extension tool and click A, B, then C. The tool projects levels from C using the length of the A to B move.

**What is the difference between Fibonacci extension and retracement?**
Retracement measures how far a pullback might go back into the previous move. Extension measures how far the next move might go past the previous extreme. See [Fibonacci retracement vs extension](/compare/fibonacci-retracement-vs-extension/).

**Is 161.8% the best target?**
It is the most popular, which gives it some weight, but it is not reliably better than other levels. Whether it fits depends on the trend and nearby levels. Test targets on your market.

**Does the extension tell me when to exit?**
It gives possible areas to consider taking profit. It does not say price will stop there. Many traders scale out at levels and trail a stop on the remainder.
