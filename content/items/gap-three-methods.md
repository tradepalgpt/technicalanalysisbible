Upside gap three methods is a three-candle continuation pattern. In an uptrend, a bullish candle is followed by a second bullish candle that gaps above it, and then a bearish candle opens inside the second body and closes back inside the first, filling the gap. The downside version is the mirror image. It suggests the trend is pausing, not ending.

## What is the gap three methods pattern?

The pattern shows a trend with a gap in it that is then closed. In an uptrend, buyers push price up and leave a gap. A bearish candle then pulls price back and shuts the gap. If the trend is healthy, buyers step in again and the move continues.

Steve Nison explained the pattern in the Western literature in his 1991 book *Japanese Candlestick Charting Techniques*. Nison treats it as a continuation pattern in which the filling of the gap does not by itself break the trend. Gaps are often seen as support, so closing one is less bullish than leaving it open, which is why many traders regard this pattern as a modest signal compared with others. See [Tasuki gap](/tasuki-gap/) for a similar pattern where the gap is not filled.

## How it is identified

The upside version has three candles:

1. **A long bullish candle**, in an uptrend.
2. **A second bullish candle** that opens above the first candle's close, leaving a gap between the two bodies.
3. **A bearish candle** that opens inside the body of the second candle and closes inside the body of the first, so the gap is closed.

The downside version mirrors this: two bearish candles with a gap down, then a bullish candle that opens inside the second body and closes inside the first.

**Worked example (upside).** Candle 1 opens at 100 and closes at 108. Candle 2 opens at 110 and closes at 114, leaving a gap from 108 to 110. Candle 3 opens at 113 and closes at 107. It opens within candle 2's body (110 to 114) and closes within candle 1's body (100 to 108), so the gap is filled. The pattern is complete.

Definitions vary. Some sources count the gap as the space between the shadows, others only between the bodies. Platforms and scanners differ.

## How to read it

**Pause.** The gap-fill shows a pullback. If it stays inside the first candle's body, the market has retraced only as far as the start of the gap.

**Resumption.** The pattern is confirmed when the next candle or two close in the trend direction, above the high of candle 2 in the upside version.

**Warning.** A close below the first candle's low means the pullback went too far and the pattern has failed.

**Weight.** Nison and others note that this pattern is less compelling than those where the gap stays open. Treat it as one piece of evidence.

## Best market states

**Established trends (best).** The pattern needs a trend with gaps in it to make sense.

**Ranges (weak).** Without a trend, there is nothing for the pattern to continue.

**Reversals (poor).** A gap that is fully closed is sometimes the start of a reversal, so the pattern can fail there.

**Markets without gaps.** Round-the-clock markets such as forex and many crypto pairs rarely show real gaps, so the pattern appears seldom.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear trend in the direction of the first two candles |
| Gap | A visible gap between candle 1 and candle 2 |
| Close of the gap | Candle 3 closes inside the body of candle 1 |
| Follow-through | Next candle closes beyond candle 2's extreme in the trend direction |
| Volume | Higher on the trend candles, lower on the pullback (a common guide) |
| Stop | Beyond the low of candle 1 for the upside version |

## Common mistakes

- Treating the pattern as a reversal because the gap closed.
- Trading it in a sideways market.
- Ignoring that definitions differ about bodies and shadows.
- Not waiting for follow-through after the third candle.
- Using it in markets that rarely gap and then relying on a few examples.

## FAQ

**Is gap three methods bullish or bearish?**
The upside version is a bullish continuation pattern, and the downside version is a bearish continuation pattern. Both suggest the trend will resume after a pullback that fills the gap.

**How is it different from rising three methods?**
Rising three methods has five candles and no gap. Upside gap three methods has three candles and a gap that the third candle closes. Both are continuation patterns.

**How is it different from a tasuki gap?**
In a tasuki gap, the third candle closes inside the gap without filling it, which is more positive for the trend. In gap three methods, the third candle fills the gap completely.

**Is the gap three methods pattern reliable?**
Candlestick patterns are not proven predictors by themselves, and this one is regarded as a modest signal. It needs a clear trend, follow-through and risk control. Test it on your market before relying on it.

**Does it work in forex and crypto?**
It is rare there, because round-the-clock markets seldom gap. It appears more often in stocks and futures that close overnight.
