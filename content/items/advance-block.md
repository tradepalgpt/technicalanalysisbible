An advance block is a three-candle pattern in an uptrend. It looks like three white soldiers, but the climb is losing force: the bodies shrink or the upper wicks grow. It warns that buyers are tiring. It is a warning of a possible stall, and many traders wait for a bearish candle before acting on it.

## What is an advance block?

Three advancing candles in a row look strong. The advance block is a case where the detail says otherwise. Each candle still closes higher, but buyers are getting less for their effort, and sellers are pushing back inside each period.

The name comes from the idea that the advance is "blocked", or held back, by supply. It is related to [three white soldiers](/three-white-soldiers/), which is the healthy version, and to the [deliberation pattern](/deliberation-pattern/), a close cousin.

## How it is identified

Three consecutive bullish (white or green) candles in an uptrend, with:

1. **Each close higher than the last.**
2. **Each open within the body of the previous candle**, rather than gapping above it.
3. **Fading strength**, shown by one or both of:
   - Bodies that get smaller from the first to the third candle.
   - Upper shadows that get longer, especially on the second and third candles.

Rules vary. Some sources want both signs of weakness, others accept either, so check how your charting software defines it.

**Worked example.** Candle 1 opens at 100 and closes at 106, with a small upper wick. Candle 2 opens at 103 and closes at 108, with an upper wick up to 110. Candle 3 opens at 106 and closes at 109, with an upper wick up to 113. Closes rise (106, 108, 109), bodies shrink (6, 5, 3), and upper wicks grow. This is an advance block.

## How to read it

The long upper wicks show that buyers pushed higher during each period and were then forced back. The shrinking bodies show the same thing: less net progress each candle.

It does not say the trend has ended. It says the advance is less convincing than it looks. Traders use it to tighten stops, take some profit, or watch for a bearish candle that confirms a turn. A bearish candle that closes below the third candle's body adds weight.

Acting on it before then means betting against an uptrend that is still making higher closes.

## Best market states

**Reversal (best).** A mature uptrend, especially near resistance, is where the pattern has most meaning.

**Early in a trend (poor).** Shrinking candles can simply be a pause before the next leg.

**Range (weak).** Three rising candles in a sideways market are just a swing inside the range.

**Breakout (weak).** A breakout can start with candles of uneven size and still carry on.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | An established rise before the pattern |
| Weakness | Shrinking bodies, longer upper wicks, or both |
| Location | Near resistance or a prior high |
| Next candle | A bearish candle closing below the third body |
| Momentum | RSI divergence or a failing volume trend |

## Common mistakes

- Treating any three rising candles with a long wick as an advance block.
- Selling at once without waiting for a bearish candle.
- Using it early in a new trend, where pauses are normal.
- Confusing it with three white soldiers, which has strong, similar bodies.
- Ignoring that definitions differ between sources.

## Pairs well with

- **[RSI](/rsi/)**: a high or diverging reading supports the idea of fading strength.
- **[Volume](/volume/)**: falling volume across the three candles adds weight.

- **[Divergence](/divergence/)**: price making higher closes with weaker momentum fits the pattern's story.

## FAQ

**How is an advance block different from three white soldiers?**
Three white soldiers have long, similar bodies and short wicks, which shows steady buying. An advance block shows the same three rising closes with shrinking bodies or long upper wicks, which shows the buying is fading.

**Is an advance block bearish?**
It is a warning, not a bearish signal on its own. It says an advance is losing force. Most traders look for a bearish candle after it before treating it as a possible turn.

**Do I need both shrinking bodies and long wicks?**
Sources differ. Some accept either sign of weakness, some want both. Pick one definition and apply it consistently so you can review your results fairly.

**Does the pattern have a bearish mirror?**
Not under this name. The standard description covers uptrends only. Some traders look for a similar fade in falling markets, but it has no widely used name.

**Does it work in forex?**
It can appear in any market. Volume-based confirmation is less available in spot forex, so traders there lean on location and momentum readings.
