A gravestone doji is a candlestick with open, low and close at or near the same price and a long upper wick, shaped like an upside-down "T". Price rose sharply, then fell all the way back. After a rise or at resistance it can warn that buyers are being rejected, but it needs confirmation.

## What is a gravestone doji?

A gravestone is a type of [doji](/doji/): the open and close are equal or nearly equal, so the body is a thin line. The shape is what defines it. The candle's low sits at the open and close, and a long wick rises above them.

The story is simple. During the period, buyers pushed price well higher. By the close, sellers had pushed it all the way back to where it began. The buyers' effort was rejected. That is why traders watch for the candle at the top of a rise.

Its shape is the same as a [shooting star](/shooting-star/) with almost no body, and the two are read in much the same way.

## How it is identified

A gravestone doji has:

1. **Open and close at or near the same price**, usually a body below about 5 to 10% of the candle's range.
2. **Open and close at or near the low**, so there is little or no lower wick.
3. **A long upper wick**, making up most of the candle's range.

**Worked example.** A candle opens at 80.00, rises to 82.00, falls back and closes at 80.00 with a low of 79.95. Range = 2.05. Body = 0, lower wick = 0.05, upper wick = 2.00. About 98% of the range is upper wick, so it is a gravestone doji.

## How to read it

**After a rise.** A gravestone after several up candles suggests buying pressure may be fading.

**At resistance.** Strongest where price pushed into a known resistance level and was turned back. The wick shows the level absorbing the buying.

**The next candle matters.** A strong bearish candle after it, closing below its low, is a better sign than the gravestone alone. A close above the gravestone's high means the idea has failed.

**After a fall.** At the bottom of a downtrend it is less clear. Nison noted that its meaning depends on location, so treat it with caution there.

## Best market states

**Reversals at resistance (best).** Buyers rejected at a clear level is the pattern's main story.

**Strong uptrends (weak).** Price can dip after a gravestone and then continue higher.

**Ranges (weak).** In quiet, sideways markets the shape appears by chance.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Context | A rise or a clear resistance level |
| Shape | Tiny body at the bottom, long upper wick, almost no lower wick |
| Next candle | A bearish close below the gravestone's low |
| Volume | Higher volume on the rejection adds weight (where volume is reliable) |
| Stop idea | Above the gravestone's high; a break above means the idea is wrong |

## Common mistakes

- Selling the gravestone itself without waiting for the next candle.
- Ignoring context. A gravestone in the middle of a range means little.
- Accepting candles with real bodies or long lower wicks as gravestones.
- Forgetting that in a strong uptrend a gravestone often marks only a pause.
- Reading it on very short time frames where the shape appears constantly.

## Pairs well with

## FAQ

**Is a gravestone doji bearish?**
It is often read as bearish after a rise or at resistance, because buyers were rejected. It is not a signal by itself. Its meaning depends on where it forms, and the next candle usually decides.

**What is the difference between a gravestone doji and a shooting star?**
A shooting star has a small body near the low and a long upper wick. A gravestone has almost no body at all, with open and close at the low. Both show rejection of higher prices. See [Shooting star](/shooting-star/).

**What does a gravestone doji mean at the bottom of a downtrend?**
It is less clear. Price pushed up and failed, which can hint that sellers are still in charge. Treat it as unconfirmed and wait for the next candle.

**How long should the upper wick be?**
There is no fixed rule. Many traders look for a wick that makes up most of the candle's range, with little or no lower wick. Choose a rule and stay with it.

**What is a dragonfly doji?**
It is the opposite shape: a long lower wick with open and close at the high. See [Dragonfly doji](/dragonfly-doji/).
