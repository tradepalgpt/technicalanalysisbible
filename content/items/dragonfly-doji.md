A dragonfly doji is a candlestick with open, high and close at or near the same price and a long lower wick, shaped like a "T". Price fell sharply, then recovered fully. After a decline or at support it can hint that sellers are being rejected, but it needs confirmation.

## What is a dragonfly doji?

A dragonfly is a type of [doji](/doji/): the open and close are equal or nearly equal, so the body is a thin line. What sets it apart is the shape. The candle's high is at the same level as the open and close, and a long wick runs down from them.

The story the candle tells is simple. During the period sellers pushed price well lower. By the close, buyers had pushed it all the way back. The sellers' effort was rejected. That rejection is why traders watch for it at the bottom of a fall.

It looks the same as a [hammer](/hammer/) with almost no body, and the two are read in a similar way. Context decides the meaning, as with the [hanging man](/hanging-man/) after a rise.

## How it is identified

A dragonfly doji has:

1. **Open and close at or near the same price**, usually with a body below about 5 to 10% of the candle's range.
2. **Open and close at or near the high**, so there is little or no upper wick.
3. **A long lower wick**, often at least about two or three times the size of a typical body or most of the candle's range.

**Worked example.** A candle opens at 50.00, falls to 48.00, rallies back and closes at 50.00 with a high of 50.05. Range = 2.05. Body = 0, upper wick = 0.05 and lower wick = 2.00, so about 98% of the range is lower wick. That is a dragonfly doji.

## How to read it

**After a fall.** A dragonfly after several down candles suggests selling pressure may be easing.

**At support.** Strongest where price has dropped into a known support level and bounced. The wick shows the level absorbing the selling.

**The next candle matters.** A strong bullish candle after the dragonfly, closing above its high, is a stronger sign than the dragonfly alone. A close below the dragonfly's low is a failure.

**After a rise.** If it appears at the top of an uptrend, some traders read it as a warning that the move is stretched. Nison noted that its meaning depends on location. Treat it with caution.

## Best market states

**Reversals at support (best).** Sellers rejected at a clear level is the pattern's main story.

**Strong downtrends (weak).** Price can bounce briefly off a dragonfly and then keep falling.

**Ranges (weak).** In quiet, sideways markets, it appears by chance and tells you little.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Context | A decline or a clear support level |
| Shape | Tiny body at the top, long lower wick, almost no upper wick |
| Next candle | A bullish close above the dragonfly's high |
| Volume | Higher volume on the rejection adds weight (where volume is reliable) |
| Stop idea | Below the dragonfly's low; a break of it means the idea is wrong |

## Common mistakes

- Buying the dragonfly itself without waiting for the next candle.
- Ignoring context. A dragonfly in the middle of a range means little.
- Accepting candles with large bodies or long upper wicks as dragonflies.
- Forgetting that a dragonfly in a strong downtrend often marks only a pause.
- Reading it on very short time frames where the shape appears constantly.

## Pairs well with

## FAQ

**Is a dragonfly doji bullish?**
It is often read as bullish after a fall or at support, because sellers were rejected. It is not a signal on its own. Its meaning depends on where it forms, and the next candle usually decides.

**What is the difference between a dragonfly doji and a hammer?**
A hammer has a small body, possibly with a short upper wick. A dragonfly has almost no body at all, with open and close at the high. Both show rejection of lower prices. See [Hammer](/hammer/).

**What does a dragonfly doji mean at the top of an uptrend?**
It is less clear. Some traders see it as a warning, since the market had to recover from a big drop in the session. Others treat it as bullish. Wait for the next candle rather than guess.

**How long should the lower wick be?**
There is no fixed rule. Many traders look for a wick that makes up most of the candle's range, with a tiny or no upper wick. Pick a rule and apply it consistently.

**What is a gravestone doji?**
It is the opposite shape: a long upper wick with open and close at the low. See [Gravestone doji](/gravestone-doji/).
