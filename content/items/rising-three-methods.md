Rising three methods is a five-candle bullish continuation pattern. A long bullish candle is followed by three small candles that stay within its range, then another long bullish candle closing at a new high. It shows an uptrend pausing, not ending, before it continues.

## What is rising three methods?

The pattern shows a trend taking a breath. A strong bullish candle shows buyers in charge. Then three small candles drift lower in a mild pullback, but none of them breaks out of the first candle's range. Sellers tried and failed to do real damage. A final long bullish candle shows the buyers are back and the trend resumes.

Nison explained the idea as a pause in which the small candles show the market is resting, while the pullback is too weak to threaten the trend. It is a continuation pattern, so it works with the trend, unlike most candlestick patterns.

## How it is identified

The classic version has five candles:

1. **A long bullish candle**, in an uptrend.
2. **Three small candles** (usually bearish, but colour is flexible), each with a body inside the range of the first candle. They tend to move down in a mild pullback.
3. **A fifth long bullish candle** that opens above the previous close and closes above the close of the first candle.

Some sources count wicks as part of the "inside the range" test; others only count the bodies. Platforms and scanners differ.

**Worked example.** Candle 1 opens at 100 and closes at 110 (high 111, low 99). Candles 2 to 4 trade between 102 and 109, with closes at 107, 105 and 106. Candle 5 opens at 107 and closes at 114. The small candles stayed inside 99 to 111, and the last close (114) is above 110, so this is rising three methods.

## How to read it

**Trend pause.** The small candles show a pause. The shorter and calmer they are, the clearer the message.

**Volume.** Ideally volume is higher on the long candles and lower on the small pullback candles, which shows the pullback lacked conviction. This is a common guideline, not a rule.

**Confirmation.** The fifth candle closing above the first close is what completes the pattern. If price closes below the first candle's low during the pause, it fails.

**Use.** Traders use it as evidence that a pullback is routine and the trend is intact, for example to stay with a position or time an entry on the break above the pattern.

## Best market states

**Established uptrends (best).** It needs a trend to continue.

**Ranges (weak).** Sideways markets contain many small candles within bigger ones, so the pattern appears by chance and means little.

**Reversals (weak).** If the pullback candles break the first candle's low, the pattern has failed.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear uptrend before candle 1 |
| First candle | Long bullish body |
| Middle candles | Three small candles inside the first range |
| Last candle | Long bullish; closes above candle 1's close |
| Volume | Lower on the pause, higher on the long candles |
| Failure | A close below the low of the first candle |

## Common mistakes

- Counting any pullback as rising three methods without checking the first candle's range.
- Forgetting that the final candle must close above the first one's close.
- Expecting exactly three candles every time. Some traders accept two to four small candles, though the classic form has three.
- Using it against the larger trend.
- Entering the pause and ignoring that it can still fail.

## FAQ

**What does rising three methods mean?**
It means an uptrend paused and then resumed. A strong candle was followed by three small candles that stayed inside its range, then another strong bullish candle. The small candles show the pullback was weak.

**Is rising three methods bullish?**
Yes. It is a bullish continuation pattern, so it works with an existing uptrend. It does not signal a new trend on its own.

**Do the three small candles have to be bearish?**
Classically they are small and mostly bearish, which makes the pullback look real. Many sources allow other colours as long as the bodies stay inside the first candle's range.

**How reliable is rising three methods?**
No pattern works every time. It is rare, and its value depends on a clear trend, a quiet pause and a decisive final candle. Treat it as supporting evidence, not a stand-alone signal.

**What is the bearish version?**
Falling three methods. See [Falling three methods](/falling-three-methods/).
