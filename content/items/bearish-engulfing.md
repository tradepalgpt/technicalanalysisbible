A bearish engulfing pattern is two candles after a rise. The first is a small rising candle, and the second is a falling candle whose body fully covers the first one's body. It shows sellers took over from buyers in a single period, and it can mark the start of a pullback or a turn down.

## What is a bearish engulfing pattern?

It is the mirror of the [Bullish Engulfing](/bullish-engulfing/) pattern. On day one, buyers are still in charge and the candle closes higher. On day two, price opens higher, possibly extending the rise, but sellers push it all the way down to close below the previous day's open. The second body covers the first.

Nison describes it in *Japanese Candlestick Charting Techniques*. The point is a shift in control. A market that has been rising shows sellers with more force than buyers had a day earlier.

Like its bullish twin, the pattern is well known and often over-read. Many examples do not lead to a turn, so context does most of the work.

## How it is identified

A bearish engulfing pattern has:

1. **A prior advance.** It needs a rise to reverse.
2. **A first candle with a rising body** (close above open), usually small.
3. **A second candle with a falling body** (close below open).
4. **The second body fully covers the first.** The second open is at or above the first close, and the second close is at or below the first open.

Strict versions compare bodies only. Looser or stricter versions may also look at the wicks. Say which you use.

**Worked example.** Day one opens at 200.0 and closes at 201.5 (rising, body of 1.5). Day two opens at 201.8, falls and closes at 199.4.
- Day two open (201.8) is above day one close (201.5).
- Day two close (199.4) is below day one open (200.0).
- Day two body = 2.4, larger than day one's 1.5.

The second body covers the first, so this is a bearish engulfing pattern.

## How to read it

**Size of the engulfing candle.** A second candle much larger than the first shows a decisive shift. A candle that only just covers a tiny first candle is weaker.

**Location.** At a known resistance level, or after a long run up, the pattern carries more meaning. In the middle of an uptrend with nothing above, it may be a brief dip.

**The close.** A close near the low of the second candle shows sellers kept control through the end of the period.

**The next candle.** A following candle that stays below the pattern supports the idea. A close back above the second candle's open shows the shift failed.

**Stop reference.** Some traders place a protective stop just above the higher of the two highs, since a break over that level cancels the idea. That is a common method, not advice.

## Best market states

**Reversals (best).** After a long rise into resistance, the pattern can mark the point where buyers lose control.

**Ranges (weak).** At the top of a range it can help. Elsewhere in a range, engulfing candles are frequent and mostly noise.

**Trends (weak).** In a strong uptrend, a bearish engulfing candle is often a brief pullback before the rise resumes.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior move | A clear advance into the pattern |
| Shape | Second falling body fully covers the first rising body |
| Size | Second candle clearly larger than the first |
| Level | Resistance, a prior high or a trendline nearby |
| Volume | Higher volume on the second candle adds weight |
| Next candle | Stays below the pattern rather than recovering above it |

## Common mistakes

- Calling any large falling candle after a rising one an engulfing pattern without checking the bodies.
- Treating the pattern as a sell signal without regard for trend or level.
- Using it in sideways markets, where it appears constantly.
- Mixing up body-only and full-range definitions across charts.
- Entering late, after the second candle has already fallen a long way, which leaves a wide stop.
- Expecting every example to work. Many engulfing patterns lead nowhere.

## FAQ

**Is a bearish engulfing pattern reliable?**
It is widely watched but not certain. Many examples fail, particularly in sideways or strongly rising markets. It works best after a clear advance at a known resistance level, with confirmation. Test it on your own market first.

**Do the wicks need to be engulfed?**
Nison defines it by the bodies, so the wicks need not be covered. Some traders use a stricter rule requiring the whole range to be covered. Choose one rule and apply it the same way every time.

**What is the difference between a bearish engulfing and a dark cloud cover?**
A dark cloud cover opens above the previous high and closes below the midpoint of the previous rising body, but not below its open. A bearish engulfing closes below the whole body, so it is the stronger version. See [Dark Cloud Cover](/dark-cloud-cover/).

**Does it mean the trend has reversed?**
Not by itself. It shows sellers had the upper hand for one period. A real reversal needs follow-through, such as lower closes and a break of nearby support.

**What time frame works best?**
The shape means the same on any time frame, but daily and higher charts usually carry more weight. On very short time frames engulfing candles appear often and are less meaningful.
