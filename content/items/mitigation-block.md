A mitigation block is an ICT and smart money term for a zone that forms when a trend fails to make a new extreme and then breaks structure. The last candle or candles before the break are marked, and traders watch for price to return to them and react. It is a trader-education concept with no academic evidence base.

## What is a mitigation block?

A mitigation block is one of several zone ideas taught in smart money concepts. The word "mitigation" means that large participants who are caught in losing positions return to the price area to exit or reduce them. That is the teaching's explanation, and it is an interpretation that has not been tested in academic work.

The block is similar to an [order block](/order-block/) and a [breaker block](/breaker-block/). The way teachers separate them is by what happened before the break:

- A **breaker block** forms after price sweeps a high or low (takes liquidity) and then reverses and breaks structure.
- A **mitigation block** forms when price fails to take that extreme. The trend stalls short of a new high or low, then structure breaks.

Teachers' definitions differ in detail, and some use the terms interchangeably. Treat the labels with caution and focus on the rules you can state clearly.

## How it is identified

Take a bearish case, after an uptrend:

1. Price makes a swing high, pulls back and makes a lower high. It fails to make a new high.
2. Price then breaks below the last swing low. This is a [market structure shift](/market-structure-shift/).
3. Mark the last up-candle or small group of up-candles before the break. This is the bearish mitigation block.
4. Wait for price to rally back to the zone and watch for a reaction.

The bullish case is the mirror: a failure to make a new low, a break above the last swing high, and the last down-candle before the break as the zone.

**Worked example.** An uptrend peaks at 120. Price pulls back to 110, rallies to 117 (a lower high) and then falls and closes at 108, below 110.

- The failure to reach 120 and the close below 110 is a structure shift.
- The last up-candle before the fall had a body from 114 to 117.
- The zone is 114 to 117.
- If price returns to the zone, a short entry is considered, with the stop just above the zone's high at 117, for example at 118.

The exact candle chosen differs between teachers, and some use the whole body, some include wicks.

## How to read it

**Retest.** The expectation is that price returns to the zone and is rejected. A reaction, such as a rejection candle, is the usual trigger for an entry.

**Strength of the break.** A break with [displacement](/displacement/) is seen as more meaningful than a slow break.

**Premium and discount.** A bearish block in the upper half of the range (premium) is considered better placed than one in the lower half. See [premium and discount](/premium-discount/).

**Failure.** If price closes beyond the zone, the idea has failed and the stop is hit. Many zones are never retested, or are retested and broken.

## Best market states

**Reversals (best).** The zone is built to catch a change from one trend to another.

**Trends (caution).** In a strong trend, a pullback that looks like a structure break may only be a pause, and the zone fails.

**Ranges (weak).** Sideways markets break small swing points all the time, so many "blocks" form with no meaning.

**Volatility.** Fast markets make zones wide and the stop large.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Failure | Price fails to make a new extreme before the break |
| Break | A close beyond the swing point, ideally with displacement |
| Zone | A clear last candle or group, not a tiny one |
| Retest | Price returns to the zone and shows rejection |
| Stop | Beyond the zone or the failed extreme |
| Context | The bigger trend or a higher time frame level supports the idea |

## Common mistakes

- Marking a block on every structure break.
- Mixing up mitigation, breaker and order blocks without a written rule.
- Entering at the first touch with no reaction.
- Ignoring the higher time frame trend.
- Changing which candle counts as the block after the fact.

## FAQ

**What is a mitigation block?**
It is a smart money zone marked at the last candle or candles before a break of structure, when the trend failed to make a new extreme. Traders watch for price to return to it and react.

**What is the difference between a mitigation block and a breaker block?**
In the common teaching, a breaker forms after price takes liquidity beyond a high or low, and a mitigation block forms when price fails to take that extreme. Teachers define them differently, and some use the terms interchangeably.

**What does mitigation mean in ICT?**
It refers to large participants returning to a price area to reduce or exit positions that are losing. This is an explanation from the teaching, and no academic evidence supports it.

**How do you trade a mitigation block?**
A common approach is to wait for price to return to the zone, look for a rejection, and place the stop beyond the zone. Write the rules down and test them on past charts before using real money.

**Does a mitigation block always get retested?**
No. Price often moves away without returning, or returns and breaks through. The zone is a possible area of interest, not a level that must hold.
