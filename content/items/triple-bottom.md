A triple bottom is a reversal chart pattern that forms at the end of a downtrend. Price tests the same support level three times and holds each time, leaving three lows at about the same height. A close above the highest peak between the lows is the usual signal that the downtrend has ended and a rise may follow.

## What is a triple bottom?

A triple bottom shows sellers failing three times at the same floor. Price falls to a low, rebounds, falls to about the same low, rebounds again, then makes a third attempt that also holds. Each hold at support shows that buyers are willing to step in at that price.

The pattern is related to the [double bottom](/double-bottom/) and the [inverse head and shoulders](/inverse-head-and-shoulders/), and is less common than either. It also looks like a [rectangle](/rectangle-pattern/), which some analysts treat as neutral until it breaks. The prior downtrend and the break of resistance are what make a triple bottom bullish.

## How it is drawn

A valid pattern needs these parts, in order:

1. **A prior downtrend.** Without one there is nothing to reverse.
2. **Three lows** at about the same level. Exact matches are rare; a small band is normal.
3. **Two peaks** between the lows.
4. **Resistance line:** a horizontal line through the higher of the two peaks.
5. **Break:** a close above that line.

**Measured target.** Measure the vertical distance from the resistance line to the lows. Add that distance to the breakout point.

**Worked example.** The lows are at 40 and the resistance line through the peaks is at 50: a height of 10. If price closes above resistance at 51, the measured target is 51 + 10 = **61**.

The target is a guide, not a promise. Many patterns fall short, and some go further.

## How to read it

**Resistance break.** The pattern is only complete once price closes above the resistance line. Until then it may simply be a range, and price can still break downward.

**Volume.** Volume often tends to fade through the three declines, which suggests weakening selling, and rise on the break. This is a common guideline and not a rule.

**Retest.** After the break, price often falls back to the broken resistance, which may now act as support. This is [role reversal](/role-reversal/). Some traders enter on the retest instead of the break.

**Third low.** Weaker downside momentum at the third low, for example a higher [RSI](/rsi/) low, is a commonly watched early warning.

**Failed pattern.** If price breaks above resistance and then closes back below it, the pattern has failed. A close below the three lows removes the pattern entirely.

## Best market states

**Reversals after a long downtrend (best).** The pattern only makes sense when there is a meaningful fall to reverse.

**Ranges (weak).** Three touches of a level inside a sideways market are an ordinary range, not a reversal. The question is whether there was a trend into it.

**Strong trends (caution).** Repeated tests of a low can be a pause before a break lower.

**Volatility.** Choppy markets produce uneven lows and frequent false breaks, so stops usually have to be wider.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear downtrend before the first low |
| Lows | Three lows within a tight band, with clear rebounds between |
| Time | Lows well spaced; very tight patterns are less meaningful |
| Break | Close above the resistance line, not just a wick |
| Volume | Fading on the declines, rising on the break |
| Stop | Below the lowest low (a tighter stop sits below the retest of the broken resistance) |

## Common mistakes

- Buying before resistance breaks. Until then it is only a possible pattern.
- Calling any three dips a triple bottom without a prior downtrend.
- Forcing lows that are not near each other into the pattern.
- Treating the measured target as certain.
- Ignoring the failure case: a close back below resistance means the idea is wrong.
- Using a very tight tolerance on the lows and missing real patterns, or a loose one that accepts anything.

## FAQ

**What is a triple bottom?**
A triple bottom is a chart pattern with three lows at about the same price, formed after a downtrend. It shows that sellers failed three times at the same floor. A close above the resistance through the peaks completes it.

**Is a triple bottom bullish or bearish?**
It is a bullish reversal pattern. It signals that a downtrend may be ending. Its mirror image, the triple top, is the bearish version that forms at the end of an uptrend.

**How is a triple bottom different from a double bottom?**
A double bottom has two lows and one peak, and a triple bottom has three lows and two peaks. Both are confirmed by a break of resistance. The triple bottom takes longer to form and is less common.

**How do you find the target for a triple bottom?**
Measure from the lows up to the resistance line, then add that height to the breakout point. Treat the result as a guide only. Many patterns stop short, and others run further.

**Where does the stop loss go on a triple bottom?**
Many traders place it below the lowest low, where the pattern would be proven wrong. A tighter stop below the retest of the broken resistance risks less but is hit more easily.
