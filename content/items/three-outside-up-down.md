Three outside up and three outside down are three-candle reversal patterns. An engulfing pattern (a candle whose body covers the previous candle's body) is followed by a third candle that continues in the new direction. Three outside up appears after a decline. Three outside down appears after a rise.

## What is three outside up / down?

The pattern starts with a [bullish engulfing](/bullish-engulfing/) or [bearish engulfing](/bearish-engulfing/) pattern. An engulfing pattern is two candles where the second has a body that fully covers the first. It is one of the better known reversal signals, but it can fail.

The third candle adds a check. In three outside up it is a bullish candle that closes higher than the engulfing candle's close. In three outside down it is a bearish candle that closes lower than the engulfing candle's close. The follow-through is the point.

It is a close relative of [three inside up and down](/three-inside-up-down/), which is built on a harami instead.

## How it is identified

For **three outside up** (bullish, after a decline):

1. **Candle 1:** a bearish candle in a downtrend.
2. **Candle 2:** a bullish candle whose body engulfs candle 1's body.
3. **Candle 3:** a bullish candle that closes above candle 2's close.

**Three outside down** (bearish, after a rise) is the mirror image: a bullish candle, a bearish engulfing candle, then a bearish candle that closes lower.

**Worked example.** After a fall, candle 1 opens at 48 and closes at 46. Candle 2 opens at 45.5 and closes at 49, covering candle 1's body (46 to 48). Candle 3 opens at 49 and closes at 51, above 49. That is a three outside up.

Platforms differ on whether the engulfing must cover the wicks or only the bodies. The usual rule is bodies only.

## How to read it

The engulfing candle shows a sudden change of control. The third candle shows that the change held on the next period. That is more than a lone engulfing pattern offers.

It is still not proof. A trend can resume, especially when the pattern forms in the middle of a move and away from key levels.

## Best market states

**Reversal points after a clear move (best).** The pattern needs a trend to reverse.

**At support or resistance (good).** A known level adds context.

**Ranges and strong trends (weak).** In ranges, engulfing patterns are common and mean little. In strong trends they often fail.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear move before candle 1 |
| Engulfing | Candle 2's body covers candle 1's body |
| Candle 3 | Closes beyond candle 2's close |
| Level | Forms at support or resistance |
| Volume | Higher volume on the engulfing candle, where available |

## Common mistakes

- Acting on the engulfing candle alone.
- Using it without a real prior trend.
- Missing that candle 3 must close beyond candle 2's close.
- Treating it as certain.
- Confusing it with three inside up and down, which uses a harami.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between three outside up and a bullish engulfing?**
A bullish engulfing is two candles. Three outside up adds a third bullish candle that closes above the engulfing candle's close. The third candle is the extra confirmation.

**What is the difference between three outside and three inside?**
Three outside starts with an engulfing pattern, where the second candle covers the first. Three inside starts with a harami, where the second candle sits inside the first.

**Is three outside down bearish?**
Yes. It follows a rise and points to a possible fall. Three outside up is the bullish version after a decline.

**Is it reliable?**
No pattern always works. It needs context, such as a key level and a clear prior trend, and results vary by market.

**Should I wait for the third candle?**
Yes. Until it closes, the pattern is only an engulfing pattern.
