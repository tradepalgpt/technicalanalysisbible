Counterattack lines are a two-candle reversal pattern. A long candle in the trend's direction is followed by a long candle of the opposite colour that gaps in the same direction but then closes at the first candle's close. The recovery stops short of a full reversal, so it is a weak warning that needs confirmation.

## What are counterattack lines?

Counterattack lines are a milder cousin of the [piercing line](/piercing-line/) and [dark cloud cover](/dark-cloud-cover/). In those patterns the second candle closes deep inside the first body. Here the second candle only climbs back (or falls back) to the first candle's close. It claws back the gap but goes no further.

Nison describes the pattern as showing a sudden change of mood after a gap. The market opens with more of the same, and then the other side wins the session back.

## How it is identified

**Bullish counterattack lines** (after a downtrend):

1. A long bearish candle continues the fall.
2. The next candle gaps down at the open, then rallies. It is a long bullish candle.
3. It closes at, or very near, the previous candle's close.

**Bearish counterattack lines** (after an uptrend) reverse the picture: a long bullish candle, then a gap up, then a long bearish candle that closes at the previous close.

How close is "the same" close is a judgement. Some software allows a small tolerance, such as a fraction of the average candle size.

**Worked example.** In a downtrend, candle 1 opens at 60 and closes at 52. Candle 2 opens at 49, a gap below 52, falls no lower than 48, and closes at 52. Both candles are long, they are opposite colours, and the closes match, so these are bullish counterattack lines.

## How to read it

The gap down looks like fresh selling. The recovery to the prior close shows buyers absorbed it all. The net result for the two days is that the gap is gone and price is back where it was.

The signal is weaker than an engulfing candle or a piercing line, because the second candle does not take back any of the first candle's body. Traders usually want a further candle in the new direction, or a key level close by, before treating it as more than a pause.

## Best market states

**Reversal (best).** After a long move into a level, the pattern can show the move losing control.

**Range (weak).** Gaps and long candles are less meaningful in sideways action.

**Trend (weak).** The pattern is easily shrugged off by a strong trend, which can resume next candle.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Prior trend | A clear move to be reversed |
| Both candles long | Bodies longer than recent candles |
| Matching closes | Same close, or very nearly |
| Next candle | Follow-through in the new direction |
| Location | A support or resistance level nearby |

## Common mistakes

- Treating it as strong as an engulfing pattern.
- Accepting closes that are clearly different.
- Using it on markets that rarely gap, then forcing the pattern to fit.
- Ignoring the lack of confirmation.
- Trading it in the middle of a range.

## FAQ

**How do counterattack lines differ from a piercing line?**
In a piercing line the second candle closes above the midpoint of the first body. In counterattack lines it only returns to the first close. That makes counterattack lines a weaker, more tentative signal.

**Do the two closes have to match exactly?**
In theory yes, but real data rarely allows it. Many traders accept a tiny gap between the closes. Whatever tolerance you pick, apply it consistently.

**Are they also called meeting lines?**
Some books use "meeting lines" for the same idea. Nison uses the counterattack name in his text, and names vary by source.

**Do I need a gap?**
The classic pattern includes the opening gap. In 24-hour markets there is often no gap, so many traders use a softer rule of an open beyond the previous close.

**Is it a strong signal?**
Treat it as a weak one. It describes a failed continuation, not a clear reversal.
