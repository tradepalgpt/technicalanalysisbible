The previous day high and low are the highest and lowest prices traded in the last full session. Day traders draw them as horizontal lines on today's chart. They are simple, widely watched levels where price often pauses, reverses or breaks out, and they act as the edges of yesterday's range.

## What are the previous day high and low?

Each day's high and low show the furthest buyers and sellers pushed price before it was rejected. The next day, traders remember those prices. Stop orders sit beyond them, and many traders watch for a reaction or a break there. That is why price often slows down near these lines.

They sit between the very short-term view of today's range and the longer view of weekly levels. They are also the raw material for [pivot points](/pivot-points/), which use yesterday's high, low and close.

No single person invented the idea. It appears in many day-trading books as a basic building block of the day's plan. The levels have no special power beyond the attention paid to them, so their usefulness depends on how crowded and liquid the market is.

## How it is identified

1. Pick the session. For shares, this is the regular trading day. For forex or crypto, choose a daily close time and keep to it.
2. Read the highest and lowest price of that session, including or excluding extended hours. Decide which and stay consistent.
3. Draw a horizontal line at each from the open of the new day.
4. Optionally add yesterday's close and today's open.

**Worked example.** Yesterday a share traded between 48.20 and 50.40. The previous day high is 50.40 and the low is 48.20, so yesterday's range was 2.20. If today opens at 49.50, price is inside yesterday's range, and the two lines are the edges to watch.

## How to read it

**Inside the range.** While price stays between the two lines, the day is behaving like a range day. Traders often fade moves toward the lines, with stops just beyond.

**Break and hold.** A clear move beyond the high that holds, ideally with strong volume, suggests buyers are in control. The old high may then act as support on a retest. See [role reversal](/role-reversal/).

**Sweep and reject.** Price pokes beyond a line, triggers stops, then closes back inside. This is a failed break, as in [false breakout](/false-breakout/) and [liquidity sweep](/liquidity-sweep/), and some traders trade it as a reversal.

**Gap opens.** If today opens beyond yesterday's high or low, the level is already behind price and may act as support or resistance for a test.

**Relation to the open.** Where price opens inside yesterday's range gives a rough hint of the day's balance.

## Best market states

**Ranges (best).** On quiet days the lines frame the action, and moves from one edge toward the other are common.

**Breakouts (good).** The lines are natural trigger points. A break with volume and a hold beyond the level is the usual signal.

**Trends (weak).** On strong trend days price moves far past yesterday's range and the lines become irrelevant within the first hour.

**Volatility.** After a very wide day the lines are far apart and rarely reached. After a very narrow day, a break is more likely, and [NR7](/nr7/) is a related idea.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Session | Same definition of "day" every time |
| Break | A close beyond the line, not just a wick |
| Volume | Rising on a break, falling on a failed test |
| Hold | Price stays beyond the line for several bars or retests it |
| Stop | Just back inside yesterday's range for a break trade |
| Context | Agrees with the higher time frame trend |

## Common mistakes

- Using different "day" definitions on different days, so the lines move.
- Trading every touch of a line as if it were a signal.
- Ignoring the daily trend: fading the high in a strong uptrend is costly.
- Putting stops exactly at the line, where many other stops sit.
- Forgetting that extended-hours trade can change the high and low.
- Using the levels in thin markets where the previous day's high was a single odd print.

## FAQ

**What are PDH and PDL?**
They are shorthand for previous day high and previous day low. They are the highest and lowest prices of the last full session, drawn as horizontal lines on today's chart as reference levels.

**Do I include pre-market and after-hours trading?**
Both choices are used. Including extended hours gives the true extreme price. Excluding it matches the regular session range that most traders see. What matters is picking one and sticking with it.

**Are the previous day high and low support and resistance?**
In practice yes. They act as short-term support and resistance because many traders watch them. They are usually less significant than levels from higher time frames or levels tested many times.

**What happens when price breaks the previous day high?**
Sometimes price keeps going and the line becomes support. Often it fails and returns inside the range. Waiting for a close beyond the line, a hold and a volume increase helps filter some failures but cannot remove them.

**Can I use weekly or monthly highs and lows too?**
Yes. The same idea applies to the previous week or month. Those levels are watched by slower traders and usually carry more weight, though price reaches them less often.
