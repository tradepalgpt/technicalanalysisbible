A runaway gap, also called a measuring gap, is a price gap that appears in the middle of a strong trend. It shows that the trend is still driving hard. Classic teaching says the trend often covers about as much distance after the gap as it did before it, which is where the name "measuring" comes from.

## What is a runaway gap?

A gap is a space on the chart where no trading happened. In a runaway gap, a trend already under way jumps again: an up-trend opens higher, leaving a hole, or a downtrend opens lower. The gap does not reverse anything. It shows that the trend is still being pushed by demand or supply.

Chart-pattern writers split trend gaps into three kinds. A [breakaway gap](/breakaway-gap/) opens a move from a base. A runaway gap occurs part-way along. An [exhaustion gap](/exhaustion-gap/) comes near the end. Runaway gaps are also called measuring gaps because of a rule of thumb: the point of the gap is roughly half way through the move.

No one person is credited with the term. It comes from the early technical analysis literature, including *Technical Analysis of Stock Trends* by Edwards and Magee (first edition 1948).

As with all gap types, the label is only certain afterwards. At the time, a mid-trend gap could also be the start of an exhaustion move.

## How it is identified

1. **An established trend.** Price has already moved well beyond its starting base and is making a series of higher highs (or lower lows).
2. **A gap in the direction of the trend.** The new bar opens beyond the previous bar's range with a visible hole.
3. **No immediate fill.** Price stays away from the gap for some time.
4. **Volume.** Often firm, though not as explosive as at a breakaway gap.

**Measuring rule.** Take the distance from the start of the trend to the gap, and add the same distance beyond the gap for a rough target.

**Worked example.** A stock breaks out of a base at 40 and climbs to 50. It then gaps up, opening at 52 after a close at 50. The gap is the mid-point candidate.

Distance so far = 50 - 40 = 10
Rough target = 52 + 10 = **62**

This is a guide, not a forecast. Some trends stall well before the target and others go further. Many traders use the target as a point to review the trade, not as a firm exit.

## How to read it

**Trend still healthy.** A gap in the direction of the trend confirms that buyers (or sellers) remain in charge. It adds weight to an existing view rather than creating a new one.

**Not a new entry signal on its own.** Because price has already moved, chasing a gap often means entering at a poor risk-reward point. Many traders use it to hold a position, not to open one.

**Exit planning.** The measuring rule gives a rough area where the trend might be half done or nearing a target. Combined with a [trailing stop](/trailing-stop/), it can help decide when to take profits.

**Gap as support.** In an up-trend, the gap area may act as support on a pullback. A close back below the start of the gap weakens the case.

**Watch for exhaustion.** A second or third gap in the same direction, particularly on very high volume followed by a sharp reversal, may be an exhaustion gap instead. See [Exhaustion gap](/exhaustion-gap/).

## Best market states

**Trends (best).** The pattern needs a trend that is already running. It describes the middle of a move.

**Breakouts (follow-up).** After a breakaway gap starts a trend, a runaway gap may appear if the trend continues strongly.

**Ranges (weak).** In a range, gaps are usually filled, so a mid-range gap is a poor sign of continuation. See [Gap fill](/gap-fill/).

**Volatility.** Volatile trends produce more gaps, but also more false labels. Treat the pattern with more caution when news is driving prices.

## Confirmation rules

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Existing trend | Clear series of higher highs and higher lows (or the reverse) |
| Gap direction | The gap follows the trend, not against it |
| Hold | Price does not close back into the gap within a few bars |
| Volume | Not collapsing; ideally steady or rising |
| Target | The measuring rule is used as a rough guide, not a promise |
| Stop | Below the gap for a long trade; a close inside the gap is a warning |

## Common mistakes

- Treating the measuring rule as an exact target. It is a loose rule of thumb.
- Buying a gap in a late trend without checking whether it could be an exhaustion gap.
- Calling a gap a runaway gap in a range with no clear trend.
- Assuming gaps never fill. Many trend gaps are partly or fully filled eventually.
- Ignoring volume and the news behind the gap.
- Using the label to justify a trade after the fact.

## FAQ

**What is a runaway gap?**
A runaway gap is a price gap in the middle of a strong trend, in the same direction as the trend. It suggests the move is still being driven by strong demand or supply and has not run out of steam.

**Why is it called a measuring gap?**
Classic teaching says the move after the gap is often about the same size as the move before it, so the gap marks roughly the halfway point. It is a rule of thumb, and many trends fall short or go further.

**How do I tell a runaway gap from an exhaustion gap?**
You cannot be certain at the time. An [exhaustion gap](/exhaustion-gap/) comes late in a long run and is followed by a reversal. A runaway gap is followed by more trend. The difference is clear only after the next few bars.

**Do runaway gaps get filled?**
Often not quickly, but many are filled eventually. A fill soon after the gap suggests the move lacked strength. See [Gap fill](/gap-fill/).

**Can I trade a runaway gap as an entry?**
Some do, but price has already moved, so risk-reward may be poor. Many traders prefer to use it as confirmation to hold or add to an existing trade, with a stop below the gap.
