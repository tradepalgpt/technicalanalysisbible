No. RSI above 70 means recent gains have been much larger than recent losses; it does not mean price will fall. In a range near resistance it can line up with a top. In a strong uptrend it is normal and can last for weeks.

## What 70 actually measures

[RSI](/rsi/) is the share of recent movement that was upward, smoothed over 14 bars by default. Written plainly:

RSI = 100 x average gain / (average gain + average loss)

So RSI at 70 means about 70 per cent of the average movement over the window was gains. At 80, about 80 per cent. That is a description of the past few weeks: one-sided, with buyers in charge. It says nothing about value, about whether price is "too high", or about what happens next.

"Overbought" is a shorthand for that one-sided move. The [overbought and oversold](/overbought-oversold/) guide covers the term across indicators. The word suggests the move must reverse. The arithmetic does not.

## What one more up day does to RSI at 70

A useful way to see why 70 is not a top: work out what happens if the rally simply continues.

Take an RSI(14) at about 70 with an average gain of 0.70 and an average loss of 0.30. Now add one more day: a gain of 1.00, no loss.

- New average gain = (0.70 x 13 + 1.00) / 14 = 10.10 / 14 = 0.721
- New average loss = (0.30 x 13 + 0) / 14 = 3.90 / 14 = 0.279
- RSI = 100 x 0.721 / (0.721 + 0.279) = **72.1**

One more strong day nudges RSI up only two points, because Wilder's smoothing moves slowly. Each further up day adds a little more. A steady rally can carry RSI from 70 to the high 70s or low 80s and keep it there. Nothing in the formula pulls it back. Only down days do that. [Can RSI stay overbought for a long time?](/answers/can-rsi-stay-overbought-for-a-long-time/) follows this further.

## When 70 lines up with a top

The reading becomes useful when something else on the chart is already suggesting a top.

| Context | What RSI above 70 usually means | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Sideways range, price at resistance | The rally to the top of the range was one-sided | Useful warning, with the level |
| Strong uptrend, rising averages | Buyers in control; normal state | Not a sell reason |
| Fresh breakout from a range | Breakout had strength | Sign of strength, not weakness |
| Long rally, RSI above 80, price stalling for days | Speed falling at a high level | Tiring, worth watching |
| Second high with lower RSI peak | [Divergence](/divergence/) | Momentum fading, no timing |

In the first row, RSI is a supporting detail. The [resistance level](/support-and-resistance/) is the reason to look for a short; RSI only confirms that the move into it was stretched. Take the level away and the same reading means much less.

## What sellers wait for instead

Traders who use RSI to look for tops usually wait for one of these after the reading above 70:

1. **RSI falling back below 70.** Simple, and late by design. It filters out rallies that stay overbought.
2. **A [failure swing](/rsi-failure-swing/).** Wilder's own signal: RSI peaks above 70, pulls back, rallies to a lower peak, then breaks below its pullback low. [What is an RSI failure swing?](/answers/what-is-an-rsi-failure-swing/) explains it.
3. **A price signal at the level.** A bearish candle, a failed breakout or a break of a short-term trend line at resistance.
4. **A break of RSI's trend band.** In an uptrend, RSI's pullbacks often hold around 40 to 50. A drop through that zone says the trend has changed character, which matters more than any reading above 70.

## A worked example

A share trades between 48 and 52 for two months. It rallies from 48.50 to 51.80 in seven days. Daily RSI(14) reaches 73.

- The level: 52 has turned price back three times.
- The reading: RSI 73, consistent with a one-sided rally into that level.
- The trigger: the next day opens at 51.90, trades to 52.10 and closes at 51.40. A rejection of the level.

A trader looking for a short might sell below 51.40 with a stop above 52.20. The risk is 0.80 per share (1R). A 2R target would be 49.80, inside the range and above its floor.

Now change one thing. Instead of a two-month range, the share has risen from 30 to 50 over six months and has just broken to a new high at 52. RSI reads the same 73. The same "sell" would be selling a new high in a strong trend, with no resistance above. Same number, opposite situation.

## Verdict

RSI above 70 is a description, not an instruction. It tells you the recent move was one-sided. In a range at resistance, that supports a short case that the level is already making. In a trend or a breakout, it is a sign of strength and selling it is the most common way people lose money with RSI. Wait for the level, the market state and a price trigger to agree; RSI alone never makes the case.
