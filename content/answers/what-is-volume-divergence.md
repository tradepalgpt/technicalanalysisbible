Volume divergence is when price makes a new high or low but volume, or a volume-based indicator such as OBV, does not confirm it. It is read as a sign that fewer participants are behind the move, which can come before a pause or reversal, though it often comes to nothing.

## The idea behind it

A classic view in volume analysis is that volume should expand in the direction of the trend. In an uptrend, rallies should be busier than pullbacks. When price pushes to a new high on less [volume](/volume/) than the previous high, the move is said to lack participation. That mismatch is a [divergence](/divergence/).

## Three ways to measure it

| Type | Compare | Bearish version |
|---|---|---|
| Raw volume divergence | Volume at each swing high | Higher price high on lower volume |
| Indicator divergence | A volume indicator, such as [OBV](/on-balance-volume/) or A/D | Higher price high, lower indicator high |
| Delta divergence | [Cumulative volume delta](/cumulative-volume-delta/) | Higher price high, lower cumulative delta |

The bullish versions mirror these at lows: a lower price low on lighter selling volume, or with the indicator making a higher low.

## Worked example

A share makes three swing highs over two months. The average volume on the three days that made each high:

| Swing high | Price | Volume on the high day | OBV at the high |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 48.00 | 2.6m | 14.2m |
| B | 50.50 | 2.1m | 13.8m |
| C | 52.10 | 1.5m | 13.1m |

Price rose from A to C by 52.10 - 48.00 = 4.10. Volume on the high days fell from 2.6m to 1.5m, and OBV made lower highs. This is a bearish volume divergence on both measures.

What it does not tell you is when, or whether, the trend will turn. Trends often keep rising for months with falling volume, especially in mature bull markets and in quiet seasonal periods. A divergence can repeat at highs D, E and F before anything happens, or price can simply keep rising.

## Why divergence often fails

- **Volume falls for other reasons.** Holiday periods, summer months and fewer index events all lower volume without any change in demand.
- **Trends mature quietly.** Many long trends rise on gently falling volume. Divergence is then the normal state.
- **Selection bias.** Divergences that preceded a turn are memorable; the many that did not are forgotten. Any honest test counts them all.
- **Indicator start dates.** OBV and A/D values depend on where you start them, which can change the shape of small divergences.
- **Forex.** On tick volume, a divergence is a fall in quote activity, not in traded size.

We have not run our own test of volume divergence, so we quote no hit rate. See [how OBV is calculated](/answers/how-is-obv-calculated/) for the indicator most often used, and [what RSI divergence means](/answers/what-does-rsi-divergence-mean/) for the same idea with a momentum oscillator.

## How traders use it sensibly

1. Treat divergence as a reason to watch, not a reason to act.
2. Wait for price confirmation: a break of the last swing low in an uptrend, or a failure at a level.
3. Define the trade in R from the actual fill to the first stop, and keep the stop where the idea is shown to be wrong.
4. Prefer markets with real volume, and keep the same data feed for any comparison.

## Common mistakes

- Selling short because volume is falling in an uptrend.
- Comparing volume on swing highs that formed in very different seasons.
- Drawing divergences only after the turn, then believing they were obvious.
- Using a different indicator each time until one shows a divergence.

## Verdict

Volume divergence is a mismatch between a new price extreme and weaker volume or a weaker volume indicator. It points to thinning participation and is worth noting. On its own it is a poor timing tool: it can last a long time, it often resolves with no turn, and seasonal volume changes create false ones. Wait for price to confirm.
