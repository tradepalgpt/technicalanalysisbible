The golden pocket is a retracement zone between the 61.8% and 65% levels of a swing. It is a trader nickname, popular in crypto, rather than a textbook Fibonacci level: 0.618 comes from the golden ratio, but 0.65 is a round-number convention that turns a single line into a narrow zone.

## Where the idea comes from

The 61.8% [Fibonacci retracement](/fibonacci-retracement/) is the most quoted level because 0.618 is 1 divided by the [golden ratio](/golden-ratio/). Traders noticed that price rarely turns exactly on a line, so they widened it into a band. The upper edge of the band is 0.618; the lower edge (in a rise) is 0.65, though some write 0.66. The name and the 0.65 edge are not found in the classic Fibonacci or harmonic texts; they spread through online trading communities.

## How to calculate it

In a rise from a swing low to a swing high:

- Top of pocket = high - 0.618 x (high - low)
- Bottom of pocket = high - 0.65 x (high - low)

In a fall, measure up from the low instead.

## Worked example

A rise from 100 to 200. The move is 100.

| Edge | Calculation | Price |
|---|---|---|
| 0.618 | 200 - 0.618 x 100 | 138.2 |
| 0.65 | 200 - 0.65 x 100 | 135.0 |
| 0.66 (some versions) | 200 - 0.66 x 100 | 134.0 |

So the golden pocket is 135.0 to 138.2, a band 3.2 points wide on a 100-point move, or 3.2% of the swing. Using 0.66 widens it to 4.2%.

For a fall from 200 to 100, the pocket is 161.8 to 165.0.

## How traders use it

- **As an entry zone.** A pullback into the pocket is treated as a place to look for a turn, often with other evidence such as a prior high or low.
- **As a confluence anchor.** Traders look for the pocket to overlap other levels; see our [confluence](/confluence/) guide.
- **For stops.** Some place stops below the 78.6% level or the swing low rather than just under the pocket, because the pocket is narrow. Our answer on [Fibonacci stop placement](/answers/fibonacci-stop-loss-placement/) compares the choices in R.

## What to be careful about

**The width is arbitrary.** 0.65 and 0.66 are not derived from anything; they are chosen to make a zone. A band of 3% to 4% of the swing will contain some turns by chance.

**It is still one swing's arithmetic.** If two traders pick different swing lows, their pockets differ, sometimes by more than the pocket's own width.

**Popularity is not evidence.** Many traders watching the zone can make price react there, but crowded zones also gather stop orders just beyond them, which can lead to sharp moves through them.

**No published test.** We know of no independent, rules-based study showing the pocket performs better than nearby levels or a random zone of the same width. If you test it, compare it with a band of the same width placed elsewhere in the swing.

## Golden pocket versus the single 61.8% line

| | Single 61.8% line | Golden pocket |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | One price | A band from 0.618 to 0.65 |
| Origin | Golden ratio | Golden ratio plus a convention |
| Width on a 100-point swing | 0 | 3.2 points |
| How a "touch" is judged | Needs a tolerance anyway | Built into the band |

In practice, anyone using a single line also allows some tolerance around it. The golden pocket simply writes one particular tolerance down, on one side of the line. That is a reasonable habit, as long as the band is treated as a choice rather than a discovery.

## Verdict

The golden pocket is the 61.8% to 65% band of a swing. It is a convenient way to turn the most watched Fibonacci line into a zone, not a separate Fibonacci discovery. Treat it as a narrow area to watch, judge it against same-width bands elsewhere, and remember the 0.65 edge is a convention.
