When support breaks, the stop-loss orders sitting below it turn into sell orders, buyers at the level are trapped, and price often moves quickly towards the next support. Sometimes price snaps back above instead, a false break. A close below the zone and follow-through help tell the two apart.

## What happens step by step

1. **Buyers at support are exhausted.** The orders waiting there have been filled.
2. **Stops trigger.** Long traders' stops just below support become market sell orders. They add to the selling in a burst.
3. **Breakout sellers join.** Traders who sell breakdowns enter short.
4. **Trapped buyers decide.** Those who bought at support and did not have stops are now losing. Some sell now; others wait to get out at break-even later.
5. **Price looks for the next level.** With no nearby buyers, price often moves towards the next support quickly.
6. **The retest.** If price comes back to the old support, trapped buyers may sell there, turning it into resistance. See [role reversal](/role-reversal/).

## Real break or false break?

Not every move below support is a real break. [False breakouts](/false-breakout/) are common at obvious levels, often because the stops below are the target.

| Sign | Real break more likely | False break more likely |
|---|---|---|
| Close | Closes below the zone, on your timeframe | Closes back inside the zone |
| Follow-through | Next bars continue lower | Next bars reclaim the level |
| Range of breakdown bar | Wide, closes near its low | Long lower wick, closes near the high |
| Volume | Rises on the break | Little change, or spike then fade |
| Trend | Break in the direction of the larger trend | Break against a strong uptrend |
| Level history | Bounces had been getting weaker | Level held cleanly before |

None of these settles it on its own. Together they tilt the odds.

## Worked example: two breaks of the same level

A share has support at 24.60 to 24.90, tested four times over three months. The last two bounces were smaller than the first two.

**Break A.** A daily bar opens at 25.10, falls through the zone and closes at 24.10, near its low, on volume about twice the recent average. The next day it opens at 24.05 and falls to 23.40. A real break. The next support on the weekly chart is 22.00 to 22.30.

**Break B.** A different month. A daily bar falls to 24.30, below the zone, then rallies to close at 25.00, back inside, with a long lower wick. Volume was average. The next day it rises to 25.60. A false break. Traders who sold the breakdown are trapped; stops below 24.60 were cleared out.

Same level, same "break" on the intraday chart. The close and the next bar told them apart.

## How traders respond

**If you are long at support.** Your stop should already be below the zone plus a buffer. If it triggers, the plan worked as a plan; you were wrong on the level. See [where to put a stop near support](/answers/where-to-put-a-stop-loss-near-support/).

**If you trade breakdowns.** Wait for the close below the zone. Some traders wait for follow-through the next bar, or a retest of the old support from below. Stop above the old zone.

**If you trade false breaks.** Wait for the close back inside. Buy with a stop below the low of the poke.

## How far does price go?

Usually to the next level, but there is no fixed distance. Common ways traders estimate it:

- The next support zone on a higher timeframe.
- A measured move: the height of the range above the support, projected below.
- A round number below.

Treat these as places to watch, not targets that must be reached.

## Why a break can matter more than the bounces did

A support that held four times has gathered many traders' attention. When it fails, all of them are affected at once: longs are stopped or trapped, and shorts who waited for the break act together. That can make the move after a clean break larger than any of the earlier bounces. The [support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/) guide covers this under "why levels fail".

## Verdict

When support breaks, stops fire, buyers are trapped and price usually heads for the next level, with the old support often acting as resistance on the way back. But many breaks are false. A close below the zone, follow-through, a wide bar and rising volume are the usual signs of a real break. Decide in advance which you will trade, and where you are wrong.
