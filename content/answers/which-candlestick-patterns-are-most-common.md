The most common candlestick patterns are the doji, the engulfing patterns, the hammer and its twin the hanging man, and the shooting star. On our detectors' count over almost five years of daily bars on 10 US markets, those single and two-candle patterns appeared tens of times more often than famous three-candle patterns like the morning star.

## Why single candles dominate

The more candles a pattern needs, and the more exact the rules, the rarer it is. A [doji](/doji/) only needs one candle whose open and close are close together. A morning star needs three candles in a precise order, with a gap and a strong recovery. Every extra condition cuts the count.

There is also a market effect. Patterns that need a gap between candles (piercing line, dark cloud cover, stars, abandoned baby, kicker) can only form when the market opens away from the previous close. On stocks that happens most days, but usually by a small amount. On 24-hour markets like forex and crypto it almost never happens during the week.

## What the count tells you

**The famous patterns are not the common ones.** The morning star, three white soldiers and abandoned baby fill pages of candlestick books. On these 10 markets, strictly defined, they appeared a handful of times in almost five years. The doji appeared hundreds of times.

**Bearish counts ran higher than bullish for some pairs.** Bearish engulfing (202) outnumbered bullish engulfing (132); the doji after a rise (507) outnumbered the doji after a fall (300). These markets rose over most of the period, so there were simply more "after a rise" moments for bearish patterns to appear in. The count depends on the market's history as much as on the pattern.

**Being common did not mean being useful.** Every pattern in the top seven showed no evidence of an edge over random entry in our test. Common patterns are common partly because their rules are loose; a loose rule catches many ordinary candles.

**Definitions change the count.** These figures use our detectors' standard rules. A platform with a looser doji rule or a looser tweezer tolerance will find more. A detector that drops the gap rule for stars will find many more morning stars. Always ask how a pattern was defined before comparing counts.

## What this means for a trader

If you watch one daily chart, the patterns you will actually see each month are dojis, hammers and hanging men, shooting stars and engulfing candles. Learn those well, including their weaknesses. Three-candle patterns are worth knowing, but you may go a long time between clean examples on any one market, which also means you will have little personal evidence about them.

It also means most of your pattern decisions will be about common, noisy signals. That is where context does the work: the level, the trend before the pattern, volume, and the timeframe.

## Verdict

The doji, engulfing patterns, hammer, hanging man and shooting star are the most common candlestick patterns by a wide margin; on 10 US markets over almost five years, the doji alone produced over 800 trades while the morning star produced 9. None of the common patterns showed evidence of an edge over random entry in our tuning-period test.
