A marubozu candle means one side controlled the entire period: a long body with little or no shadow, so price opened at one extreme and closed at the other. A green marubozu shows buyers in charge from open to close; a red one shows sellers. It signals strength, not reversal, and its meaning depends on where it appears.

## What the candle shows

A [marubozu](/marubozu/) is a long-bodied candle with no shadows, or shadows so small they barely show. The word is usually translated from Japanese as "close-cropped" or "bald", describing the missing wicks.

- **Green (bullish) marubozu:** opens at or near the low, closes at or near the high. Buyers pushed price up from the first trade to the last, and sellers never managed to push it back.
- **Red (bearish) marubozu:** opens at or near the high, closes at or near the low.

Some traders distinguish partial versions. An "opening marubozu" has no shadow at the open end but a small one at the close; a "closing marubozu" has no shadow at the close end. The [belt hold](/belt-hold/) is a close relative: a long candle with no shadow at the open, appearing against the prior trend.

## Strength, not reversal

Most candlestick patterns are about rejection: a long wick, an engulfing reversal, a stall. The marubozu is the opposite. Nothing was rejected. The market moved one way and stayed there.

That makes it a signal of **strength in the body's direction**. What that strength means depends on context:

| Where it appears | Usual reading |
|---|---|
| Breaking out of a range, on high volume | Start of a move; bullish or bearish in the body's direction |
| Early in a new trend | Continuation; the trend has force |
| Against the prior trend (belt hold) | Possible reversal |
| At the end of a long, stretched trend | Possible exhaustion: late traders piling in |
| In a quiet range on low volume | Little meaning |

A marubozu that closes right at the high also says something about the next period: there was still buying at the close. Some traders read that as a sign the next period may open strong too.

## Where it is most useful

The clearest use is in [breakout trading](/breakout-trading/). A range has formed. One day a green marubozu closes above the top of it, on volume well above normal. That is the market moving decisively through a level, with no hesitation shown in the candle. [Relative volume](/relative-volume/) is a simple check: a breakout marubozu at twice normal volume is a stronger statement than one at half.

The weakest use is treating every marubozu as a buy or sell signal. In a trending market, long candles with small shadows appear often. Most are just part of the trend.

## What our test found

We tested the marubozu in its own direction (buy after a green one, sell after a red one) on daily bars for 10 US stocks and funds (SPY, QQQ, IWM, Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Tesla, Amazon, JPMorgan, Exxon), January 2021 to October 2025, the tuning period. Entry at the next open, 2R target, 20-day limit, costs both ways, compared with random entries in the same markets and quarters.

| Marubozu trade | Stop | Trades | Edge over random entry, R per trade | 95% range | Same-day exits | Label |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Long (green) | 1 ATR from close | 35 | -0.11 | -0.64 to +0.54 | 34% | No evidence of an edge |
| Long (green) | Beyond the candle | 35 | +0.10 | -0.36 to +0.65 | 6% | Exploratory, not a tested result |
| Short (red) | 1 ATR from close | 33 | 0.00 | -0.60 to +0.57 | 21% | No evidence of an edge |
| Short (red) | Beyond the candle | 30 | +0.20 | -0.41 to +0.71 | 0% | Exploratory, not a tested result |

([How we test](/methods/).)

Three things the marubozu guide does not tell you:

1. **Strict marubozus are rare.** Only 30 to 35 trades per variant over almost five years on 10 markets: barely above our minimum, and the ranges are very wide.
2. **The stop result is the reverse of single-candle reversal patterns.** For hammers and dojis, a stop beyond the candle was too tight. For the marubozu, the stop beyond the candle is the far end of a long body, so it is wide, and almost no trades ended on the entry day. The ATR stop was the tighter one here.
3. **No variant showed a clear edge.** The best-looking ones are exploratory on samples too small to judge.

## Worked example

This is an illustration with round numbers, not a real trade.

A stock trades between 74 and 80 for six weeks. One day it opens at 79.80, rises steadily, and closes at 83.90, with a low of 79.75 and a high of 84.00. Body 4.10, shadows 0.05 and 0.10. Volume is 2.4 times the 20-day average. The average daily range is 1.80.

That is a green marubozu breaking out of a range on heavy volume. A trader buying at the next open, 84.10:

- **Stop below the candle's low**, 79.70: risk 4.40, more than twice a normal day.
- **Stop at the top of the old range**, 79.90, a common breakout stop: risk 4.20.
- **Stop one average range below the close**, 82.10: risk 2.00, but inside the breakout candle.

The choice changes position size by more than half. Many breakout traders prefer the range-top stop, because a close back inside the range means the breakout failed.

## Verdict

A marubozu means one side controlled the whole period, with no rejection at all. It is a sign of strength in the body's direction, most useful when it breaks a range on heavy volume and least useful in the middle of a trend. In our tuning-period test, on only 30 to 35 trades per variant, it showed no clear edge over random entry.
