An inside bar is usually traded as a breakout: a buy stop above its high or a sell stop below its low, with a stop on the other side of the inside bar or of the larger "mother" bar before it. The choice of stop changes the size, the R target and how often the trade gets shaken out.

## What an inside bar is

An [inside bar](/inside-bar/) is a bar whose high is at or below the previous bar's high and whose low is at or above the previous bar's low. The previous bar is called the mother bar. It is a pause: the market traded inside yesterday's range, and most plans treat it as a small [breakout trading](/breakout-trading/) setup.

The candlestick version, where the body (not the range) sits inside the previous body, is the harami, covered in [what is a harami and how do you trade it?](/answers/what-is-a-harami-and-how-do-you-trade-it/). The inside bar uses the full high-low range, so it is a stricter test.

## The common ways to trade one

| Approach | Entry | Stop | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| With-trend breakout | Break of the inside bar in the trend's direction | Other side of the inside bar or mother bar | Pause, then continuation |
| Either-way breakout | Orders above and below; first one filled wins | Other side | No direction bias |
| Failed break ("fakey") | Price breaks one side, then closes back inside | Beyond the failed break | False break, reverse |
| Range trade | Fade the mother bar's edges | Just beyond the edge | Contraction continues |

The third row is the [fakey pattern](/fakey-pattern/), a name used in some price action courses for an inside bar whose first break fails. It is a form of false breakout.

## Worked example: three stop choices

GBP/USD on the daily chart, in an uptrend.

- Mother bar: high 1.3020, low 1.2940 (80 pips).
- Inside bar: high 1.3000, low 1.2960 (40 pips).
- Target in all cases: 1.3100, near the last swing high.
- Money at risk per trade: 200.

| Plan | Entry fill | Stop | Fill to stop | Size for 1R = 200 | Target in R |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: inside-bar break, inside-bar stop | 1.3002 | 1.2958 | 44 pips | 45,455 units | +2.23R |
| B: inside-bar break, mother-bar stop | 1.3002 | 1.2938 | 64 pips | 31,250 units | +1.53R |
| C: mother-bar break, mother-bar stop | 1.3022 | 1.2938 | 84 pips | 23,810 units | +0.93R |

Entry fills are on the ask, so the spread is already inside each fill-to-stop distance. In each plan, a clean stop-out is exactly -1R (-200), with commission on top. If the stop is moved, it can only be tightened, for example under a new higher low after entry.

The tight plan (A) has the best target in R but is the easiest to stop out with noise. The wide plan (C) waits for more confirmation, which costs reward. Nothing in this table says which gets stopped more often; that needs a test on real data. The sizes show the general rule: the stop sets the size, so the same 200 at risk buys a smaller position as the stop widens.

## What helps an inside bar setup

Location and context matter more than the bar itself.

- **Trend.** An inside bar in an established trend, near a pullback level, is the classic continuation setup.
- **Size.** A small inside bar after a large mother bar shows a sharp contraction. A nearly full-size inside bar is a weak pause.
- **Level.** An inside bar sitting just under resistance has little room to run.

## Common mistakes

- Entering on the break of a tiny inside bar on a low timeframe, where the spread is a big share of the stop.
- Placing orders both ways and then widening the losing side's stop.
- Ignoring that daily inside bars often break during thin overnight trading, when slippage is worse.
- Treating several inside bars in a row as a stronger signal without a rule for which bar to use.

## Verdict

Trade an inside bar as a breakout of a short pause, with the stop on the other side of the inside or mother bar. Tighter stops give better R targets but less room; wider stops do the opposite. Pick one plan, size from the stop so 1R is the same each time, and judge it over many trades, not one chart.
