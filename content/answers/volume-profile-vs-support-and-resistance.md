Not better, different. Swing-based support and resistance shows where price turned. Volume profile shows where most trading happened, which can reveal levels swing points miss, such as busy prices in the middle of a range. Used together, high-volume prices near swing levels make stronger candidates than either alone.

## What volume profile shows

[Volume profile](/volume-profile/) plots the volume traded at each price over a chosen period, as a sideways histogram. Common terms:

| Term | Meaning | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Point of control (POC) | The price with the most volume | A magnet and a pivot |
| Value area | The band holding about 70% of volume (a common default) | The "fair" range |
| Value area high / low | Edges of the value area | Support and resistance |
| High volume node | A peak in the profile | Price tends to slow and rotate here |
| Low volume node | A gap in the profile | Price tends to move through quickly |

## How it differs from swing levels

| | Swing-based support and resistance | Volume profile |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Where price turned | Where volume traded |
| Inputs | Price only | Price and volume |
| Middle of a range | Usually no level | Often a POC or high volume node |
| Thin areas | Not marked | Marked as low volume nodes |
| Depends on | Your drawing | The period you choose |
| Weak where | Too many lines | Volume data is poor |

The big difference is in the middle of ranges. Swing levels mark the edges. Volume profile marks where the market spent its time, which is often in the middle.

## Worked example

A share traded between 30 and 36 for two months.

**Swing levels.** Support 30.00 to 30.40 (three lows). Resistance 35.60 to 36.00 (two highs).

**Volume profile for the two months.**
- POC at 33.20.
- Value area 31.80 to 34.60.
- A low volume node at 34.80 to 35.40 (price moved through quickly).

Now price breaks above 36.00 and later pulls back.

- Swing reading: the old resistance at 35.60 to 36.00 should act as support.
- Volume reading: below that is a low volume node at 34.80 to 35.40, which price may move through quickly, and then the value area high at 34.60.

Put together: if 35.60 to 36.00 fails as support, the volume picture suggests price may slide quickly to about 34.60 rather than stopping halfway. That changes where a trader puts the stop and how they think about the risk.

## When they agree

The strongest areas are where both point the same way:

- A swing high at the same price as a high volume node.
- A value area edge on a round number.
- A POC that is also the previous day's close or a session's [VWAP](/vwap/).

## The forex caveat

Spot forex has no central exchange and no total volume. Volume profile in forex uses tick volume, the count of price changes, from one broker. Tick volume often moves with real volume, but it is a stand-in, not the real thing. Futures on the same currency do have exchange volume, and some forex traders use the futures profile for that reason.

## The period problem

A volume profile depends on the period it covers. A profile of the last week, the last month and the last year can show three different POCs. Common choices:

- Session profiles for day traders.
- A profile of the current range, from its start.
- A profile anchored to a major event, like an earnings gap.

Pick a rule and stick to it; otherwise you can always find a profile that "explains" any move.

## Verdict

Volume profile is not a replacement for support and resistance. It answers a different question: where the market accepted price, not where it turned. It is most useful for spotting busy prices inside ranges and thin areas price may cross quickly. Combine it with swing levels, use real exchange volume where you can, and fix how you choose the profile's period. The [support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/) guide shows how it fits with other level methods.
