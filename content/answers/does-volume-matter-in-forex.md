Participation matters in forex, but true volume is not available to you. Spot forex trades over the counter across many banks and brokers, so the "volume" bars on a forex chart are usually tick volume: a count of price changes on your broker's feed, not the amount traded.

## Why spot forex has no single volume number

On a stock exchange or a futures exchange, every trade passes through one venue. The exchange counts the shares or contracts and publishes the total. That number is [volume](/volume/) in the strict sense.

Spot forex works differently. A euro-dollar trade can happen between two banks, between a bank and a broker, inside an electronic trading network, or inside one broker's own book. No body collects all of those trades in real time. Some large venues publish their own figures, and central-bank surveys estimate turnover every few years, but none of that is a live bar-by-bar volume series you can put under a chart.

So when a retail platform shows volume bars on EUR/USD, it is showing something else.

## What the bars on a forex chart actually show

Most retail forex platforms show [tick volume](/tick-volume/). Each time the quoted price changes on the broker's feed, the counter goes up by one. A bar with 2,000 ticks saw 2,000 price updates; it does not say how many euros changed hands.

| Data source | What it counts | Covers the whole market? | Same on every platform? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exchange volume (stocks, futures) | Shares or contracts traded | Yes, for that exchange | Yes, apart from session settings |
| Forex tick volume | Price changes on one broker's feed | No | No: each feed differs |
| Currency futures volume | Contracts traded on the futures exchange | Only that futures market | Yes |
| Broker's own client volume | Trades booked by that broker | No, one broker only | No |

Because each broker's feed is built differently, two platforms can show different tick volume for the same hour. The pattern through the day is usually similar; the exact numbers are not.

## Does tick volume tell you anything?

It tells you how busy the market was. When more trading happens, prices update more often, so tick counts rise. That is why forex tick volume tends to climb at the London open, peak in the London and New York overlap, and fall in the late US and Asian hours. It also jumps around big scheduled news.

What it cannot tell you:

- **Size.** One tick could be a small trade or a very large one.
- **Direction.** A tick is a price change, not a buy or a sell.
- **The whole market.** It is one feed's view of activity.

So ideas that rely on exact traded size, such as [volume profile](/answers/volume-profile-vs-support-and-resistance/) value areas or order-flow delta, are rough on forex tick data. Ideas that only ask "was this bar busier than normal?", such as [relative volume](/relative-volume/), cope better because they compare the feed with itself.

## Practical ways forex traders use activity

- **Session context.** A breakout in the dead hours of the Asian session, on low ticks, is a different event from one at the London open. Tick volume helps you see which one you are in.
- **Compare like with like.** Compare a bar's ticks with the same hour on previous days, not with the day's average. Quiet hours are always quiet.
- **News spikes.** Very high tick counts around a data release mostly reflect fast quote changes and wide spreads. They are a warning about fills, not a signal in themselves.
- **Currency futures as a cross-check.** Exchange-traded currency futures publish real volume for their own market. Some traders glance at them for big sessions, knowing they cover only part of the trading.

## Common mistakes

- Treating forex tick volume as a count of money traded.
- Comparing tick volume between two brokers and drawing conclusions from the difference.
- Running a volume indicator built for stocks, such as on-balance volume, and reading it as if it saw real buying and selling.
- Reading a quiet holiday session as "no interest" in a level, when it is just a quiet day.
- Expecting a volume rule tested on a stock to carry straight over to a currency pair.

## Verdict

Activity matters in forex, but you only see a proxy for it. Tick volume is a fair guide to how busy a session is, and works best when you compare each hour with the same hour on other days. It is weak for anything that needs real size or direction. Treat it as context, not as proof of buying or selling.
