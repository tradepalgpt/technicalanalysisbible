Pivot points are calculated from the previous period's high, low and close, so every trader gets the same levels and they reset each day. Drawn support and resistance comes from past turning points on the chart, so it reflects where orders actually sat but varies by trader. Many day traders use pivots as a map and watch hardest where both agree.

## The core difference

| | Pivot points | Drawn support and resistance |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Formula on H, L, C (sometimes O) | Past swing highs and lows |
| Objective? | Yes, given the same data | No; traders draw differently |
| Resets | Every day (or week, month) | When the chart changes |
| Uses chart history? | Only the last period | All of it |
| Number of levels | Fixed (usually 5 to 9 per day) | As many as you draw |
| Best for | Intraday maps | Any timeframe |
| Main weakness | Can land at prices with no history | Hindsight and clutter |

## Why pivots can work

[Pivot points](/pivot-points/) came from futures floor traders who needed quick levels for the day. Their strength is that everyone using the standard formula on the same data gets the same numbers. If enough day traders watch the same R1 and S1, orders gather there. That is the attention reason for levels in its purest form.

They also include the previous day's range by construction. R1 and S1 are built from the high and low, so they often sit near the [previous day's high and low](/previous-day-high-low/), which are levels in their own right.

## Why drawn levels can work

Drawn [support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/) comes from places where price actually turned. Those are places where real orders were filled and where traders have memories and regrets. A pivot level has no such history unless it happens to land on one.

## Worked example: where they agree

A stock index future yesterday: high 5,082, low 5,018, close 5,070.

- Pivot P = (5,082 + 5,018 + 5,070) / 3 = 15,170 / 3 = **5,056.7**
- R1 = 2 × 5,056.7 − 5,018 = **5,095.3**
- S1 = 2 × 5,056.7 − 5,082 = **5,031.3**
- R2 = 5,056.7 + 64 = **5,120.7**
- S2 = 5,056.7 − 64 = **4,992.7**

From the chart, the trader also has:

- A swing high from last week at 5,100.
- The round number 5,000 and 5,100.
- A swing low two days ago at 5,035.

Putting them together:

| Area | Pivot | Chart level | Agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5,095 to 5,100 | R1 5,095.3 | Swing high 5,100; round 5,100 | Strong |
| 5,031 to 5,035 | S1 5,031.3 | Swing low 5,035 | Good |
| 5,057 | P | None | Pivot only |
| 4,993 to 5,000 | S2 4,992.7 | Round 5,000 | Good |
| 5,121 | R2 | None | Pivot only |

The trader's watch list for the day: 5,095 to 5,100 above, 5,031 to 5,035 and 4,993 to 5,000 below. The central pivot and R2 are noted but given less weight.

## The data problem

Pivot levels depend completely on the high, low and close. Change the session and the pivots move. For the index future above, using regular trading hours only (rather than the full overnight session) could give a different high and low, and so different pivots. In forex, a broker whose daily bar closes at 5pm New York gives different pivots from one closing at midnight UTC.

Drawn levels have the same problem in a milder form, because daily swing points also depend on the session. But drawn levels are usually zones, so small data differences matter less.

## When to use which

- **Intraday, liquid markets:** pivots are a fast, shared map. Add the obvious chart levels.
- **Swing trading:** daily pivots reset too often to matter much. Weekly or monthly pivots can help; drawn levels usually do more.
- **Markets with thin trading:** fewer traders watch pivots, so the attention effect is weaker. Drawn levels are more useful.

## Verdict

Pivots and drawn levels answer different questions. Pivots say "where are today's shared reference points?" Drawn levels say "where did orders actually sit before?" Neither is better in general. Used together, the useful levels are the ones where they agree, and the ones that rest on a single method deserve less of your attention.
