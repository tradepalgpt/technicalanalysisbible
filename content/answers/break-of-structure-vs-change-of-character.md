A break of structure (BOS) is a break of the last swing in the direction of the trend, so it signals continuation. A change of character (CHoCH) is the first break against the trend, such as an uptrend losing its last higher low, and is read as an early sign the trend may be ending.

## Side by side

| | Break of structure | Change of character |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | With the trend | Against the trend |
| In an uptrend | Price passes the last swing high | Price passes the last higher low |
| In a downtrend | Price passes the last swing low | Price passes the last lower high |
| Usual reading | Trend continuing | Trend may be turning or pausing |
| Older name | New high or new low (Dow theory) | Trend violation, failed higher low |
| How often it is wrong | Often late in trends | Often, because pullbacks overshoot |

Both terms come from the smart money concepts vocabulary. A [break of structure](/break-of-structure/) and a [change of character](/change-of-character/) use the same swing points; only the direction differs.

## Where sources differ

There is no single official definition, and course writers disagree on three points.

1. **Which low counts.** Some use the last higher low that produced a new high. Others use any internal swing low. The second gives many more CHoCH signals, most of them small.
2. **Wick or close.** Some count a wick through the swing; others need a candle close beyond it.
3. **CHoCH vs market structure shift.** Some writers use [market structure shift](/market-structure-shift/) (MSS) as a synonym for CHoCH. Others say an MSS needs a strong, fast move (often called displacement) through the swing, while a CHoCH can be any break. Treat the labels as interchangeable unless a source states its rule.

## Worked example

A pair is in an uptrend on the 4-hour chart. Confirmed swings, using a close rule:

| Step | Event | Price | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Higher low | 1.2550 | Structure intact |
| 2 | Close above prior high 1.2620 | 1.2634 | BOS (bullish) |
| 3 | New swing high confirmed | 1.2680 | Uptrend |
| 4 | Higher low confirmed | 1.2595 | Uptrend |
| 5 | Rally fails below 1.2680, swing high 1.2660 | 1.2660 | Lower high, warning |
| 6 | Close below 1.2595 | 1.2588 | CHoCH (bearish) |
| 7 | Swing low 1.2575 forms, rally to 1.2630 fails, then a close below 1.2575 | 1.2563 | BOS (bearish) |

Step 2 is a BOS: it continues the uptrend. Step 6 is the CHoCH: the first close below the last higher low. Step 7 is a bearish BOS, because the market is now treated as a downtrend and has made a new lower low. The same kind of event, a close past a swing, gets a different name depending on which trend it is measured against.

## How traders use the pair

The common pattern is: wait for a CHoCH, then look for an entry on the pullback, then treat later BOS events as confirmation. A long after a CHoCH from a downtrend might fill at 1.2612 with a first stop at 1.2585 under the new low, making 1R = 27 pips times size. If price returns to 1.2585 cleanly, the loss is exactly -1R, with costs on top. After a later BOS higher, the stop can be raised under the new higher low, but not lowered.

## Limits

- A CHoCH happens at every real reversal, but also at many deep pullbacks that later resume the trend. It is a necessary sign of a turn, not a sufficient one.
- Labels depend on the swing rule. Change the rule and the labels change.
- Neither event has a published, independently tested edge that we can cite. They are descriptions of structure, not signals with known odds.

## Verdict

BOS and CHoCH are the same measurement in two directions: a break of a swing with the trend is a BOS, against it a CHoCH. The terms are new; the idea is Dow theory. Use them as a consistent vocabulary for structure, define the swing and close rules, and treat a CHoCH as a warning rather than a confirmed reversal.
