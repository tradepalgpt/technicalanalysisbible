Start on the weekly and daily charts, mark the clear swing highs and lows where price turned sharply, and group nearby turns into zones rather than single lines. Size each zone with the bar bodies and wicks or a fraction of ATR. Then delete every level you would not act on.

## The routine in five steps

The full guide covers why levels matter. This post is the practical checklist, with one chart worked from start to finish.

1. **Top down.** Open the weekly chart, then the daily, then your trading timeframe. Higher-timeframe levels go on first.
2. **Swing points.** Mark clear [swing highs and lows](/swing-highs-lows/): turns with a real move away, not small wobbles.
3. **Group them.** Where two or more turns sit close together, they form one level.
4. **Make it a zone.** Draw from the bodies to the wicks of the turning bars, or use a quarter to half of [ATR](/atr/) as the width.
5. **Delete.** Keep the nearest one or two zones above and below price, plus major higher-timeframe levels. Remove the rest.

## Worked example: a share on the daily chart

Here is a share that has moved between about 40 and 60 over a year. Daily ATR(14) is currently 1.10.

**Weekly chart first.** Two big turns stand out: a high at 59.80 eleven months ago and a low at 40.60 five months ago. Both get a zone.

**Daily chart.** Swing highs at 52.10, 51.80 and 52.40 over the last three months. Swing lows at 46.30 and 46.70. One earlier swing low at 49.90 that later became a swing high at 50.20.

**Group them.**

| Cluster | Turns | Zone (bodies to wicks) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Major resistance | 59.80 | 59.00 to 59.80 | Weekly; far from price |
| Near resistance | 51.80, 52.10, 52.40 | 51.60 to 52.40 | Three daily tests |
| Middle level | 49.90 (low), 50.20 (high) | 49.70 to 50.20 | Has acted both ways; also the round number 50 |
| Near support | 46.30, 46.70 | 46.30 to 46.80 | Two daily tests |
| Major support | 40.60 | 40.60 to 41.40 | Weekly |

**Check zone widths against ATR.** Half of 1.10 is 0.55. The near resistance zone is 0.80 wide, a little loose. The middle level is 0.50, about right. The near support zone is 0.50. Fine.

**Delete.** Price is at 48.50. The chart now has five zones. The two weekly ones are far away; keep them faint. The three near price stay. Earlier in the process there were also small swings at 47.60 and 48.90 on the daily chart. They had small reactions and no match on the weekly chart, so they went.

The result is a chart a trader can use: support at 46.30 to 46.80, a middle level at 49.70 to 50.20, resistance at 51.60 to 52.40.

## What to look for in a swing point

| Better swing point | Weaker swing point |
|---|---|
| Sharp turn, large move away | Small pause inside a range |
| Visible on the weekly or daily chart | Only on a 5-minute chart |
| Matches another swing nearby | Stands alone |
| Near a round number or session extreme | Random price |
| Price left it quickly | Price drifted away slowly |

The two-bars-each-side rule (a high with two lower highs on each side) is a common objective definition. It is a starting point. A trader's eye usually does better on which swings matter, but the fixed rule is useful for testing because it does not use hindsight.

## Lines that cross price history

A good check: does the zone cut through a lot of bars in the middle of a range? If price spent weeks trading back and forth through your zone without reacting, it is not a level for that period. Levels should sit at the edges of activity, not in the middle of it, unless they mark a place where price paused clearly.

The exception is a volume-based view, where the busiest price in a range can act as a magnet. That is a different idea; see the [volume profile](/volume-profile/) guide.

## Common drawing mistakes

- **One line per swing.** Ten lines a few pence apart are one zone.
- **Drawing only from wicks.** One news spike can leave a wick nobody cares about later.
- **Drawing only on your trading timeframe.** You miss the levels that most traders are watching.
- **Never redrawing.** Levels that broke weeks ago and were never retested can go.
- **Drawing after the move.** It is easy to draw the level that held. Draw before price gets there, and note what you expected.

## Verdict

Drawing levels is easy; drawing few is the skill. Work top down, take clear swing points, group them into zones sized against ATR, and delete anything you would not trade. A chart with three zones near price is more useful than one with thirty lines, because it tells you where your decisions are.
