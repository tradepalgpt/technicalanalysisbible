A golden cross is when a shorter moving average, usually the 50-day, crosses above a longer one, usually the 200-day. It says that the medium-term average price has moved above the long-term one. It is a late, slow signal that describes a recovery already under way, not a prediction of what comes next.

## How it forms

The [golden cross](/golden-cross/) uses two [simple moving averages](/simple-moving-average/) on a daily chart: the 50-day and the 200-day. When a market has been falling, the 50 sits below the 200. As price recovers, the 50 rises faster because it reacts sooner. At some point it climbs through the 200. That day is the cross.

The opposite event, the 50 crossing below the 200, is the [death cross](/death-cross/).

Some traders use EMAs instead, or other pairs such as 20 and 50. The original and most-quoted version is the 50 and 200 simple averages on daily closes.

## Why it is always late

Both averages lag. The 50-day average is, in rough terms, describing where price was about 25 days ago. The 200-day describes where price was about 100 days ago. For the 50 to overtake the 200, price has to have been rising for weeks, usually months.

A simple way to see this: the 50-day average only climbs above the 200-day once recent prices have been high enough, for long enough, to pull the short average up past a long one that still contains the low prices from months ago. By then the low is often well behind.

### A worked example

A stock falls from 120 to 80 over six months, then recovers.

| Stage | Price | 50-day SMA | 200-day SMA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 80 | 88 | 104 |
| Six weeks later | 95 | 86 | 101 |
| Three months after low | 104 | 97 | 98 |
| Cross day (about 14 weeks after low) | 106 | 98.5 | 98.3 |

The numbers are made up, but the shape is typical. Price bottomed at 80. The cross arrives with price at 106, around 26 points above the low. A trader buying on the cross day has missed the first part of the move. That is not a flaw to be fixed. It is what a slow, smoothed signal is.

## What it can tell you

- **The trend has changed on a medium-term view.** The average price of the last ten weeks is now above the average of the last ten months.
- **A recovery has lasted.** A short bounce cannot produce a golden cross; it needs months of higher prices.
- **What many people are watching.** The cross gets news coverage. That may bring attention and some buying around the event, but there is no reliable way to know how much.

## What it cannot tell you

- **That price will go higher.** The cross reports the past. Some crosses happen near the start of a long rise, others near the end of a rally that then fades.
- **When to enter.** Price is often stretched above both averages on the cross day. A pullback afterwards is common.
- **Anything in a range.** When a market goes sideways for months, the 50 and 200 flatten and can cross several times, each time cancelling the last signal.

## How traders use it

Few traders use the golden cross on its own as an entry signal. More common uses:

1. **As a regime filter.** Only take long setups while the 50 is above the 200. This keeps a trader out of long trades during long downtrends, at the cost of missing early recoveries.
2. **As context.** A breakout on a stock whose 50 has just crossed above its 200 is read as more in line with the bigger picture than one in a long downtrend.
3. **As a reason to look.** The cross puts a market on a watch list. The entry then comes from something else, such as a pullback to the 50.

## The research, briefly

Long-average rules have been studied for decades. Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron (1992) in the Journal of Finance tested moving-average rules on a long history of the Dow and reported results that differed from chance in their sample. Later work showed that much of this weakens once you account for the many rules that could have been tested on the same data, and for later years. There is no solid case that the 50/200 pair is special compared with nearby pairs.

## Verdict

The golden cross is a slow, late confirmation that a recovery has lasted months. It is useful as a filter or as context. As a timing signal it is weak: entries come after much of the move, and in sideways markets the cross flips back and forth.
