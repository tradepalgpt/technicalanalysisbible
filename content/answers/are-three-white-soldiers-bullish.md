Yes, three white soldiers are a bullish pattern: three long green candles in a row, each opening within the previous body and closing near its high. They are strongest after a fall or a long base; after an extended rally they can instead mark a move that is already stretched.

## What the pattern is

[Three white soldiers](/three-white-soldiers/) are three consecutive green candles where:

- each candle has a long body;
- each opens within the previous candle's body (not with a big gap above it);
- each closes near its high, with small upper shadows;
- each close is higher than the one before.

"White" comes from the traditional drawing of rising candles as hollow white bodies. The mirror pattern, three long red candles stepping lower, is [three black crows](/three-black-crows/).

## Why it reads as bullish

The pattern is not a hint of a turn; it *is* the turn. One strong green candle can be a one-day bounce. Three in a row, each closing near the top of its range and each opening inside the last one's body, show buyers in control over several sessions, absorbing every attempt to sell at the open and pushing to new highs by the close.

After a fall or a long sideways base, that is a clear change of control. That is when the pattern is usually rated most bullish.

## When it is less bullish

The same three candles mean something different in other places:

| Where it appears | Usual reading |
|---|---|
| After a fall or a long base | Strong bullish reversal |
| Breaking out of a range on rising volume | Bullish breakout |
| After a long rally, far above the moving averages | Possible exhaustion; the move may be stretched |
| With shrinking bodies and growing upper shadows | [Advance block](/advance-block/): buyers tiring |
| With a small third candle | Deliberation pattern: buyers hesitating |

The third row matters most in practice. By the time three long green candles have printed, price has already moved a long way. An oscillator like the [RSI](/rsi/) may be above 70. The pattern describes strength that has already happened, and a late entry can buy the top of a short-term burst.

The advance block and deliberation variants are the soldiers losing step. Some traders watch for them as warnings when holding a long position.

## How rare it is

The strict pattern is rarer than its fame suggests. Three long bodies in a row, each opening inside the last and closing near its high, is a lot to ask.

On daily bars for 10 large US stocks and funds (SPY, QQQ, IWM, Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Tesla, Amazon, JPMorgan, Exxon), January 2021 to October 2025, the tuning period, our detector found **5 three white soldiers trades** and **3 three black crows trades**. The advance block gave 13 and the deliberation pattern 1. All are below our 30-trade minimum, so we publish no test result for any of them. ([How we test](/methods/).)

That count is what the guide does not show. Over almost five years, on markets that rose strongly, a strict three white soldiers formed only a handful of times. Most "three white soldiers" seen on screeners use looser rules, such as allowing gaps between candles or not requiring closes near the high.

## How traders use it

1. **Look left.** Is there a fall or a base before it? If price has already rallied hard, be wary.
2. **Check the stretch.** How far is price above its 20-day average, in average daily ranges? Three long candles from an already stretched level may be the end, not the start.
3. **Entry options.** Buy the next open (late but simple), or wait for a pullback towards the middle of the third candle's body.
4. **Stop.** Below the first candle's low is the pattern's logic, but that is often a very wide stop after three long candles. Many traders use the second candle's low or the midpoint of the pattern instead.
5. **Watch for an advance block** forming later, as the move tires.

## Worked example

This is an illustration with round numbers, not a real trade.

A fund has moved sideways between 48 and 52 for two months. Then:

- Day 1: opens 50.20, closes 52.10, high 52.20.
- Day 2: opens 51.60 (inside Day 1's body), closes 53.80, high 53.90.
- Day 3: opens 53.20 (inside Day 2's body), closes 55.60, high 55.70.

Three white soldiers, breaking out of the range on rising volume. A stop below Day 1's low (say 50.00) from an entry at the next open, 55.70, risks 5.70, almost three times the 2.00 average range. A trader using Day 2's low (say 51.50) risks 4.20. Either way, a 2R target is far away (64.10 with the Day 2 stop, 67.10 with the wider one), and much of the move has already happened.

That is the pattern's trade-off: the clearest sign of buyers in control arrives when the easy part of the move is done.

## Verdict

Three white soldiers are bullish: three strong green candles in a row are the clearest candlestick picture of buyers taking control. They are most meaningful after a fall or a base, and least after a long rally, where they can mark a stretched move. Strictly defined, they are rare; we found 5 on 10 US markets in almost five years, too few to test.
