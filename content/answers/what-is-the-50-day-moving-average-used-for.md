The 50-day moving average is used to read the medium-term trend, roughly the last ten weeks of trading. Traders watch it as a pullback zone in trends, as half of the golden and death cross, and as a quick test of whether a stock is in a healthy or weak phase.

## What it measures

The 50-day is usually a [simple moving average](/simple-moving-average/) of the last 50 daily closes. On a stock that trades five days a week, that is about ten weeks, or one quarter. On a crypto market that trades every day, 50 daily bars is about seven weeks.

It sits between the fast lines (9, 20) and the long-term 200-day. It is slow enough to ignore a few bad days, but quick enough to turn within a few weeks of a real change in trend.

## Five common uses

| Use | How it is read | Main limit |
|---|---|---|
| Trend direction | Rising 50 = medium-term up; falling = down | Lags turns by weeks |
| Pullback zone | In uptrends, deeper pullbacks often stall near it | Only in trends; a zone, not a line |
| [Golden cross](/golden-cross/) / death cross | 50 crossing the 200 | Very late; whipsaws in ranges |
| Health check | Price above a rising 50 = healthy trend | Says nothing about the next move |
| Market breadth | Share of stocks above their 50-day | Only for indexes and groups |

The last use is a breadth indicator: [percent above moving average](/percent-above-moving-average/) counts how many stocks in an index trade above their own 50-day. A high reading means the advance is broad; a falling reading while the index rises means fewer stocks are carrying it.

## The 50-day as a pullback zone

Many swing traders watch the 50-day in established trends. Pullbacks that hold the 20 are shallow; those that reach the 50 are deeper but still normal in a trend. A close well below a flat or falling 50 is a sign that the medium-term trend may have changed.

### A worked example

A stock has been rising for five months. Its 50-day SMA is at 142 and rising about 0.30 a day. ATR is 3.

- Price pulls back from 158 over two weeks.
- It reaches 143.50, two days later dips to 141.20, and closes at 144.00.
- The 50-day is now at 142.60. The zone (half an ATR either side) is 141.10 to 144.10. The dip held inside it.
- A trader enters at 144.00 with a stop below the zone at 140.50. Risk = 3.50 = 1R.
- Prior high: 158. Reward if reached: 14, or 4R.

The 50-day did not predict the bounce. It gave a place to watch, a clear point where the idea was wrong, and a reasonable ratio. If the stock closes below 140.50, the trader is out for 1R plus costs. See [pullback trading](/pullback-trading/).

## SMA or EMA?

The widely quoted 50-day in news and stock screeners is usually the SMA. Some traders use a 50 EMA, which sits a little closer to price in trends. The difference is small next to the choice of period. If you want to see what most market commentary is referring to, use the SMA.

## How it behaves on different markets

- **Stocks:** 50 trading days, about ten weeks. The most common use.
- **Indexes:** the same, and widely reported.
- **Forex:** 50 daily bars, but the daily close time depends on the platform. Values can differ slightly between charts.
- **Crypto:** 50 calendar days, as bars form every day. A shorter span in calendar time than on stocks. See [moving averages on crypto](/answers/moving-averages-on-crypto-do-the-usual-periods-apply/).

## When the 50-day is least useful

- **In ranges.** A flat 50 sits in the middle of the range and price crosses it constantly.
- **After a sharp spike.** If price jumps 20% in a week, the 50 is far below and will take weeks to catch up. It says little about where a pullback might stop.
- **On young assets.** A stock or coin with only a few months of history barely has a valid 50-day.

## Why 50?

There is nothing special about the number itself. It is roughly a quarter of a trading year, a round number, and widely used, so it has become a shared reference point. A 45 or 55 would look almost identical. Its usefulness, if any, comes from being watched, not from the number.

## Verdict

The 50-day moving average is a solid summary of the medium-term trend and a sensible zone to watch for pullbacks in established trends. As a signal on its own, especially in crosses with the 200, it is late and prone to whipsaw in flat markets. It is most useful as context for other decisions.
