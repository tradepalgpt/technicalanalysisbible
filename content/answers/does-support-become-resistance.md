Often, yes. When support breaks clearly, the old level frequently acts as resistance on the way back up, because traders trapped long and traders who missed the break both sell there. It is a tendency, not a rule: false breaks, deep retests and old levels all break the pattern.

## Who is trading at the old level

The idea is called [role reversal](/role-reversal/). It makes sense once you list who is active when price comes back to a broken support.

Say support at 50 held three times, then broke. Price fell to 46 and now rallies back to 50.

| Group | Position | What they do at 50 |
|---|---|---|
| Buyers from the old support | Long from about 50, now at break-even | Sell to get out without a loss |
| Short sellers who missed the break | Flat, watched price fall without them | Sell short at the old level |
| Short sellers who caught the break | Short from about 49.50 | Hold, or add to the short |
| New buyers | Flat | Some buy; but there are fewer of them |

Three of the four groups add selling at 50. That is why the old support often behaves like resistance.

## The same in reverse

Resistance becomes support by the same logic. Short sellers trapped above a broken resistance buy back at the old level. Buyers who missed the breakout buy there. Holders who sold too early before the break buy back in.

## What a good reversal looks like

The cleanest version, step by step:

1. A level that mattered: several clear tests, ideally visible on the daily chart.
2. A clear break: a close well beyond the zone, not a poke.
3. Some distance: price moves away, at least a fraction of a day's range.
4. A retest: price returns to the old zone.
5. A hold: the retest bar closes on the new side, often with a rejection wick into the zone.

The retest is where traders who use [breakout retest](/breakout-retest/) entries act, with a stop back through the old level.

## Worked example

A currency pair has support at 1.2480 to 1.2500 that held four times over two months. On a Tuesday it closes at 1.2440, below the zone. It falls to 1.2380 by Thursday.

On the following Monday it rallies to 1.2495, into the old zone, and closes at 1.2465 with a long upper wick. A trader sells short at 1.2465 with a stop at 1.2525, above the zone plus a small buffer. Risk 60 pips (1R). The next support below is the recent low at 1.2380, 85 pips away, about 1.4R. Lower down there is a weekly support at 1.2300, about 2.75R.

Whether that trade is worth taking depends on whether the trader is aiming for the nearby low or the weekly level. The level reversal gives the entry and the stop. The next levels decide the reward.

## The three ways it fails

**1. The break was false.** Price closed below support once, then rallied straight back above it. The level never changed sides. See [false breakout](/false-breakout/). This is the most common failure, and is why many traders want a close beyond the zone and some follow-through before they treat it as broken.

**2. The retest goes too deep.** Price returns to the old support and closes well above it. The break has failed; the old support is back in charge, and shorts from the retest are trapped.

**3. Too much time.** A level that broke six months ago and was never retested has fewer trapped traders left. Role reversal works best soon after the break.

| Sign | Reversal more likely | Reversal less likely |
|---|---|---|
| Break | Clear close beyond, follow-through | Single poke, close back inside |
| Time to retest | Days to a few weeks | Many months |
| Retest | Rejection near the zone | Close well back through |
| Level history | Several tests, daily or weekly | One minor test |
| Trend | Break in the direction of the larger trend | Break against a strong trend |

## Does it always need a retest?

No. Many strong breaks never come back. A trader who only trades retests misses those. Our answer on [whether to wait for a retest after a breakout](/answers/should-you-wait-for-a-retest-after-a-breakout/) covers that trade-off. This post is about what happens if price does come back.

## Verdict

Support often becomes resistance after a clear break, and the reason is simple: trapped buyers and late sellers act at the same price. Treat it as a likely area for a reaction, not a certain one. Want a real break first, act on a hold, not a touch, keep the stop back through the zone, and set the target at the next level.
