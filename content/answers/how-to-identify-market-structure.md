You identify market structure by marking confirmed swing highs and swing lows, then reading their order: higher highs and higher lows mean an uptrend, lower highs and lower lows a downtrend, and overlapping swings a range. The hard part is not the reading, it is deciding which swings count.

## Step 1: choose a swing rule and write it down

[Market structure](/market-structure/) is only as objective as your swing rule. Three common rules are:

| Swing rule | How a swing high is confirmed | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fractal (2 bars each side) | A high with two lower highs on each side | Simple, mechanical | Many small swings; confirms two bars late |
| N-bar rule (for example 5 each side) | A high with N lower highs on each side | Fewer, more important swings | Confirms N bars late |
| Percentage or ATR reversal | Price falls a set amount (say 1.5 ATR) from a high | Adapts to volatility | Needs a setting; still confirms late |

The two-bars-each-side version is Bill Williams' [fractal](/williams-fractals/). Whatever rule you pick, every swing is confirmed after it happens. That delay is normal, not a flaw to hide. The [swing highs and lows](/swing-highs-lows/) guide covers each rule in more depth.

## Step 2: label each confirmed swing

Compare every new swing high with the previous swing high, and every new swing low with the previous swing low.

- Higher high (HH): a swing high above the last swing high.
- Lower high (LH): a swing high below the last swing high.
- Higher low (HL): a swing low above the last swing low.
- Lower low (LL): a swing low below the last swing low.

## Worked labelling example

Here are nine confirmed swings on a share price, in order, using a fractal rule.

| # | Swing | Price | Compared with | Label |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Low | 40.00 | (first) | Start |
| 2 | High | 44.50 | (first) | Start |
| 3 | Low | 42.10 | 40.00 | HL |
| 4 | High | 46.80 | 44.50 | HH |
| 5 | Low | 44.20 | 42.10 | HL |
| 6 | High | 46.30 | 46.80 | LH |
| 7 | Low | 43.60 | 44.20 | LL |
| 8 | High | 45.90 | 46.30 | LH |
| 9 | Low | 42.80 | 43.60 | LL |

Swings 1 to 5 form an uptrend: higher highs and higher lows. Swing 6 is the first warning, a lower high. Swing 7 breaks below the last higher low (44.20), which many traders call a change of character. Swings 8 and 9 confirm a downtrend. Between swings 5 and 7 the structure was mixed, which is common: trends rarely flip cleanly.

## Step 3: decide what counts as a break

When price passes a prior swing, decide whether a wick through it counts or only a close beyond it. Close rules give fewer false breaks; wick rules react sooner. Either is fine if it is written down and applied the same way every time. The difference is explained in [what is a break of structure?](/answers/what-is-a-break-of-structure/).

## Step 4: check the timeframe

Structure is fractal: a downtrend on a 1-hour chart can be a pullback inside a daily uptrend. Label the timeframe you trade, then glance at one higher timeframe for context. Labelling five timeframes usually produces conflicting stories rather than clarity.

## Common mistakes

- Picking swings by eye and changing the rule from chart to chart.
- Counting the latest bar as a swing before it is confirmed.
- Treating one lower high as a trend change. One lower high is a warning; a break of the last higher low is the usual confirmation.
- Ignoring that a range produces alternating HH, LL, LH, HL labels that mean little on their own.

## Verdict

Market structure is identified by labelling confirmed swings and reading the sequence. Write down the swing rule, the break rule (wick or close) and the timeframe before you start. With those three fixed, two people should label the same chart the same way. Without them, structure becomes whatever the reader wants to see.
