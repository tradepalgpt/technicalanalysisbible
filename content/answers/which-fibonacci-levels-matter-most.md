The most widely watched [Fibonacci retracement](/fibonacci-retracement/) levels are 38.2%, 50% and 61.8%, and the most widely used extension is 1.618. "Matter most" here means the most quoted and most used in textbooks and charting software; there is no published, independent evidence that any one level works better than the others.

## Where the standard levels come from

Every standard ratio is a power or root of the golden ratio, about 1.618, or a convention. Dividing a Fibonacci number by the next one gives about 0.618; by the one two places on, about 0.382; by the one three places on, about 0.236. The [golden ratio](/golden-ratio/) guide covers the maths.

| Level | How it is derived | Use | Standing in textbooks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.236 | 0.618 cubed | Shallow retracement | Standard, often called minor |
| 0.382 | 0.618 squared | Retracement | Core level |
| 0.5 | Halfway; not a Fibonacci ratio | Retracement | Core level, from Dow theory and Gann |
| 0.618 | 1 / 1.618 | Retracement | Core level |
| 0.786 | Square root of 0.618 | Deep retracement | Standard; key in harmonic patterns |
| 0.886 | Fourth root of 0.618 | Deep retracement | Harmonic patterns (the bat) |
| 1.272 | Square root of 1.618 | Extension | Common nearer target |
| 1.618 | The golden ratio | Extension | Core extension |
| 2.618 | 1.618 squared | Extension | Used for strong trends |

The derived values check out with a quick calculation: 0.618 x 0.618 = 0.382, 0.618 x 0.382 = 0.236, the square root of 0.618 is 0.786 and the square root of 1.618 is 1.272 (all rounded to three places).

## Why 38.2%, 50% and 61.8% get the attention

These three sit in the middle of the range and match older ideas about pullbacks. Dow theory writers described secondary reactions of roughly one third to two thirds of the prior move, and 38.2% to 61.8% falls inside that band. Our answer on [how deep a pullback should be](/answers/how-deep-should-a-pullback-be/) covers that background.

The 61.8% level gets extra attention because it is the golden ratio itself. The 23.6% level is close to the end of the move and is often passed through in a strong trend. The 78.6% level is deep enough that many traders treat a break of it as a sign the move has failed.

For [Fibonacci extensions](/fibonacci-extension/), 1.618 is the headline level, with 1.272 as a nearer target and 2.618 for extended trends.

## Being widely watched cuts both ways

If many traders place orders near 61.8%, price may react there simply because orders sit there. That is an attention effect, not a property of the number. The same crowding also makes levels easy to run through: stops placed just beyond a popular level can be triggered together.

There is a second problem. Five retracement levels spread across a move leave little room between them. If you count a touch within 2% of the move as "at a level", the five standard levels cover about 20% of the range. Some level will often be close to wherever price turns. Our answer on [whether Fibonacci levels work](/answers/do-fibonacci-levels-work/) works through that arithmetic.

## How traders use the hierarchy

- **Retracement entries:** 38.2% in strong trends, 50% to 61.8% in ordinary pullbacks, 78.6% as a last line before the swing is considered broken.
- **Extension targets:** 1.272 first, 1.618 next, 2.618 only in extended moves.
- **Harmonic patterns:** 0.786 and 0.886 matter more here, because the pattern rules are written around them.

## Verdict

If you use only a few levels, 38.2%, 50% and 61.8% for retracements and 1.618 for extensions are the standard set, and they are what most other traders see. That is a statement about popularity, not evidence of an edge. Treat every level as a zone to watch and judge it by what price does there, not by the number itself.
