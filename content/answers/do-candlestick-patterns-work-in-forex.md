Candlestick patterns form on forex charts and are read the same way, but the market's structure changes them: the daily candle depends on your platform's closing time, gaps only appear at weekends, and volume is tick volume. Our own tests do not yet cover forex, so treat any precise forex pattern statistic with care.

## Why forex candles are different

A [candlestick](/candlestick-chart/) records the open, high, low and close of a period. On a stock, the daily open is set by an auction after a night of no trading, and the close by the exchange's closing auction. Spot forex has neither. It trades around the clock from Sunday evening to Friday evening, across banks and brokers, with no central exchange. That creates three differences.

### 1. The daily close is a convention

Most forex platforms end the daily candle at 5pm New York time, a widely used market convention. Some brokers' servers use a different time. If yours closes at midnight UK time, you get a short Sunday candle each week and five weekday candles with different opens and closes from everyone else's. A shooting star on one platform can be an ordinary candle on another.

| Daily close setting | What you see |
|---|---|
| 5pm New York | Five daily candles a week, the most common convention |
| Midnight in another time zone | Often a small extra Sunday candle; weekday candles shifted |
| Broker server time | Varies; check before trusting a daily pattern |

### 2. Gaps only at the weekend

During the week each candle opens at or very near the previous close. Patterns whose classic rules need a gap rarely form:

- the classic piercing line and [dark cloud cover](/dark-cloud-cover/), which need the second candle to open beyond the first one's high or low;
- the morning and evening star, where the middle candle gaps away;
- the abandoned baby and the kicker.

Most forex traders relax these rules so the second candle only needs to open near the first close. That makes the patterns possible, but it changes them. A relaxed dark cloud cover on EUR/USD is a weaker thing than one on a stock that gapped up on news and then sold off all day.

The weekend gap, between Friday's close and Sunday's open, is the one regular exception. Patterns that include a Monday open sometimes form there.

### 3. Tick volume, not volume

There is no central record of how much currency changed hands. Platforms show [tick volume](/tick-volume/), the number of price changes in the period. It tends to rise and fall with real activity and is a rough stand-in, but it is not the same thing as exchange volume, and it varies between brokers.

## Sessions matter

The forex day is really three sessions: Asia, London and New York. Most movement happens in London and in the London-New York overlap. A long wick formed in thin late-Asian trading means less than one formed at the London [session open](/session-open/). On intraday charts it is common to read candles only during active hours.

## Practical adjustments for forex

1. **Use a 5pm New York close** for daily charts, so your candles match most other traders'.
2. **Write down your gap rules.** If you relax "opens below the prior low" to "opens at or below the prior close", say so in your journal and apply it every time.
3. **Ignore thin-hours wicks** on intraday charts, or mark them.
4. **Check the news calendar.** A long-wick candle on a central-bank decision is a news reaction.
5. **Size the stop from the average range**, not just the wick. Major pairs often move 60 to 100 pips a day; a stop 15 pips beyond a hammer's low may be inside the noise.

## Worked example

This is an illustration with round numbers, not a real trade.

GBP/USD rises from 1.2500 to 1.2800 over two weeks on a 5pm New York daily chart. Last month's high was 1.2810. Today opens at 1.2790, trades up to 1.2850, and closes at 1.2770. Upper wick 60 pips, body 20 pips: a shooting star above last month's high.

On a broker whose day closes at midnight UK time, the same move is split differently: one candle shows the rally to 1.2850 and a close at 1.2820, the next opens at 1.2820 and falls. Neither looks like a shooting star.

A trader on the New York chart sells at the next open, 1.2768, with a stop one average daily range (say 80 pips) above the signal close at 1.2850. Risk 82 pips; target at 2R, 1.2604.

## Verdict

Candlestick patterns work in forex in the sense that the shapes form and tell the same story about each period. But the daily close time decides which candles you see, gap-based patterns need relaxed rules, and volume is a rough proxy. We have no forex test result of our own yet.
