Day traders mark yesterday's high and low as the first levels of the day. Price breaking out of yesterday's range and holding suggests a directional day; price poking beyond it and closing back inside suggests a failed break and often a move back across the range. Opening inside or outside the range also sets the tone.

## Why these two levels matter

The [previous day high and low](/previous-day-high-low/) mark the edges of where the market was willing to trade yesterday. Breaking above yesterday's high means buyers paid a price that nobody paid yesterday. Failing there means the market refused, again.

They also have the attention advantage: they are objective, they appear on most platforms, and almost every day trader can see them. Stops from yesterday's trades often sit just beyond them.

## Four standard readings

| Reading | What happens | Typical response |
|---|---|---|
| Break and hold | Price trades beyond the level and holds on retests | Trade with the break; target the next higher-timeframe level |
| Break and fail | Price trades beyond, then closes back inside | Trade back into the range; stop beyond the poke |
| Open outside | Day opens beyond yesterday's range (a gap) | Watch whether price returns into the range |
| Inside day | Price stays within yesterday's range | Range tactics; levels mark the edges |

## Worked example 1: break and hold

A share's previous day: high 82.40, low 80.10. It opens at 81.90. In the first hour it rises through 82.40 to 82.90, pulls back to 82.50, holds above 82.40 on the 15-minute closes, and rises again.

A trader buys at 82.70 on the second push, with a stop at 82.15, below the old high plus a buffer. Risk 0.55. The next level is a daily swing high at 84.00, about 2.4R. The previous day's high acted as support on the retest: [role reversal](/role-reversal/) on an intraday scale.

## Worked example 2: break and fail

Same share another day. Previous high 82.40. Price rises to 82.65 in the first half hour, then sells off and closes the 15-minute bar at 82.10, back inside yesterday's range.

That is a [false breakout](/false-breakout/). Buyers above 82.40 are trapped. A trader sells at 82.05 with a stop at 82.80, above the poke. Risk 0.75. Yesterday's midpoint is 81.25, about 1.1R; yesterday's low is 80.10, about 2.6R.

## Worked example 3: open outside

The share gaps up and opens at 83.20, above yesterday's high of 82.40. Two common readings:

- If price holds above 82.40, the gap is accepted, and yesterday's high is support.
- If price falls back below 82.40 into yesterday's range, the gap has failed, and traders often look for a move towards the other side of the range.

The gap's edge and yesterday's high are the same level here, which is why this is one of the most watched setups at the open.

## Why your levels may not match

The previous day depends on how your data defines a day.

| Market | Common difference |
|---|---|
| Shares | Including pre-market and after-hours trading or not |
| Futures | Full electronic session or regular trading hours only |
| Forex | Daily close time: 5pm New York for many brokers, midnight for some |
| Crypto | 24/7: midnight UTC is common but not universal |

Two traders can look at "yesterday's high" and see different numbers. The level most traders watch is usually the one from the main session in the main time zone. If you are unsure, plot both and see which the market reacts to.

## Combining with other levels

Yesterday's high and low are a starting map. Add:

- The [initial balance](/initial-balance/): the first hour's range. A break of both the initial balance and yesterday's range is a stronger trend sign than either alone.
- Daily and weekly swing levels, which often sit beyond yesterday's range and make sensible targets.
- Round numbers near the levels.

## Common mistakes

- Buying the first tick above yesterday's high, which is where false breaks catch traders.
- Stops right beyond yesterday's high or low, where everyone else's sit.
- Using the levels in a market with a different session definition from the one most traders use.

## Verdict

The previous day's high and low are among the most useful intraday levels because they are objective and widely watched. The readings are simple: break and hold, break and fail, open outside, or inside day. Want a close and a hold before trusting a break, keep stops beyond the poke, and check that your platform's "day" is the one most traders use.
