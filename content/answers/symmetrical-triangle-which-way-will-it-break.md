You cannot know in advance. A symmetrical triangle shows buyers and sellers both giving ground, so it is a bilateral pattern: it can break either way. Textbooks often say it tends to continue the prior trend, but that is a lean, not a rule, and most traders simply wait for a close outside it.

## What the pattern shows

A [symmetrical triangle](/symmetrical-triangle/) has falling highs and rising lows. The two lines converge towards a point called the apex. Each swing is smaller than the last. The market is going quiet: neither side is willing to push.

Compare it with its two cousins:

| Pattern | Upper line | Lower line | Who is pressing | Textbook bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Ascending triangle](/ascending-triangle/) | Flat | Rising | Buyers | Up |
| [Descending triangle](/descending-triangle/) | Falling | Flat | Sellers | Down |
| Symmetrical triangle | Falling | Rising | Neither | With the prior trend, loosely |

In the ascending and descending versions, one side is clearly more eager. In the symmetrical version, both sides are retreating at a similar rate. That is why its direction is so hard to call.

## Clues traders look at

None of these settles it. Each is a reason to lean, not to commit.

| Clue | Leaning up | Leaning down |
|---|---|---|
| Prior trend | A rise into the triangle | A fall into the triangle |
| Higher timeframe | Above a rising average, room above | Below a falling average, room below |
| Where price sits inside | Pressing the upper line | Pressing the lower line |
| Volume (stocks, futures) | Higher on up-swings | Higher on down-swings |
| Last swing | Last low barely pulled back | Last high barely bounced |
| Momentum | Oscillator making higher lows | Oscillator making lower highs |

We have not tested any of these clues on triangles.

## Timing: early break or apex?

A common observation in the books, including Edwards and Magee, is that breaks well before the apex are cleaner than breaks right at it. By the time the lines meet, the range is so narrow that a "break" may be just a small move. Many traders treat a triangle that reaches its apex without breaking as no pattern at all.

## Handling both outcomes

Because the direction is unknown, many traders plan both sides before the break:

1. Mark the upper and lower lines.
2. Decide the trigger: a close beyond either line.
3. Set the measured target for each side: the triangle's widest height projected from the break.
4. Set the stop for each side: beyond the last swing inside the triangle on the opposite side.
5. Check that both trades are worth taking in R. If one side runs straight into a big level, that side may not be.

### Worked example in R

This is an illustration with round numbers, not a real trade.

A share has risen from 30 to 50. It then forms a triangle: highs at 50, 48 and 46.50; lows at 42, 43.50 and 44.50. Widest height: 50 − 42 = 8. The last high is 46.50 and the last low is 44.50.

**Upside break:** closes at 46.30, above the upper line (now about 46).

- Entry: next open, 46.40. Stop below the last low: 44.30. Risk: 2.10.
- Target: 46 + 8 = 54. Reward: 7.60. **3.6R.**

**Downside break:** closes at 44.00, below the lower line (now about 44.70).

- Entry: next open, 43.90. Stop above the last high: 46.70. Risk: 2.80.
- Target: 44.70 − 8 = 36.70. Reward: 7.20. **2.6R.**

Both sides are worth taking on paper. The prior rise is the reason some traders would lean up and size the upside trade normally while sizing the downside one smaller, or skip it. That lean is a judgement call, not a tested rule.

Note the stop in each case sits beyond the last swing inside the triangle. As triangles narrow, this stop gets tighter, which is part of why triangle breakouts often show attractive R multiples.

## False breaks in triangles

Triangles have a known trap: price breaks one way, fails, and then breaks hard the other way. Some traders treat that failure as a signal in itself. Our answer on [false breakouts](/answers/what-is-a-false-breakout/) covers the warning signs.

## Verdict

A symmetrical triangle does not tell you which way it will break. It is a pause where both sides are giving ground. The prior trend gives a loose lean, and the higher timeframe, volume and momentum can add context, but none settles it. Plan both sides, wait for a close outside the lines, and keep the stop beyond the last swing inside.
