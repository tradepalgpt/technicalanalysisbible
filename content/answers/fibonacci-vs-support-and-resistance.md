Neither is better in general, and they answer slightly different questions. Support and resistance marks prices where the market has actually turned before; Fibonacci marks fractions of a recent swing where it might turn. Many traders use Fibonacci only where there is no earlier price history, or as a check on existing levels.

## What each one is based on

[Support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/) levels come from price history: earlier swing highs and lows, ranges, gaps and heavily traded prices. They are evidence of where buying or selling once appeared.

[Fibonacci retracement](/fibonacci-retracement/) levels come from arithmetic on one swing. They exist even where price has never traded before, such as inside a fresh rally.

| Feature | Support and resistance | Fibonacci retracement |
|---|---|---|
| Based on | Where price turned before | Fractions of a chosen swing |
| Main subjective choice | Which old turns count; zone width | Which swing to measure |
| Works in new territory | No, needs history | Yes |
| Number of levels | Depends on the chart | Five per swing, always |
| Who sees the same level | Anyone looking at the same old turn | Only traders who chose the same swing |
| Evidence of an edge | Mixed; see our [support and resistance answer](/answers/does-support-and-resistance-work/) | Not established; see [do Fibonacci levels work?](/answers/do-fibonacci-levels-work/) |

## Worked example: when they disagree

A rise runs from 100 to 200. Before the rise, the market had a range with a ceiling at 145 that capped price three times. The Fibonacci levels of the rise are 176.4, 161.8, 150.0, 138.2 and 121.4.

As price pulls back, a trader using support and resistance watches 145, the old ceiling that may now act as support. A Fibonacci trader watches 150 and 138.2. The two approaches point to different prices 5 to 7 points apart.

A trader who uses both might treat 138.2 to 150 as one zone, because it contains the old ceiling and two Fibonacci levels. That is the idea behind [confluence](/answers/what-is-fibonacci-confluence/). It can also mean the zone is simply wide.

Planned in R, the zone changes position size. A long filled at 145.40 (the ask) with a first stop at 134.90, below the zone and the 61.8% level, risks 10.50 per unit. A long filled at 150.40 with the same stop risks 15.50 per unit, so it needs about a third less size for the same 1R in money. A clean stop is exactly -1R either way, with costs and gaps on top.

## Strengths and weaknesses side by side

**Support and resistance** is grounded in what happened, and many traders see the same old high. But old levels can be far apart, and in a new trend there may be none nearby.

**Fibonacci** always gives levels, at regular spacing, in any market. But the levels depend on the swing chosen, and five levels per swing means price is often near one by chance.

**Round numbers** are a third source many traders combine with both; see [round numbers](/round-numbers/).

## How traders combine them

- Use support and resistance as the main map, and Fibonacci to fill gaps where there is no history.
- Use Fibonacci to judge pullback depth (shallow, normal, deep) and support and resistance to choose the exact zone.
- Prefer zones where both agree, while remembering that agreement is common when there are many levels.

## Verdict

Support and resistance is based on where price actually turned; Fibonacci is based on arithmetic on a swing. Neither has established predictive power over the other. Support and resistance is more grounded where history exists; Fibonacci is more useful where it does not. Many traders use both and plan every trade in R regardless of which level they chose.
