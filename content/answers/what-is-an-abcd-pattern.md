An AB=CD pattern is a four-point swing in which the second leg, CD, is the same length in price as the first leg, AB. In its harmonic form, C retraces a Fibonacci ratio of AB, and CD is a matching Fibonacci projection of the BC pullback, so the ratio rules point to where D should land.

## Equal legs and measured moves

The equal-leg idea is old and simple. It is the same as a [measured move](/measured-move/): the next leg roughly repeats the previous one. Our answer on [what a measured move is](/answers/what-is-a-measured-move/) covers that general version. This answer covers the Fibonacci ratio rules that harmonic traders attach to it.

The [ABCD pattern](/abcd-pattern/) is also the base of most [harmonic patterns](/harmonic-patterns/): a Gartley or a bat is an AB=CD with an extra X point in front.

## Textbook ratio pairs

Harmonic writers, including Scott Carney and Larry Pesavento, pair each BC retracement with a CD projection. The pairs are reciprocals: when C retraces 0.618 of AB, a CD of 1.618 times BC makes the legs equal.

| C retraces AB by | CD projects BC by | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 0.382 | 2.24 to 2.618 | Equal legs at 2.618; slightly short at 2.24 |
| 0.5 | 2.0 | Equal legs |
| 0.618 | 1.618 | Equal legs |
| 0.786 | 1.272 | Equal legs |
| 0.886 | 1.13 | Equal legs |

The pairs are rounded; 0.618 x 1.618 = 1.00 and 0.786 x 1.272 = 1.00 to two places. Some authors also accept CD at 1.272 or 1.618 times AB, an extended version.

## Worked example: bullish AB=CD

A fall from A = 200 to B = 150 (AB = 50), a bounce to C, then a second fall to D.

| Step | Calculation | Price |
|---|---|---|
| C at 0.618 of AB | 150 + 0.618 x 50 | 180.9 |
| BC | 180.9 - 150 | 30.9 |
| D by equal legs | 180.9 - 50 | 130.9 |
| D by 1.618 x BC | 180.9 - 1.618 x 30.9 | 130.9 |
| D by 1.272 x BC (shallower) | 180.9 - 1.272 x 30.9 | 141.6 |

The equal-leg and 1.618 projections agree at 130.9, which is why harmonic traders treat that as the completion point for a 0.618 C. The 1.272 projection at 141.6 is a nearer level that some watch first.

## A trade in R

A long fills at 131.10 (the ask) near D, with a first stop at 127.00 below D. 1R = 4.10 per unit times size. A common first target is a 38.2% retracement of CD: 130.9 + 0.382 x 50 = 150.0, near B.

| Outcome | Exit | Result in R |
|---|---|---|
| Target, exit at 149.95 (bid) | 149.95 | (149.95 - 131.10) / 4.10 = +4.60R |
| Stop hit cleanly | 127.00 | -1.00R |
| Gap through stop | Worse than 127.00 | Worse than -1R |

Commission is added on top of each result. After the fill the stop may only be tightened.

## What to be careful about

- **Time is ignored by the price rule.** Some writers also want CD to take a similar number of bars to AB; others do not.
- **Several possible Ds.** With 1.272, 1.618 and equal legs all in play, the "completion" is really a zone.
- **Swing choice.** Different A and B points give different Ds.
- **No established edge.** We know of no independent, rules-based test that shows AB=CD turns beat a random baseline.

## Verdict

AB=CD is the equal-legs idea with Fibonacci rules attached: C retraces AB by a standard ratio and D sits where CD matches AB or projects BC by the reciprocal ratio. It is a clear way to mark a zone and plan a trade in R. Treat D as a zone to watch, not a forecast.
