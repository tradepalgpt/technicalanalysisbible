The bullish engulfing is usually ranked the stronger of the two, because its second candle closes above the whole of the first candle's body, while a piercing line only reaches past the midpoint. In practice the engulfing is also far more common, which made it the only one of the two we could test properly.

## How they differ

Both are two-candle bullish reversal patterns after a fall. Both start with a red candle. The difference is how far the second, green candle gets.

A [bullish engulfing](/bullish-engulfing/) pattern: the green body completely covers the red body. It opens at or below the red close and closes above the red open. In one period, buyers wiped out all of the previous period's selling and more.

A [piercing line](/piercing-line/): the green candle opens below the red candle's low (some sources say below its close), then closes above the midpoint of the red body but below its open. Buyers recovered more than half the previous period's selling, but not all of it.

| | Bullish engulfing | Piercing line |
|---|---|---|
| Second candle opens | At or below the first close | Below the first low (classic) or close |
| Second candle closes | Above the first open | Above the first body's midpoint, below its open |
| How much selling is erased | All of it | Over half |
| Needs a gap? | Not by most definitions | Yes, in the classic version |
| Usual ranking | Stronger | Weaker |
| Our test, 10 US markets | 127 to 132 trades | 27 trades: too few to report |

The usual ranking makes sense. A pattern that erases all the selling shows more buying power than one that erases half. If the green candle in a piercing line had kept going, it would have become an engulfing pattern.

## The gap problem

The classic piercing line needs the green candle to open below the red candle's low: a [price gap](/price-gaps/) down, followed by a strong recovery. That is common on stocks, which reopen each morning at a fresh price. It is rare on forex and crypto, where each candle usually opens at the previous close. On those markets traders either relax the rule (opening below the close is enough) or rarely see the pattern at all.

The engulfing pattern, by most definitions, only needs the green candle to open at or below the red close. On a 24-hour market that happens all the time. So the two patterns are not only different in strength; they are different in how often they are possible.

## What our test found

Daily bars for 10 US stocks and funds (SPY, QQQ, IWM, Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Tesla, Amazon, JPMorgan, Exxon), January 2021 to October 2025, tuning period. Entry at the next open, 2R target, 20-day limit, costs both ways, compared with random entries in the same markets and quarters.

- **Bullish engulfing, stop 1 ATR from the signal close:** 132 trades, edge over random entry -0.05R per trade (95% range -0.30 to +0.21). **No evidence of an edge.**
- **Bullish engulfing, stop just beyond the pattern:** 127 trades, edge +0.10R per trade (95% range -0.10 to +0.31). **Exploratory, not a tested result**; the range includes zero.
- **Piercing line:** 27 trades in almost five years. Under our rules, fewer than 30 trades means no figure is published.

([How we test](/methods/).)

So the honest answer to "which is stronger?" from our data is: the stronger-looking one showed no clear edge, and the weaker-looking one was too rare to measure on these markets. The comparison page for these two patterns covers shape and psychology; this count is the part it does not have.

One more detail: for the engulfing pattern, the stop just beyond the pattern did slightly better than the ATR stop. That is the reverse of what we saw on single-candle patterns. An engulfing pattern's low is the lower of two candles, so a stop under it is usually wider and sits outside the next day's noise; only 16% of those trades ended on the entry day.

## Worked example

This is an illustration with round numbers, not a real trade.

Day 1: a stock opens at 52, closes at 50. Red body from 52 to 50, midpoint 51.

**Engulfing version.** Day 2 opens at 49.80 and closes at 52.40, above the red open. A trader buys at the next open, 52.50, with a stop below the two-candle low of 49.50 at 49.40. Risk: 3.10. Target 2R: 58.70.

**Piercing version.** Day 2 opens at 49.30, below Day 1's low of 49.50, and closes at 51.40, above the 51 midpoint but below 52. A trader buys at 51.50 with a stop at 49.20. Risk: 2.30. Target 2R: 56.10.

The engulfing entry is higher and its risk larger, because the pattern itself already contains more of the move.

## Verdict

By definition the bullish engulfing is the stronger pattern: it erases all of the prior selling. In our tuning-period test it still showed no evidence of an edge over random entry with an ATR stop, and the piercing line was too rare on these 10 markets to measure. Choose between them on location and context, not on which name sounds stronger. If the second candle is small and sits inside the first, you have a different pattern: see [What is a harami and how do you trade it?](/answers/what-is-a-harami-and-how-do-you-trade-it/).
