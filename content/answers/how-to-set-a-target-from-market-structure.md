The usual structure targets are the next opposing swing high or low, the edge of a prior range, and a measured move that repeats an earlier leg. Each gives a price; dividing the distance from your fill to that price by your 1R distance tells you what the target is worth in R.


## Three structure targets

This post takes the measured move as one of three methods. For the measured move on its own, with both of its versions, see our existing post [what is a measured move?](/answers/what-is-a-measured-move/).

| Method | Where the target goes | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Next opposing swing | Just before the last swing high (for a long) | Obvious level others watch | Often close; caps the trade early |
| Prior range edge | The far side of an earlier consolidation | Clear on most charts | May be far away or absent |
| [Measured move](/measured-move/) | Pullback low plus the length of the previous leg | Gives a target in open space | A rough guide, often missed or exceeded |
| Trail instead | No fixed target; a [trailing stop](/trailing-stop/) follows structure | Captures long trends | Gives back part of each move |

## One trade, three targets

An index on the 4-hour chart.

- First leg: from a swing low of 4,410 to a swing high of 4,510, 100 points.
- Pullback low: 4,490.
- Entry on a break back above 4,510: the long fills at 4,512.5 (the ask).
- First stop under the pullback low: 4,488.
- 1R in points: 4,512.5 - 4,488 = 24.5. With 4 contracts at 10 per point, 1R = 24.5 x 4 x 10 = 980.

The daily chart shows an older swing high at 4,560.

| Target | Price | Fill to target (points) | Target in R |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily swing high | 4,560 | 47.5 | +1.94R |
| Measured move: 4,490 + 100 | 4,590 | 77.5 | +3.16R |

A clean stop-out is exactly -1R, -980, with commission and any slippage on top. A gap below 4,488 would make it worse, recorded as filled.

## Choosing between them

The swing target is closer and was a real turning point before, so it is more likely to be reached than a target further away. But nothing on this chart says how much more likely. The measured move gives more reward per unit of risk, but measured moves are approximations; the market has no reason to stop at exactly 4,590.

Many traders combine them: take part of the position off at the nearer level and hold the rest towards the further one, with the stop raised under the next higher low. Raising the stop is allowed; lowering it is not. That turns a fixed-target trade into a partly trailed one.

The [risk-reward ratio](/risk-reward-ratio/) is only half the picture. A +3R target that is rarely hit can be worse than a +1.5R target that usually is. Expectancy needs both the size of wins and how often they happen, which only comes from a recorded set of trades.

## Rules that keep targets honest

- **Set the target before entry.** Moving it closer when the trade goes your way is a form of hindsight.
- **Check room first.** If the nearest opposing swing is less than 1R away, the setup may not be worth taking at all.
- **Use confirmed swings only.** Our [swing highs and lows](/swing-highs-lows/) guide explains confirmation. An unconfirmed high may still extend.
- **Allow for the spread on exit.** A long exits at the bid. A target placed exactly at a swing high can miss by the spread.

## Common mistakes

- Using a far measured-move target to make a weak setup look good in R.
- Ignoring a higher-timeframe swing sitting between entry and target.
- Counting a target "almost hit" as a win in a trade log.

## Verdict

Structure targets come from the next opposing swing, a prior range edge or a measured move. Work out each one in R from your real fill before you enter. Nearer swings give smaller, likelier targets; measured moves give larger, rougher ones. Pick a rule, write it down, and let your trade record show which works for you.
