A morning doji star is a morning star whose middle candle is a doji, with the open and close almost equal, rather than a small body. Both are three-candle bullish reversals after a fall; the doji version is usually rated slightly stronger, and both are rare when defined strictly.

## The morning star

A [morning star](/morning-star/) has three candles after a fall:

1. **A long red candle.** Sellers are in full control.
2. **A small-bodied candle, the star,** that opens below the first candle's close (classically with a gap between the two bodies). It can be either colour. Selling has stalled.
3. **A long green candle** that closes well into the first candle's body, usually above its midpoint. Buyers have taken over.

The name comes from the idea of the morning star (the planet Venus) appearing before sunrise: the small middle candle comes just before the turn.

## The morning doji star

A [morning doji star](/morning-doji-star/) is the same pattern with one change: the middle candle is a doji. Its open and close are equal or almost equal, so buyers and sellers ended the period exactly level.

That is why it is usually rated stronger. In a morning star, the middle candle shows selling has slowed. In a morning doji star, it shows the two sides perfectly balanced at the low. The third candle then shows buyers winning outright. Some writers warn that a doji star is more uncertain, because a doji can come before a further fall too, so it still depends on the third candle.

## How rare they are

The classic rules need a gap below the first candle's close, then a strong recovery. On stocks that is possible because each day opens at a fresh auction. Even so, all three candles have to line up, and the third must close well into the first body.

Our detectors applied the standard rules to daily bars for 10 large US stocks and funds (SPY, QQQ, IWM, Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Tesla, Amazon, JPMorgan, Exxon), January 2021 to October 2025, the tuning period. They found:

- **Morning star: 9 trades.**
- **Morning doji star: 3 trades.**
- **Evening star: 16 trades.** Evening doji star: 8.
- **Abandoned baby: 0.**

All are under our 30-trade minimum, so we publish no result for any of them. ([How we test](/methods/).) This is the part the pattern guides cannot tell you: on liquid US markets, strictly defined stars are very rare. You might wait a year or more to see a clean one on a single stock's daily chart.

On markets that do not gap, such as forex and crypto, the gap rule almost never holds. Most traders and many platforms drop it, which makes the patterns much more common but also looser.

## How to trade them

The approach is the same for both:

1. **Context first.** A clear fall, ideally into a support level.
2. **The third candle is the confirmation.** Wait for it to close above the midpoint of the first candle's body.
3. **Enter** at the next open.
4. **Stop** below the star's low, which is the low of the pattern. Check it against the average daily range.
5. **Target** the next resistance, or a multiple of risk.

Because confirmation is built in, the entry is late. Much of the first leg of the move may already be done.

## Worked example

This is an illustration with round numbers, not a real trade.

A stock falls from 65 to 54 over three weeks.

- Day 1: opens 57, closes 54. Long red body; midpoint 55.50.
- Day 2: opens 53.40 (gap below 54), trades between 52.60 and 54.00, closes 53.40. Open equals close: a doji. Morning doji star in progress.
- Day 3: opens 53.80, closes 56.20, above the 55.50 midpoint.

A trader buys at the next open, 56.30, with a stop below the doji's low at 52.50. Risk 3.80. The 2R target is 63.90, just below the 65 start of the fall.

If Day 2 had closed at 53.90 instead of 53.40, the pattern would be an ordinary morning star. The trade would be identical.

## Verdict

The only difference is the middle candle: a doji in the morning doji star, a small body in the morning star. The doji version is usually rated slightly stronger, but both rely on the third candle and both are rare: our detectors found 9 morning stars and 3 morning doji stars on 10 US markets in almost five years, too few to measure. Treat them as signals you will rarely see cleanly.
