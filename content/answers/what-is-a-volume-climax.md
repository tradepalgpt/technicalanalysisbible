A volume climax is a bar with extreme volume, usually several times normal, that comes after an extended move. It is read as a possible sign that one side has spent its strength: a buying climax near a high, or a selling climax near a low.

## Buying and selling climaxes

A [volume climax](/volume-climax/) is named for which side appears to be exhausted.

| Type | Where it appears | Typical bar | Classic reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buying climax | After a long rise | Wide range, very high volume, often closes off the high | Late buyers met heavy supply |
| Selling climax | After a long fall | Wide range, very high volume, often closes off the low | Panic selling met heavy demand |
| Breakout bar | At the edge of a base or range | High volume, closes near the extreme | Not a climax: the start of a move |

The last row is the trap. A high-volume bar at the start of a move looks very similar to one at the end. Location is what separates them. For the breakout case, see [what volume tells you about a breakout](/answers/what-does-volume-tell-you-about-a-breakout/) and our [breakout trading](/breakout-trading/) guide.

## Where the idea comes from

The [Wyckoff method](/wyckoff-method/), from Richard Wyckoff's work in the early twentieth century, names both climaxes as stages in its accumulation and distribution schematics. A selling climax is followed by an automatic rally and a secondary test; a buying climax by an automatic reaction and a test of the high. Volume spread analysis uses similar ideas. These are frameworks for reading a chart, described here neutrally, not tested rules.

## How to spot one

Most definitions combine three things:

1. **Extended move.** Price has travelled a long way, often with acceleration.
2. **Extreme volume.** Volume is a large multiple of normal. [Relative volume](/relative-volume/) is the easy way to measure it.
3. **Wide range and a weak close for the trend direction.** In a buying climax, the bar closes in the lower part of its range.

## Worked example

A share's 20-day average volume is 1.0 million.

| Day | Situation | Volume | Relative volume | Close in bar's range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Twelfth up day in fifteen | 1.6m | 1.6 | Near high |
| 2 | Gap up, wide range | 3.8m | 3.8 | Lower third |
| 3 | Inside day | 1.1m | 1.1 | Middle |
| 4 | Fails to exceed day 2 high | 0.7m | 0.7 | Lower half |

Day 2 fits the description of a buying climax: an extended run, 3.8 times normal volume, and a close in the lower third after trading to a new high. Days 3 and 4 add the follow-up many traders want: no new high, on lighter volume. Nothing here says the top is in. It says the conditions in the textbook definition were met. Many such sequences pause and then trend higher again.

For how a short trade on a climax top would be measured in R, including gap risk, see [what high volume at a top means](/answers/what-does-high-volume-at-a-top-mean/).

## Why climaxes are hard to use

- **Definitions vary.** "Extreme" might mean twice normal or five times normal; there is no standard.
- **Hindsight.** A climax is easy to name once the trend has turned. In real time, the same bar may be one of several high-volume days in a continuing trend.
- **News.** Earnings, index changes and expiries create huge volume without any exhaustion.
- **Data.** On forex tick volume, a climax shows a burst of quote changes, which also happens on any sharp move.
- **Daily bars.** Today's volume is only known at the close. Any test must act after the climax bar, not on it.

## Common mistakes

- Calling every high-volume bar a climax.
- Ignoring location and mistaking a breakout bar for an exhaustion bar.
- Acting before any follow-through shows price has stopped progressing.
- Counting only the climaxes that marked turns when judging the idea.

## Verdict

A volume climax is an extreme-volume bar at the end of an extended move, read as one side running out. It is a well-known idea from Wyckoff and volume analysis, and worth noticing. On its own it is unreliable: definitions vary, breakout bars look the same, and many climaxes do not end the trend. Wait for follow-through and judge it in context.
