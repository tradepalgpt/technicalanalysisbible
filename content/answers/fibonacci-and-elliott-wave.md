Elliott wave theory uses Fibonacci ratios as guidelines for how far each wave tends to retrace or extend. R.N. Elliott described the wave structure in the 1930s and 1940s and linked it to the Fibonacci sequence; the detailed ratio guidelines most traders use come from A.J. Frost and Robert Prechter's *Elliott Wave Principle* (1978).

## The wave structure in brief

[Elliott wave](/elliott-wave/) theory says trends unfold in five waves (1 to 5) in the direction of the trend, followed by three corrective waves (A, B, C). Waves 2 and 4 are pullbacks inside the trend. The counts themselves come in Fibonacci numbers: 5 waves up, 3 down, 8 in total, then larger and smaller degrees built from the same shapes. That counting is where the Fibonacci link began.

## The published guideline ratios

These are guidelines, not rules. Elliott's strict rules are few (for example, wave 2 never retraces more than all of wave 1, and wave 3 is never the shortest impulse wave). The ratios below describe what Frost and Prechter call common relationships.

| Wave | Common guideline | Tool |
|---|---|---|
| Wave 2 | Retraces about 50% or 61.8% of wave 1 | [Retracement](/fibonacci-retracement/) |
| Wave 3 | Often about 1.618 times wave 1 | [Extension](/fibonacci-extension/) |
| Wave 4 | Often retraces about 38.2% of wave 3 | Retracement |
| Wave 5 | Often about equal to wave 1, or 0.618 of it | Extension |
| Wave B | Varies widely by correction type | Retracement |
| Wave C | Often about equal to wave A, or 1.618 of it | Extension |

The guidelines overlap and allow alternatives on purpose. Frost and Prechter also describe "alternation": if wave 2 is a sharp, deep pullback, wave 4 is more often a shallower, sideways one.

## Worked example: an idealised count

Wave 1 runs from 100 to 150, a move of 50.

| Wave | Guideline used | Calculation | End price |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Starting move | 100 to 150 | 150.0 |
| 2 | 50% retracement of wave 1 | 150 - 0.5 x 50 | 125.0 |
| 3 | 1.618 x wave 1 | 125 + 1.618 x 50 | 205.9 |
| 4 | 38.2% retracement of wave 3 | 205.9 - 0.382 x 80.9 | 175.0 |
| 5 | Equal to wave 1 | 175 + 50 | 225.0 |

This is a textbook shape, built from the guidelines. Real charts rarely line up this neatly, and the same chart can usually be counted more than one way.

## Using the guidelines in a trade plan

Suppose a trader counts a finished wave 2 and buys at 125.40 (the ask) for wave 3, with a first stop at 99.50 below the start of wave 1. That stop sits where the count is invalid, because wave 2 cannot retrace more than all of wave 1. 1R is 25.90 per unit times size. The wave 3 guideline at 205.9 is (205.9 - 125.4) / 25.9 = 3.11R away before costs. A clean stop is exactly -1R, with commission on top and any gap making it worse, and the stop only tightens after the fill.

## What to be careful about

- **Counts change.** Elliott practitioners often hold an alternate count. When price breaks the main count, the waves are relabelled, and the Fibonacci targets move with them.
- **Many guidelines, wide ranges.** With 38.2%, 50% and 61.8% all allowed for pullbacks, and several extension ratios for impulses, most outcomes fit some guideline.
- **No established edge.** We know of no independent, rules-based study showing Elliott wave forecasts beat a simple baseline. The theory is hard to test because the count is subjective.

## Verdict

Fibonacci ratios are Elliott wave's measuring tape: guidelines for how deep waves 2 and 4 pull back and how far waves 3, 5 and C extend, set out most fully by Frost and Prechter. They are useful for laying out scenarios and invalidation points, but the guidelines are loose and the count is subjective, so treat any Elliott-Fibonacci target as a scenario, not a forecast.
