Premium and discount zones divide a chosen price range at its 50% midpoint, called equilibrium. The upper half is the premium zone, where some traders prefer to sell, and the lower half is the discount zone, where they prefer to buy.

## How they are drawn

[Premium and discount](/premium-discount/) is a smart money concepts term, but the drawing is simple.

1. Pick a range: usually from a confirmed swing low to a confirmed swing high (or the reverse).
2. Mark the midpoint: (high + low) / 2. That is equilibrium.
3. Above equilibrium is premium; below is discount.

Many traders also mark the 25% and 75% levels to split each half again.

## Worked example

Gold makes a swing low at 1,800 and a swing high at 2,000.

| Level | Calculation | Price | Zone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Range high | | 2,000 | Top of premium |
| 75% | 1,800 + 200 x 0.75 | 1,950 | Deep premium above this |
| Equilibrium (50%) | 1,800 + 200 x 0.50 | 1,900 | Midpoint |
| 25% | 1,800 + 200 x 0.25 | 1,850 | Deep discount below this |
| Range low | | 1,800 | Bottom of discount |

In an uptrend, a trader using this idea would look for longs only when price pulls back below 1,900, into discount. In a downtrend, shorts only above the midpoint.

## How it changes the R picture

The logic is mostly about reward relative to risk. Take a long with a stop under the 1,800 low, at 1,795, and a target at the 2,000 high.

| Entry (filled at the ask) | Fill to stop | Fill to target | Target in R |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,950 (premium) | 155 | 50 | +0.32R |
| 1,900 (equilibrium) | 105 | 100 | +0.95R |
| 1,850 (discount) | 55 | 150 | +2.73R |

The deeper entry has a tighter stop for the same invalidation point and more room to the target. That is just arithmetic; it says nothing about how often each entry reaches its target. Deeper pullbacks happen less often, so many deep-entry orders never fill. A clean stop-out is -1R in every row, with costs on top.

## The older ideas underneath

- **Buy low in the range, sell high** is the core of [range trading](/range-trading/), which predates the term by decades.
- **The 50% retracement** has long been watched in Dow theory writing and by Gann followers. Fibonacci retracement adds levels such as 61.8%; that topic has its own pillar, and this post does not depend on it.
- **Value and price.** Market profile traders talk about price above or below value. Premium and discount is a simpler version based on a single range.

The [smart money concepts](/smart-money-concepts/) contribution is the vocabulary and the habit of only taking longs in discount and shorts in premium. That is a sensible filter for reward-to-risk; it is not evidence that the market respects the 50% line.

## Limits

- **The range is a choice.** Pick a different swing low and equilibrium moves. On the same chart, three traders can draw three ranges.
- **Strong trends rarely offer discount.** A market that pulls back only 20 to 30% never reaches the discount zone, so the filter skips it.
- **The midpoint is not a level the market "knows".** Treat it as a planning line, not as support.

## Common mistakes

- Redrawing the range after price moves, so the entry is always "in discount".
- Ignoring the trend: buying in discount in a downtrend is still buying in a downtrend.
- Treating the 50% line as a target the market must reach.

## Verdict

Premium and discount zones are a range split at its midpoint, with buying favoured below and selling above. The idea is old range logic under a new name. It helps keep entries where reward-to-risk is better, but it does not show the odds of reaching the target, and it depends entirely on which range you draw.
