Neither is better in every market. Horizontal levels are usually more reliable for stops and targets, because many traders see the same price. Trendlines are more useful for reading the slope of a trend and spotting when it changes. Most traders use horizontal levels as the main map and a trendline as a second opinion.

## What each one measures

A [horizontal level](/support-and-resistance/) is a fixed price where the market turned before. Orders, memories and round numbers sit at that price, and it stays the same until it breaks.

A [trendline](/trendline/) joins two or more swing lows in an uptrend, or swing highs in a downtrend. It slopes, so the "level" is a different price on every bar. It measures the pace of the trend rather than a price where orders rest.

| | Horizontal level | Trendline |
|---|---|---|
| Built from | Swing highs or lows at similar prices | Two or more rising lows or falling highs |
| Price of the level | Fixed | Changes every bar |
| Agreement between traders | High: most people see the same price | Lower: anchor choice and scale change the line |
| Effect of chart scale | None | A log chart and a linear chart give different lines |
| Best for | Stops, targets, ranges | Trend pace, early warning of a change |
| Main weakness | Says nothing about trend | Easy to redraw to fit |

## Why horizontal levels are usually firmer

Two traders drawing the same swing high will land within a few ticks of each other. Two traders drawing a trendline can pick different anchor points, use wicks or bodies, and look at a linear or a log scale. Their lines may be far apart a few months later. The more people who agree on a price, the more orders tend to cluster there. That is the main reason horizontal levels make better places for stops and targets.

Trendlines also invite redrawing. When price breaks a line, it is tempting to pick a new anchor and draw a shallower one. A horizontal level is harder to adjust after the fact.

## Where trendlines help

Trendlines do something a horizontal level cannot: they show whether a trend is speeding up, holding its pace or slowing. If each pullback stops on a rising line, the trend is steady. If price starts to fall short of the line or breaks it, the trend is losing pace, often before any horizontal level breaks. Treat that as a warning to tighten up, not as a reversal signal. Trendline breaks also produce many [false breakouts](/false-breakout/), because the line is easy to poke through.

## Worked example

A share has risen from 40 to 58 over six months. A trendline through the swing lows at 41, 46 and 51 is now at about 54.50. The last swing high, at 55, is a clear horizontal level just below price.

- Price pulls back to 55. The trendline and the old high are within half a point of each other. That overlap is the area to watch. If price holds there and turns up, the trend is intact.
- Suppose instead price breaks the trendline at 54.50 but holds 55 on a closing basis. The trend has slowed, but the horizontal level still holds. Many traders would keep the position and move the stop under the 55 zone rather than exit.
- If price then closes below the 55 zone as well, both tools agree. That is a stronger case that the move is over, and the old high may now act as resistance on a bounce (see [role reversal](/role-reversal/)).

## Using them together

1. Draw the horizontal levels first, on the daily or weekly chart.
2. Add one trendline per trend, through clear swing points only. Do not keep redrawing it.
3. Pay most attention where the trendline crosses a horizontal level. Those crossings are where both groups of traders are watching.
4. Put stops beyond the horizontal zone, not just beyond the trendline, because the trendline's price moves.
5. Treat a trendline break on its own as a warning, and a horizontal break as the decision.

## Verdict

For stops and targets, horizontal levels are the better tool: they are fixed, widely shared and harder to fudge. Trendlines earn their place as a measure of trend pace and an early warning. Use both, with horizontal levels in charge. We have not backtested either yet; this is a description of how the tools are used, not a performance claim.
