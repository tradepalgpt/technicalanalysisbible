There is no official number of candlestick patterns. Reference books describe anywhere from a few dozen to over 100 named patterns, many of them bullish and bearish mirrors of each other; our own library has 45 detectors, and most charts are covered by the five basic shapes and about a dozen common patterns.

## Why there is no fixed count

Candlestick patterns are a tradition, not a standard. They came from Japanese rice trading and were brought to most Western traders by Steve Nison's *Japanese Candlestick Charting Techniques* (1991). Later authors added patterns, split existing ones into variants, and gave some patterns more than one name. Thomas Bulkowski's *Encyclopedia of Candlestick Charts* (2008) covers over 100. Charting platforms ship anywhere from a dozen to several dozen built-in detectors.

The count depends on three choices:

1. **Do mirrors count twice?** A bullish engulfing and a bearish engulfing are one idea. Counting each direction separately nearly doubles the total.
2. **Do variants count?** Is a [morning doji star](/morning-doji-star/) a separate pattern from a [morning star](/morning-star/), or a version of it? Is a dragonfly doji separate from a [doji](/doji/)?
3. **Do basic shapes count?** A marubozu or spinning top is a single-candle shape. Some lists call them patterns; others do not.

## A useful way to count

A practical grouping, by number of candles:

| Group | Examples | Rough number of distinct ideas |
|---|---|---|
| Basic shapes | Marubozu, spinning top, doji | 3 to 5 |
| Single-candle patterns | Hammer, hanging man, inverted hammer, shooting star, dragonfly and gravestone doji, belt hold | About 8 to 10 |
| Two-candle patterns | Engulfing, harami, harami cross, piercing line, dark cloud cover, tweezers, kicker, counterattack, homing pigeon, on-neck | About 10 to 15 |
| Three-candle patterns | Morning and evening star, doji stars, abandoned baby, three soldiers and crows, three inside and outside, advance block, deliberation | About 10 to 15 |
| Four or more candles | Rising and falling three methods, mat hold, three-line strike, ladder bottom | About 5 |

That gives somewhere around 35 to 50 distinct ideas, which becomes 60 to 100 or more once each direction and variant gets its own name.

## How many actually show up

This is the part a list of names does not tell you. On daily bars for 10 large US stocks and funds over almost five years (January 2021 to October 2025, tuning period), only **14 of the 42 directional patterns** (18 of 55 pattern-and-direction pairs) produced the 30 trades we need before reporting any figure. Many famous patterns barely appeared:

| Pattern | Trades in almost five years, 10 markets |
|---|---|
| Doji (short side, stop 1 ATR) | 507 |
| Bearish engulfing | 202 |
| Hammer | 191 |
| Morning star | 9 |
| Three white soldiers | 5 |
| Morning doji star | 3 |
| Abandoned baby | 0 |

Trade counts undercount sightings slightly, because only one trade per pattern per market was open at a time. But the gap between the top and bottom of the list is huge. The patterns that fill candlestick books are not the ones that fill charts. For the full ranking, see [Which candlestick patterns are most common?](/answers/which-candlestick-patterns-are-most-common/).

Among the patterns with enough trades, the best-known ones showed **no evidence of an edge** over random entries (hammer: 191 trades, edge +0.02R per trade; bearish engulfing: 202 trades, -0.06R).

## How many you need to know

You do not need to learn 100 patterns. A sensible order:

1. **The five basic shapes:** marubozu, spinning top, doji, hammer-type, shooting-star-type. These let you read any candle.
2. **The common single and two-candle patterns:** hammer, hanging man, shooting star, inverted hammer, engulfing, harami. These appear on every chart.
3. **The main three-candle patterns:** morning and evening star, three soldiers and crows, three inside and outside. Rarer, but widely discussed.

That is roughly a dozen names. Beyond them, most patterns are variants or are rare enough that you may wait years to see a clean one. A trader who reads each candle as a story of the period (who won, how cleanly, where it closed) can describe an unfamiliar pattern without knowing its name. The [candlestick chart](/candlestick-chart/) guide covers reading a single candle, and the [bullish engulfing](/bullish-engulfing/) guide is a good model of a two-candle pattern.

## Verdict

There is no official count: over 100 named patterns exist across the reference books, roughly 35 to 50 distinct ideas once mirrors and variants are merged, and 45 in our detector library. Far fewer matter in practice. On 10 large US markets over almost five years, only 14 directional patterns appeared often enough to measure at all. Learn the five shapes and a dozen patterns well, rather than a hundred names. The [candlestick patterns guide](/guide/candlestick-patterns/) walks through all the main ones.
