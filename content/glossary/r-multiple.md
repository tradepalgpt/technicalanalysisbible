An R-multiple expresses a trade's result as a multiple of the amount initially risked, where 1R is the loss if the stop is hit and a 2R trade gains twice that.

## Explained

"R" stands for risk. Before entering, a trader fixes the distance to the [stop-loss](/stop-loss/). That distance, in money, is 1R. Every outcome is then measured against it: a full stop-out is minus 1R, a gain of twice the risk is plus 2R, and a trade closed at half the stop distance against you is minus 0.5R.

This makes trades of different sizes and prices comparable. A 5 point gain on one stock and a 200 point gain on an index can both be 2R. Adding up R-multiples across many trades shows whether winners outweigh losers.

It is closely related to the [risk-reward ratio](/risk-reward-ratio/), which is the planned R at the [profit target](/profit-target/). The R-multiple is the realised result. The size of 1R comes from [position sizing](/position-sizing/).

## Example

A trader buys at 100 with a stop at 98, so 1R is 2 points per share. The trade is closed at 106, a gain of 6 points, which is plus 3R. A second trade is stopped out for minus 1R, and a third closes at 101 for plus 0.5R. The three trades total plus 2.5R.
