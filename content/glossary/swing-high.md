A swing high is a peak in price that has lower highs on both sides of it, marking the top of a rally before price turned down.

## Explained

Swing highs are the building blocks of [market structure](/market-structure/). Traders mark them by looking left and right: a bar whose high is above the highs of the bars on either side. The number of bars checked is a choice, so a swing high on a one-minute chart is much smaller than one on a daily chart.

Connecting a series of swing highs shows the trend. A rising run of higher highs suggests an uptrend. A swing high is also a common place for stop orders and resistance. The mirror image is the swing low. See [swing highs and lows](/swing-highs-lows/) for detail.

## Example

Daily highs run 48, 50, 53, 51, 49. The 53 bar is higher than the two bars on each side, so 53 is a swing high. If price later closes above 53, that is a break above the previous swing high.
