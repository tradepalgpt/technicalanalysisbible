A session is a defined block of trading hours, such as the Asian, London or New York session in currency markets, or a single exchange day for shares.

## Explained

Markets can trade for many hours, but activity is not even. Each session has its own character. Share markets have an official open and close. Currency markets run around the clock and are split informally by the main financial centre that is active.

Sessions matter because volume, volatility and direction often change when one begins. Many tools restart at the open, such as [VWAP](/vwap/), the [initial balance](/initial-balance/) and the [opening range breakout](/opening-range-breakout/). The [session open](/session-open/) price is a common reference point, and [kill zones](/kill-zones/) mark the most active windows.

Times depend on the market and on daylight saving, so a trader should check the hours for the instrument rather than assume them.

## Example

A currency trader notes that price moved 20 pips during the Asian session, then 60 pips in the first two hours of London. The first hours of London carry three times the movement of the whole Asian session, so the trader treats the London open as the key window for that pair.
