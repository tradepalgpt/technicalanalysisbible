A retracement is a temporary price move against the trend, measured as a percentage of the swing that came before it.

## Explained

Retracement describes how deep a pullback goes. After a rise from a low to a high, price often gives back part of the gain. The size of that give-back, divided by the original move, is the retracement.

Traders commonly watch the 38.2%, 50% and 61.8% levels, which come from [Fibonacci retracement](/fibonacci-retracement/). Shallow retracements suggest a strong trend, and deep ones suggest the trend is weaker. A fall beyond 100% means the whole prior move has been undone, so it is no longer a retracement.

The related word "pullback" names the move itself, as in [pullback trading](/pullback-trading/). Retracement names its measured depth. Where several levels meet, as in [Fibonacci confluence](/fibonacci-confluence/), traders pay closer attention.

## Example

Price rises from 100 to 150, a swing of 50 points. It then falls to 125. The fall of 25 points is 50% of the swing, so price has retraced 50%. A retracement to 130.9 would be 38.2%, and to 119.1 would be 61.8%.
