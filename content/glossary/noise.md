Noise is the small, random price movement that does not reflect the underlying trend. It makes a chart look jagged and can trigger false signals.

## Explained

Every market has small swings caused by random orders and short-term trading. These swings hide the larger direction. Moving averages and [Heikin Ashi](/heikin-ashi/) candles smooth them out, but smoothing adds delay.

Some tools measure noise directly. The [choppiness index](/choppiness-index/) rates how sideways a market is, and [KAMA](/kama/) slows itself down when noise is high. Shorter time frames usually contain more noise than longer ones, which is one reason a signal on a one-minute chart is less reliable than on a daily chart.

## Example

Over a day, a stock drifts from 50.00 to 51.00, a steady gain. Along the way it zigzags by 0.20 up and down every few minutes. The 0.20 zigzags are noise. The 1.00 net rise is the trend.
