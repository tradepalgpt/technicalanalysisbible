Resistance is a price area where rising price has repeatedly stalled or turned down because sellers appear in numbers, acting like a ceiling above the market.

## Explained

Resistance forms where sellers have shown up before. Holders who bought at a higher price may sell to break even when price returns. Others sell there because the level looks expensive. The extra supply stops the rise.

Resistance can be a flat level, a [round number](/round-numbers/), a previous high, or a sloping [trendline](/trendline/). The more times price turns from an area, the more traders notice it. It is best seen as a zone rather than an exact line.

Resistance does not last forever. If price closes clearly above it, the level often becomes support, a change explained under [role reversal](/role-reversal/). Traders then look for a [breakout retest](/breakout-retest/). The full topic is in [support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/).

## Example

A stock turns down from 50 on three separate occasions over two months, so 50 is resistance. Later it closes at 52 on strong volume. Resistance has broken. A trader now watches whether price falls back to 50 and holds there as new support.
