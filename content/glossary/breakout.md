A breakout is a move in price beyond a defined level, such as support, resistance, a trendline or the edge of a range.

## Explained

Breakouts matter because a level that held many times has now failed. Traders see it as a sign that a range may be ending and a new move may start.

Many breakouts fail. Price pushes through, then falls back inside the range, which is called a [false breakout](/false-breakout/). Traders therefore often wait for a close beyond the level, extra volume, or a retest of the broken level. See [Breakout Trading](/breakout-trading/) for the common methods and [Breakout Retest](/breakout-retest/) for the retest approach.

## Example

A stock has failed to close above 50 four times in six weeks. On the fifth try it closes at 51 on higher volume. Price has broken out above resistance at 50. If it drops back below 50 the next day, the move is treated as a false breakout.
