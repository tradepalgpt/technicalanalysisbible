A leading indicator tries to signal a possible turn or move before price makes it, usually by measuring momentum or how stretched price has become.

## Explained

Oscillators such as [RSI](/rsi/) and the [Stochastic Oscillator](/stochastic-oscillator/) are called leading because they can turn before price does. A common clue is [divergence](/divergence/), where price makes a new high but the indicator does not.

The word "leading" is a loose label. These tools do not predict anything. They often give early signals that turn out wrong, especially in a strong trend, where an oscillator can sit at an extreme for a long time. Traders usually pair them with a lagging indicator to confirm. See [momentum indicators](/momentum-indicator/) for more.

## Example

Price climbs from 100 to 110 and then to 112, a new high. Over the same swings, RSI peaks at 75 and then only reaches 68. The weaker RSI peak is an early hint that momentum is fading, even though price has not yet turned.
