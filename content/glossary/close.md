The close is the last traded price of a chart period, such as a minute, an hour, a day or a week.

## Explained

Each bar or candle has four prices: open, high, low and close. The close is often treated as the most important of the four, because it shows where buyers and sellers settled when the period ended.

A [line chart](/line-chart/) usually connects closing prices only. Most indicators, such as moving averages and RSI, are calculated from closes by default.

Traders often wait for a close beyond a level before acting, because price can cross a level during a period and still finish back on the other side. This is a common way to filter a breakout, as described in [Breakout Trading](/breakout-trading/).

On 24-hour markets, the daily close depends on the platform's chosen cut-off time. See [Time Frames](/time-frames/).

## Example

A stock has resistance at 50. During the day it trades up to 50.80, but at the end of the day it finishes at 49.70. The close is 49.70, below resistance, so a trader who waits for a close above 50 has not been given a signal.
