Volatility is how much and how quickly price moves around, whether up or down, over a given period.

## Explained

A calm market has small bars and low volatility. A fast, jumpy market has large bars and high volatility. Volatility describes the size of moves, not their direction.

It can be measured from past prices, as in [historical volatility](/historical-volatility/), the [Average True Range](/atr/) and [standard deviation](/standard-deviation/). It can also be taken from option prices, which gives [implied volatility](/implied-volatility/) and indexes such as the [VIX](/vix/). Volatility tends to come in clusters, so quiet spells often lead to active ones. Traders use it to set stop distances, to size positions, and to spot a squeeze.

## Example

Two shares both trade near 50. Share A moves about 0.50 a day, or 1 percent. Share B moves about 2.50 a day, or 5 percent. Share B is five times as volatile, so a stop 1 point away would be hit far more often on B than on A.
