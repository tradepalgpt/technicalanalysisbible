A retest is a return of price to a level it has just broken, to check whether the old barrier now holds from the other side.

## Explained

After price breaks through resistance, it often drifts back to the broken level before moving on. This is the retest. If the old resistance now acts as support and price bounces, the break looks real. If price falls back through the level, the break was probably a [false breakout](/false-breakout/).

Retests matter because they give traders a second chance to join a move at a defined price, with a clear place for a stop just beyond the level. The idea is the core of [role reversal](/role-reversal/) and is set out in [breakout retest](/breakout-retest/).

Not every breakout retests. Strong moves can run away without looking back, and a trader waiting for a retest may miss them. Retests can also overshoot the level slightly before bouncing.

## Example

A stock breaks above resistance at 50 and rises to 54. It then slips back to 50.20 and holds for two candles, then turns up. The return to 50.20 is the retest. A trader might place a stop at 49.50, below the level, and judge the idea wrong if price closes under it.
