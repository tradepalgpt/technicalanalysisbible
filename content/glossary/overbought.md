Overbought describes a market whose price has risen far and fast enough that an indicator reading is unusually high compared with its recent history.

## Explained

"Overbought" is a label from oscillators such as [RSI](/rsi/) and the [Stochastic Oscillator](/stochastic-oscillator/). Each one sets a level, for example 70 on RSI or 80 on the Stochastic, above which recent buying has been unusually strong.

It does not mean price must fall. In a strong uptrend, an indicator can stay overbought for weeks while price keeps rising. Overbought readings are most useful in ranging markets, where they often line up with resistance. See [overbought and oversold](/overbought-oversold/) for how traders use these readings.

## Example

A stock rises on nine of its last ten days and its 14-period RSI reaches 78. RSI is now above 70, so the stock is called overbought. If the stock is in a sideways range and near its top, some traders look for a turn down. If it has just broken out to new highs, the same reading simply shows strength.
