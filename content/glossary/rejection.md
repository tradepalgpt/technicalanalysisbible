Rejection is when price reaches a level, fails to hold beyond it and moves back sharply, usually leaving a long wick or tail on the candle.

## Explained

A rejection shows that traders on one side tried to push through a level and lost. If price climbs into resistance, trades there, then closes well below the high, buyers were rejected. The same happens at support when sellers push down and price snaps back up.

On a candlestick chart, rejection appears as a long shadow in the direction of the failed attempt and a small body. Candles built this way include the [pin bar](/pin-bar/), the [shooting star](/shooting-star/) and the [hammer](/hammer/).

One rejection is a hint, not proof. Repeated rejections at the same [key level](/key-levels/) are stronger. A rejection that fails and is followed by a close beyond the level may be a [false breakout](/false-breakout/) in the other direction, so traders wait for a close before acting.

## Example

A stock has stalled near 100 for a week. One day it rises to 101, then closes at 98.5. The candle has a 2.5 point upper wick, more than twice its body. Price was rejected above 100. Two days later it again reaches 100.8 and closes at 98.7, a second rejection that strengthens the level.
