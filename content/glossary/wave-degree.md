Wave degree is the size or time scale of an Elliott wave, from tiny waves inside minutes to giant waves lasting decades.

## Explained

In [Elliott wave](/elliott-wave/) theory, waves nest inside one another. A five-wave [impulse](/impulse-wave/) is made of smaller waves, and it is itself one part of a bigger wave. Each level of this nesting is a degree.

Elliott named nine degrees, from Subminuette up to Grand Supercycle, and each has its own labelling style, such as numbers, letters or Roman numerals. The same wave shapes repeat at every degree, which is why the scheme is called fractal. In practice the labels matter less than staying clear about which degree you are counting, because mixing degrees is a common source of confusion. See [corrective waves](/corrective-waves/) for the three-wave moves.

## Example

On a daily chart you count a five-wave rise over three months and label it Intermediate degree. Zooming into one of its up waves on an hourly chart, you find five smaller waves inside it, labelled at a lower degree such as Minor.
