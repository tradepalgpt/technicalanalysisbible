The spread is the gap between the best price a buyer will pay (the bid) and the lowest price a seller will accept (the ask), and it is a cost every trade pays.

## Explained

When you buy at the market you normally pay the ask, and when you sell you receive the bid. The difference is the spread. It is paid on the way in and, in effect, again on the way out, so it acts like a small fee built into the price.

Spreads are tight in busy, liquid markets and wide in thin ones or around news. They matter most for short-term trading, where the target may be only a few times the spread. You can see the current bid and ask sizes in [depth of market](/depth-of-market/). A wide spread also makes slippage more likely.

## Example

A currency pair shows a bid of 1.1000 and an ask of 1.1002. The spread is 0.0002, or 2 pips. If you buy at 1.1002 and price does not move, you could only sell at 1.1000, so you are 2 pips behind the moment you enter.
