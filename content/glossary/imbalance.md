An imbalance is a stretch of price where buying or selling was so one-sided that the market moved quickly and left little or no two-way trading behind.

## Explained

When far more buyers than sellers rush in, price jumps through levels without giving sellers a fair chance to trade there. The result is a fast, thin move, often a long single candle. Traders using [smart money concepts](/smart-money-concepts/) call this area an imbalance and expect price may return to it.

The most common form is the [fair value gap](/fair-value-gap/), a three-candle gap where the first and third candles do not overlap. Strong imbalances often follow a [displacement](/displacement/) move. Whether price really returns to fill them is debated, so treat them as areas of interest rather than rules.

## Example

Price trades at 100 and a large buy order lifts it in one candle to 106. The next candle's low is 105 and the one before it had a high of 101. The range from 101 to 105 saw little trading, so it is an imbalance.
