Confirmation is a second piece of evidence that supports a first signal, such as a pattern, a level or an indicator reading, before a trader acts on it.

## Explained

A single signal is often wrong. Confirmation asks for something more before relying on it. This can be a candle that closes in the expected direction, a rise in volume, a second indicator that agrees, or a move on a higher time frame that points the same way.

The cost is timing. Waiting for confirmation usually means a worse entry price and fewer trades, in return for fewer false signals. Nothing makes a signal certain.

Related ideas are [Confluence](/confluence/), where several tools meet at one price, and [Multiple Time Frame Analysis](/multiple-time-frame-analysis/).

## Example

A trader sees a bullish engulfing candle at support at 100. Before acting, the trader waits for the next candle to close above the engulfing candle's high of 103. That later close is the confirmation. If the next candle closes at 101, the pattern is not confirmed.
