A timeframe is the length of time each bar or candle on a chart represents, such as one minute, one hour or one day.

## Explained

Each bar on a one-hour chart covers sixty minutes of trading and shows that period's open, high, low and close. Shorter timeframes show more detail and more noise. Longer ones show the bigger picture, with each bar hiding the moves inside it.

The same market can look bullish on one timeframe and bearish on another, so it matters which one you use. Many traders look at a longer timeframe for direction and a shorter one for entries, a habit covered in [multiple time frame analysis](/multiple-time-frame-analysis/). See [time frames](/time-frames/) for how to choose, and [tick charts](/tick-charts/) for charts not tied to time at all.

## Example

Price opens a day at 100, trades as high as 105 and as low as 98, and closes at 103. On a daily chart that is one candle. On an hourly chart the same day is about 24 candles, and the 105 high may sit in only one of them.
