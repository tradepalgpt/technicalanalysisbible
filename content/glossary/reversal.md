A reversal is a change in the direction of a trend, such as an uptrend that stops rising and begins making lower highs and lower lows.

## Explained

A reversal ends one trend and begins another. It differs from a pullback, which is a pause inside a trend. The difference is hard to see in real time, and many apparent reversals turn out to be pullbacks.

Chart patterns that often mark reversals include the [head and shoulders](/head-and-shoulders/), the [double top](/double-top/) and the [double bottom](/double-bottom/). A sharp one is the [V reversal](/v-reversal/). In price-structure language, a [market structure shift](/market-structure-shift/) is an early sign that direction has changed.

Because reversals are only confirmed after the fact, traders look for evidence such as a break of a key swing level, not a single candle. Waiting for confirmation costs part of the move but avoids many false alarms.

## Example

A stock makes higher lows at 40, 43 and 46, with highs at 50 and 55. It then rallies to 56, falls to 44, which is below the last low of 46, and fails to regain 50. The uptrend has broken, with a lower low and lower high. Many traders would call that a reversal.
