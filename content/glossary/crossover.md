A crossover is the point where one line moves across another on a chart, such as a fast moving average crossing a slow one.

## Explained

Crossovers are among the simplest signals in technical analysis. When a faster line crosses above a slower line, momentum is said to be turning up. When it crosses below, momentum is said to be turning down.

The best-known examples are the [Moving Average Crossover](/moving-average-crossover/), the [Golden Cross](/golden-cross/) and the [Death Cross](/death-cross/). Indicators like [MACD](/macd/) also produce signals when their signal line is crossed.

Crossovers lag price, because they use past data. They work best in trends and give many false signals in sideways markets.

## Example

A 10-day average is at 49 and a 30-day average is at 50. Over three days the 10-day rises to 51 while the 30-day rises to 50.3. The 10-day has crossed above the 30-day, which is a bullish crossover.
