An envelope is a pair of lines drawn a set distance above and below a central line, usually a moving average, to form a band around price.

## Explained

The distance is either a fixed percentage or a calculated amount. In a [Moving Average Envelope](/moving-average-envelope/), the bands sit a fixed percentage away from the average. Other band tools set the distance from volatility, such as [Bollinger Bands](/bollinger-bands/) and [Keltner Channels](/keltner-channels/).

Traders watch for price touching the outer lines. In a range, a touch of the upper line may mark a high area and a touch of the lower line a low area. In a strong trend, price can ride along one band for a long time, so a touch alone is not a reversal signal.

## Example

A 20-day average is at 100 and the envelope is set at 3 percent. The upper line is 103 and the lower line is 97. If price rises to 103.5, it has moved outside the envelope, which some traders read as stretched and others as a sign of strength.
