An auction is the process by which a market searches for a price that brings buyers and sellers together, moving up and down until trade is balanced.

## Explained

In auction thinking, price is not a random line. It moves to find where buyers and sellers agree. When price rises, the market is testing whether buyers will pay more. If they do not, price falls back and tests lower levels.

This view is the base of [Auction Market Theory](/auction-market-theory/). Tools such as [Market Profile](/market-profile/) and the [Value Area](/value-area/) show where most trade took place, which is where the market found the most agreement.

Price tends to spend time where trade is balanced and move quickly through levels where it is not.

## Example

A market trades between 100 and 102 all morning, with most volume near 101. Price then jumps to 104 on thin volume and returns to 102 within the hour. In auction terms, the market tested 104, found few willing buyers, and went back to where trade was accepted.
