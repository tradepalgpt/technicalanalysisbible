Order flow is the study of actual buy and sell orders as they hit the market, rather than just the price they leave behind on a chart.

## Explained

A normal chart shows where trades happened. Order flow shows who was pushing: buyers hitting the offer or sellers hitting the bid. Tools include the [footprint chart](/footprint-chart/), which shows volume at each price, and [cumulative volume delta](/cumulative-volume-delta/), which tallies buying minus selling.

The [depth of market](/depth-of-market/) shows resting orders that have not yet traded. Readers look for [absorption](/absorption/), where heavy selling fails to push price down. Order flow needs detailed data, so it is mostly used in futures and some stocks. Spot currency has no central record of orders.

## Example

In one minute, 800 contracts trade at the offer price (buyers lifting) and 300 trade at the bid (sellers hitting). The difference, +500, is positive delta, showing buyers were more aggressive, even if price barely moved.
