A lagging indicator is calculated from past prices, so it changes after price has already moved. It confirms what has happened rather than predicting what comes next.

## Explained

Most trend tools lag because they average past data. A [simple moving average](/simple-moving-average/) of 50 days reacts only after several days of new prices pull the average along. The same is true of [MACD](/macd/) and a [moving average crossover](/moving-average-crossover/).

The delay has a benefit and a cost. It filters out small moves, which helps a trader stay in a trend. But signals arrive late, so part of the move is already gone. Shorter settings lag less and give more false signals. Longer settings lag more and give fewer. The opposite idea is a leading indicator.

## Example

Price rises from 100 to 110 over ten days. A 10-day average of the closes sits near 105, still well below price. When price then turns down to 108, the average keeps rising for a while. The average lagged behind the turn.
