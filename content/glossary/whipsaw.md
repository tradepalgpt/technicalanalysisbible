A whipsaw is a sharp price move one way that quickly reverses, triggering a signal or stop and then leaving the trader on the wrong side.

## Explained

Whipsaws are most common in sideways or choppy markets. A [moving average crossover](/moving-average-crossover/) may signal a new trend, but price turns straight back and the signal reverses again. A [false breakout](/false-breakout/) is a close cousin, where price leaves a range and then returns.

Each whipsaw produces a small loss, and several in a row can add up. Traders try to reduce them with filters, such as requiring a minimum [ADX](/adx/) reading or checking the [Choppiness Index](/choppiness-index/), or by waiting for a confirmed close. No filter removes them entirely, and a stricter filter means later entries. Trend tools suffer most inside a trading range.

## Example

A 10-day average crosses above a 30-day average at 100, giving a buy signal. Two days later price falls and the averages cross back at 98, giving a sell signal. Buying at 100 and selling at 98 loses 2 points, and the market then drifts sideways with no trend.
