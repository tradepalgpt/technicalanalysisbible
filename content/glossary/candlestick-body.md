The candlestick body is the thick part of a candle, drawn between the opening price and the closing price of the period.

## Explained

The body shows where price started and where it finished. A long body means price moved a long way from open to close, so one side was in control. A short body means open and close were near each other, which shows indecision.

The thin lines above and below the body are the wicks, or shadows. They show the high and low of the period.

On most charts a rising candle has a light or green body and a falling candle has a dark or red body. Patterns such as [Marubozu](/marubozu/), [Doji](/doji/) and [Spinning Top](/spinning-top/) are described mainly by body size. See [Candlestick Chart](/candlestick-chart/) for how a candle is built.

## Example

On a daily chart, a stock opens at 100, trades as high as 106 and as low as 99, and closes at 105. The body runs from 100 to 105. The upper wick runs from 105 to 106 and the lower wick from 99 to 100. This is a long rising body.
