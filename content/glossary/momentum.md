Momentum is the speed of a price move, found by comparing the current price with the price a set number of bars ago. Rising momentum means price is accelerating.

## Explained

Price can keep rising while its speed slows, much like a ball thrown upward. Momentum tools try to measure that speed. The simplest is [rate of change](/rate-of-change/), which compares today's close with the close some bars back. [RSI](/rsi/) and [MACD](/macd/) are more refined versions.

Traders watch for momentum that agrees with price, which supports a trend, and for [divergence](/divergence/), where price makes a new high but momentum does not. Momentum can also mean the tendency of rising assets to keep rising. See [momentum indicators](/momentum-indicator/) for a full list.

## Example

A stock closed at 100 ten days ago and closes at 108 today, so 10-day momentum is 108 minus 100, or +8. A week later it closes at 109, and momentum is +3 because the price from ten days earlier was higher. Price rose but its speed fell.
