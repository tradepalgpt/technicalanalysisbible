A neckline is the line drawn through the lows (or highs) between a pattern's peaks. When price breaks it, the reversal pattern is usually treated as confirmed.

## Explained

In a [head and shoulders](/head-and-shoulders/) top, the neckline joins the two troughs on either side of the head. It acts as support. A close below it signals that the pattern may be complete. In an [inverse head and shoulders](/inverse-head-and-shoulders/), the neckline joins the peaks and acts as resistance.

[Double tops](/double-top/) and [double bottoms](/double-bottom/) use a similar line at the dip or rally between the two extremes. The line can slope. Many traders wait for a close beyond it, and some wait for a retest, because early breaks often fail.

## Example

In a head and shoulders top, the two troughs are at 95 and 96, so the neckline sits near 95.50. The head reaches 110. Price closes at 94.80, below the neckline, and the pattern is treated as confirmed.
