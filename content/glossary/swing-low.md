A swing low is a trough in price that has higher lows on both sides of it, marking the bottom of a decline before price turned up.

## Explained

A swing low is found by looking left and right: a bar whose low sits below the lows of the bars on either side. How many bars you check decides how large a swing you find, so the same chart can show many small swing lows or a few big ones.

Swing lows show where buyers last took control, so they often act as support and as places to put stop orders. In an uptrend each new swing low is higher than the last. A close below a prior swing low is a sign of a lower low and may signal a [break of structure](/break-of-structure/). The opposite is the swing high. See [swing highs and lows](/swing-highs-lows/).

## Example

Daily lows run 52, 50, 47, 49, 51. The 47 bar is lower than the two bars on each side, so 47 is a swing low. If the next decline stops at 49, that is a higher low and the uptrend structure is intact.
