Drawdown is the fall from a peak in the value of an account, strategy or price to a later low, usually shown as a percentage.

## Explained

Drawdown measures how much has been lost from the highest point reached. It is calculated from the peak, not from the starting balance, so it captures the pain of giving back gains.

The maximum drawdown is the largest such fall in a period. Traders use it to judge how rough a strategy has been and whether they could stand it. Losses also need larger gains to repair: a 50 percent fall needs a 100 percent rise to get back to the peak.

Controlling drawdown is mainly a matter of [Position Sizing](/position-sizing/) and the use of a [Stop Loss](/stop-loss/). The [Ulcer Index](/ulcer-index/) is an indicator that measures drawdown depth and length.

## Example

An account grows from 10,000 to 12,000, then falls to 9,000 before recovering. The drawdown at the low is (12,000 minus 9,000) divided by 12,000, which is 25 percent. It is measured from the 12,000 peak, not the 10,000 start.
