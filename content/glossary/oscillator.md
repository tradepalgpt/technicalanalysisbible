An oscillator is an indicator that moves back and forth around a centre line or between fixed limits, such as 0 and 100, to show momentum or how stretched price is.

## Explained

Oscillators are drawn in a panel below the price chart. Some are bounded, like [RSI](/rsi/) and the [Stochastic Oscillator](/stochastic-oscillator/), which stay between 0 and 100. Others, like [MACD](/macd/), swing above and below zero with no fixed ceiling.

Traders use them to spot [overbought and oversold](/overbought-oversold/) readings, momentum shifts and [divergence](/divergence/). They work best in ranging markets. In a strong trend they can stay at an extreme for a long time, so their signals are often early or wrong.

## Example

A 14-period RSI moves between 0 and 100. When it reads 25, it is near the bottom of its range, so recent selling has been heavy. When it later climbs to 75, it is near the top. The line has oscillated between the two extremes.
