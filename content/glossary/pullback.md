A pullback is a short move against the prevailing trend that does not break it, such as a brief dip inside an uptrend before price resumes higher.

## Explained

Trends rarely move in a straight line. They advance, pause and step back, then advance again. The step back is the pullback. In an uptrend it shows as a dip, and in a downtrend as a small rally.

Traders watch pullbacks because they offer a way to join a trend at a better price than chasing it. Common areas to watch are a rising moving average, a prior support level, or a [Fibonacci retracement](/fibonacci-retracement/) zone. The approach is set out in [pullback trading](/pullback-trading/), and some pullbacks come in two parts, as in the [two-legged pullback](/two-legged-pullback/).

A pullback can also grow into a full reversal. The difference is only clear afterwards, which is why traders define in advance the level that would cancel the idea.

## Example

A stock climbs from 40 to 50, then slips to 46 over three days. The 4-point dip is 40% of the prior rise, so it is a pullback. If price turns up from 46 and passes 50, the trend has resumed. If it falls below the earlier low at 40, it was a reversal, not a pullback.
