Oversold describes a market whose price has fallen far and fast enough that an indicator reading is unusually low compared with its recent history.

## Explained

"Oversold" is a label from oscillators such as [RSI](/rsi/) and the [Stochastic Oscillator](/stochastic-oscillator/). Each one sets a level, for example 30 on RSI or 20 on the Stochastic, below which recent selling has been unusually strong. It is the opposite of overbought.

It does not mean price must rise. In a strong downtrend, an indicator can stay oversold for weeks while price keeps falling. Oversold readings are most useful in ranging markets, where they often line up with support. See [overbought and oversold](/overbought-oversold/) for how traders use these readings.

## Example

A stock falls on eight of its last ten days and its 14-period RSI drops to 22. RSI is now below 30, so the stock is called oversold. If it is in a sideways range near the bottom, some traders look for a bounce. If it has just broken to new lows, the reading simply shows weakness.
