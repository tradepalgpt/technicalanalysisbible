A Fibonacci ratio is a percentage taken from the Fibonacci number sequence, such as 38.2, 50, 61.8 and 161.8 percent, and used to mark possible price levels.

## Explained

The Fibonacci sequence runs 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13 and so on, where each number is the sum of the two before. Dividing one number by the next in the sequence gives about 0.618, and by the number two places on gives about 0.382. The ratio 0.618 is the [Golden Ratio](/golden-ratio/).

Traders apply these ratios to a swing. In [Fibonacci Retracement](/fibonacci-retracement/) they mark how far a pullback might go. In [Fibonacci Extension](/fibonacci-extension/) they mark how far a move might run. The 50 percent level is not a true Fibonacci ratio but is usually included.

There is no proof that markets respect these levels. They work, when they work, partly because many traders watch them.

## Example

Price rises from 100 to 150, a move of 50 points. A 38.2 percent pullback is 19.1 points, to about 130.9. A 61.8 percent pullback is 30.9 points, to about 119.1. These are the levels a trader would watch.
