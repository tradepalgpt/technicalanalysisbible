A gap is an empty space on a chart where price jumps from one level to another with no trading in between, so the next bar opens clear of the previous bar's range.

## Explained

Gaps appear when buyers and sellers cannot agree on a price until the market moves a long way. They are common after news, earnings or a weekend, when the market was closed. On a daily chart, a gap up means today's low is above yesterday's high. A gap down is the reverse.

Traders sort gaps by where they occur. A [breakaway gap](/breakaway-gap/) starts a move, a [runaway gap](/runaway-gap/) appears mid-trend and an [exhaustion gap](/exhaustion-gap/) near the end. Many gaps are later retraced, which is called a [gap fill](/gap-fill/). See [price gaps](/price-gaps/) for the full set.

## Example

A stock closes at 50.00 with a day's high of 50.50. News arrives overnight and it opens at 52.00, trading no lower than 51.80 all day. The space between 50.50 and 51.80 has no trades, so it is a gap up of about 1.30.
