Position size is the number of shares, contracts or lots a trader buys or sells in one trade, usually chosen so that a stop-out costs a fixed, small part of the account.

## Explained

Position size answers "how much?" rather than "what?" or "when?". Two traders can take the same trade and risk very different amounts because they size it differently.

A common method works backwards from the stop. First decide how much money you accept losing on the trade. Then measure the distance from entry to [stop-loss](/stop-loss/). Divide the money at risk by that distance, and you have the size. A wider stop means a smaller position, and a tighter stop allows a larger one.

Sizing does not change whether a setup works. It changes how much a run of losses hurts. The full method is covered in [position sizing](/position-sizing/), and an [ATR stop](/atr-stop/) is one way to set the distance used in the sum.

## Example

An account holds 10,000. The trader accepts risking 1%, which is 100. She buys a share at 50 with a stop at 48, so each share risks 2. Position size is 100 divided by 2, which is 50 shares. If the stop is hit, she loses about 100. If she moved the stop to 46, the risk per share would be 4, and the size would fall to 25 shares.
