Accumulation is a phase in which large buyers gradually build a position, usually inside a sideways range, without pushing the price up quickly.

## Explained

Big buyers cannot buy all at once without moving price against themselves. So they buy in pieces over time. On a chart this often looks like a quiet, sideways range after a decline.

The idea is central to the [Wyckoff Method](/wyckoff-method/), which describes the full sequence in [Wyckoff Accumulation](/wyckoff-accumulation/). Volume tools such as [On-Balance Volume](/on-balance-volume/) are used to look for signs of buying inside the range.

Accumulation can only be confirmed in hindsight. A range that looks like accumulation may simply break down instead.

## Example

A stock falls from 50 to 30, then moves between 30 and 33 for two months. Down days show small volume, and up days show larger volume. Some traders read this as accumulation. If price later closes above 33 on strong volume, the reading looks right. If it closes below 30, it was not.
