A signal line is a smoothed average of an indicator, plotted beside the indicator itself, so that crossings between the two can be used as buy or sell cues.

## Explained

Some indicators, such as [MACD](/macd/), draw a main line that moves quickly. A second line is made by averaging the first, which makes it slower. That slower line is the signal line. When the main line crosses above it, momentum is said to be turning up, and a cross below suggests it is turning down.

In standard MACD, the signal line is a 9-period exponential moving average of the MACD line. The gap between the two lines is shown as the [MACD histogram](/macd-histogram/). The same idea appears in [PPO](/ppo/), [TRIX](/trix/) and the [Stochastic Oscillator](/stochastic-oscillator/), where it is usually a 3-period average.

Because the signal line lags, a cross arrives after the turn has begun. In a sideways market, lines cross often and give false cues.

## Example

A MACD line reads 0.8 and its 9-period signal line reads 0.6. MACD is above the signal line by 0.2, a bullish setup. Next day MACD drops to 0.5 while the signal line eases to 0.58. MACD has crossed below the signal line, a bearish cross.
