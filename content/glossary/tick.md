A tick is either the smallest price move a market allows, or a single trade or price update, depending on the context.

## Explained

The word has two common meanings. The first is the minimum price increment. A market that moves in steps of 0.01 has a tick size of 0.01, and each step is one tick. Futures contracts also assign a money value to each tick.

The second meaning is a single data event: one trade or one change in quote. A [tick chart](/tick-charts/) draws a new bar after a set number of trades, instead of after a set amount of time, so bars form faster when the market is busy. In markets without central volume, [tick volume](/tick-volume/) counts these price updates as a stand-in for activity.

## Example

A futures contract has a tick size of 0.25 and each tick is worth 12.50. Price moves from 4,000.00 to 4,001.00, which is four ticks. The move is worth 4 x 12.50 = 50 per contract.
