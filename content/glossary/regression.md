Regression is a statistical method that fits the straight line, or curve, lying closest to a set of points, used in charting to show price's central path and slope.

## Explained

Given a series of closing prices, regression finds the line that minimises the total squared distance between the line and each price. The result is a "line of best fit" through the recent data.

On a chart, this line shows the average direction of price over a chosen number of bars. Its slope says how fast price is rising or falling, as in [linear regression slope](/linear-regression-slope/). Adding bands a set number of [standard deviations](/standard-deviation/) above and below creates a [linear regression channel](/linear-regression-channel/), which shows how far price has strayed from its fitted path.

Regression describes the past window only. A line that fits well does not promise that price will continue along it. Its value is in measuring trend and stretch, and the full method is in [linear regression](/linear-regression/).

## Example

Closing prices over five days are 10, 11, 12, 13 and 14. The best fit is a straight line rising exactly 1 point per day, so the slope is plus 1. If the closes were 10, 12, 11, 14 and 13, the fitted line would still rise about 0.8 per day, with price sitting above and below it.
