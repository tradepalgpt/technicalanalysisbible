Risk-reward compares how much a trade could lose, measured to the stop, with how much it could gain, measured to the target, before the trade is taken.

## Explained

Every planned trade has two distances: from entry to the [stop-loss](/stop-loss/), and from entry to the [profit target](/profit-target/). Risk-reward puts them side by side. If the target is twice as far as the stop, the trade has 2 to 1 reward for each unit of risk.

The measure helps set rules in advance. A trader who only takes trades with at least 2 to 1 can be wrong more than half the time and still come out ahead, provided the targets are reached with reasonable frequency. A good ratio on paper means little if the target is unlikely to be hit.

The calculation and its limits are covered in [risk-reward ratio](/risk-reward-ratio/). The amount actually staked comes from [position sizing](/position-sizing/).

## Example

A trader buys at 100 with a stop at 98 and a target at 106. Risk is 2 points and reward is 6 points, so the risk-reward is 1 to 3. If only 3 trades in 10 reach the target and 7 lose 2 points, the result is 18 points gained and 14 lost, a net 4 points.
