A trading range is a period when price moves sideways between a roughly steady high and a roughly steady low, without making clear progress up or down.

## Explained

In a trading range, the top acts as resistance and the bottom as support. Price swings between them as neither buyers nor sellers take control. Ranges are a pause in the trend and can last days or years.

Range traders buy near the bottom and sell near the top, as described in [range trading](/range-trading/). Others wait for a [breakout](/breakout-trading/) from the range, knowing that many breakouts fail, as covered in [false breakout](/false-breakout/). Tools that suit trends, such as moving average crossovers, tend to whipsaw inside a range. A tight, drawn rectangle is the [rectangle pattern](/rectangle-pattern/).

## Example

A stock trades between 40 and 45 for two months. It touches 45 four times and 40 three times, and never closes outside that band. The range is 5 points wide, or about 12 percent of the low. A close at 46 on heavy volume would be a possible breakout.
