A projection is an estimate of where price might travel next, made by copying the size of an earlier move or applying set ratios to it, starting from a chosen point.

## Explained

Projections look forward. A trader measures a previous swing, then lays that distance, or a ratio of it, from a new starting point. The result is a target level, not a forecast that price will arrive there.

The simplest kind is the [measured move](/measured-move/): a leg of 10 points is expected to be followed by another leg of about 10 points. [Fibonacci projection](/fibonacci-projection/) uses ratios such as 61.8%, 100% and 161.8% of the first leg instead. Patterns like the [ABCD pattern](/abcd-pattern/) are built on this idea.

A projection is only a reference point. Many traders use it to place a [profit target](/profit-target/) or to see where a move may tire. Price often stops short of a level, or runs past it, so projections work best next to other evidence.

## Example

Price rises from 100 to 120, pulls back to 110, then turns up again. Projecting 100% of the first leg (20 points) from the low at 110 gives 130. A trader might set a target near 130 and watch for slowing momentum as price approaches it.
