A wick, also called a shadow, is the thin line above or below a candle's body that shows the highest and lowest prices reached during the period.

## Explained

A candle has a body between the open and close, and wicks reaching out to the high and low. The upper wick runs from the top of the body to the high. The lower wick runs from the bottom of the body to the low. Wick and shadow are two names for the same thing.

Long wicks show that price travelled far but was pushed back by the close, a sign of rejection. Single candles built on this idea include the [hammer](/hammer/), the [shooting star](/shooting-star/) and the [pin bar](/pin-bar/). A [marubozu](/marubozu/) has almost no wicks, showing one side stayed in control all period.

## Example

A candle opens at 100, rises to 108, falls to 99 and closes at 102. The body runs from 100 to 102. The upper wick runs from 102 to 108, which is 6 points long. The lower wick runs from 100 down to 99, which is 1 point long.
