A higher high is a price peak that sits above the previous peak. Together with higher lows, a series of higher highs is the standard picture of an uptrend.

## Explained

Price moves in waves. Each wave up ends at a peak, called a swing high. If the next peak finishes above the last one, that is a higher high. It shows buyers pushed further than they did on the previous attempt.

[Dow theory](/dow-theory/) defines an uptrend as higher highs plus higher lows. When price fails to make a new higher high and then falls below the last higher low, the trend may be changing. Many traders mark this as a [break of structure](/break-of-structure/) or a [market structure shift](/market-structure-shift/). See [market structure](/market-structure/) for the wider idea.

## Example

Price rises to 100, pulls back to 95, then rallies to 104. The 104 peak is above 100, so it is a higher high. If the next rally stalls at 102, that is a lower high, and the uptrend is weaker.
