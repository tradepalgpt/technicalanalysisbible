A squeeze is a period when volatility falls to unusually low levels and price compresses into a narrow range, often before a larger move.

## Explained

Markets alternate between quiet and active phases. A squeeze marks the quiet phase. Price bars get smaller, and volatility tools such as [Bollinger Bands](/bollinger-bands/) narrow. The [Bollinger Squeeze](/bollinger-squeeze/) reads this directly from band width, while the [TTM Squeeze](/ttm-squeeze/) compares the bands with [Keltner Channels](/keltner-channels/).

A squeeze does not say which way price will go. It only says that the range is tight compared with recent history, so a break of that range may be sharper than usual. Many squeezes end in a false start before the real move, so traders usually wait for price to leave the range before acting.

## Example

A stock has traded between 49 and 51 for three weeks, while its usual weekly range is about 4 points. Band width is at a six-month low. That is a squeeze. If price then closes at 52 on rising volume, the squeeze has resolved upward.
