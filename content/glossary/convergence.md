Convergence is when two lines, prices or indicator values move closer together over time.

## Explained

The word appears in several places. Two moving averages converge when the gap between them narrows. Converging trendlines form shapes such as the [Symmetrical Triangle](/symmetrical-triangle/). The "C" in [MACD](/macd/) stands for convergence, because the indicator measures how a fast and a slow moving average come together and move apart.

Convergence can also describe price and an indicator moving in the same direction, which is the opposite of [Divergence](/divergence/). In that sense it means the indicator agrees with price.

Narrowing gaps often come before a larger move, but they do not show which direction it will take.

## Example

A 20-day average sits at 105 and a 50-day average at 100, a gap of 5. A week later they are at 103 and 101, a gap of 2. The two averages are converging. If the 20-day falls below the 50-day, they have crossed.
