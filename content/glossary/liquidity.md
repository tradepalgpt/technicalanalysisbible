Liquidity is how easily an asset can be bought or sold at a stable price. A liquid market has many orders close to the current price, so large trades barely move it.

## Explained

In a liquid market the gap between the buy price and sell price is small, and there is plenty of volume at each level. Major currency pairs and large stocks are examples. Thinly traded assets are illiquid, so a single order can push price a long way.

Traders also use the word for clusters of resting orders, such as stop-losses sitting above [equal highs](/equal-highs-lows/). Price is sometimes drawn to those levels, which is the idea behind a [liquidity sweep](/liquidity-sweep/). You can see the orders waiting at each price in the [depth of market](/depth-of-market/), and [relative volume](/relative-volume/) shows how busy trading is.

## Example

A stock has 5,000 shares offered at 20.00 and 5,000 at 20.01. A buyer of 1,000 shares fills at 20.00 and price does not move. In an illiquid stock with only 200 shares at 20.00, the same order would push the price up several levels.
