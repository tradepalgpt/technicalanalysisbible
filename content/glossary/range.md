Range has two meanings in charting: the distance between a bar's high and low, and a market phase in which price moves sideways between a ceiling and a floor.

## Explained

For a single bar, range is the high minus the low. Measures such as the [average daily range](/average-daily-range/) average this figure to show how far a market typically moves.

For a market, a range is a sideways phase. Price repeatedly stops near a lower level, called support, and an upper level, called resistance, without making progress in either direction. The pattern is drawn as a [rectangle pattern](/rectangle-pattern/) when the edges are flat and clear.

Traders handle ranges in two ways. Some buy near the floor and sell near the ceiling, as in [range trading](/range-trading/). Others wait for a break out of the box. Ranges end eventually, and a close beyond an edge is the usual sign.

## Example

A stock trades between 48 and 52 for six weeks. The range is 4 points, from floor to ceiling. Its daily bar with high 51 and low 49.5 has a bar range of 1.5. A close at 53 would be a break above the 4-point range.
