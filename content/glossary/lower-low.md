A lower low is a price trough that falls below the previous trough. Together with lower highs, a series of lower lows is the standard picture of a downtrend.

## Explained

Each wave down ends at a trough, called a swing low. If the next trough finishes below the last one, that is a lower low. It shows sellers pushed further than on the previous attempt.

[Dow theory](/dow-theory/) defines a downtrend as lower highs plus lower lows. When price stops making lower lows and then rises above the last lower high, the trend may be changing. Traders often mark this as a [change of character](/change-of-character/) or a [market structure shift](/market-structure-shift/). See [market structure](/market-structure/) for the wider idea. It is the mirror of a higher high.

## Example

Price falls to 90, bounces to 95, then drops to 86. The 86 trough is below 90, so it is a lower low. If the next drop only reaches 88, that is a higher low, and the downtrend is weaker.
