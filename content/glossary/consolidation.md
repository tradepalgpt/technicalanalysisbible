Consolidation is a period in which price moves sideways in a fairly narrow range, usually after a larger move, while the market decides its next direction.

## Explained

During consolidation, neither buyers nor sellers are in clear control. Highs and lows stay within a band and ranges often shrink. The pause can lead to a continuation of the earlier move or a reversal, and the chart rarely says which in advance.

Several patterns are forms of consolidation, including [Rectangle Pattern](/rectangle-pattern/), [Pennant](/pennant/) and [Bull Flag](/bull-flag/). Tools such as the [Bollinger Squeeze](/bollinger-squeeze/) try to measure how tight the range has become.

Traders either trade inside the range, as in [Range Trading](/range-trading/), or wait for a break, as in [Breakout Trading](/breakout-trading/).

## Example

A stock rises from 60 to 80, then trades between 77 and 80 for three weeks. The 3-point band is the consolidation. A close above 80 would suggest the earlier rise is resuming. A close below 77 would suggest the pause was a top.
