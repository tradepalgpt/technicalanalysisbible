Reversal amount is the minimum price move against the current direction needed before a time-independent chart starts a new column, line or brick in the opposite direction.

## Explained

Some chart types ignore time and only record price change. They need a rule for when a move counts as a change of direction. That rule is the reversal amount. It can be a fixed number of points, a percentage, or a multiple of a measure such as the average true range.

A small reversal amount makes the chart sensitive and busy. A large one filters out minor swings and shows only major turns, though it reacts later. The setting is central to the [Kagi chart](/kagi-chart/), the [Renko chart](/renko-chart/) and [point and figure](/point-and-figure/) charts, where it is often written as a box size multiplied by a number of boxes.

There is no best value. Traders usually choose one that fits the market's normal volatility and the time they plan to hold a trade.

## Example

A Kagi chart uses a reversal amount of 2 points. Price rises from 100 to 110, so the line goes up. Price then falls to 108.5, only 1.5 points, so the line stays up. When price falls to 108, a 2 point drop from the high of 110, the chart turns and draws a downward line.
