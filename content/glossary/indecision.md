Indecision describes a point where neither buyers nor sellers are in control, so price moves up and down but ends close to where it started.

## Explained

On a candlestick chart, indecision shows as a small body with wicks on one or both sides. Price travelled in both directions during the period, yet the open and close are almost the same. A [doji](/doji/) and a [spinning top](/spinning-top/) are the classic examples. A [long-legged doji](/long-legged-doji/) shows a wider fight.

One indecision candle says little on its own. It matters most after a long move, where it can hint that momentum is fading, or when it is followed by a strong candle that settles the question. Inside a range, indecision candles are normal and mean little.

## Example

A stock opens at 40.00, rises to 41.00, falls to 39.00 and closes at 40.05. The body is only 0.05 wide while the range is 2.00. Both sides pushed hard and neither won, so the candle shows indecision.
