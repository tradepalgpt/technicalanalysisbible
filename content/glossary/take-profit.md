A take-profit is a preset price at which a trade is closed to lock in a gain, usually placed as an order when the trade is opened.

## Explained

A take-profit order sits above the entry on a long trade, or below it on a short. When price reaches it, the trade closes automatically. It is the partner of the [stop-loss](/stop-loss/), which caps the loss. Together they define the [risk-reward ratio](/risk-reward-ratio/) of a trade before it starts.

Traders choose the level in different ways: a prior resistance area, a [measured move](/measured-move/), a [Fibonacci extension](/fibonacci-extension/), or a multiple of the risk. Some prefer to take part of the profit and let the rest run, which is called [scaling out](/scaling-out/). See [profit target](/profit-target/) for methods.

## Example

You buy at 100 with a stop-loss at 95, risking 5. You set a take-profit at 110, a gain of 10. The reward is twice the risk, a 2:1 ratio. If price touches 110, the order fills and the trade ends with a gain of 10 per share before costs.
