A flag and a pennant are both short continuation patterns that follow a sharp move, the flagpole. They differ in the shape of the pause. A flag drifts between two roughly parallel lines, usually against the pole. A pennant squeezes between two converging lines, like a small triangle. Both look for a break in the pole's direction.

## At a glance

| | Bull flag | Pennant |
|---|---|---|
| Pause shape | Parallel lines, slightly down or sideways | Converging lines, a tiny triangle |
| Prior move | Sharp rise (flagpole) | Sharp move (flagpole), up or down |
| Confirmation | Close above the upper flag line | Close beyond either line, in the pole's direction |
| Measured target | Pole length added to the breakout | Pole length added or subtracted at the breakout |
| Origin | Not attributed to one person; described by Edwards and Magee | Not attributed to one person; described by Edwards and Magee |
| Best market state | Strong uptrends | Strong trends |
| Main weakness | Sideways markets, weak trends with no sharp pole | Sideways markets, slow trends with no sharp pole |

## When to use the flag

The [bull flag](/bull-flag/) is the pattern to look for after a sharp rise followed by a shallow, orderly drift. The upper line gives a clear breakout level. The pause should be short compared with the pole and retrace only a modest part of it. A deep pullback suggests the pole was not a strong move. Its mirror is the [bear flag](/bear-flag/).

## When to use the pennant

The [pennant](/pennant/) fits when the pause tightens, with highs falling and lows rising, after a sharp move. It works in either direction, so the same shape can follow a fall as well as a rise. It should form quickly, normally within a few weeks on daily charts. A long, drawn-out squeeze is a [symmetrical triangle](/symmetrical-triangle/) rather than a pennant.

## Using both together

On one chart you read whichever shape price has drawn; the two are not alternatives you choose between. Traders sometimes find both in the same trend, a flag followed later by a pennant. Both use the same measuring idea, based on the half-mast reading, where the pause forms around the middle of a fast move. The target is a guide, not a promise. See [Measured Move](/measured-move/).

## Common mix-ups

- **Calling a long squeeze a pennant.** If it drags on, it is a triangle.
- **Ignoring the pole.** Without a sharp, steep move first, neither pattern applies.
- **Assuming a flag always slopes down.** It can drift sideways, and its lines are roughly parallel.
- **Treating the target as certain.** Many patterns fall short, and some go further.

## FAQ

**What is the main difference between a flag and a pennant?**
The shape of the pause. A flag has roughly parallel lines. A pennant has converging lines.

**Do both need a flagpole?**
Yes. Both are continuations after a sharp, steep move, usually on strong volume.

**Can a pennant break the other way?**
It can. The usual expectation is a break in the direction of the pole, but a close beyond the opposite line would not support that reading.

**Is there a flag for downtrends?**
Yes, the bear flag. The pennant works in both directions.
