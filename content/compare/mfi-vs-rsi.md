The Money Flow Index and RSI are both momentum oscillators on a 0 to 100 scale with 14-period defaults. The difference is the input. RSI uses closing prices only. MFI multiplies the typical price by volume, so heavy-volume bars count for more. MFI is often called volume-weighted RSI.

## At a glance

| | MFI | RSI |
|---|---|---|
| Measures | Money flow in vs out, using price and volume | Average gains vs average losses |
| Created by | Often credited to Gene Quong and Avrum Soudack; year not firmly established | J. Welles Wilder Jr., 1978 |
| Default settings | 14; levels 80 / 20 | 14; levels 70 / 30 |
| Uses volume? | Yes | No |
| Best market state | Range, reversal | Range, reversal |
| Main weakness | Stays at extremes in strong trends; needs reliable volume | Stays overbought in strong trends |

## When to use MFI

The [Money Flow Index](/money-flow-index/) suits markets with real exchange volume, such as stocks and futures. It asks whether the recent move was backed by volume. A price rise on thin volume lifts MFI less than the same rise on heavy volume. Traders use it for range timing and divergence at turning points.

## When to use RSI

[RSI](/rsi/) suits any market, including spot forex where volume is only a tick count. It is smoother, widely available and easy to compare across markets. Its 50 line works as a simple trend filter, and its divergences are heavily watched.

## Using both together

Some traders compare the two. If RSI reaches an extreme but MFI does not, the price move had less volume behind it. If both roll over together, the signal is a little stronger. They share a similar structure, so agreement is not independent proof. Add a price level such as [support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/) for different evidence.

## Common mix-ups

- **Same levels for both.** MFI usually uses 80 and 20; RSI usually uses 70 and 30.
- **Calling MFI real cash flow.** It is an estimate from typical price and volume, not a record of who bought or sold.
- **Using MFI on forex without care.** Spot forex has only [tick volume](/tick-volume/), so readings are approximate.
- **Treating overbought as a sell signal.** Both can stay high through a strong trend.

## FAQ

**Is MFI the same as RSI with volume?**
Close, which is why it is called volume-weighted RSI. The idea is similar, but MFI uses typical price times volume rather than closing changes alone.

**Which is better, MFI or RSI?**
Neither is better in general. MFI adds volume information where volume is reliable. RSI works everywhere. Test both on your market.

**Do they use the same overbought levels?**
No. MFI is usually read at 80 and 20, and RSI at 70 and 30.

**Can I use MFI on forex?**
You can, but spot forex volume is tick volume, so the result is an approximation. Stocks and futures give a better reading.
