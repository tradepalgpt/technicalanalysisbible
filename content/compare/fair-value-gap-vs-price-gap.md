A price gap is a real empty space on the chart: one bar opens beyond the previous bar's high or low. A fair value gap (FVG) is a three-candle pattern where the wicks of the first and third candles do not overlap, leaving a space across a strong middle candle. They look alike but are defined differently, and the FVG comes from trader education rather than classic charting.

## At a glance

| | Fair Value Gap | Price Gap |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Three-candle pattern | Space between two bars |
| Definition | Wicks of candle 1 and candle 3 do not overlap | New bar's low is above the old high (or the reverse) |
| Where it appears | Inside continuous trading, including 24-hour markets | Mostly at the open after news, or over weekends |
| Origin | ICT and smart money concepts teaching | Early charting texts; called a window in candlesticks |
| Common use | Pullback entry area, target | Breakout confirmation, support and resistance |
| Best market state | Trends | Breakouts, reversals |
| Weak in | Ranges | Ranges |
| Evidence | Trader-education term, no published tests | Long-studied, with gap types judged after the fact |

## When to use Fair Value Gap

The [Fair Value Gap](/fair-value-gap/) suits traders who follow smart money concepts and want a defined area to watch after a strong move. It can be found on any market and time frame, since it only needs three candles. After a break of structure, price often pulls back, and the gap gives a zone to mark. Many traders watch the midpoint and treat a close through the far edge as failure.

## When to use Price Gap

The [Price Gap](/price-gaps/) suits traders of shares and indices, where gaps follow news and earnings. The edges of the gap act as levels, and a gap through a known level on heavy volume is one of the clearer breakout signs. It is mostly a confirmation tool, because it appears after the event that caused it.

## Using both together

On a chart with real gaps, a session gap often leaves a fair value gap inside the same move, because the opening bar is large. Some traders use the price gap to judge strength and the FVG to mark an area for a pullback. Keep the two labelled separately so you know which rule you are applying. Add [volume](/volume/) and [support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/) to judge the move.

## Common mix-ups

- **Using the words as if they were the same.** The price gap is a space between bars. The FVG is a space between wicks across three bars.
- **Expecting every gap to fill.** Many do not, for either type.
- **Looking for FVGs only after gaps.** FVGs form in continuous markets with no session gap at all.
- **Treating "fair value" as a measured number.** It is only a label.
- **Marking every tiny gap.** Small gaps are common and usually ignored.

## FAQ

**Is a fair value gap the same as a gap?**
No. A price gap is a visible space between one bar and the next. An FVG is made from the wicks of three candles and can appear in a market with no gaps between bars.

**Do both types of gap fill?**
Sometimes. Many fill, but many stay open for a long time. Neither type is a promise that price will return.

**Which is better for forex?**
Forex trades nearly around the clock, so true price gaps are mostly seen over weekends. FVGs can form at any time. That makes the FVG easier to find, not proven to be more useful.

**Does the FVG have research behind it?**
No published research supports it. It is a way to organise a chart, so test it before relying on it.
