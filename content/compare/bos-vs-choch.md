Break of structure (BOS) and change of character (CHoCH) both describe price moving beyond a swing point. The difference is direction. A BOS breaks the last swing in the direction of the trend, which reads as continuation. A CHoCH is the first break against the trend, which reads as an early warning of a possible reversal. Teachers use the words slightly differently.

## At a glance

| | Break of Structure | Change of Character |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | With the trend | Against the trend |
| Uptrend example | Close above the last swing high | Close below the last higher low |
| Reading | Trend continuing | Trend may be turning |
| Best market state | Trend, breakout | Reversal after a clear trend |
| Weak in | Ranges | Ranges |
| Best use | Confirmation of continuation | Early warning of a change |
| Origin | Smart money concepts and ICT teaching; built on Dow theory | Same |
| Evidence | Trader-education term, no academic test | Same |

## When to use Break of Structure

The [Break of Structure](/break-of-structure/) suits traders who want to stay with a trend. Each break past the last swing shows the trend still making progress. Many wait for a pullback after the break instead of chasing it, then look for entries in the direction of the trend.

## When to use Change of Character

The [Change of Character](/change-of-character/) suits traders who want an early sign that a trend may be weakening. It comes earlier than a moving average crossover, at the cost of more false alarms. Traders often wait for a lower high (or higher low) afterwards, then look at an [order block](/order-block/) for an entry.

## Using both together

The two read as a sequence. In an uptrend, a run of BOS events shows continuation. Then a CHoCH appears when price closes below the last higher low, and the next BOS may happen in the other direction. Reading them together needs consistent [swing points](/swing-highs-lows/) and a clear view of [market structure](/market-structure/). Use the same swing rule on every chart.

## Common mix-ups

- **Mixing up the direction.** BOS is with the trend and CHoCH is against it.
- **Calling a CHoCH a reversal.** It is a warning. Price often breaks a swing, pulls back, and continues.
- **Using a wick instead of a close.** Stricter rules need a close beyond the swing.
- **Trading either in a range.** Swings break in both directions all the time there, so both labels become noise.
- **Treating the terms as fixed.** Some teachers also use "market structure shift". See [Market Structure Shift](/market-structure-shift/).

## FAQ

**What is the difference between BOS and CHoCH?**
A BOS breaks the last swing in the trend's direction and confirms continuation. A CHoCH is the first break against the trend and warns of a possible reversal.

**Is a CHoCH a sell signal?**
No. It is a warning that the trend has stopped behaving as before. Many CHoCH events are followed by a new high.

**Is CHoCH the same as market structure shift?**
Many traders use them for the same event. Others keep "shift" for a break with a strong, fast move.

**Do I need a candle close for either?**
Definitions vary. A close gives fewer false signals but arrives later. Pick one rule and keep it.
