Bullish engulfing and the piercing line are both two-candle bullish reversal patterns that appear after a decline. The difference is how far the second candle goes. A bullish engulfing candle covers the whole body of the first candle. A piercing line closes above the middle of the first body, but not above its open.

## At a glance

| | Bullish Engulfing | Piercing Line |
|---|---|---|
| Candles | Two | Two |
| First candle | Small falling candle | Long bearish candle |
| Second candle | Rising body fully covers the first body | Opens below the first low, closes above its midpoint |
| Strength | Clearer shift to buyers | Milder; needs confirmation |
| Origin | Japanese; popularised in the West by Steve Nison, 1991 | Japanese; popularised in the West by Steve Nison, 1991 |
| Mirror pattern | Bearish engulfing | Dark cloud cover |

## When to use Bullish Engulfing

[Bullish engulfing](/bullish-engulfing/) shows buyers taking over from sellers in one period. The second body fully covers the first, which makes the shift easy to see. It can mark the start of a bounce or a turn after a decline, particularly at a support level.

## When to use Piercing Line

The [piercing line](/piercing-line/) shows sellers rejected after a long bearish candle. The second candle opens below the first low but recovers past the halfway point. It is a milder sign than an engulfing pattern, so it usually needs confirmation, such as a following bullish candle.

## Using both together

You do not read both from the same pair of candles. They are different shapes with different strength. Use the shape to judge how much weight to give the reversal, and combine either with [support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/) or [volume](/volume/). A close above the second candle's high is a common confirmation. Both patterns matter most at a clear support level, and they matter less in the middle of a range where there is no obvious reason for buyers to step in.

## Common mix-ups

- **Thinking a piercing line engulfs.** It does not cover the full first body.
- **Using either without a decline.** Both need a prior downtrend.
- **Ignoring the midpoint rule.** A piercing line must close above the first body's midpoint.
- **Treating the pattern as a signal alone.** Both need confirmation.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between bullish engulfing and the piercing line?**
Bullish engulfing covers the full first body. The piercing line only closes above the midpoint of the first body.

**Which is stronger?**
Bullish engulfing is generally read as the clearer sign, but both need confirmation.

**Do both need a downtrend?**
Yes. Both are reversal patterns that follow a decline.

**What is the mirror of the piercing line?**
[Dark cloud cover](/dark-cloud-cover/), its bearish counterpart.
