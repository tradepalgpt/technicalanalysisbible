A moving average is one line that smooths price to show direction. The Ichimoku Cloud is a five-line system that shows direction, momentum and support and resistance on one chart. Its two core lines, the Tenkan-sen and Kijun-sen, are midpoints of highs and lows rather than averages of closes. Moving averages are simpler and easier to test. Ichimoku gives more information, with more parts to read.

## At a glance

| | Moving average (SMA) | Ichimoku Cloud |
|---|---|---|
| Lines | One, or a few for crossovers | Five, plus a shaded cloud |
| Built from | Average of closing prices | Midpoints of highest high and lowest low |
| Default settings | None fixed; 20, 50, 200 are common | 9, 26, 52; 26-period shift |
| Projected ahead? | No | Cloud drawn 26 bars ahead |
| Support and resistance | Single dynamic line | Cloud edges and Kijun-sen |
| Created by | No single inventor | Goichi Hosoda, often given as 1969 |
| Weak in | Sideways ranges | Sideways ranges |

## When to use Moving Averages

A [simple moving average](/simple-moving-average/) suits traders who want a clear, testable rule: price above a rising line, or a short average crossing a longer one. It is easy to calculate and easy to explain. The [exponential moving average](/exponential-moving-average/) reacts faster. Both lag, and in ranges price keeps crossing the line, producing false signals called whipsaws.

## When to use Ichimoku Cloud

The [Ichimoku Cloud](/ichimoku-cloud/) suits traders who want a full read of the trend from one indicator. Price above or below the cloud gives direction. The cloud's thickness shows how strong a support or resistance zone is. The Tenkan and Kijun cross and the Chikou Span add momentum and confirmation. It was designed for daily charts and works best in trends.

## Using both together

Some traders add a longer moving average to Ichimoku as a separate trend check, and others keep only the cloud. Both lag and both struggle in ranges, so agreement is mostly useful as a trend filter. Using [ADX](/adx/) to confirm that a trend exists helps with either.

## Common mix-ups

- **Calling the Tenkan and Kijun moving averages.** They are midpoints of highs and lows, though they look similar.
- **Forgetting the shift.** The cloud under today's candle was calculated 26 bars ago.
- **Treating the cloud as a fixed level.** Price can pass through it.
- **Using every Ichimoku part as a checklist.** Decide which parts your rules use.
- **Using either in a sideways market.** Signals flip often.

## FAQ

**Is Ichimoku just a set of moving averages?**
Not quite. Its lines use the midpoint of the highest high and lowest low over a period, not an average of closes. It also adds a shifted cloud and a lagging span.

**Which is simpler to use?**
A moving average. It has one line and a few rules. Ichimoku has five parts and a cloud to interpret.

**Does the cloud predict price?**
No. It is drawn ahead, but built from past highs and lows. It projects current levels and does not forecast.

**Which works better in ranges?**
Neither. Both give frequent false signals when price moves sideways.
