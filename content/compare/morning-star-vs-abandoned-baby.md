The morning star and the abandoned baby are both three-candle bullish reversal patterns that form after a fall. The difference is the middle candle. A morning star needs a small body that sits low. An abandoned baby needs a doji whose whole range, shadows included, is gapped away from both neighbours. The abandoned baby is stricter and much rarer.

## At a glance

| | Morning Star | Abandoned Baby |
|---|---|---|
| Candles | Three | Three |
| Middle candle | Small body, either colour | Doji |
| Gaps needed | Star's body gaps below the first body (often relaxed) | Doji's full range gaps clear on both sides |
| Third candle | Long rising body, closes past the midpoint of candle one | Strong rising candle, gapped above the doji |
| How common | Fairly common | Rare, especially in 24-hour markets |
| Mirror pattern | Evening star | Bearish abandoned baby |
| Origin | Japanese candlestick charting; Steve Nison, 1991 in the West | Same |
| Best market state | Reversal after a decline | Reversal after an extended move |

## When to use Morning Star

The [Morning Star](/morning-star/) is the more practical pattern. Its rules are looser, so it appears often enough to study on any market, including forex and crypto where true gaps are uncommon. Many traders accept a small body that simply stays low instead of insisting on a gap. It suits traders who want a recognisable end-of-decline picture at a support level, then wait for the next bar to follow through.

## When to use Abandoned Baby

The [Abandoned Baby](/abandoned-baby/) suits traders who want a strict filter. Both gaps must be complete, so only dramatic turns qualify, and most near misses are excluded. It is mainly seen in stocks and indices, where gaps form at the open. Its rarity means few signals, so it works better as a confirmation or warning than as a regular entry trigger.

## Using both together

You do not need both at once, because the abandoned baby is a stricter version of the morning star with a [doji](/doji/) in the middle. A useful habit is to treat any abandoned baby as a morning star that met extra conditions. Either way, look for [support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/) nearby and wait for the third candle to close before acting.

## Common mix-ups

- **Calling any doji star an abandoned baby.** If the shadows touch or overlap a neighbouring candle, it is an ordinary star pattern.
- **Treating rarity as strength.** A rarer pattern is not a more reliable one.
- **Insisting on gaps everywhere.** Markets that trade almost continuously rarely gap, so strict rules can hide real examples.
- **Confusing it with the morning doji star.** That pattern has a doji in the middle but does not need the full gaps. See [Morning Doji Star](/morning-doji-star/).

## FAQ

**Is an abandoned baby better than a morning star?**
Not necessarily. It is stricter and rarer, but that does not make it more reliable. Test both on your own market and time frame.

**Why is the abandoned baby so rare?**
It needs two complete gaps in a row, with a doji between them. Gaps are mostly seen at stock session opens, and much less in forex.

**Can a morning star have a doji in the middle?**
Yes. A doji as the star shows stronger indecision, and some traders then call the pattern a morning doji star. It does not need the full gaps of an abandoned baby.

**Do both patterns need confirmation?**
Most traders wait for the third candle to close and look for support or other evidence before acting. Neither pattern is a signal on its own.
