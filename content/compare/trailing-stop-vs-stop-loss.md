A stop loss stays at one price and limits the loss if the trade goes wrong. A trailing stop starts in the same place but follows price as the trade moves in your favour, never moving back. The stop loss defines risk before entry. The trailing stop then locks in open profit and gives some of it back when it is hit.

## At a glance

| | Stop Loss | Trailing Stop |
|---|---|---|
| Moves? | No, stays fixed | Yes, only in the trade's favour |
| Main job | Cap the loss, define 1R | Protect profit, exit a trend |
| Set by | The level that proves the idea wrong | A distance behind the best price |
| Typical settings | Beyond a swing or range edge, or ATR-based | Fixed distance, percentage, ATR multiple, swing points |
| Best market states | All states | Trends |
| Weak in | Thin or gapping markets | Ranges, where ordinary swings hit it |
| Main trade-off | Can be hit by noise | Gives back part of the profit |

## When to use Stop Loss

A [Stop Loss](/stop-loss/) is needed on every trade, because it fixes the risk before entry. The distance from entry to stop sets 1R and drives position size. It suits setups with a clear invalidation point, such as a break back inside a range or beyond a swing low. It works in trends, ranges, breakouts and reversals, with the placement adapted to each.

## When to use Trailing Stop

A [Trailing Stop](/trailing-stop/) suits trades in a running trend where you want to stay in while protecting gains. Once the stop passes entry, the worst outcome is a gain. The distance matters: too tight and a normal pullback ends the trade, too loose and much of the profit is returned. Distances based on ATR or swing points are more logical than a random number.

## Using both together

They are two stages of one plan. The initial stop loss is placed at the point where the idea fails. Once price has moved far enough in your favour, such as 1R, the stop may start trailing. Until then the original stop stays. Decide the rule before the trade, not during it, and use [position sizing](/position-sizing/) from the initial stop distance.

## Common mix-ups

- **Widening a stop and calling it trailing.** A trailing stop only moves in the trade's favour.
- **Using a trailing stop in a range.** Price swings back and forth and hits it early.
- **Setting the initial stop by affordability.** It belongs where the idea is wrong.
- **Expecting an exact exit price.** Gaps, spread and slippage can make the fill worse than the stop.
- **Counting a trailing exit as a failure.** It is a planned way to lock in a gain.

## FAQ

**Is a trailing stop a type of stop loss?**
Yes, in effect. It is a stop that moves. It begins as protection against loss and becomes a way to lock in profit as it rises behind price.

**Can I use a trailing stop from the start?**
You can, but many traders keep the original stop until price has moved a set amount, such as 1R, and then begin to trail. It is a choice to settle before the trade.

**Does either guarantee my exit price?**
No. In fast markets or over a gap, the fill can be worse than the stop price, and the spread adds to the cost.

**Which one limits risk better?**
The initial stop loss defines the risk. A trailing stop reduces risk as it moves up, and once past entry it protects profit instead.
