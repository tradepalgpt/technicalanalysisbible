Head and shoulders and double top are both reversal patterns at the end of an uptrend. A head and shoulders has three peaks, with the middle one highest, and breaks a neckline. A double top has two peaks at roughly the same price and breaks the low between them. Each is confirmed by a close beyond its support line.

## At a glance

| | Head and shoulders | Double top |
|---|---|---|
| Peaks | Three: left shoulder, higher head, lower right shoulder | Two at about the same level |
| Support line | Neckline through the two pullback lows; can slope | The trough between the peaks |
| Confirmation | Close below the neckline | Close below the trough |
| Measured target | Head to neckline height, subtracted from the break | Peaks to trough height, subtracted from the break |
| First described | Richard Schabacker, 1932; popularised by Edwards and Magee, 1948 | Not attributed to one person |
| Best market state | Reversals at the end of uptrends | Reversals at the end of uptrends |
| Main weakness | Easy to see in sideways markets | Pause in a strong trend mistaken for a top |

## When to use head and shoulders

[Head and shoulders](/head-and-shoulders/) is the pattern to look for when an uptrend has made a higher peak, then failed with a lower one. The neckline gives a clear line to watch, and it can slope, which makes the pattern flexible. The shape has more parts, so there is more to judge, but it also gives more evidence that buyers are tiring.

## When to use double top

[Double top](/double-top/) is the simpler shape: price hits about the same high twice and fails. The trough between the peaks is the line to watch. It is easier to spot than head and shoulders, though a second peak well above the first is a breakout, and one well below is a lower high, which is a different signal. Peaks only a few bars apart suggest a minor blip.

## Using both together

You do not usually choose between them on one chart; you read what price has drawn. They can appear in sequence, and some traders treat a failed second peak as an early hint of a larger pattern. Both give a measured target using the same idea. The target is a guide, not a promise, and other evidence such as [volume](/volume/) or nearby [support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/) helps check it.

## Common mix-ups

- **Calling a double top before the break.** The pattern is confirmed only by a close below the trough.
- **Forcing a neckline.** Without a prior uptrend and clear shoulders, the shape may not be there.
- **Ignoring peak height.** The two peaks of a double top should be at similar prices.
- **Treating the target as certain.** Many patterns fall short, and some go further.

## FAQ

**Which is more reliable?**
Neither is reliable on its own. Each is a guide that needs confirmation from a close beyond its support line, and each can fail.

**Do both need an uptrend first?**
Yes. Without a prior uptrend, there is nothing to reverse.

**What is the difference in how they are measured?**
Head and shoulders measures from the head to the neckline. Double top measures from the peaks to the trough. Each is then subtracted from the breakout point.

**What are the bullish versions?**
[Inverse head and shoulders](/inverse-head-and-shoulders/) and [double bottom](/double-bottom/).
