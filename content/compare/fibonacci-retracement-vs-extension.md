Fibonacci retracement and extension use the same family of ratios for opposite jobs. Retracement measures how far a pullback might travel back into the previous swing, so it marks possible entry zones. Extension projects beyond the end of the swing, so it marks possible profit targets. Both are areas of interest, not exact turning points.

## At a glance

| | Fibonacci retracement | Fibonacci extension |
|---|---|---|
| Question answered | Where might a pullback stall? | Where might the move run to? |
| Default levels | 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, 78.6% | 100%, 127.2%, 161.8%, 200%, 261.8% |
| Points needed | Two: swing start and swing end | Three: A, B and the pullback end C |
| Levels sit | Inside the prior swing | Beyond the end of the swing |
| Created by | No single inventor | No single inventor |
| Best uses | Entry zones, stop placement | Profit targets, exit planning |
| Best market state | Trend pullbacks | Trend, after a pullback |

## When to use retracement

[Fibonacci retracement](/fibonacci-retracement/) suits traders who want to plan an entry during a pullback in a trend. Draw it from a clear swing low to a swing high in an uptrend, and the levels mark where price might pause on the way back. It needs a clean swing to measure, so it is weak in sideways ranges.

## When to use extension

[Fibonacci extension](/fibonacci-extension/) suits traders who want target levels after a pullback. The three-point tool measures the A to B swing and projects it from point C. The levels give places to consider taking profit or tightening a stop. It also needs a trend to extend, so it is weak in ranges.

## Using both together

The two tools often work as a sequence on one move. Retracement helps judge where the pullback may end, which gives point C. Extension then projects targets from that point. For example, a trader might see a pullback pause near the 61.8% retracement, then use the extension levels from that low for targets. Neither tool tells you the pullback will stop there, so confirm with something else, such as [support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/).

## Common mix-ups

- **Mixing up their jobs.** Retracement is mainly for entries, and extension is mainly for exits.
- **Drawing from the wrong end.** Check that 0% sits at the end of the swing and 100% at the start.
- **Using two points for extension without noting it.** Some traders extend the swing past its end with two points, which gives different levels from the three-point method.
- **Treating levels as exact.** Price may react near them, through them, or not at all.

## FAQ

**Which is better, retracement or extension?**
They do different jobs, so neither replaces the other. Retracement helps plan entries during pullbacks. Extension helps plan targets after one.

**Do retracement levels predict where a pullback ends?**
No. They mark areas where price may pause. A close beyond the start of the swing shows the idea has failed.

**Why do extension levels exceed 100%?**
They project beyond the length of the original swing, measured from the pullback end.

**Can I use them in a range?**
They are weak there. Both need a clear trend swing to measure from.
