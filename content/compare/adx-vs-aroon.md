ADX and Aroon both help judge whether a market is trending, but they are built differently. ADX measures trend strength from smoothed price ranges and has no direction of its own. Aroon measures how recently the highest high and lowest low occurred, and its two lines show direction as well as strength. ADX is the more standard filter; Aroon is simpler to read as a picture.

## At a glance

| | ADX | Aroon |
|---|---|---|
| Measures | Trend strength from directional movement | Time since the highest high and lowest low |
| Created by | J. Welles Wilder Jr., 1978 | Tushar Chande, 1995 |
| Default settings | 14 periods; 25 as a common threshold | 25 periods; 70 and 30 as common levels |
| Lines | ADX, plus +DI and -DI in the full system | Aroon Up and Aroon Down |
| Shows direction? | Not by itself | Yes, through the two lines |
| Smoothing | Wilder's smoothing, applied twice | None in the basic formula |
| Weak in | Spotting reversals | Fast breakouts |

## When to use ADX

[ADX](/adx/) is useful as a filter that tells you whether trend-following tools are worth using. A reading below 20 suggests a range, and above 25 suggests a trend. Because it only shows strength, it is paired with a direction tool such as a moving average or the +DI and -DI lines. It is smoothed twice, so it lags, and it is poor at calling turns.

## When to use Aroon

[Aroon](/aroon/) suits traders who want direction and recency in one view. Aroon Up near 100 means a new high was made lately. Both lines low or tangled points to consolidation. Because it uses only the highest high and lowest low, a single spike can change readings sharply, and it can be slow to reflect a sharp breakout.

## Using both together

They answer different questions, so they can complement each other. ADX says how strong the trend is, and Aroon says which side has made the more recent extremes. Agreement, such as a rising ADX with Aroon Up high, gives a clearer picture than either alone. Both lag, so neither suits early entries. Pair them with [support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/).

## Common mix-ups

- **Reading ADX as a direction signal.** It shows strength only.
- **Treating Aroon at 100 as overbought.** It means a recent high, a sign of strength.
- **Assuming they measure the same thing.** One uses price ranges, the other uses time since extremes.
- **Treating 25 or 70 as exact switches.** They are conventions.
- **Comparing values across platforms.** Smoothing and window length can differ.

## FAQ

**Which is better, ADX or Aroon?**
Neither is better in general. ADX is the standard strength filter and is more widely used. Aroon gives direction and recency in one view. Choose by what you need to know, and test it on your market.

**Does Aroon show direction?**
Yes. Aroon Up above Aroon Down means more recent highs than lows, and the reverse suggests a downtrend. ADX alone does not show direction.

**Do they use the same lookback?**
Not by default. ADX uses 14 periods and Aroon uses 25, although some older guides use 14 for Aroon.

**Which works better in a sideways market?**
Both are used there as filters. A low, flat ADX and tangled, low Aroon lines each suggest no clear trend.
