The Stochastic Oscillator and Williams %R are close to the same indicator. Both show where the latest close sits within the recent high-low range. Williams %R is the Fast Stochastic %K flipped upside-down, shifted by 100. The differences are the scale, 0 to 100 against 0 to -100, and the smoothing: the usual Stochastic adds smoothing and a signal line, while %R is unsmoothed and jumpier.

## At a glance

| | Stochastic Oscillator | Williams %R |
|---|---|---|
| Measures | Close within the high-low range | Same, from the top of the range |
| Created by | George Lane, 1950s | Larry Williams, 1973 |
| Scale | 0 to 100 | 0 to -100 |
| Default settings | 14, 3, 3 (slow); levels 80 / 20 | 14 periods; levels -20 / -80 |
| Smoothing | Yes in the slow version | None in the standard formula |
| Signal line | %D | None |
| Best market states | Range, reversal | Range, reversal |

## When to use Stochastic Oscillator

The [Stochastic Oscillator](/stochastic-oscillator/) suits traders who want a smoother line and a signal line. The %K and %D crossover gives a defined event, often read near 20 or 80. The slow version, with the extra smoothing, gives fewer and steadier signals. Check whether your platform plots the fast or slow version before comparing numbers.

## When to use Williams %R

[Williams %R](/williams-r/) suits traders who want a quick, unsmoothed read of the close within its range. It reacts at once because there is no smoothing, so it is jumpy. Readings near 0 mean the close is near the top of the range and near -100 near the bottom. Many traders wait for it to leave the zone, above -80 or below -20, before acting.

## Using both together

There is little reason to plot both, because they carry the same information. Williams %R equals Fast Stochastic %K minus 100, so a %R of -20 matches a %K of 80. Their signals match. If you want a second view, use a different kind of tool, such as [RSI](/rsi/) or [support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/).

## Common mix-ups

- **Reading -80 as high.** The negative scale puts the bottom of the range at -100.
- **Treating them as different tools.** %R is a flipped Fast Stochastic %K.
- **Comparing fast and slow values.** Platforms label them differently.
- **Selling every time they hit an extreme in an uptrend.** Both can stay at an extreme for long periods.
- **Counting both as two confirmations.** They show the same thing.

## FAQ

**Is Williams %R the same as the Stochastic?**
They are closely related. %R is the Fast Stochastic %K flipped and shifted by 100. The usual Stochastic adds smoothing and a signal line, so it looks calmer.

**Which one is better?**
Neither. The signals match. Choose the scale and smoothing you find easier to read, and test on your market.

**Why is %R negative?**
It is a charting convention. Williams plotted it from 0 at the top of the range to -100 at the bottom.

**Do the levels match?**
Yes in effect. Stochastic 80 and 20 correspond to %R -20 and -80.
