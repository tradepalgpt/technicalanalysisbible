A falling wedge and a descending triangle both squeeze price between two lines, and both have a falling upper line. In a falling wedge, both lines slope downward and converge, and the break is usually up. In a descending triangle, the lower line is flat and only the upper line falls, and the break is usually down.

## At a glance

| | Falling Wedge | Descending Triangle |
|---|---|---|
| Upper line | Slopes down | Slopes down |
| Lower line | Slopes down, less steeply | Flat |
| Usual read | Bullish | Bearish continuation |
| Confirmation | Close above the upper trendline | Close below the flat line |
| Origin | Not attributed to one person; Edwards and Magee, Bulkowski | Not attributed to one person; Edwards and Magee |
| Mirror pattern | Rising wedge | Ascending triangle |

## When to use the Falling Wedge

A [falling wedge](/falling-wedge/) is a way to spot selling pressure fading. Highs and lows keep falling, but the range narrows. It is usually read as bullish, whether it forms after a fall or inside an uptrend. A close above the upper line is the usual signal.

## When to use the Descending Triangle

A [descending triangle](/descending-triangle/) shows price pressing the same support while making lower highs. It is usually read as a bearish continuation pattern. A close below the flat line is the usual signal.

## Using both together

You do not use these two on the same pattern. The task is telling which one you have. Check whether the lower line is flat or falling. A flat bottom points to a triangle; a falling bottom points to a wedge. Some shapes sit in between, so wait for the break and a [volume](/volume/) check before acting.

## Common mix-ups

- **Calling any falling squeeze a triangle.** A triangle needs a flat bottom.
- **Expecting a downward break from a wedge.** Wedges are usually read as bullish.
- **Acting before the close.** Wait for a close beyond the line.
- **Drawing lines to fit.** Lines should touch several highs and lows.

## FAQ

**How do I tell a falling wedge from a descending triangle?**
Look at the lower line. If it is flat, it is a triangle. If it slopes down with the upper line, it is a wedge.

**Is a falling wedge bullish or bearish?**
Usually read as bullish, because it ends with a break above the upper line.

**Is a descending triangle always bearish?**
It is usually read as bearish, but breaks can fail. Wait for a close below the flat line.

**Do I need volume to confirm?**
A rise in volume on the break is the usual extra check.
