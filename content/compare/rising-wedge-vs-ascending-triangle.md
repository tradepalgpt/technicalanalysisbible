A rising wedge and an ascending triangle both squeeze price between two lines, and both have a rising lower line. In a rising wedge, both lines slope upward and converge, and the break is usually down. In an ascending triangle, the top line is flat and only the lower line rises, and the break is usually up.

## At a glance

| | Rising Wedge | Ascending Triangle |
|---|---|---|
| Upper line | Slopes up | Flat |
| Lower line | Slopes up, more steeply | Slopes up |
| Usual read | Bearish | Bullish continuation |
| Confirmation | Close below the lower trendline | Close above the flat line |
| Origin | Not attributed to one person; Edwards and Magee, Bulkowski | Not attributed to one person; Edwards and Magee |
| Mirror pattern | Falling wedge | Descending triangle |

## When to use the Rising Wedge

A [rising wedge](/rising-wedge/) is a way to spot rallies losing strength. Highs and lows keep rising, but the range narrows. It is usually read as bearish, whether it forms after a rise or inside a downtrend. The close below the lower line is the usual signal.

## When to use the Ascending Triangle

An [ascending triangle](/ascending-triangle/) shows price pressing the same resistance while making higher lows. It is usually read as a bullish continuation pattern. A close above the flat line is the usual signal.

## Using both together

You do not use these two on the same pattern. The task is telling which one you have. Check whether the upper line is flat or rising. A flat top points to a triangle; a rising top points to a wedge. Some shapes sit in between, so wait for the break and a [volume](/volume/) check before acting.

## Common mix-ups

- **Calling any rising squeeze a triangle.** A triangle needs a flat top.
- **Expecting an upward break from a wedge.** Wedges are usually read as bearish.
- **Acting before the close.** Wait for a close beyond the line.
- **Drawing lines to fit.** Lines should touch several highs and lows.

## FAQ

**How do I tell a rising wedge from an ascending triangle?**
Look at the upper line. If it is flat, it is a triangle. If it slopes up with the lower line, it is a wedge.

**Is a rising wedge bullish or bearish?**
Usually read as bearish, because it ends with a break below the lower line.

**Is an ascending triangle always bullish?**
It is usually read as bullish, but breaks can fail. Wait for a close above the flat line.

**Do I need volume to confirm?**
Rising volume on the break is the usual extra check.
