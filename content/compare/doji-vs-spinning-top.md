A doji and a spinning top both show indecision, but they differ in body size. A doji has almost no body, because open and close are equal or nearly so. A spinning top has a small but visible body with wicks on both sides that are longer than the body. The doji is the sharper signal of balance. The spinning top is the softer one.

## At a glance

| | Doji | Spinning Top |
|---|---|---|
| Body | Almost none (open about equals close) | Small but visible |
| Wicks | Short, long or one-sided, depending on type | Both longer than the body, similar length |
| Common body rule | About 5 to 10% of the range or less | Under about a third of the range |
| Signal | Indecision, stronger | Indecision, weaker |
| Variants | Dragonfly, gravestone, long-legged, four-price | None standard |
| Best location | Reversal points, edges of ranges | Edges of ranges, pauses after a move |
| Origin | Japanese candlestick charting; Steve Nison, 1991 | Same |

## When to use Doji

The [Doji](/doji/) suits traders who want the clearest picture of a stalemate. Price moved, then finished where it started. After a long run or at a key level, that can show the side in control is no longer winning easily. The variants add detail: a dragonfly at support shows sellers were rejected, and a gravestone at resistance shows buyers were rejected.

## When to use Spinning Top

The [Spinning Top](/spinning-top/) is a gentler warning. The body is small but real, so one side had a slight edge by the close. It fits as the middle candle of a morning or evening star, where a small-bodied candle is expected. It is common, so it works best at a range edge or after a strong move, not in the middle of quiet trading.

## Using both together

Reading both on the same chart shows how strong the indecision is. A doji after a strong move is a bigger flag than a spinning top in the same place. Neither is a trigger. Most traders wait for the next candle to confirm, and look at [support and resistance](/support-and-resistance/) and [volume](/volume/) for context.

## Common mix-ups

- **Calling any small candle a doji.** A visible body points to a spinning top.
- **Mixing the rules.** Pick one body rule for each and apply it every time.
- **Confusing the spinning top with a hammer or shooting star.** Those have one long wick, not two.
- **Treating either as a reversal on its own.** Both are warnings that need confirmation.
- **Reading them in the middle of a range.** There they are common and mean little.

## FAQ

**Is a spinning top a type of doji?**
No. A doji has almost no body. A spinning top has a small but clear body. They are close relatives, and a spinning top with a tiny body starts to look like a long-legged doji, but they are treated as separate patterns.

**Which is the stronger signal?**
The doji is usually seen as the stronger sign of indecision, since open and close nearly match. Both are weak on their own and depend on where they appear and what the next candle does.

**How small must the body be for a doji?**
There is no official rule. Many traders count a body of about 5 to 10% of the candle's range or less. Choose a rule and stay with it.

**Do colour and direction matter?**
For a spinning top, very little. For a doji, the type matters more than any colour, since a dragonfly and a gravestone point in opposite directions.
