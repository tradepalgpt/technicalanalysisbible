Renko and Heikin-Ashi are both chart types that smooth price so trends are easier to see. Renko draws equal-sized bricks and ignores time. Heikin-Ashi keeps time and draws candlesticks whose values are averaged with the previous bar. Both lag and show filtered or averaged prices rather than every move.

## At a glance

| | Renko | Heikin-Ashi |
|---|---|---|
| Draws | Equal-sized bricks at 45-degree steps | Candles averaged with the previous candle |
| Time axis | None; a brick forms only on a set move | Yes, one candle per period |
| Origin | Japanese; introduced to the West by Steve Nison, 1994 | Not attributed to one person; Japanese origin |
| Main setting | Brick size, often a multiple of ATR(14) | None |
| Best use | Seeing trends and key levels | Judging whether a trend is intact |
| Main weakness | Timing information lost; can lag | Prices are averages, not real prices |

## When to use Renko

A [Renko chart](/renko-chart/) suits traders who want noise removed. Small moves do not make a brick, so trends and levels stand out. It takes a brick size, often based on [ATR](/atr/). It is useful for spotting support and resistance, but it hides how long moves took.

## When to use Heikin-Ashi

[Heikin-Ashi](/heikin-ashi/) suits traders who want to stay in a trend longer. It keeps normal time bars, so you can see when moves happened. A run of same-coloured candles suggests an intact trend. There are no settings to choose.

## Using both together

Some traders use Heikin-Ashi on a higher time frame for the trend and Renko for cleaner levels. Both hide real prices, so confirm entries and stops on a normal [candlestick chart](/candlestick-chart/). They are both smoothing methods, so agreement between them is only a weak confirmation, and neither adds independent evidence.

## Common mix-ups

- **Reading Heikin-Ashi prices as real.** The values are averages.
- **Expecting time on Renko.** Bricks form on price moves, not on a clock.
- **Using the wrong brick size.** Too small leaves noise; too large hides trades.
- **Treating either as a signal.** Both are ways of viewing price.

## FAQ

**What is the main difference between Renko and Heikin-Ashi?**
Renko ignores time and draws equal bricks. Heikin-Ashi keeps time and draws averaged candles.

**Which is better for trends?**
Neither is better in general. Both make trends clearer but lag. Choose the one that fits your time frame.

**Does Renko have settings?**
Yes, mainly brick size, fixed or based on ATR. Heikin-Ashi has no settings.

**Can I trade directly from either chart?**
Prices are not the real prices, so check entry and stop levels on a normal chart.
